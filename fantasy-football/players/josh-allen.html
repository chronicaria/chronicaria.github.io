The one rushing QB the market prices as a genuine edge — and taking him at 24 or 25 means paying a second-round price for the position, which Late-round quarterback argues against unless the price is right.

The role

He is a rushing QB: a quarterback who runs by design rather than only when a play collapses. That matters because rushing yards pay 0.1 a yard and a rushing touchdown pays six against a passing touchdown's four, so he collects a second income stream nobody else at his position has. The note column of Data/players.csv puts him at 567 projected rushing yards — comfortably past the 350-yard line that separates the archetype from a pocket QB, who throws and nothing else.

The number

366.2 projected points, from the proj_points column of Data/players.csv — a projection dated 2026-07-23, not an observation, computed from a stat line under this league's own scoring. That is the largest raw total of anyone at your turn, and it is exactly the trap. Every team starts one quarterback and the twelfth-best is close behind the fourth, so raw points at this position convert into very little edge over the quarterback you could have had for nothing. Measured against a typical twelfth-ranked starter, the best quarterback is worth about 4.4 points a week — against 10.7 for the best running back and 8.5 for the best receiver.

The risk

The CSV flags him low risk, and that is the wrong axis. The risk here is not that he underperforms; it is the opportunity cost. His ADP is 28.1 — a Fantasy Football Calculator PPR proxy, not ESPN's own — so taking him at 24 or 25 means paying a second-round price for the position with the smallest edge on the board, and spending the one turn that is your only shot at a second startable receiver. Availability does not rescue the case either — he has not been listed on an official injury report once in three seasons, which leaves the argument against him purely one of opportunity cost.

The context

He is 30 on the September 9 opener, drafted 7th overall by Buffalo in 2018 — first-round capital that has been repaid (Draft capital dominance). He is the most available player at this turn by a distance: 17, 16 and 16 games, 49 of a possible 51. Across three full seasons of official weekly injury reports he was never listed Out, never listed Questionable or Doubtful, and no body part appears against his name at all. The two missed games never touched the report, so they were not medical. In 2025 he played 93 percent of Buffalo's snaps, ran 112 carries for 579 rushing yards and threw for 3,668 — so the 567 rushing yards the projection leans on is essentially what he already did. He is paid $55 million a year and is not a free agent until 2031.

Open questions

  • Whether the 4.4-point weekly edge holds once his rushing projection is stress-tested — a rushing QB is the only case where the flat-position argument can genuinely break.
  • Bye week 7. Quarterback is the easiest position to cover for one week, so this is a cost you can absorb.
