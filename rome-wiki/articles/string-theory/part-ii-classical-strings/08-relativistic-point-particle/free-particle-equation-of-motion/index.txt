1f:T3ccce,<h2 id="straightness-from-extremal-length">Straightness from extremal length</h2>
<p>A free particle sweeps out a worldline, and its action measures one thing: the proper length <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>∫</mo><mi>d</mi><mi>s</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\int ds</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1.1111em;vertical-align:-0.3061em;"></span><span class="mop op-symbol small-op" style="margin-right:0.1945em;position:relative;top:-0.0006em;">∫</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal">s</span></span></span></span> of that curve (equivalently the proper time <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>∫</mo><mi>d</mi><mi>s</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\int ds/c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1.1111em;vertical-align:-0.3061em;"></span><span class="mop op-symbol small-op" style="margin-right:0.1945em;position:relative;top:-0.0006em;">∫</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal">s</span><span class="mord">/</span><span class="mord mathnormal">c</span></span></span></span> its onboard clock ticks off). Nature picks the worldline that extremizes this length, and the extremal worldline turns out to be straight — where "straight in spacetime" is exactly the statement that the four-momentum never changes. That is Newton's first law — a free body moves in a straight line at constant velocity — written so that every inertial observer sees the same equation.</p>
<p>One deliberate choice before the algebra: we refuse to privilege time as the clock ticking off the motion. We use an arbitrary label <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>τ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\tau</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.1132em;">τ</span></span></span></span> along the curve, so space and time enter symmetrically, and the answer comes out parameter-blind — it holds no matter which ruler we lay along the worldline. Why the parameter drops out is proved once and for all in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-ii-classical-strings/08-relativistic-point-particle/reparameterization-invariance/" class="wiki-link">Reparameterization invariance</a>; this note is the derivation half of that story.</p>
