How much to spend on running backs

In this 10-team PPR league, start with an elite anchor at 1.01, then let the board—not an old zero-RB slogan—decide when the first and second backs belong.

The structural answer

The lineup starts two RBs and one RB/WR/TE FLEX. The current 10-team replacement model therefore uses RB24, against WR26, TE10, and QB10. That is a shallower demand pool than the prior 12-team build: a merely usable RB2 is easier to replace, while an elite, workload-secure back remains a meaningful weekly advantage.

If the best 1.01 asset is a running back, Hero/Anchor RB is the default shape: take that premium back, then build receiving depth and return to RB only when the current 10-team board says the RB edge beats the alternatives. If the 1.01 decision is Puka Nacua instead, this becomes a selective-RB plan rather than a reason to force a back. “Hero RB” and “Anchor RB” are the same idea: one premium back, then selective spending rather than automatic RB2.

Robust RB—two early backs—is viable only if a remaining back is clearly above RB24 and the receiver/TE alternatives are not. Zero RB remains a high-variance construction, but it is a worse fit here because weekly inverse-standings waiver priority makes the best in-season breakout claims hardest to win once your team is successful.

The 1.01 draft rhythm

Your 16 picks are 1 · 20 · 21 · 40 · 41 · 60 · 61 · 80 · 81 · 100 · 101 · 120 · 121 · 140 · 141 · 160. After 1.01, eighteen selections disappear before the 20/21 turn. That blind window is long enough that chasing one named player or forcing a positional sequence is brittle.

  • Pick 1: take the top 1.01 option from the freshly rendered board; this guide does not override the dedicated 1.01 decision.
  • 20/21 and 40/41: prioritize the best tier value among WR, TE, and a true RB2. Do not take an RB simply because you drafted one at 1.01.
  • 60/61 through 100/101: add RBs when they have a credible path to workload or receiving work; avoid paying a starter price for a fragile committee role.
  • 120/121 onward: target contingent upside, direct handcuffs to your own cornerstone, and roles that can become usable after one depth-chart change. Do not hoard low-upside handcuffs across the league.

League settings that change the risk tradeoff

The regular season runs Weeks 1–14, with the top six of ten teams advancing to a Weeks 15–17 playoff. Prizes remain $500/$250/$150 for the top three. That asks for a reliable weekly floor early enough to make the six-team field, then upside from the bench and waiver wire once the roster has a foundation.
