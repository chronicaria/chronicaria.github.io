17:Tdbe,<h2 id="the-core-idea">The core idea</h2>
<p>In the Standard Model, an electron or a quark is a mathematical <em>point</em> — zero size. String theory replaces each point with a string roughly the Planck length (~10⁻³⁵ m), far too small to see directly. Like a violin string, it can vibrate in different modes, and <strong>each mode looks like a different particle</strong> with a particular mass and charge. So one kind of object, vibrating differently, could in principle reproduce the whole particle zoo.</p>
<h2 id="why-physicists-care">Why physicists care</h2>
<ul>
<li>A string's vibration spectrum <em>automatically</em> includes a massless spin-2 mode — and a massless spin-2 particle is exactly what a <strong>graviton</strong> (the quantum of gravity) must be. Gravity falls out of the theory rather than being bolted on, which is why string theory is the leading candidate for <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/the-graviton-and-quantum-gravity/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">quantum gravity +</a> — uniting general relativity with quantum mechanics.</li>
<li>It is highly constrained: mathematical consistency forces specific features (see below) rather than letting you tune anything.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="the-strings-attached-the-hard-parts">The strings attached (the hard parts)</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Extra dimensions.</strong> Consistency requires more than the 3+1 spacetime dimensions we observe — 26 for the early <em>bosonic</em> string, <strong>10</strong> for <em>superstring</em> theory. The extra ones are presumed curled up too small to notice → <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/why-string-theory-needs-extra-dimensions/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Why string theory needs extra dimensions +</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Five theories, then M-theory.</strong> There were five consistent superstring theories; in the 1990s they were shown to be limits of a single 11-dimensional framework, <strong>M-theory</strong> → <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/m-theory-and-the-five-string-theories/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">M-theory and the five string theories +</a>.</li>
<li><strong>No experimental confirmation.</strong> The natural energy scale is the Planck scale, hopelessly beyond current colliders. As of now there is <em>no</em> direct experimental evidence, which is the central criticism of the program.</li>
<li><strong>The landscape.</strong> The extra dimensions can curl up in a staggering number of ways (~10⁵⁰⁰ estimates), each giving different physics — so the theory struggles to predict <em>our</em> universe uniquely → <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/the-landscape-problem/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">The landscape problem +</a>.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="open-questions-for-me-to-chase">Open questions (for me to chase)</h2>
<ul class="contains-task-list">
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> <em>Why</em> does consistency force exactly 10 dimensions? What breaks otherwise?</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> What does "supersymmetry" add over the bosonic string, concretely?</li>
<li class="task-list-item"><input type="checkbox" disabled> Is there <em>any</em> near-term experiment that could falsify it?</li>
