Snapshot

  • Role: starter-or-sixth-man coin flip and secondary ballhandler; likely closer either way. A published lineup projection places him in the bench unit, and Minnesota's alternative five fields no true small forward without a McDaniels-to-power-forward slide that Chris Finch has declined.
  • 2025-26 baseline: 69 games at 27.3 MPG. Weighting his Chicago and Minnesota stints gives roughly 14.8 PTS, 3.4 REB and 3.6 AST; the 14.4 figure that circulates is his Minnesota-only split, not the full season.
  • Transaction: re-signed July 10 after agreeing to a reported five-year, $112 million contract.
  • Current market: Alex Barutha ranked him 90th; Hashtag's July keeper crowd ranked him 85th.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: 75-110. Efficient scoring, useful steals, and low turnovers make him a roto-friendly late pick.
  • Points: 70-110. Ball and Edwards cap primary-playmaker volume, so the scoring system must reward efficiency or defensive events.
  • Dynasty: 70-110. The long contract and two-way role stabilize value, but star-level usage is unlikely.

Role, floor and ceiling

The previous version of this section had Minnesota starting Dosunmu at small forward on the theory that McDaniels handles larger wings. That required McDaniels to play power forward, which Finch has said he does not intend at the outset, so the more likely shape is Dosunmu leading the second unit at 26-28 minutes and closing games. Floor: 26 minutes as the sixth man, producing useful percentages without enough counting stats for 12-team relevance. Ceiling: 33 minutes as a permanent starter and closer, with 16 points, five assists, and 1.3 steals for top-65 value. The gap between those branches is smaller than it looks, because the closing minutes — the ones that carry his counting stats — are his in both.

Risk monitor

Calf tightness affected his 2025-26 playoff run. His extreme shooting efficiency may regress, and Ball's arrival limits lead-guard repetitions. The more important positive is contract and lineup fit: Minnesota paid for a high-minute decision-maker, not a fringe reserve.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Illinois, three seasons (2018-19 through 2020-21). 13.8 points, 4.0 rebounds, 3.3 assists on 43.5/35.2/69.5 as a freshman; 16.6/4.3/3.3 on 48.4/29.6/75.5 as a sophomore; 20.1/6.3/5.3 on 48.8/39.0/78.3 as a junior. Won the 2021 Bob Cousy Award as the nation's top point guard, was a consensus first-team All-American, took Big Ten Tournament Most Outstanding Player, and made All-Big Ten first team in 2020 and 2021. Illinois honored his No. 11 in 2022. Draft: 2021, second round, 38th pick, Chicago Bulls.

Dosunmu was traded from Chicago to Minnesota on February 4, 2026, with Julian Phillips, for Rob Dillingham, Leonard Miller, and four second-round picks. His 2025-26 season therefore splits 45 games in Chicago and 24 in Minnesota, 69 in total. Weighting the two stints gives a full-season line of roughly 27.3 MPG, 14.8 points, 3.4 rebounds, 3.6 assists, 0.9 steals, 0.3 blocks, 1.8 threes, 1.4 turnovers. Combined shooting percentages are not recomputed here because attempt totals were not verified; the per-team splits above are the verified figures.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag were not verifiable for any season in this research pass, so no year-by-year finish is asserted. The one external marker found is a Yahoo positional rank of about 60th among guards for 2025-26, which is consistent with late-round overall value but is not an overall nine-category finish. Shape rather than rank is the reliable read: he has never been a points-league asset at his usage, because the counting stats are thin and the volume modest, but the category profile is friendlier — strong field-goal percentage, a free-throw percentage that has climbed into genuinely positive territory, low turnovers for a ballhandler, and steals that hover around neutral. Treat him as a categories specialist whose value comes from not hurting you anywhere.

Durability: 77, 80, 76, 46, 69. The first three seasons were close to ironman. The 2024-25 collapse to 46 games was a single event, a left shoulder subluxation that ended his season outright in February 2025 rather than an accumulation of absences. In 2025-26 he missed time early with a quadriceps injury and dealt with finger and calf complaints later; the calf recurred in the 2026 playoffs and cost him Game 6 of the Denver series. No chronic lower-body pattern is established, but the calf is the item to track into camp.

Trajectory: Two distinct steps, and only the second one is interesting. The 2023-24 jump from 8.6 to 12.2 points was mostly opportunity, since his minutes rose and his per-36 scoring moved only from 11.8 to 15.1. The 2025-26 jump was different: his scoring rose while his minutes fell, from 12.3 points in 30.3 minutes to 15.0 in 26.4 in Chicago, a per-36 move from 14.6 to 20.5 that reflects a genuine change in shot diet and aggression rather than playing time. The caution is that the efficiency carrying it sits on his least stable skill. His three-point percentage by season reads .376, .312, .403, .328, then .451 in Chicago and .414 in Minnesota, a sawtooth that has never held two good years together, so the 2026-27 projection should regress the shooting hard even while crediting the volume change as real. The free-throw improvement is the durable gain, a steady climb from .679 as a rookie to the mid-.800s and above, and that is a skill that tends to stick. At 26 he is at the top of his physical curve with no aging discount yet, and Minnesota just paid him five years and $112 million, which argues for a real role; but note that his signature performance, the 43-point Game 4 against Denver, came off the bench, so the dossier's assumption of a starting job is a projection rather than an established fact.
