His last live baseline was 24.7 points, 4.8 rebounds, 4.6 assists, and 40% three-point shooting before the March 2025 injury. The desk discounts usage, burst, and minutes while preserving the elite FT% and efficient-guard archetype.

Correction 2026-07-19: base GP lowered from 60 to 55. Sixty games would exceed either of his last two active seasons (58 in 2023-24, 50 in 2024-25 per StatMuse) in his first year back from ACL reconstruction at age 34, after a season with zero games played. His July self-assessment is "definitely close to being over at 100%" — close, not cleared (ClutchPoints, July 2026) — and the evidence boundary above already notes that camp-readiness optimism is not a medical clearance. 55 preserves the expected opening-night availability while pricing in the back-to-back and ramp management that first-season-back ACL guards reliably receive.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: per-game top-45 upside; rounds 5–7 only if the league has IL capacity. Pairing him with another fragile early pick compounds weekly lineup risk.
  • Points: strong active-game scorer but totals formats punish missed games more directly. Weekly-lock leagues require a larger discount than daily moves.
  • Dynasty: win-now asset, not a rebuilding cornerstone. Age and the lost 2025-26 season make any top-75 valuation fragile.
  • External marker: SI placed him 34th in a broad early top-50; that ranking describes talent and return narrative more than injury-adjusted nine-category totals.

Role, floor, ceiling, uncertainty

  • Median role: starting lead guard and primary late-clock creator for 31 MPG, with Flagg sharing initiation.
  • Floor: fewer than 45 games, 26–28 MPG, reduced rim pressure, and intermittent rest; that can be a roster-clogging season.
  • Ceiling: 65+ games at 33 MPG and roughly 24/4/5 on elite percentages, a top-30 per-game return.
  • Evidence boundary: Dallas ruled him out for all of 2025-26 on February 18. A February 20 team story said the longer runway should make him ready for 2026-27 camp, but that was not a medical clearance or live-contact result.
  • Contingency winners: Flagg receives usage; Nembhard receives assists; Sasser receives shots; De Larrea receives development minutes.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Duke, one season (2010-11). He played only 11 games — a right big toe ligament injury in the ninth game cost him 26 games before he returned for the NCAA tournament — and averaged 17.5 points, 3.4 rebounds and 4.3 assists on .529 from the field (55-of-104), .462 from three (18-of-39) and .901 at the line (64-of-71), scoring in double figures in all 11 appearances. He was a Freshman of the Year contender before the injury and left after one year. Draft: 2011, first round, first overall, Cleveland. Not traded on draft night.

The 2022-23 season is shown as two rows because he was traded from Brooklyn to Dallas on February 6, 2023; the combined line was 60 games at roughly 27.1 points, 5.1 rebounds and 5.5 assists. He did not appear in a game in 2025-26, so no row is stated rather than a zero line.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks across a 15-year career are not verifiable from public archives and are not reconstructed here. The category shape is stable and well documented by the raw table: he has been a first-tier free throw asset for essentially his entire career, clearing .870 in every season and .900 or better in six of the last eight, with strong field goal percentage from 2016-17 onward and three-point volume that climbed from roughly 1.5 makes in Cleveland to 3.0-plus in Brooklyn and Dallas. Rebounds are thin, blocks are near zero, and steals are league-average for a guard. Turnovers, which were a genuine problem early at 3.1 and 3.2, became a strength — 1.8 and 2.2 in the two Dallas seasons on high usage. Points leagues have consistently rated him below his category rank because the profile leans on percentages, which carry no weight there, while the low rebound and assist totals do.

Durability: 51, 59, 71, 75, 53, 72, 60, 67, 20, 54, 29, 60, 58, 50, 0. He has cleared 70 games three times in fifteen seasons and has not done it since 2016-17. The causes are not one recurring thing, which is part of why the history is hard to project from: a broken right hand and a fractured face in the Cleveland years, a fractured left kneecap in the 2015 Finals that delayed his 2015-16 debut to December 20 and required follow-up procedures in March and April 2018, a right shoulder impingement that cost 26 games in 2019-20 before season-ending surgery announced February 20, 2020, the New York City vaccine mandate that made him a part-time player in 2021-22 until a January 5 debut, a five-game suspension in November 2022, and assorted foot and thumb issues in Dallas. The current one dominates everything else: he tore his left ACL in the first quarter against Sacramento on March 3, 2025, and Dallas ruled him out for the entire 2025-26 season on February 18, 2026. He will enter 2026-27 having not played an NBA game in roughly nineteen months.

Trajectory: The per-game skill curve here is unusually flat for a player his age — he scored 24.7 per game at 32 on .473/.401/.916, which is not materially different from what he did at 26, and his turnover rate actually improved as he aged. That is the case for him. Everything against him is structural rather than skill: the games-played line has been trending down since 2016-17, and an ACL reconstruction at 33 in a knee that already had a patella fracture and two cleanup procedures is the single worst combination in this profile. The relevant comparison is not his own 2024-25 baseline but what guards in their mid-thirties look like in year one back from ACL surgery, which is typically a reduced burst, fewer rim attempts, more pull-up jumpers, and a minutes cap. His game is already jumper-heavy and his free throw and three-point efficiency should survive intact, so the per-game floor is higher than most returning-ACL cases. The realistic 2026-27 outcome is a good but not elite per-game line across a games total that decides whether he was worth the pick, and Dallas has both a reason (Flagg's development) and the depth to sit him.
