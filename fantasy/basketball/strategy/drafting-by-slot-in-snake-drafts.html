Drafting by slot in snake drafts

Every snake slot receives identical total pick capital after any even round, so slot strategy is entirely a question of how that capital is distributed — and on the encyclopedia's own board the distribution favours pick 1 and hands pick 12 a forced-variance problem it must solve deliberately.

The arithmetic every slot argument rests on

In an \(N\)-team snake, the overall pick number for slot \(p\) in round \(r\) is

\[ k(p,r)=\begin{cases}(r-1)N+p & r \text{ odd}\\[2pt] rN-p+1 & r \text{ even}\end{cases} \]

Consecutive gaps for a fixed slot alternate between $2(N-p)+1$ and $2p-1$. Two consequences matter.

Total capital is exactly equal. Summing $k(p,r)$ over any even number of rounds gives the same total for every slot. Over four rounds in a 12-team league every slot's picks sum to 98; over four rounds in a 10-team league every slot sums to 82. There is no "good slot" in the sense of more board.

Distribution is not equal. Slot 1 in a 12-team draft owns picks \(\{1,24,25,48\}\); slot 12 owns \(\{12,13,36,37\}\). Same mean (24.5), very different spread (sum of squared deviations 1105 versus 577). Because value per rank is convex decreasing — steep early, flat later — the more dispersed pick set is worth more.

The convexity is not an assumption here; it is visible in the tier widths of 2026-27 fantasy basketball rankings:

Tier Rank range Width
1 — league-defining engines 1–4 4
2 — first-round anchors 5–12 8
3 — second-round anchors 13–24 12
4 — early core 25–48 24
5 — middle-round core 49–84 36
6 — build shapers 85–120 36
7 — standard-roster depth 121–156 36
8 — replacement and watch pool 157–200 44

Tier width grows monotonically. A slot that reaches into the width-4 tier is buying something no later pick can replicate; a slot whose earliest access is width-12 is paying the same total capital for a flatter distribution. The pick-1 advantage is real but small and entirely front-loaded.

What the turn actually buys. The compensation for the turn is not value, it is certainty about a pair. Back-to-back picks are the only place in a snake draft where a manager can guarantee a two-player combination that no opponent can break up (Draft Day Authority). The cost is the 23-pick blind window that follows, which is where positional and category runs do their damage.

Gap structure, 12-team

Middle slots minimise the longest blind window. Slot 6 never faces more than 13 unseen picks; slot 1 and slot 12 each face 23. This is why middle slots are the easiest to draft badly but adequately, and why the ends reward preparation.

Branch decisions by slot

Slot 1. Wembanyama is one of four assets the rankings label with no modeled hard drain and a Stable risk code. Having bought certainty, spend the 24/25 turn on variance rather than doubling down on safety: Jamal Murray plus one of Mobley (27) or Harden (29). The recurring slot-1 error is taking two more Stable balanced players at the turn, which converts a convex advantage into a flat roster.

Slot 4. Dončić hands you PTS/AST/3s with a turnover drag already installed. At 21 the board offers Embiid — a per-game rate coordinate the encyclopedia explicitly warns is a 44-game median. If you take him you have spent two picks on categories that overlap (PTS) and inherited two risk flags. The defensible branch is to pass Embiid for LaMelo Ball (22) only if you intend a punt-FG build, or drop to Holmgren (18, if he survives) for orthogonal BLK/REB/FG.

Slot 6. The most balanced slot on this board and the one with the least excuse. Cunningham and Flagg both project as multi-category engines without hard drains, meaning you arrive at pick 30 with no build committed. That is the ideal state for a best-player-available drafter and the worst state for anyone who wants a punt.

Slot 9. Jalen Johnson at 9 is the encyclopedia's most explicit editorial restoration — the mechanical table undersold him while four external boards placed him 8–11. Pairing him with Towns at 16 stacks REB/AST and leaves you needing STL/BLK by pick 33, which is exactly when Amen Thompson (32) and Derrick White (34) are live.

Slot 12 — the interesting case. Strict board order hands you Giannis and Haliburton. This is category-incoherent: Giannis carries a TO/FT drag, Haliburton is an elite FT and 3PM engine. Buying both means you have no punt and two availability flags (Giannis Y·60 GP, Haliburton O·66 GP post-Achilles).

The better branch is to pass Haliburton for Holmgren (18) — a five-rank concession that buys a coherent punt-FT spine of BLK/REB/FG. The tiers note asserts that punt-FT players "can jump a tier," and a tier at that depth is 12–24 ranks wide. Trading five ranks of board for a tier of build coherence is favourable if you complete the punt, which the round 3/4 turn does naturally: Jackson Jr. (36) and Duren (37) both fit. Slot 12 does not get to choose whether it takes variance; it only gets to choose which variance, and it should choose the kind that stacks.

10-team, nine-cat: the same structure with better inventory

Slot R1 R2 R3 R4
1 Wembanyama (1) Murphy III (20) Embiid (21) Jalen Williams (40)
3 Gilgeous-Alexander (3) Holmgren (18) Durant (23) Adebayo (38)
5 Maxey (5) Towns (16) Reaves (25) Jackson Jr. (36)
8 Tatum (8) Haliburton (13) Markkanen (28) Irving (33)
10 Edwards (10) Barnes (11) Young (30) Giddey (31)

Four rounds of a 10-team draft consume 40 picks against 48 in a 12-team draft. Every 10-team roster therefore finishes round four entirely inside Tiers 1–4, while 12-team slots 1 and 4 are already reaching to ranks 45–48.

The turn is also qualitatively different. Slot 10 receives Edwards and Barnes — both coded Stable, both balanced, both with no modeled hard drain. Slot 12 in a 12-team league receives Giannis and Haliburton, both flagged. The "turn is great" folk claim is board-dependent, not structural: this year it is true at 10 teams and false at 12.

The practical consequence is that a 10-team drafter should be more willing to draft best-player-available deep into round four, because the board has not yet flattened. A 12-team drafter is forced into build decisions a full tier earlier.

Best player available versus steering a build

Let \(r_{\text{BPA}}\) be the best available rank and \(r_{\text{fit}}\) the best available rank that fits your emerging build. Steer when

\[ r_{\text{fit}} - r_{\text{BPA}} < \Delta_{\text{build}} \]

where \(\Delta_{\text{build}}\) is the rank-equivalent of category synergy. The tiers note's "can jump a tier" language puts \(\Delta_{\text{build}}\) at roughly one tier width — but tier width is not constant, and that is what resolves the question.

  • Rounds 1–2 (tier widths 4, 8, 12): \(\Delta_{\text{build}}\) is small in absolute ranks and you do not yet know your build. BPA dominates. Passing 8 ranks in round 1 to force a punt is a large concession against a build you have not established.
  • Round 3 onward (widths 24, 36, 36): the board is flat, so a 5–8 rank concession costs almost nothing while synergy still pays a full tier. Steering dominates.

This is why punts should emerge organically rather than be declared pre-draft — the standard advice from NBC Sports' punt guide and RotoBaller, and here it has a structural reason attached rather than just being a maxim. The crossover point is round 3 in a 12-team league and round 3–4 in a 10-team league.

Concretely: if your first two picks are Giannis and Holmgren, your build declared itself. From pick 36 onward you should be paying up to a tier for Sarr (55), Clingan (61), Edey (62), or Daniels (63) over nominally higher-ranked FT-positive guards.

Variance by slot

The general principle — early slots can afford certainty, late slots need variance — is correct in expectation but backwards in execution this year.

An early slot's round-1 asset is genuinely certain (Tier 1, Stable, no modeled hard drain), and its 24/25 turn is the cheapest place in the draft to buy variance because the board is already flattening. Early slots should therefore be the most aggressive buyers of post-injury stars and high-band rookies at the turn: Anthony Davis (15, H!·50 GP), Embiid (21, H!·44 GP), Boozer (58, Rk).

A late slot in a 12-team league has already absorbed involuntary variance at picks 12–13. Adding a rookie or a post-injury star at 36/37 compounds correlated availability risk across the roster's top four assets. Slot 12's correct move is to buy the stable version of its build — Duren (Stable per-game, G/X contract flag only) over Mark Williams (71, H, O/R·55 GP) — and take its rookie swings at picks 60 and later, where a miss costs a bench slot rather than a starting slot.

For rookie handling specifically, 2026-27 rookies and the tiers note both flag Rk as "widen the role and rate distribution" rather than a value claim. Flagg (19) is the only rookie the board places inside the top 24, and he is coded Stable — he is a certainty purchase, not a variance purchase. Every other rookie on the board is a variance purchase.

Positional runs and eligibility

Counting C and F/C shapes from the tiers note across the top 48 of the rankings board gives eleven: Wembanyama (1), Jokić (2), Towns (16), Holmgren (18), Embiid (21), Mobley (27), Jackson Jr. (36), Duren (37), Adebayo (38), Kessler (39), and Şengün (42). Eleven big-man shapes across four rounds, against 24 centre slots in a standard two-C 12-team league. That is a genuine scarcity, and it matches the external read that centres are the scarcest position while guards are the deepest (Athlon, 2026-27 position scarcity).

When the reach is correct. What is actually scarce is the bundle of FG% plus rebounding volume plus a real minutes floor. That bundle exhausts around rank 60 and does not come back.

When it is a trap. Blocks alone are not scarce. The tiers note is explicit that blocks remain available late, and the board bears it out: Myles Turner (130), Peyton Watson (148), Isaiah Jackson (190). Reaching two rounds early for a centre when your actual deficit is BLK is a positional run you should let pass. Reaching for one when your deficit is FG% and REB is correct.

Assists behave the opposite way — the tiers note states assists dry up faster than points and threes, and the board has no assist engine between Şengün (42) and Harden (29)/Quickley (65) worth the wait. If you leave round four without an assist source, you are buying Quickley or Dejounte Murray (51) at a premium.

One honest caveat: 2026-27 fantasy basketball rankings carries no sourced platform eligibility yet, and the tiers note states its position codes are "broad draft-room archetypes, not a promise of Yahoo, ESPN, Fantrax, or Sleeper eligibility." Do not run a scarcity reach off these labels until you have pulled your platform's actual eligibility.

The endgame: rounds 10–13

Roster arithmetic drives everything here. A 13-round draft consumes 130 players at 10 teams and 156 at 12 teams. Against a 200-player board:

League Players drafted Ranked players left on waivers Best undrafted player (board rank) Encyclopedia's label for that tier
10-team 130 70 ~131 Tier 7 — standard-roster depth
12-team 156 44 ~157 Tier 8 — replacement and watch pool

The marginal drafted player in a 12-team league is roughly the best available free agent in a 10-team league. This is the whole endgame difference, and it explains the standard advice that in a 10-team league there is almost always someone to plug in while a 12-team league forces genuine depth selection (RotoWire).

10-team, rounds 10–13: take darts. A failed late pick is replaced from a 70-player ranked pool whose top is Tier 7 depth. The cost of a zero is close to nothing. Target upside mechanisms: rookies with role growth, M-coded players one injury from 30 minutes, and the 2026-27 sleepers, breakouts, and fades medium-confidence bets. Do not draft safe 22-minute veterans — you can add them in November.

12-team, rounds 10–13: buy minutes. With 44 ranked players left and the rankings note stating outright that ranks 157–200 "are not automatic 12-team holds," a roster spot spent on a zero has a real chance of being uncoverable after two injuries. Prefer projected-GP floors and confirmed rotation roles: Cason Wallace (124, 76 GP), Toumani Camara (134, 76 GP), Max Christie (149, 74 GP). Take at most one dart, and take it in the final round.

The exception in both formats is the injured-list slot. If your platform grants a genuine IL spot, the last pick should be the highest-rate H! player available — Lively II (110, 38 GP) or Butler III (111, 30 GP) — because an IL slot converts a roster cost into a free option.

Open questions

  • The pick-by-pick boards above assume strict board order. Without a defensible 2026-27 ADP, the ±6-rank uncertainty band is an editorial estimate, not a measured draft-position distribution — the whole slot analysis should be re-run once real ADP exists.
  • The convexity argument treats tier width as a proxy for the value gradient. It is directionally right but is not the same as computing \(\Delta z\) per rank from the projection snapshot, which the encyclopedia cannot yet do cleanly because FGA and FTA are incomplete for many players.
  • \(\Delta_{\text{build}}\) — the rank-equivalent of punt synergy — is asserted at "roughly one tier width" from the tiers note's own language. No one has measured it. A simulation over the projection CSV could produce a real number and would change the BPA/steer crossover round.
  • Position eligibility is unsourced across the whole board, so the eleven-bigs-in-48 count is an archetype count, not a count of players who will actually fill a C slot on the user's platform.
