Fantasy profile

Vassell's 2025-26 line was 13.9 points, 2.5 threes, 4.0 rebounds, 2.5 assists, 0.9 steals and only 0.9 turnovers in 30.5 minutes. His 43.7% FG limits the value of the low-turnover scoring profile, while the crowded roster makes a return to his earlier 19-point usage unlikely. ESPN stats, accessed 2026-07-18

Tobias Harris and Julian Champagnie can absorb forward minutes; Dylan Harper can absorb guard minutes. Vassell therefore needs his two-way playoff role, not more shots, to protect fantasy value.

Ceiling and floor

  • Ceiling: top 80 if he plays 30 minutes, restores 1.3 steals and shoots 46%.
  • Floor: outside top 150 if he drops below 27 minutes while remaining a 14-point specialist.
  • Best use: late-round threes and turnover stability, especially in guard-heavy builds.
  • Dynasty: hold at a discount; the contract supports a continuing role, but the young-guard hierarchy blocks star usage.

Hashtag's No. 119 expert dynasty rank and No. 104 crowd rank appropriately treat Vassell as a useful but replaceable middle asset. Hashtag dynasty, accessed 2026-07-18 · Hashtag keeper, updated 2026-07-18

Career record

Pre-NBA: Florida State, two seasons. As a freshman in 2018-19 he was a bench specialist — 33 games, 10.7 MPG, 4.5 points, 1.5 rebounds — but led all ACC freshmen at .419 from three. The sophomore year, 2019-20, was the draft season: 30 starts, 28.8 MPG, 12.7 points, 5.1 rebounds on .490/.415 shooting, second-team All-ACC, Most Outstanding Player of the 2019 Emerald Coast Classic, and a career-high 27 points on 7-for-7 from three against Virginia Tech, on a Florida State team that won the ACC regular season. He came out of Peachtree Ridge High School in Suwanee, Georgia, where he averaged 21.6 points and 8.9 rebounds as a senior. Draft: 2020, round 1, pick 11, San Antonio — no draft-night trade; he has never played for another franchise.

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-category ranks from a rank provider (Basketball Monster / Hashtag / ESPN Player Rater) were not verifiable for this entry, so no per-season numbers are asserted; the Hashtag figures cited above in this dossier are forward-looking dynasty ranks, not historical finishes. What the box score supports: 2023-24 was unambiguously the category peak, and it is the only season where he cleared a positive contribution in six or more columns at once — 19.5 points, 2.4 threes, 4.1 assists, 1.1 steals, .472 from the field and .801 from the line against only 1.6 turnovers. The two seasons since have subtracted from that in every counting column except threes. The current shape is narrow but clean: threes and free-throw percentage are positives, turnovers at 0.9 are a genuine and underrated positive, blocks have never mattered, and field-goal percentage has slid to roughly neutral. Points leagues treat him worse than categories do, because a 0.9-turnover season is worth real money in nine-cat and almost nothing in a format that only counts what he produces.

Durability: GP by season — 62, 71, 38, 68, 64, 67. The rookie year was a rotation limit, not an injury. 2022-23 is the worst of it: a left knee problem that led to an arthroscopic procedure on 2023-01-11, roughly two months out, a return on 2023-03-02 against Indiana, and then shutdown management for the last stretch of a lost season. 2023-24 ended eight games early with a stress reaction in the third metatarsal head of his right foot, repaired surgically on 2024-06-26. That surgery cost him training camp and the first nine games of 2024-25 — he debuted on 2024-11-09 against Utah — and a knee contusion took him out again four games later. 2025-26 was a single clean cause: a left adductor strain suffered on 2025-12-29 against Cleveland, 13 games missed, return on 2026-01-26 against New Orleans on a minutes restriction, plus scattered ankle-soreness absences. He started 65 of his 67 appearances. The pattern is not a chronic condition but it is a consistent one — he has cleared 70 games once in six years, and the two structural injuries, knee and foot, were both lower-body load problems.

Trajectory: The honest reading of this arc is that Vassell peaked at 23 and has been losing usage ever since, and that the cause is roster construction rather than decline. Per 36 minutes his scoring went 16.2, 21.5, 21.2, 18.9, 16.4 across the last five seasons — the 2025-26 rate is back to where he was in 2021-22, while minutes barely moved from 33.1 to 31.0 to 30.5. That is the signature of shots being removed, not of a player getting worse, and the removal has a name: San Antonio kept adding creators around him, so the assists fell 4.1 to 2.9 to 2.5 and the turnovers fell with them to 0.9. The efficiency story argues the same way — his three-point percentage in 2025-26 was a career-best .384 and his free-throw percentage .815, both fine, while overall field-goal percentage sat near a career low, which means the shots he lost were the good self-created ones and what remains is a spot-up diet with a thin two-point profile. He turns 26 in August, so nothing here is an aging curve; he is in his physical prime and being squeezed. For 2026-27 that makes him a bet on opportunity rather than development, and the depth chart is moving the wrong way with Fox, Castle and Harper all needing possessions. The 2.5 threes and the low turnovers are the portable, repeatable core; the 19-point season is not coming back in this roster context, and the 0.9 steals are the number most worth watching, because a rebound there is the cheapest path back to real category value.
