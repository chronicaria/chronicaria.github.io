The Season

Weather and the fantasy playoffs

Football is played outdoors in December, which suppresses passing and kicking but not running — and the fantasy playoffs land in exactly the three weeks where that starts to bite.

Why you would otherwise never think of this

Basketball is entirely indoors. Every game, every arena, every month — the playing conditions are a constant, so they are not a variable you have ever had to hold in your head. This is the first structural feature of football with no basketball analogue at all, which is precisely why it is easy to miss: it is not a wrong instinct transferred, it is a blank where an instinct should be.

Roughly half the league plays outdoors, and the NFL plays through the coldest weeks of the year.

Why it belongs in this build specifically

Our playoffs are weeks 15, 16 and 17 — roughly December 17 through January 4 (see The 2026 season calendar). Those are the three coldest, windiest, wettest weeks of the NFL season, and they are the only three weeks that pay. Four of ten teams miss the six-team playoff entirely; the $900 is decided inside that window. Weather bites in exactly the weeks you cannot afford a quiet score from a starter.

That is the whole argument for the note. It is not that weather is a large effect. It is that the small effect is concentrated on the dates that matter.

What weather actually does

The mechanism is simple: throwing and kicking a ball through air are precision acts over distance, and handing a ball to someone three feet away is not.

Wind is the variable that matters most — more than temperature, and by a clear margin. Roughly 15 mph or more is where passing accuracy and field-goal range start to degrade materially, and above about 20 mph the effect is severe: deep throws come off the menu, and kicks from 45 yards out become genuinely uncertain. Below 15 mph, mostly ignore it.

Cold is a smaller effect than the broadcast coverage implies. Below about 30°F the ball is harder to grip and catch and there are more drops, but offences function. Cold alone is not a reason to bench anyone who matters.

Precipitation — rain, wet snow — mostly acts through grip and footing. Similar direction, smaller magnitude, and heavily dependent on how hard it is actually coming down at kickoff rather than what the forecast said on Thursday.

The position-by-position read:

Position Effect of bad weather
Kicker Largest downgrade. Distance and accuracy both suffer, and this league charges minus one point for any miss.
Quarterback Real downgrade, concentrated on volume and deep throws. A pocket QB loses more than a rushing QB, whose rushing income is unaffected.
Wide receiver Real downgrade, worst for a field-stretching Z whose value is deep targets. A big slot working short throws loses least.
Tight end Modest. Short-area targets, similar to a big slot.
Running back Close to neutral, and occasionally better. An early-down committee back or a three-down bell cow on a team playing conservatively may see more carries, not fewer.
D/ST Mildly positive. Sloppy conditions mean more fumbles and fewer yards allowed, which this league's yards-allowed brackets reward.

The false friend to avoid: "bad weather, low-scoring game, fade everyone." Half right. Fade the passing game. Do not fade the running back — the same conditions that shrink the pie also push a larger share of it toward the ground. See Opportunity share.

Where weather can never apply

Eleven teams play home games under a roof. For these, forecast is irrelevant to the home game, always. Roof status as of 2026-07-25:

Retractable roofs are essentially always closed in December, so treat all ten venues as indoor for playoff purposes.

Where it does apply

The cold-weather outdoor sites — these are the December venues to actually check:

Buffalo · Green Bay · Chicago · Cleveland · New England · New York (both Giants and Jets, same stadium) · Pittsburgh · Philadelphia · Baltimore · Cincinnati · Denver · Kansas City · Washington

Of those, the reliably windy ones are Chicago and Cleveland (both on Lake Erie/Michigan), Buffalo, New England, and the New York stadium, whose open bowl produces swirling wind that ruins kicks in particular.

Denver is a special case worth knowing: it is cold, but the altitude thins the air and the ball travels noticeably farther. Kickers are helped there, not hurt.

The remaining outdoor teams — Miami, Tampa Bay, Jacksonville, Carolina, Tennessee, San Francisco, Seattle — play in climates where December weather rarely reaches the thresholds above. Seattle is wet but not usually windy or cold.

Remember this is about the venue of the game, not the team. A cold-weather team playing in Miami in week 16 has no weather problem.

What this is, and what it is not

It is a start/sit input for kickers and marginal passing games. It is not a draft input.

Do not draft around December weather in August. The effect is real, it is small, and it is unknowable in July — you cannot forecast a specific Sunday five months out, and you do not yet know which of your players will still be starters. Nothing on the 2026 draft board moves because of this note.

The one place it earns its keep is Sunday morning, on the three decisions you actually have live: FLEX, kicker, defence (Start-sit — a standing rule, not a research project). Check the wind for outdoor games only, and only in weeks 15–17. If your streamed kicker is in a 20 mph wind and an alternative is playing indoors, switch. That is close to the entire practical application.

One caution about how much edge is here. Betting markets price forecasts in before you see them — by Sunday, a windy game's total has usually already dropped, so Implied team total has partly absorbed the information. The place the market absorbs it least is the kicker, because a miss costing minus one point is a scoring quirk of this league that has nothing to do with the game total. Kicker is where this note pays.

Open questions

  • Roof status above is from 2026-07-25 general knowledge, not verified against a 2026 source. Buffalo's new open-air stadium and Tennessee's planned domed replacement both sit near this window; confirm both before week 15.
  • The 15 mph threshold is a widely-repeated rule of thumb, not something measured from this league's scoring. The direction is solid; the exact cut is not.
  • How much a forecast is already priced into the game total is unmeasured here. If it is fully priced, the only residual edge is the kicker, and this note should shrink to one line.
