Josh Lloyd and Locked On Fantasy Basketball

Josh Lloyd's Locked On Fantasy Basketball is a high-frequency analyst feed for role changes, statistical regression, category construction, draft prospects, and offseason news; as of July 18, it supplied substantial 2026-27-forward analysis but no public, versioned all-player rankings or projection table that could be imported as a numerical source of record.

Source identity and canonical surfaces

  • Host/creator: Josh Lloyd; the public show description calls Locked On Fantasy Basketball a daily fantasy-basketball podcast with news, analysis, and an explicit fantasy lens. Official Locked On show page
  • Canonical audio feed: Simplecast RSS. The feed is the cleanest source for exact title, publication time, episode sequence, description, duration, and enclosure. It was current through July 17, 2026 at 01:43 UTC when extracted.
  • Apple Podcasts: show page. Individual Apple episode pages expose useful descriptions and dates but returned intermittent HTTP 500 responses during a link audit; the RSS feed remained available.
  • YouTube: @LockedOnFantasyBasketball, canonical channel ID UC1I9lmlRwqSehJzlRo6DKqg; XML video feed. The channel includes full episodes plus short clips, so clip titles must not be mistaken for separate rankings.
  • Cadence: daily during the season and frequent through the offseason. The source is therefore best treated as a dated stream of revisions, not a static preseason publication.

Bottom line for the encyclopedia

  1. Usable now: attributed opinions about changing roles, statistical sustainability, category relationships, rookie/dynasty tiers, free-agency fallout, and team/player watch lists.
  2. Not publicly available at cutoff: a complete, season-labelled 2026-27 rank, ADP set, auction board, game projection, minute projection, or all-player nine-category table authored by Lloyd.
  3. Do not manufacture ranks from episode order. “Top 9,” “seven rookies,” mock-draft order, and discussion sequence answer different questions. None is automatically a 12-team redraft board.
  4. Episode descriptions are metadata, not transcripts. This note records subjects and the claims explicitly present in those descriptions. It does not put unverified wording, numerical ranks, or projections in Lloyd's mouth.
  5. Freshness matters more than reputation. A 2025-26 team preview, April schedule episode, or season recap remains useful historical context but cannot override July transactions or become a 2026-27 projection merely because it is still searchable.

Methodology signals worth preserving

  • Separate skill from opportunity. The offseason role episode and prospect series repeatedly make landing spot, minutes, usage, and development horizon explicit. A player can be a strong prospect and a poor Year 1 redraft pick.
  • Regress unstable inputs. July episodes isolate shooting percentages, defensive stats, late-season splits, and team context instead of rolling the last leaderboard forward unchanged.
  • Builds change rank. Category correlations mean one universal rank conceals strategy: assists/points tend to travel together; high 3PM can fight FG%; blocks and steals can be scarcer independent levers; turnover value changes between roto and head-to-head.
  • Do not worship last year's rank. The July 7 regression episode directly frames 2025-26 results as a starting point, not a forecast.
  • Use ranges around unresolved roles. Free-agency and camp battles make precise July minute forecasts fragile. The encyclopedia should retain base/floor/ceiling branches and update after official signings and preseason rotations.
  • Format must always travel with the take. Rookie dynasty, nine-category, points, roto, and head-to-head advice answer different questions.

Access and data-quality limitations

  • Apple descriptions sometimes read like promotional summaries. They are sufficient to verify episode subjects and named players, not every conclusion or numerical assertion made in the audio.
  • The public feeds do not supply a stable written transcript. YouTube may expose automatic captions interactively, but no complete transcript corpus was imported in this pass.
  • Apple episode URLs intermittently returned HTTP 500 in automated checks. The same publication dates/titles were corroborated in the Simplecast RSS and the official YouTube feed where available.
  • The podcast uses guests. Store speaker, episode, timestamp, format, and date for every exact take; never flatten a guest opinion into “Josh Lloyd says.”
  • Episode numbering in Apple/search output was inconsistent and occasionally displayed abbreviated counts such as 3.2K. Use title + publication timestamp + URL as the identity key.
  • No public revision history exists for an analyst's evolving view. Preserve the dated snapshot instead of silently overwriting it.
  • Full audio is copyrighted. The vault should paraphrase narrowly, quote only short necessary excerpts, and link the episode rather than republishing transcripts.

Import policy for player and team pages

  1. Official NBA/team transactions, contracts, injuries, and rosters remain authoritative facts.
  2. Use Locked On as dated analyst evidence for role, category, regression, prospect, and team-outcome arguments.
  3. If an episode supplies a numerical rank/projection, record scoring format, league size, per-game versus totals, games-played assumption, speaker, timestamp, and publication date.
  4. If only the title/description is accessible, say “episode covers/flags/discusses,” not “Lloyd projects/ranks.”
  5. A team recap is a retrospective prior. A trade-reaction episode is an immediate scenario update. A final preseason team preview, when published, should receive greater forward weight.
  6. Keep disagreement visible. Do not average Lloyd, RotoWire, Hashtag, Basketball Monster, Fantrax, and community ranks unless their formats and horizons match.
