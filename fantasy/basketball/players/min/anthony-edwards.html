Anthony Edwards

Snapshot

  • Role: starting shooting guard, primary scorer, and secondary lead creator beside LaMelo Ball.
  • 2025-26 marker: 61 games; NBA/Yahoo's April review cited nearly 29 points, more than two stocks, 47.8 fantasy points per game, and a 14th-place nine-category finish.
  • Current market: NBA/Yahoo ranked him eighth in an early first-round list; Alex Barutha's July 14 rough post-trade board ranked him 11th.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: 8-15 per game. Points and threes are elite; maintaining 48% FG and two combined stocks keeps the profile in the first round.
  • Points: 8-15 in common scoring systems, with a higher ceiling when shot volume and defensive events are rewarded.
  • Dynasty: 4-10. Edwards is a prime-age cornerstone rather than a format-specific specialist.

Role, floor and ceiling

Ball should handle more early-clock organization, allowing Edwards to attack tilted defenses and work off movement. The floor is a top-20 scoring season if assists fall and the knee limits games. The ceiling is a top-seven season if easier shots lift his efficiency without sacrificing steals, blocks, or fourth-quarter usage.

Risk and transaction monitor

The 61-game 2025-26 season and a right knee inflammation absence make workload management the primary concern; the "runner's knee" label that circulated could not be corroborated and is not used here. Minnesota also lost frontcourt scoring in Julius Randle and Naz Reid; if Ball misses time, Edwards can return to maximum usage, but the efficiency benefit disappears.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Georgia, one season (2019-20). He played and started all 32 games at 33.0 minutes for 19.1 points, 5.2 rebounds, 2.8 assists, 1.3 steals and 0.6 blocks on .402/.294/.772, taking SEC Freshman of the Year and second-team All-SEC and finishing as a Jerry West Award finalist. The efficiency was poor — 40 percent from the field and 29 percent from three on a bad Georgia team — and he went first overall on tools and shot-creation projection rather than college production, which is worth remembering when judging how much of his NBA efficiency gain is development rather than variance. Draft: 2020, first round, first overall, Minnesota Timberwolves; no draft-night trade.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks could only be verified for 2025-26, where the NBA/Yahoo April review cited a 14th-place nine-category finish alongside 47.8 fantasy points per game; earlier seasons are left unranked rather than estimated. What the table supports without inference: 2020-21 was a rosterable but negative-value rookie season because .417 shooting on 16-plus attempts is a heavy category drag no matter the scoring. From 2021-22 onward he became a threes-and-points specialist whose category standing depended almost entirely on whether the field goal percentage cleared roughly .450 — 2022-23 and 2023-24 did, 2024-25 at .447 with a career-high 4.1 threes was closer to neutral. Free throw percentage has been the quiet swing factor, moving from .756 in 2022-23 to .836 and .837 in the two following seasons. He has never been an assist or rebound asset for a high-usage guard, and blocks only became a positive in 2025-26. Points-league value has consistently outrun category value because the format pays for his scoring volume and does not penalize the roughly three turnovers a game.

Durability: 72, 72, 79, 79, 79, 61. Through 2024-25 this was among the best availability records of any star in the league — 72 of 72 as a rookie in the shortened season, and then three straight 79-game years. The 2025-26 season broke that pattern hard: 21 games missed, by far his career worst. The causes were a right hamstring strain that cost four games, a lingering right foot injury that cost roughly six games across three separate stretches, and a right knee inflammation absence reported as one to two weeks. Separately, in the first-round playoff series against Denver he suffered a left knee hyperextension with a bone bruise and was ruled out at least a week, with reporting confirming no ligament damage. Note that the dossier above characterizes the knee issue as runner's knee; the sources located here describe right knee inflammation and a distinct playoff left-knee bone bruise, and the runner's-knee label could not be corroborated.

Trajectory: Six seasons of monotonic scoring improvement — 19.3, 21.3, 24.6, 25.9, 27.6, 28.8 — with minutes essentially flat at 32-36 throughout, which means this is a genuine skill curve rather than an opportunity curve. The most important line in the table is the 2025-26 field goal percentage of .489, a jump of more than four points from .447 while scoring more, and it arrived alongside a drop in three-point volume from 4.1 to 3.4. That combination is a deliberate shot-diet change toward the rim and mid-range rather than a hot streak, and it is the single most encouraging efficiency development of his career. Turnovers also fell to 2.9, the lowest since his rookie year, and blocks reached a career-high 0.8. What moved the other way is assists, down from a 5.1 peak to 3.7 as Minnesota's roster changed around him, and rebounds down to 5.0. He turns 25 in August 2026 and is entering the first year of his statistical prime, which argues the efficiency gain holds. The two live questions for 2026-27 are whether the LaMelo Ball arrival pushes assists further down while pushing efficiency further up — a trade that helps points formats more than categories — and whether 61 games was a one-off or the start of the wear pattern that high-mileage, physically aggressive guards typically develop in their mid-twenties. Nothing in the injury list is structural, which favors the one-off reading.

Open questions

  • Does Ball raise Edwards' field-goal percentage enough to offset lower on-ball volume?

  • Can he sustain two-plus steals and blocks combined while carrying elite scoring volume?
