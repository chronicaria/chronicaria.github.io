3:T3e4f,<h2 id="measurement-is-the-starting-point">Measurement is the starting point</h2>
<p>The model begins with season-end NBA snapshots from <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/rapm-lineup-model/" class="wiki-link">RAPM lineup model</a>, not the full future-aware career record. Because each daily rating only uses games available by that date, a season-end row is an as-of feature rather than a retrospective smoothing of later seasons.</p>
<p>For every player-season, the training panel builds:</p>
<ul>
<li>current, one-year-lagged, and two-year-lagged values;</li>
<li>recent deltas and a three-point trajectory slope;</li>
<li>all six current factors and current net;</li>
<li>the box prior as a second opinion;</li>
<li>age, years in the league, and draft signal;</li>
<li>historical minutes and games-played rates;</li>
<li>interactions between availability, minutes, and nonnegative impact;</li>
<li>a “young improver” indicator;</li>
<li>optional leakage-safe predictive box-rate features sourced through the EPM data contract.</li>
</ul>
<p>Raw EPM impact ratings are forbidden as features. The EPM-derived columns are underlying predictive rates such as steals, blocks, rebounding, usage, shot location, and shooting percentages. The current ablation record is deliberately mixed: 14 factor-horizon cells approve those features and 16 leave them dormant, so the feature family should not be advertised as a universal improvement.</p>
<h2 id="one-model-for-each-target-and-horizon">One model for each target and horizon</h2>
<p>The forward system trains a deterministic XGBoost model for each of the six factors at horizons one through five. It also trains a separate aggregate-net target at each horizon. Each target uses an expanding-window temporal evaluation: training seasons precede test seasons, and horizon purging prevents a label that lands in the test period from entering the training set.</p>
<p>Three structural choices matter:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Monotonicity:</strong> the model is constrained so a larger current value for the target cannot, by itself, force a lower forecast.</li>
<li><strong>Survivorship treatment:</strong> players who leave the league are not simply deleted; missing future targets after a career ends decay toward a replacement-tail floor.</li>
<li><strong>Recency weighting:</strong> recent training seasons receive more weight, with a six-season half-life.</li>
</ol>
<p>Every factor-horizon cell is compared against its stronger simple fallback—carry-forward or an aging adjustment. The July 9 gate selects XGBoost for all 30 factor-horizon cells. That is an internal temporal gate, not proof that every downstream season forecast beats an external model.</p>
<h2 id="the-b-prime-aggregate-correction">The B-prime aggregate correction</h2>
<p>Projecting six small components independently can make aggregate aging wash out: positive and negative component errors cancel, and an older player's net may fail to decline even when the aggregate historical pattern says it should. The current B-prime path therefore also models net directly.</p>
<p>The production artifact approves the aggregate-net model for all five horizons. For each player-horizon it first obtains the six factor forecasts, then computes the gap</p>

<p>This is an important distinction: measured net in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/six-factor-rapm-decomposition/" class="wiki-link">Six-factor RAPM decomposition</a> is not separately solved, but forecast net can be separately modeled and then re-decomposed so the published output still obeys <code>net = sum6</code>.</p>
<p>The inspected production metadata reports 906 players receiving the XGBoost overlay. Players or cells without an approved artifact fall back explicitly rather than receiving silent nulls.</p>
<h2 id="forecast-intervals">Forecast intervals</h2>
<p>The model retains a baseline path built from carry-forward, per-factor aging decisions, measurement error, and estimated process variance. XGBoost cells use out-of-fold residual distributions. A calibration table supplies residual quantiles by target, horizon, source, and—when sample size permits—player stratum such as role or age group.</p>
<p>The output records point forecasts plus p10, p50, and p90 bands, source flags, calibration source, and fallback indicators. The current projection metadata reports one-step net 80% coverage of about 84.5%, within the project's broad acceptance band but not exact nominal calibration.</p>
<p>Forecast uncertainty is different from today's measurement uncertainty. A player can be well measured now and still have a wide five-year forecast because aging, role change, injury, survival, and development add process variance. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/rapm-evaluation-and-uncertainty/" class="wiki-link">RAPM evaluation and uncertainty</a> keeps those two bands separate.</p>
<h2 id="from-player-forecasts-to-team-outcomes">From player forecasts to team outcomes</h2>
<p>Per-100 player trajectories do not predict wins alone. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/nba-team-season-projection-model/" class="wiki-link">NBA team season projection model</a> adds expected games played, minute allocations, roster identity, rookie cold starts, a game model, schedule uncertainty, and playoff simulation. A strong player forecast can still yield modest team value if availability or minutes are low; minutes are not allowed to inflate the player's per-100 rating itself.</p>
<h2 id="open-questions">Open questions</h2>
<ul>
<li>Does the proportional re-decomposition of aggregate net preserve believable factor trajectories for unusual player profiles?</li>
<li>Should aggregate net and six factors be modeled jointly under a coherence constraint rather than reconciled afterward?</li>
<li>How stable are the 30 XGBoost gate decisions under a truly frozen future season?</li>
<li>Can injury history and role transitions improve forecast distributions without leaking realized availability?</li>
