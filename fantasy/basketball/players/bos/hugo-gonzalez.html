Nine-category: deep-league bench target and standard-league watch-list player; rebounds, steals and low turnovers can make him useful before the scoring arrives. Points: likely a waiver player in the full-roster base case. Dynasty: Boston's best non-core upside stash, with a credible top-75 long-term route if shooting and secondary creation develop.

Why the line looks like this

As a teenager in 2025-26, Gonzalez played 74 games but only 14.6 minutes, averaging 3.9 points, 3.3 rebounds, 0.5 assists, 0.6 steals and 0.3 blocks while shooting 47.6%. The assist figure is the relevant constraint on any projection: 0.5 per game is 1.2 per 36, and nothing in his senior European record argues for a higher rate. The 50% FT mark came on tiny volume but remains a skill flag. His defense and rebounding earned real minutes; his half-court offensive role was much smaller.

Two data points show the upside without proving it. He posted 18 points, 16 rebounds, three steals and two blocks in his best March NBA night, then delivered 24 points, 10 rebounds and five assists in a July 15 Summer League game. The latter included eight makes on 18 shots and three threes. Summer League gave him far more on-ball freedom than a healthy Celtics rotation will, so the base projects only a modest assist/scoring rise. Paul George maintenance or a veteran-wing injury is the clearest acceleration path.

Sources: Basketball Reference 2025-26 Celtics, NBA Summer League Celtics–Kings recap, El País report on his 18-point, 16-rebound NBA game.

Category shape

  • Early strengths: wing rebounds, steals, low turnovers and efficient cutting.
  • Developing: assists, three-point volume and free-throw touch.
  • Risk: low scoring volume means one cold shooting category can erase standard-league value.
  • Build fit: deep balanced or punt-points teams; dynasty managers can wait on the offensive skill curve.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: crowded wings hold him near 12 minutes and the July creation never appears against NBA starters; 4-5 points with streamer-only stocks.
  • Base: 16 minutes, 5.4 points, 3.7 rebounds, 1.0 assists and 1.0 combined stocks.
  • Ceiling: earns 25-27 minutes through development and George absences, adds a respectable corner three and reaches roughly 10-6-2 with 1.4 stocks; top-120 category value.

Career record

Pre-NBA: No college. Born 2006-02-05 in Madrid to two professional basketball players, he joined Real Madrid's youth system in 2017-18 and debuted in Liga ACB on 2022-10-02 against Monbus Obradoiro at 16 years and 7 months. His three senior Real Madrid seasons were developmental rather than productive: 4 ACB games at 1.5 points in 2022-23, 10 games at 3.0 in 2023-24, and 36 games at 4.5 in 2024-25, when he made the All-Liga ACB Young Players Team. He also played 27 EuroLeague games in 2024-25, averaging 1.9 points and 1.4 rebounds in 8.0 minutes on .320/.292/.688 — a genuinely small and inefficient sample against the best competition in Europe. He won the Spanish Super Cup (2023), Copa del Rey (2024) and two ACB titles (2024, 2025), and left Real Madrid on 2025-06-30. The evidence for his talent came from age-group play rather than senior minutes: back-to-back EuroLeague Next Generation Tournament titles with 2024 MVP honors (17.5 points per game, 23 and six assists in the final), U17 World Cup silver in 2022, U18 European Championship silver in 2023, and 20.7 points per game across seven games at U18 EuroBasket in 2024. He made his senior Spain debut on 2025-02-20. Draft: 2025, first round, 28th overall, Boston, on 2025-06-25. Not traded on draft night.

He made his NBA debut on 2025-10-24 against New York (6 points, 4 rebounds), recorded his first double-double on 2025-12-20, and had three starts. His three-point percentage on the season was .362, which is the number most at odds with his pre-NBA shooting record.

2026 Summer League: through three Las Vegas games he averaged 18.3 points, 8.0 rebounds and 5.7 assists, leading the team in scoring and assists, but shot roughly 31.8 percent from the field and 25.9 percent from three across that stretch. The 2026-07-16 game against Sacramento was the efficient one: 24 points on 8-of-18 with three threes, 10 rebounds, five assists, 3-of-3 from the line. The usage was dramatically higher than anything Boston gave him in the regular season, which is what makes the sample informative about his ceiling and uninformative about his 2026-27 role.

Fantasy history: 2025-26: no rosterable value in any standard format — 3.9 points and 0.6 combined threes at 14.6 minutes is outside the top 300 in 9-cat. A precise season-end rank was not locatable and is not worth estimating. His one distinguishing category as a rookie was rebounds for a wing (3.3 in 14.6 minutes, roughly 8.1 per 36), with steals and a low turnover rate as secondary positives. The points-league shape is worse than the category shape, not better, because his contribution is concentrated in rebounds and defense rather than counting production. The .500 free-throw mark on tiny volume is the single largest category liability if his role grows.

Durability: 74 of 82 games as a 19-year-old rookie, with three starts. No injury absence of note is documented for 2025-26 and no G League assignment appears in the record reviewed. ESPN listed him as game-time decision at one point during the season without a documented multi-game absence. Availability is the least concerning part of this file; the eight missed games are consistent with rotation management on a deep roster rather than a health pattern.

Trajectory: The arc so far is unusual in a specific way: he was a heavily hyped age-group player whose senior European production never arrived — 8.0 minutes and 1.9 points in EuroLeague at 18 is a genuinely poor line, and it is why he fell to 28 rather than going in the lottery. Boston then gave him 74 games and 14.6 minutes as a teenager, which is more real NBA development than most late first-rounders get, and the return was defensive and on the glass rather than offensive. That is the correct order of operations for a wing, and the on-court defensive rating cited in Boston's coverage suggests the team's minutes were a judgment about impact rather than a rebuilding gift. The two things the record does not yet establish are shooting and creation. His .362 from three as a rookie is better than anything he shot in Spain and rests on 0.6 makes per game, so it is not yet a skill; his .500 from the line is the counterweight and the reason to be skeptical that the jumper is real. The Summer League playmaking is the interesting new variable, but 31.8 percent shooting across those same three games is the reason to treat it as a role experiment rather than a breakout. For 2026-27, at 20, the sensible read is another year of rebounds, steals and low turnovers in a bench role, with dynasty value resting almost entirely on whether the free-throw line starts telling a different story.
