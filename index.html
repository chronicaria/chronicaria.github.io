How the model works

  • Ratings. Published in points against an average team: 0 is average, and on a neutral court a +10 team is favoured by 10 points over an average one. A game's spread is the difference of the two ratings plus home court and the other context terms. The engine runs in Elo underneath; one point is — Elo.
  • Priors. Each team opens the season at the rating its preseason Vegas win total implies (times a fitted scale), so nobody starts level. Last season's rating adds nothing once the Vegas line is known.
  • Updates. After every game both sides move by K × a margin-of-victory multiplier × (result − expected). Margins are capped, so blowouts stop counting past the cap, and an autocorrelation term shrinks the multiplier when a favourite wins big, so strong teams' ratings don't inflate.
  • Game context. Home court and back-to-backs always shift a game's expected result. Each other factor was tested by leave-one-season-out fits and stays in only if it lowers held-out log loss. In: —. Out: —.
  • Forecast. The rest of the schedule is played — times with ratings updating game by game, then the play-in and playoffs with NBA seeding.
