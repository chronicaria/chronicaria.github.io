    Twisted sectors on orbifolds

    An orbifold is a space folded onto itself by a discrete symmetry, leaving sharp fixed points . Quantizing a closed string there forces two changes to the spectrum: you keep only the symmetric states (a projection ), and you must add a brand-new twisted sector of strings that close only up to the symmetry — strings glued shut across the fold, and therefore pinned to the fixed points.

    Folding a space onto itself

    Fold a sheet of paper in half. Points come in mirror pairs x↔−xx \leftrightarrow -xx↔−x, except the crease, which maps to itself — the fixed point. The folded paper is the orbifold. A point particle sitting on the crease has nowhere sensible to go; the geometry is singular there. But a closed string is a loop, and a loop has a new option a point does not: it can run from a point to its mirror image and close up across the crease. In the unfolded picture that string looks like an open arc; in the folded picture its two ends are identified, so it is a legitimate closed loop that is trapped at the fold. Those trapped loops are the twisted sector.

    Orbifolding does two opposite things at once — it removes states (kills the mirror-antisymmetric ones) and adds states (the twisted loops). This is the exact same "double strike" that turning a line into a circle performs (see Toroidal compactification): a circle adds winding and removes the momentum continuum; an orbifold adds twisted states and removes the reflection-odd states. Same mechanism, different group.

    What an orbifold is

    Take a manifold MMM and a discrete group Γ\GammaΓ acting on it. The orbifold is the quotient M/ΓM/\GammaM/Γ: glue together points related by Γ\GammaΓ. The new feature versus a plain circle or torus is that Γ\GammaΓ can have fixed points — points sent to themselves.

    The minimal example is the real line with a reflection, x∼−x(Z2: x↦−x).x \sim -x \qquad (\mathbb Z_2:\ x\mapsto -x).x∼−x(Z2​: x↦−x).

    • x=0x=0x=0 is the unique fixed point (−0=0-0=0−0=0).
    • A fundamental domain is the half-line x≥0x\ge 0x≥0.
    • R1/Z2\mathbb R^1/\mathbb Z_2R1/Z2​ is smooth for x>0x>0x>0 but singular at x=0x=0x=0: a neighborhood of the fixed point looks like the end of a half-line, not a piece of R\mathbb RR. The group is Z2\mathbb Z_2Z2​ because doing x→−xx\to-xx→−x twice is the identity.

    The stringy example is the circle orbifold S1/Z2S^1/\mathbb Z_2S1/Z2​: start with the circle x∼x+2πRx\sim x+2\pi Rx∼x+2πR and additionally impose x∼−xx\sim -xx∼−x. Now there are two fixed points, x=0x=0x=0 and x=πRx=\pi Rx=πR — the latter because −πR=πR−2πR∼πR-\pi R = \pi R - 2\pi R \sim \pi R−πR=πR−2πR∼πR on the circle. The quotient is a line segment (an interval) with a singular endpoint at each fixed point. In higher dimensions the Td/ZN\mathbf T^d/\mathbb Z_NTd/ZN​ orbifolds are the workhorses of compactification: flat away from the fixed points (hence trivially Ricci-flat, so they solve the vacuum condition of Einstein equations from conformal invariance) yet with all their curvature concentrated at the fixed points.

    Why anyone bothers. An orbifold is the simplest curved compactification you can actually solve. Locally it is flat, so the worldsheet theory stays a free CFT; but globally the identification breaks symmetries and reduces supersymmetry the way a genuine Calabi–Yau would. It is a tractable caricature of a Calabi–Yau — hard geometry replaced by easy geometry plus a few singular points.

    Quantizing a closed string: two sectors

    On M/ΓM/\GammaM/Γ, a closed string coordinate X(σ)X(\sigma)X(σ) need only close up to a Γ\GammaΓ transformation — the same relaxation of periodicity that gave winding on a circle (see Toroidal compactification), now generalized from "shift by a lattice vector" to "act by a group element." For Z2\mathbb Z_2Z2​ (x→−xx\to -xx→−x) this yields two families of boundary condition.

    Untwisted sector — the string closes normally, X(σ+2π)=X(σ)+(winding shift).X(\sigma+2\pi) = X(\sigma) + (\text{winding shift}).X(σ+2π)=X(σ)+(winding shift). These are the ordinary circle states, but not all of them survive. Because the parent theory has a Z2\mathbb Z_2Z2​ symmetry g:X↦−Xg:X\mapsto -Xg:X↦−X, only ggg-invariant states are well defined on the quotient. Keep them with the projector P=12(1+g).P = \tfrac12(1+g).P=21​(1+g). Concretely, for a momentum/winding state ∣n,m⟩|n,m\rangle∣n,m⟩, ggg sends ∣n,m⟩↦∣−n,−m⟩|n,m\rangle \mapsto |-n,-m\rangle∣n,m⟩↦∣−n,−m⟩, so the even combination ∣n,m⟩+∣−n,−m⟩|n,m\rangle+|-n,-m\rangle∣n,m⟩+∣−n,−m⟩ survives and the odd one is projected out. Oscillators carry a sign too: each excitation of the reflected coordinate flips sign under ggg, so a state's survival depends on the parity of its oscillator content as well. The untwisted sector is the old spectrum, symmetrized.

    Twisted sector — the string closes only after a reflection, X(σ+2π)=−X(σ)+(shift).X(\sigma+2\pi) = -X(\sigma) + (\text{shift}).X(σ+2π)=−X(σ)+(shift). This is a genuinely new closed string: open in the covering space, closed in the quotient — precisely the fold-crossing loop from the opening picture. Two consequences follow immediately, and the rest of the note makes them quantitative:

    1. It is stuck at a fixed point. Only there is −X=X-X = X−X=X consistent with the string's center of mass, so twisted strings are localized on the fixed points, one Hilbert space per fixed point.
    2. Its oscillators are half-integer moded. The antiperiodic condition X→−XX\to -XX→−X shifts every mode number by 12\tfrac1221​, which changes the zero-point energy and hence the twisted ground-state mass.

    Why the twisted sector is not optional. You might hope to define the orbifold theory as "just the projected untwisted states." That theory is inconsistent: its one-loop torus amplitude is not modular invariant, and modular invariance is the worldsheet-consistency backbone of this whole module. The twisted sector is exactly what repairs it, and the cycle-exchange argument shows why.

    A torus worldsheet has two independent cycles. Label a sector by which group element the string is twisted by around the spatial cycle (h1h_1h1​) and which element is inserted in the trace around the time cycle (h2h_2h2​), a pair (h1,h2)(h_1,h_2)(h1​,h2​). Projecting the untwisted Hilbert space onto invariant states is the insertion of P=12(1+g)P=\tfrac12(1+g)P=21​(1+g) in the trace — i.e. summing (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) and (1,g)(1,g)(1,g). But a modular transformation of the torus relabels its cycles, S: (h1,h2)⟼(h2,h1),S:\ (h_1,h_2)\longmapsto (h_2,h_1),S: (h1​,h2​)⟼(h2​,h1​), so it carries the projected untwisted trace (1,g)(1,g)(1,g) into (g,1)(g,1)(g,1) — a trace over the ggg-twisted Hilbert space. Once you allow the path integral to swap which cycle is "space" and which is "time," projection and twisting are two views of the same object: you cannot keep one without the other.

    Twisted-sector quantization for S1/Z2S^1/\mathbb Z_2S1/Z2​

    Work near a fixed point xf∈{0,πR}x_f\in\{0,\pi R\}xf​∈{0,πR} and split off the fluctuation, X(τ,σ)=xf+Y(τ,σ).X(\tau,\sigma)=x_f+Y(\tau,\sigma).X(τ,σ)=xf​+Y(τ,σ). A lone left-moving half-oscillator is not a physical closed-string state; left and right levels must balance.

    The zero-point shift and the twist weight h=116h=\tfrac1{16}h=161​

    The one number that makes twisted sectors quantitatively different is the vacuum energy. For a chiral real boson the zero-point energy is 12∑rr\tfrac12\sum_r r21​∑r​r over its positive modes. This sum is divergent; "regularize" means insert a smooth cutoff e−ϵre^{-\epsilon r}e−ϵr, expand in ϵ\epsilonϵ, and discard the 1/ϵ1/\epsilon1/ϵ piece, which is a local counterterm — the finite remainder is what ζ(−1)=−112\zeta(-1)=-\tfrac1{12}ζ(−1)=−121​ stands for. It is never the literal value of the divergent series.

    Cross-check. A complex boson twisted by phase η\etaη is known to carry h=12η(1−η)h=\tfrac12\eta(1-\eta)h=21​η(1−η); at the reflection value η=12\eta=\tfrac12η=21​ this is 18\tfrac1881​. A complex boson is two real ones, so per real boson h=116h=\tfrac1{16}h=161​ — matching the mode sum exactly. The two independent routes agree, which is the honesty check on the regularization.

    Effect on the mass. In the untwisted (or non-compact) closed string the mass-shell condition reads, per chiral half, N−1=0N-1=0N−1=0 — the "−1-1−1" being the additive normal-ordering constant a=−1a=-1a=−1 from D=26D=26D=26 (equivalently the intercept −a=1-a=1−a=1). Twisting one coordinate raises that coordinate's contribution to each chiral zero-point energy by 116\tfrac1{16}161​, so the twisted mass-shell condition becomes Ntw+116−1=0(each chiral half),N_{\rm tw}+\tfrac1{16}-1=0 \qquad(\text{each chiral half}),Ntw​+161​−1=0(each chiral half), i.e. the twisted ground state sits 116+116=18\tfrac1{16}+\tfrac1{16}=\tfrac18161​+161​=81​ in level (L0L_0L0​) units above where the naive vacuum would be. The bosonic tachyon is not cured by a single twist, but each fixed point now carries a definite, localized tower of states built on this shifted vacuum.

    For S1/Z2S^1/\mathbb Z_2S1/Z2​ there are two such twisted Hilbert spaces — one at xf=0x_f=0xf​=0, one at xf=πRx_f=\pi Rxf​=πR. On a general Td/Γ\mathbf T^d/\GammaTd/Γ orbifold you repeat this for each nontrivial group element and each fixed component, then apply the group-invariance projection.

    Why a string handles the singularity gracefully

    A point particle on R1/Z2\mathbb R^1/\mathbb Z_2R1/Z2​ crashes into a true singularity at the fixed point: the physics there is ill-defined. A string, being extended, propagates on the orbifold consistently, and the twisted-sector states are exactly the new degrees of freedom that "resolve" the fixed point — the fold-crossing loops that a point could never form. This is the same lesson as winding and T-duality: the string detects and tolerates structure that defeats a point particle.

    Open questions

    • The cycle-exchange argument gives the reason twisted sectors are required. The full theta-function partition function for S1/Z2S^1/\mathbb Z_2S1/Z2​, and the discrete-torsion phase constraints for general asymmetric orbifolds, are deferred.
    • Only the single reflected-coordinate S1/Z2S^1/\mathbb Z_2S1/Z2​ prototype is derived here. General Td/Γ\mathbf T^d/\GammaTd/Γ counting brings in fixed-point permutations and centralizer projections.

    Exercise

    The half-integer moding is the linchpin — everything downstream (the 116\tfrac1{16}161​, the localized tower) rests on it. Show it follows forced, not assumed, and check its immediate consequence.

    (a) Starting from the twisted boundary condition Y(τ,σ+2π)=−Y(τ,σ)Y(\tau,\sigma+2\pi)=-Y(\tau,\sigma)Y(τ,σ+2π)=−Y(τ,σ), show that a single chiral mode e−ir(τ±σ)e^{-ir(\tau\pm\sigma)}e−ir(τ±σ) is admissible only for r∈Z+12r\in\mathbb Z+\tfrac12r∈Z+21​. (b) Suppose you had mistakenly used integer modes for the twisted boson (the value for an ordinary boson). What chiral weight shift hhh would you get, and why does it violate the boundary condition you found in (a)?

    Solution

    (a) A chiral piece of YYY is a sum of terms e−irue^{-iru}e−iru with u=τ+σu=\tau+\sigmau=τ+σ (and similarly v=τ−σv=\tau-\sigmav=τ−σ for the other chirality). Shifting σ→σ+2π\sigma\to\sigma+2\piσ→σ+2π sends u→u+2πu\to u+2\piu→u+2π, so the term picks up a phase: e−ir(u+2π)=e−2πir e−iru.e^{-ir(u+2\pi)} = e^{-2\pi i r}\,e^{-iru}.e−ir(u+2π)=e−2πire−iru. The boundary condition demands the whole field flip sign, Y→−YY\to -YY→−Y, i.e. every mode must satisfy e−2πir=−1e^{-2\pi i r}=-1e−2πir=−1. That requires 2πr=π(2k+1)2\pi r = \pi(2k+1)2πr=π(2k+1) for integer kkk, so r=k+12∈Z+12.r = k+\tfrac12 \in \mathbb Z+\tfrac12.r=k+21​∈Z+21​. Integer rrr would give e−2πir=+1e^{-2\pi i r}=+1e−2πir=+1 (periodic, the untwisted case); only half-integer rrr produces the required minus sign. The moding is forced by antiperiodicity, not put in by hand.

    (b) The weight shift is always measured relative to the untwisted vacuum energy E0intE_0^{\rm int}E0int​. Integer modes are the ordinary-boson case, whose chiral zero-point energy is E0int=12ζ(−1)=−124E_0^{\rm int}=\tfrac12\zeta(-1)=-\tfrac1{24}E0int​=21​ζ(−1)=−241​ — identical to the untwisted vacuum, so the shift h=E0int−E0inth=E_0^{\rm int}-E_0^{\rm int}h=E0int​−E0int​ vanishes: no twist field at all. This is exactly the untwisted answer, which is the tell: integer modes describe a string that closes periodically, Y(σ+2π)=+Y(σ)Y(\sigma+2\pi)=+Y(\sigma)Y(σ+2π)=+Y(σ), the opposite of the twisted condition. They cannot represent a string that closes across the fold. The correct half-integer modes instead give E0tw=148E_0^{\rm tw}=\tfrac1{48}E0tw​=481​ and h=E0tw−E0int=148+124=116h=E_0^{\rm tw}-E_0^{\rm int}=\tfrac1{48}+\tfrac1{24}=\tfrac1{16}h=E0tw​−E0int​=481​+241​=161​. The moral: the 116\tfrac1{16}161​ is not an independent input — it is the direct arithmetic consequence of the half-integer moding, which is itself the direct consequence of antiperiodicity.
