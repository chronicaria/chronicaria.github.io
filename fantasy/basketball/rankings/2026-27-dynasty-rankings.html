Scope and reading guide

  • Dynasty tier: T1 apex assets (1-9); T2 franchise anchors (10-24); T3 core pieces (25-49); T4 high-value starters (50-79); T5 flexible top-120 assets (80-119); T6 depth/upside (120-159); T7 deep flex and speculation (160-200). Within-tier order is intentionally tradable.
  • Age · horizon: displayed age at the cutoff plus a planning window: under 23 = 5y+; 23-25 = 4–5y; 26-28 = 3–4y; 29-31 = 2–3y; 32-34 = 1–2y; 35+ = win now. Horizon is a roster-planning heuristic, not an estimate of career length.
  • Redraft: current 2026-27 nine-category per-game band from 2026-27 fantasy basketball tiers. 200+ / stash means outside that local redraft top 200, not valueless. An asterisk marks a contract-conditional band.
  • Risk: L low; M, H, and X are escalating levels. Reasons are H health, R role, E efficiency/translation, A age, and S situation/contract. Risk is downside width, not a command to fade.
  • Lean: B fits either window; R+ gains in a rebuild; C+ gains for a contender; S is a patience/taxi stash.
  • H / C / D / R: exact source ranks from Hashtag expert, Hashtag crowd, Dynatyze, and the publicly visible RotoWire excerpt. R— means RotoWire did not expose an exact rank for that player in the public article; it does not mean unranked.

Source reconciliation

The starting order uses the Hashtag expert five-season board and Dynatyze dynasty board as co-anchors. The Hashtag crowd keeper board is a lower-weight tiebreaker after extreme crowd tails are capped; its July 18 page displayed 196,650 votes but remained visibly unstable below the elite tier. Exact names available in RotoWire's June 29 three-year “win at least once” top 100 provide a shorter-horizon counterweight. The 2026 class is reconciled against Rookies and dynasty desk, 2026-27 rookies, RotoWire's first-round fantasy outlooks, and current team evidence.

This structure matters because the sources answer different questions. Hashtag's expert setting explicitly spans the next five seasons; RotoWire asks whether a roster can win at least once in three years; Dynatyze behaves like a liquid market-value board and includes future picks; the crowd page reflects preference and liquidity but compresses or wildly stretches its tail. No single source rank is treated as ground truth.

Asset-type and availability filters

Dynatyze's public 500-asset API is the canonical Dynatyze coordinate. It labels future picks as entityType player, so this board filters with isDraftPick true, position PICK, or teamName DRAFT. Eleven Dynatyze top-200 rows were therefore excluded: ranks 41, 72, 86, 104, 118, 130, 143, 151, 166, 174, and 192. The crowd board's generic placeholders at 10, 18, 40, 64, 105, 140, and 175 were also excluded. They are legitimate league assets, but they are not NBA players and cannot link to player dossiers.

DeMar DeRozan and Jonathan Kuminga were not inserted merely to preserve a vendor rank: at the cutoff they were UFA/no-current-roster cases without verified current player dossiers. LeBron James is included at No. 125 after creation of his canonical free-agent dossier; his 49–72 redraft band is conditional on signing and playing the dossier's contender scenario. Harden also remains because he has a full dossier and meaningful contender value, but his table team is explicitly UFA. Duren, Peyton Watson, and Mathurin retain their rights-team labels plus RFA.

Contender and rebuild overlays

A contender should treat the board as a liquidity baseline, then buy production where the age discount exceeds the injury discount. Jokić can reasonably move ahead of younger guards for a two-year title window. Harden, Curry, Durant, Anthony Davis, Kawhi, Embiid, Kyrie, Lillard, Paul George, and Butler can move one to three tiers up when their medical outlook and games-played rules are tolerable. The redraft band is the quick check: a large gap between redraft and dynasty position is usually the available win-now discount.

A rebuild should do the inverse: move Boozer, Peterson, Dybantsa, Caleb Wilson, Wagler, Mikel Brown, Acuff, Flemings, and the best young incumbents up inside their tiers; move 30-plus assets down; and price uncertain rookie minutes as a range rather than a promise. Age alone does not trump demonstrated production—Wembanyama, SGA, Luka, Cade, Flagg, Maxey, Edwards, and Jalen Johnson remain the cleanest cross-window anchors.

The S label is deliberate. Mara, Steinbach, Burries, Morez Johnson, the late first-round rookies, Quaintance, and the bottom-tier development bets should only displace a productive veteran when the league has enough benches, taxi slots, or patience to realize their horizon.

Cutoff and first-out queue

The formal cutoff is No. 200 Cam Spencer. The first-out source-consensus queue is Donte DiVincenzo, Naji Marshall, Andrew Wiggins, Jordan Poole, Rui Hachimura, Tarris Reed Jr., Oso Ighodaro, Ty Jerome, Noah Clowney, and Nikola Vučević. That queue is format-sensitive rather than a single archetype: a deep rebuild should bring Reed or Clowney in before several low-ceiling veterans, while a contender can prefer Wiggins, Vučević, Holiday, or Jerome.

Ranks 160-200 are intentionally porous. In a 12-team league with fewer than 200 rostered players, the bottom tier is a watch list; in 16- or 30-team formats, contract security and position scarcity become more important than this one-number cutoff.
