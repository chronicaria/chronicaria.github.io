    Microcanonical versus canonical ensemble

    Microcanonical means fix the energy and count the states; canonical means let the energy fluctuate and weight every state by e^{-\beta E} . For a large system these are two ways of asking the same counting question.

    Two ways to ask the same question

    Suppose you want to know how many quantum states a system has at a given energy. There are two ways to ask.

    Way 1 (microcanonical): stand at one energy and count. Dial the energy to exactly EEE and ask "how many microstates sit right here?" The answer is a single integer, Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E). This is the honest, primary object — a literal head-count. Its downside: the count is a hard constrained combinatorics problem (states must land on exactly EEE).

    Way 2 (canonical): don't fix energy — tax it. Attach the system to a huge heat bath at temperature TTT and let energy slosh back and forth. Now no single energy is fixed; instead every state gets a weight e−βEe^{-\beta E}e−βE that penalizes high energy. Add up all the weights and you get one number, the partition function ZZZ. This is computationally easy — the constraint is gone, so the sums over independent parts of the system factorize into a product.

    The two ways are one transform apart: ZZZ is the Laplace transform of Ω\OmegaΩ. Way 2 smears Way 1 over all energies with an exponential envelope. For a large ordinary system the smeared weight Ω(E)e−βE\Omega(E)e^{-\beta E}Ω(E)e−βE collapses to a needle-sharp spike over a single energy shell, so the two ways deliver the same physics and you use whichever is easier. Way 2 usually is, which fixes the standard move: compute ZZZ, then read Ω\OmegaΩ back off it. The move breaks in exactly one place — a string near the Hagedorn temperature, where the spike stops being sharp. All of Hagedorn physics turns on that failure.

    An exam analogy: microcanonical is counting exactly how many students scored 90. Canonical is computing a score-weighted head-count with a knob β\betaβ that sets how harshly high scores are docked — easier to compute, and once you know it for every β\betaβ it encodes the whole score histogram, including how many scored 90.

    What an ensemble is

    A quantum system has energy eigenstates ∣α⟩|\alpha\rangle∣α⟩ with energies EαE_\alphaEα​. The label α\alphaα is a microstate: the full microscopic description (e.g. every oscillator's occupation number). A macrostate keeps only coarse data — total energy, or temperature.

    An ensemble is a probability distribution pαp_\alphapα​ over the microstates compatible with a given macrostate. All of thermodynamics is averages over that distribution. Two natural choices of what to hold fixed give the two ensembles.

    Microcanonical: fix the energy

    Isolate the system completely, so its energy is pinned at EEE (or, for a continuous spectrum, in a thin window [E,E+ΔE][E, E+\Delta E][E,E+ΔE]). The fundamental postulate of statistical mechanics is:

    An isolated system in equilibrium is equally likely to be in any accessible microstate.

    If Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E) counts the microstates in that energy shell, each has probability

    pα=1Ω(E).p_\alpha = \frac{1}{\Omega(E)}.pα​=Ω(E)1​.

    Once the energy is fixed we refuse to privilege any one compatible arrangement — total ignorance beyond the constraint. Everything is built from the single counting function Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E) — the microcanonical face of the Density of states — and the entropy is Boltzmann's

    S(E)=kln⁡Ω(E),S(E) = k\ln\Omega(E),S(E)=klnΩ(E),

    the content of Entropy as log Omega. For a discrete level NNN it is cleaner to write the degeneracy as gNg_NgN​; for the quantum violin string gN=p(N)g_N = p(N)gN​=p(N), the partition number, with E=ℏω0NE = \hbar\omega_0 NE=ℏω0​N.

    The catch: extracting gNg_NgN​ directly means solving an exact integer constraint,

    N=∑ℓ≥1ℓ nℓ,N = \sum_{\ell\ge 1}\ell\, n_\ell,N=∑ℓ≥1​ℓnℓ​,

    i.e. "how many occupation lists {nℓ}\{n_\ell\}{nℓ​} hit exactly total NNN?" That hard constraint is precisely what the canonical ensemble dissolves.

    Canonical: fix the temperature

    Now couple the system to a large reservoir (heat bath) at temperature TTT. The system's energy fluctuates; what is fixed is TTT. The probability of microstate α\alphaα is the Boltzmann distribution

    pα=e−βEαZ,β≡1kT,p_\alpha = \frac{e^{-\beta E_\alpha}}{Z}, \qquad \beta \equiv \frac{1}{kT},pα​=Ze−βEα​​,β≡kT1​,

    normalized by the canonical partition function

     Z=∑αe−βEα .\boxed{\,Z = \sum_\alpha e^{-\beta E_\alpha}\,}.Z=α∑​e−βEα​​.

    High-energy states are exponentially suppressed, and β\betaβ sets how steeply. Probabilistically ZZZ is just the normalizer; but as a function of β\betaβ it packs away the energy moments and the entropy.

    For a spectrum EN=ℏω0NE_N = \hbar\omega_0 NEN​=ℏω0​N, set q=e−βℏω0q = e^{-\beta\hbar\omega_0}q=e−βℏω0​. Then

    Z(q)=∑N≥0gN qN.Z(q) = \sum_{N\ge 0} g_N\, q^N.Z(q)=∑N≥0​gN​qN.

    This is an ordinary generating function: temperature just picks a numerical value of q∈(0,1)q\in(0,1)q∈(0,1), and the gNg_NgN​ we struggled to count in the microcanonical picture now sit as the coefficients of a power series. Trading the exact constraint for a generating function is the entire computational payoff.

    Where the Boltzmann weight comes from

    This is the one derivation that justifies the canonical ensemble, so do it once. Put the system (states α\alphaα, energies EαE_\alphaEα​) in contact with a reservoir and treat the combined system as isolated with fixed total energy EtotE_{\text{tot}}Etot​ — i.e. apply the microcanonical postulate to system + reservoir.

    If the system sits in one specific microstate α\alphaα, the reservoir must carry Etot−EαE_{\text{tot}} - E_\alphaEtot​−Eα​, and the number of ways to arrange it is ΩR(Etot−Eα)\Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}} - E_\alpha)ΩR​(Etot​−Eα​). By the equal-probability postulate,

    pα  ∝  ΩR(Etot−Eα).p_\alpha \;\propto\; \Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}} - E_\alpha).pα​∝ΩR​(Etot​−Eα​).

    The system prefers states that leave the bath with the most ways to be — it is the bath's entropy doing the driving. Write SR=kln⁡ΩRS_R = k\ln\Omega_RSR​=klnΩR​ and Taylor-expand ln⁡ΩR\ln\Omega_RlnΩR​ around EtotE_{\text{tot}}Etot​, using Eα≪EtotE_\alpha \ll E_{\text{tot}}Eα​≪Etot​ (the reservoir is huge):

    ln⁡ΩR(Etot−Eα)≈ln⁡ΩR(Etot)−Eα ∂ln⁡ΩR∂E∣Etot.\ln\Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}} - E_\alpha) \approx \ln\Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}}) - E_\alpha\,\frac{\partial\ln\Omega_R}{\partial E}\Big|_{E_{\text{tot}}}.lnΩR​(Etot​−Eα​)≈lnΩR​(Etot​)−Eα​∂E∂lnΩR​​​Etot​​.

    That derivative is the definition of temperature: ∂SR/∂E=1/T\partial S_R/\partial E = 1/T∂SR​/∂E=1/T, so ∂ln⁡ΩR/∂E=1/(kT)=β\partial\ln\Omega_R/\partial E = 1/(kT) = \beta∂lnΩR​/∂E=1/(kT)=β. Hence

    ΩR(Etot−Eα)≈ΩR(Etot) e−βEα⟹pα∝e−βEα.\Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}} - E_\alpha) \approx \Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}})\,e^{-\beta E_\alpha} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_\alpha \propto e^{-\beta E_\alpha}.ΩR​(Etot​−Eα​)≈ΩR​(Etot​)e−βEα​⟹pα​∝e−βEα​.

    The reservoir's entire influence collapses to one number, its temperature; the constant ΩR(Etot)\Omega_R(E_{\text{tot}})ΩR​(Etot​) is absorbed into 1/Z1/Z1/Z. This is where β=1/(kT)\beta = 1/(kT)β=1/(kT) physically comes from: it is the rate at which the bath's log-multiplicity changes with energy — how much "phase space" the bath gives up to hand the system one more unit of energy. (The definition 1/T=∂S/∂E1/T = \partial S/\partial E1/T=∂S/∂E is motivated in Entropy as log Omega.)

    Equivalently, in optimization language: β\betaβ is the Lagrange multiplier that enforces a fixed mean energy once exact energy is allowed to fluctuate.

    How the two ensembles connect

    Group the canonical sum by energy. All Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E) states at energy EEE carry the same weight e−βEe^{-\beta E}e−βE, so

    Z=∑αe−βEα=∑EΩ(E) e−βE  →continuum  ∫0∞dE ρ(E) e−βE.Z = \sum_\alpha e^{-\beta E_\alpha} = \sum_E \Omega(E)\,e^{-\beta E} \;\xrightarrow{\text{continuum}}\; \int_0^\infty dE\,\rho(E)\,e^{-\beta E}.Z=∑α​e−βEα​=∑E​Ω(E)e−βEcontinuum​∫0∞​dEρ(E)e−βE.

    ZZZ is the Laplace transform of the density of states. Fixing TTT is a weighted superposition over all energies; fixing EEE is one slice of that superposition. To go the other way — recover Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E) or gNg_NgN​ from ZZZ — you inverse-Laplace transform (or extract a power-series coefficient), which in practice is a saddle-point estimate developed in Density of states.

    Why they agree (for large systems)

    The integrand is ρ(E)e−βE∼eln⁡Ω(E)−βE=eS(E)/k−βE\rho(E)e^{-\beta E}\sim e^{\ln\Omega(E) - \beta E} = e^{S(E)/k - \beta E}ρ(E)e−βE∼elnΩ(E)−βE=eS(E)/k−βE. For a large ordinary system this is sharply peaked: the exponent is extensive — of order the number of constituents — and the fluctuation computation below shows the peak's width grows only like the square root of its location. The peak sits where

    ∂∂E(Sk−βE)=0  ⟹  1k∂S∂E=β  ⟹  ∂S∂E=1T,\frac{\partial}{\partial E}\Big(\frac{S}{k} - \beta E\Big) = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{1}{k}\frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = \beta \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = \frac{1}{T},∂E∂​(kS​−βE)=0⟹k1​∂E∂S​=β⟹∂E∂S​=T1​,

    exactly the thermodynamic definition of temperature. So the canonical ensemble at temperature TTT is dominated by the single energy shell whose microcanonical temperature is TTT. In applied-math terms: the Laplace transform is controlled by one saddle.

    How sharp is the peak? In the canonical ensemble the energy moments come straight from ln⁡Z\ln ZlnZ:

    ⟨E⟩=−∂βln⁡Z,⟨(ΔE)2⟩=∂β2ln⁡Z=−∂β⟨E⟩.\langle E\rangle = -\partial_\beta\ln Z,\qquad \langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = \partial_\beta^2\ln Z = -\partial_\beta\langle E\rangle.⟨E⟩=−∂β​lnZ,⟨(ΔE)2⟩=∂β2​lnZ=−∂β​⟨E⟩.

    The curvature of ln⁡Z\ln ZlnZ is the width of the energy distribution. Since dT/dβ=−kT2dT/d\beta = -kT^2dT/dβ=−kT2,

    ⟨(ΔE)2⟩=kT2CV,CV=(∂⟨E⟩∂T)V.\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle = kT^2 C_V,\qquad C_V = \left(\frac{\partial\langle E\rangle}{\partial T}\right)_V.⟨(ΔE)2⟩=kT2CV​,CV​=(∂T∂⟨E⟩​)V​.

    For an ordinary extensive system of NNN independent constituents (in this argument NNN counts particles or oscillators, not the level of the previous sections) ⟨E⟩∼N\langle E\rangle\sim N⟨E⟩∼N and CV∼NC_V\sim NCV​∼N, so

    ⟨(ΔE)2⟩⟨E⟩∼NN=1N→N→∞0.\frac{\sqrt{\langle(\Delta E)^2\rangle}}{\langle E\rangle}\sim\frac{\sqrt N}{N} = \frac{1}{\sqrt N}\xrightarrow{N\to\infty}0.⟨E⟩⟨(ΔE)2⟩​​∼NN​​=N​1​N→∞​0.

    Relative energy fluctuations vanish in the thermodynamic limit. That is why "fixed TTT" and "fixed E=EˉE = \bar EE=Eˉ" describe the same macroscopic state for large ordinary systems: the ensembles are equivalent at leading order — the spike from the opening picture becomes a needle.

    Caveat: strings break the equivalence

    Equivalence is a sharp-peak statement, and the peak can fail to be sharp. At high energy the string density of states grows like ρ(E)∼eβHE\rho(E)\sim e^{\beta_H E}ρ(E)∼eβH​E (up to powers of EEE), so the exponent of the Laplace integrand is S(E)/k−βE≃−(β−βH)ES(E)/k - \beta E \simeq -(\beta-\beta_H)ES(E)/k−βE≃−(β−βH​)E — linear in EEE, with no interior maximum and hence no saddle at any β\betaβ. For β>βH\beta > \beta_Hβ>βH​ the integrand merely decays: ZZZ converges but no single energy shell dominates it. Once β\betaβ drops below βH\beta_HβH​ (temperature rises past the Hagedorn temperature THT_HTH​), ZZZ stops converging altogether, whereas the microcanonical count Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E) remains finite and well-defined at every energy. This is precisely why Hagedorn physics is told through entropy and density of states rather than through the canonical ZZZ: beyond THT_HTH​ Way 2 breaks and only Way 1 survives.

    Which to use when

    • Microcanonical (Ω(E)\Omega(E)Ω(E) or gNg_NgN​, S=kln⁡ΩS = k\ln\OmegaS=klnΩ): conceptually primary; the right tool when you literally want a state count, and the only safe tool when ZZZ diverges near Hagedorn.
    • Canonical (ZZZ, F=−kTln⁡ZF = -kT\ln ZF=−kTlnZ): computationally easier, because independent-oscillator sums factor into products. Compute here, then convert back.

    The practical loop: compute ZZZ (canonical, easy) →\to→ get F=−kTln⁡ZF = -kT\ln ZF=−kTlnZ, then S(T)S(T)S(T) and E(T)E(T)E(T) →\to→ eliminate TTT to get S(E)S(E)S(E) →\to→ identify Ω(E)=eS/k\Omega(E) = e^{S/k}Ω(E)=eS/k (back to microcanonical) →\to→ read off the state count. For the violin string that count is p(N)p(N)p(N), and this loop is exactly how you get its asymptotic growth without ever counting partitions by hand.

    Open questions

    • Genuine inequivalence — negative specific heat, or no saddle at all — is the Hagedorn boundary case, developed in Hagedorn temperature.
    • This note keeps only the leading saddle; subleading finite-size corrections to the equivalence are not needed here.
