    Metric, B-field, and dilaton as low-energy fields

    The massless states of the closed string are exactly three spacetime fields — a graviton G_{\mu\nu} , an antisymmetric 2-form B_{\mu\nu} , and a scalar dilaton \Phi — and each shows up as a different way of coupling to the worldsheet, which is why the string carries gravity, a new kind of charge, and its own coupling constant all at once.

    A closed string vibrating in its lowest excited level is spinning in two independent ways (a left-moving quantum and a right-moving quantum). Multiply those two spins together and a 222-index tensor ψμν\psi_{\mu\nu}ψμν​ appears. Any such tensor splits into a symmetric-traceless part, an antisymmetric part, and a trace — and that arithmetic is the statement "the massless closed string = graviton + BBB-field + dilaton." We never put a metric in by hand; it falls out of the spectrum.

    Where the three fields come from

    The massless level of the closed string (N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=1N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1, no momentum or winding) is built by acting on the momentum-ppp vacuum ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ with one left and one right oscillator: α−1μ αˉ−1ν ∣0;p⟩  ⟶  ψμν.\alpha_{-1}^\mu\,\bar\alpha_{-1}^\nu\,|0;p\rangle \;\longrightarrow\; \psi_{\mu\nu}.α−1μ​αˉ−1ν​∣0;p⟩⟶ψμν​. The two indices are independent, so ψμν\psi_{\mu\nu}ψμν​ is a general (not-yet-symmetric) tensor. Under the spacetime Lorentz group it decomposes into three irreducible pieces: ψμν=ψ(μν)−1D ημνψλλ⏟graviton Gμν  +  ψ[μν]⏟Kalb–Ramond Bμν  +  1D ημνψλλ⏟dilaton Φ.\psi_{\mu\nu} = \underbrace{\psi_{(\mu\nu)} - \tfrac1D\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\psi^\lambda{}_\lambda}_{\text{graviton }G_{\mu\nu}} \;+\; \underbrace{\psi_{[\mu\nu]}}_{\text{Kalb–Ramond }B_{\mu\nu}} \;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac1D\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\psi^\lambda{}_\lambda}_{\text{dilaton }\Phi}.ψμν​=graviton Gμν​ψ(μν)​−D1​ημν​ψλλ​​​+Kalb–Ramond Bμν​ψ[μν]​​​+dilaton ΦD1​ημν​ψλλ​​​. Each piece is a field the string can source:

    • symmetric-traceless — a massless spin-2 field. There is exactly one consistent theory of a massless spin-2 field: general relativity. So this is the graviton. Gravity was never assumed; it is forced.
    • antisymmetric — a massless 2-form, the Kalb–Ramond field BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​. A genuinely new field with no point-particle analogue at this rank.
    • trace — a single scalar, the dilaton Φ\PhiΦ.

    Every consistent closed string theory contains all three. The rest of this note is about how each couples, and how those couplings dictate the fields' spacetime dynamics.

    How each couples to the worldsheet

    Turn on all three background fields. A string moving through them is a 2d field theory (a sigma model) with three terms: S=14πα′∫d2σ h (Gμν(X) ∂αXμ∂βXν hαβ  +  i Bμν(X) ∂αXμ∂βXν ϵαβ  +  α′ Φ(X) R(2)).S = \frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{h}\,\Big( G_{\mu\nu}(X)\,\partial_\alpha X^\mu\partial_\beta X^\nu\,h^{\alpha\beta} \;+\; i\,B_{\mu\nu}(X)\,\partial_\alpha X^\mu\partial_\beta X^\nu\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta} \;+\; \alpha'\,\Phi(X)\,R^{(2)} \Big).S=4πα′1​∫d2σh​(Gμν​(X)∂α​Xμ∂β​Xνhαβ+iBμν​(X)∂α​Xμ∂β​Xνϵαβ+α′Φ(X)R(2)). The three couplings are built from the only three natural worldsheet tensors: the symmetric hαβh^{\alpha\beta}hαβ, the antisymmetric ϵαβ\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}ϵαβ, and the curvature scalar R(2)R^{(2)}R(2). Each field couples to exactly one of them — which is the deep reason there are exactly three massless fields. (The factor of iii is a Euclidean-signature bookkeeping artefact for the single-derivative BBB term.)

    Metric GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ couples through the symmetric hαβ∂X∂Xh^{\alpha\beta}\partial X\partial Xhαβ∂X∂X. This is the ordinary kinetic term; it is literally the geometry the string measures distances with. Demanding this coupling stay Weyl-invariant is what produces Einstein's equations — see Beta functions and conformal invariance and Einstein equations from conformal invariance.

    Kalb–Ramond BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ couples through the antisymmetric ϵαβ∂Xμ∂Xν\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\partial X^\mu\partial X^\nuϵαβ∂Xμ∂Xν. Read this geometrically: it is the pullback of the spacetime 2-form BBB onto the 2d worldsheet, ∫Bμν dXμ∧dXν\int B_{\mu\nu}\,dX^\mu\wedge dX^\nu∫Bμν​dXμ∧dXν. Compare a charged point particle, which couples to a 1-form AμA_\muAμ​ by dragging it along its 1d worldline, ∫Aμ X˙μ\int A_\mu\,\dot X^\mu∫Aμ​X˙μ. A string is a 1d object, so it couples to a 2-form the way a point couples to a 1-form: the string is electrically charged under BBB. The gauge symmetry mirrors electromagnetism's A→A+dαA\to A+d\alphaA→A+dα: Bμν→Bμν+∂μCν−∂νCμ,B_{\mu\nu}\to B_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu C_\nu - \partial_\nu C_\mu,Bμν​→Bμν​+∂μ​Cν​−∂ν​Cμ​, under which the action shifts by a total derivative. The gauge-invariant field strength is the 3-form H=dBH=dBH=dB, Hμνρ=∂μBνρ+∂νBρμ+∂ρBμν,H_{\mu\nu\rho}=\partial_\mu B_{\nu\rho}+\partial_\nu B_{\rho\mu}+\partial_\rho B_{\mu\nu},Hμνρ​=∂μ​Bνρ​+∂ν​Bρμ​+∂ρ​Bμν​, the exact analogue of F=dAF=dAF=dA. (HHH also acts as spacetime torsion — an antisymmetric piece of the affine connection.) On a compact circle this charge shows up as winding: the winding modes in Toroidal compactification are exactly the states charged under the reduced field A~μ=Bμ,25\tilde A_\mu=B_{\mu,25}A~μ​=Bμ,25​.

    Dilaton Φ\PhiΦ couples to the worldsheet Ricci scalar R(2)R^{(2)}R(2) — the curvature of the string's own sheet, not of spacetime. Two features make it the odd one out:

    1. It vanishes on a flat worldsheet (R(2)=0R^{(2)}=0R(2)=0), which makes its vertex operator subtle to write down.
    2. It carries an explicit α′\alpha'α′, so it is effectively "one loop up" from the other two — and on a curved worldsheet it classically breaks Weyl invariance. This looks fatal (Weyl invariance is sacred) but is rescued: the classical violation is exactly the size of the one-loop violation from the GGG and BBB terms (both are order α′\alpha'α′), so they can cancel. That cancellation is the dilaton's beta-function condition; see Beta functions and conformal invariance.

    The dilaton is the string coupling

    Take the dilaton constant, Φ(X)=λ\Phi(X)=\lambdaΦ(X)=λ. Then its coupling collapses to a pure topological number. Since 14πα′⋅α′=14π\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\cdot\alpha' = \frac{1}{4\pi}4πα′1​⋅α′=4π1​, the Gauss–Bonnet theorem gives Sdilaton=14π∫d2σ h λ R(2)=λ (14π ⁣∫ ⁣h R(2))=λ χ,S_{\text{dilaton}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt h\,\lambda\,R^{(2)}=\lambda\,\Big(\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\!\int\!\sqrt h\,R^{(2)}\Big)=\lambda\,\chi,Sdilaton​=4π1​∫d2σh​λR(2)=λ(4π1​∫h​R(2))=λχ, where χ=2−2g\chi=2-2gχ=2−2g is the Euler characteristic of a genus-ggg closed worldsheet. A constant dilaton changes no local equation of motion; it only weights the worldsheet by its topology.

    This topological weight is what fixes the string coupling. In the path integral each worldsheet is weighted by e−S⊃e−λχe^{-S}\supset e^{-\lambda\chi}e−S⊃e−λχ. Adding one handle to the string diagram — the next order in string perturbation theory — raises the genus by 111, so χ\chiχ drops by 222, and the amplitude picks up a factor e−λ Δχ=e+2λ.e^{-\lambda\,\Delta\chi}=e^{+2\lambda}.e−λΔχ=e+2λ. Each extra loop costs a fixed factor of e2λe^{2\lambda}e2λ. That is precisely how a coupling constant behaves: each loop should cost gs2g_s^2gs2​. Reading off,  gs=eΦ0 ,Φ0=lim⁡X→∞Φ(X).\boxed{\,g_s = e^{\Phi_0}\,},\qquad \Phi_0=\lim_{X\to\infty}\Phi(X).gs​=eΦ0​​,Φ0​=limX→∞​Φ(X). The string coupling is not a free parameter of the theory — it is the vacuum value of a field, on the same footing as the metric or a Higgs vev, and in principle set dynamically. (In the bosonic string nothing fixes it; in the superstring, fluxes and other effects can.) There is no dial for how strongly strings interact: the coupling strength is just how large one of the string's own vibration modes sits in the vacuum.

    The spacetime action and its two frames

    Demanding all three beta functions vanish (done in Einstein equations from conformal invariance) is equivalent to extremizing a single 26d action — the low-energy effective action of the bosonic string: S=12κ02∫d26X −G e−2Φ(R−112HμνλHμνλ+4 ∂μΦ ∂μΦ).S = \frac{1}{2\kappa_0^2}\int d^{26}X\,\sqrt{-G}\,e^{-2\Phi}\Big(\mathcal R - \tfrac{1}{12}H_{\mu\nu\lambda}H^{\mu\nu\lambda} + 4\,\partial_\mu\Phi\,\partial^\mu\Phi\Big).S=2κ02​1​∫d26X−G​e−2Φ(R−121​Hμνλ​Hμνλ+4∂μ​Φ∂μΦ). The Einstein–Hilbert term −G R\sqrt{-G}\,\mathcal R−G​R is gravity, derived not postulated. The odd features are the e−2Φe^{-2\Phi}e−2Φ prefactor (this is the tree-level 1/gs21/g_s^21/gs2​, since gs=eΦ0g_s=e^{\Phi_0}gs​=eΦ0​) and the wrong-sign-looking dilaton kinetic term (mostly-plus signature: a healthy scalar wants −12(∂Φ)2-\tfrac12(\partial\Phi)^2−21​(∂Φ)2, so the +4(∂Φ)2+4(\partial\Phi)^2+4(∂Φ)2 here looks backwards). Neither is a problem: they are artefacts of writing everything in the metric GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ that the string physically sees. This is string frame.

    Getting to Einstein frame. The e−2Φe^{-2\Phi}e−2Φ out front is ugly because it means GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ is not canonically normalized. Absorb it by rescaling the metric with the dilaton — split Φ=Φ0+Φ~\Phi=\Phi_0+\tilde\PhiΦ=Φ0​+Φ~ (constant plus varying) and define G~μν=e−4Φ~/(D−2) Gμν.\tilde G_{\mu\nu} = e^{-4\tilde\Phi/(D-2)}\,G_{\mu\nu}.G~μν​=e−4Φ~/(D−2)Gμν​. This is not a coordinate change or a symmetry — just a relabeling of fields, allowed precisely because a massless scalar Φ\PhiΦ exists to fold into the ruler. Why this exponent and no other? Write the inverse as Gμν=e2ωG~μνG_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\omega}\tilde G_{\mu\nu}Gμν​=e2ωG~μν​ with ω=2Φ~D−2\omega=\dfrac{2\tilde\Phi}{D-2}ω=D−22Φ~​, and track how the Einstein–Hilbert term transforms. Under Gμν=e2ωG~μνG_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\omega}\tilde G_{\mu\nu}Gμν​=e2ωG~μν​ in DDD dimensions, −G=eDω−G~\sqrt{-G}=e^{D\omega}\sqrt{-\tilde G}−G​=eDω−G~​ and R[G]=e−2ω(R~+⋯ )\mathcal R[G]=e^{-2\omega}(\tilde{\mathcal R}+\cdots)R[G]=e−2ω(R~+⋯), while the prefactor contributes e−2Φ→e−2Φ~e^{-2\Phi}\to e^{-2\tilde\Phi}e−2Φ→e−2Φ~ (the Φ0\Phi_0Φ0​ absorbs into the coupling). So −G e−2Φ R[G]  =  e Dω−2ω−2Φ~ −G~ R~+⋯ ,\sqrt{-G}\,e^{-2\Phi}\,\mathcal R[G] \;=\; e^{\,D\omega-2\omega-2\tilde\Phi}\,\sqrt{-\tilde G}\,\tilde{\mathcal R}+\cdots,−G​e−2ΦR[G]=eDω−2ω−2Φ~−G~​R~+⋯, and the exponent is (D−2) ω−2Φ~(D-2)\,\omega-2\tilde\Phi(D−2)ω−2Φ~. Choosing ω=2Φ~D−2\omega=\dfrac{2\tilde\Phi}{D-2}ω=D−22Φ~​ makes it vanish identically — the dilaton is expelled from the coefficient of R~\tilde{\mathcal R}R~. That is the only choice that does so, which is why the exponent is fixed.

    Carrying the same rescaling through the rest of the action (and dropping a total derivative), the D=26D=26D=26 result is S=12κ2∫d26X −G~(R~−112e−Φ~/3H2−16 ∂μΦ~ ∂μΦ~).S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}\int d^{26}X\,\sqrt{-\tilde G}\Big(\tilde{\mathcal R}-\tfrac{1}{12}e^{-\tilde\Phi/3}H^2-\tfrac16\,\partial_\mu\tilde\Phi\,\partial^\mu\tilde\Phi\Big).S=2κ21​∫d26X−G~​(R~−121​e−Φ~/3H2−61​∂μ​Φ~∂μΦ~). Now the gravity term is standard Einstein–Hilbert and the dilaton kinetic term has the healthy sign. Newton's constant emerges with the string coupling baked in: 8πGN=κ2=κ02 e2Φ0∼ℓs24 gs2.8\pi G_N=\kappa^2=\kappa_0^2\,e^{2\Phi_0}\sim \ell_s^{24}\,g_s^2.8πGN​=κ2=κ02​e2Φ0​∼ℓs24​gs2​. String frame and Einstein frame are the same physics — just two ways of packaging the fields. The mere fact that there are two natural metrics to choose from is possible only because a massless scalar Φ\PhiΦ exists to mix into the definition of length.

    Compactification payoff: KK reduction of all three

    Curl up one direction on a circle (see Toroidal compactification). A 25d observer sees each 26d field split according to whether its indices point along the circle:

    • Gμν→G_{\mu\nu}\toGμν​→ a 25d metric G~μν\tilde G_{\mu\nu}G~μν​, a U(1)U(1)U(1) gauge field Aμ=Gμ,25A_\mu=G_{\mu,25}Aμ​=Gμ,25​ (the KK photon), and a scalar σ=G25,25\sigma=G_{25,25}σ=G25,25​ (the radius modulus).
    • Bμν→B_{\mu\nu}\toBμν​→ a 25d BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ and a second U(1)U(1)U(1) gauge field A~μ=Bμ,25\tilde A_\mu=B_{\mu,25}A~μ​=Bμ,25​.
    • Φ→\Phi\toΦ→ a 25d scalar Φ\PhiΦ (a scalar reduces to a scalar).

    So a single graviton in 26d yields gravity + electromagnetism + a scalar in 25d — the original Kaluza–Klein miracle, with Maxwell theory literally falling out of higher-dimensional gravity. The two U(1)U(1)U(1)'s (from GGG and from BBB) are exactly the pair that gets enhanced to SU(2)×SU(2)SU(2)\times SU(2)SU(2)×SU(2) at the self-dual radius in T-duality.

    Under T-duality: the whole package transforms

    T-duality is not "metric only." Because GGG, BBB, and Φ\PhiΦ together define the sigma model, the duality acts on all three at once. When the coordinate along a circle isometry is dualized, the coefficient of (∂y)2(\partial y)^2(∂y)2 inverts (this is the R↦α′/RR\mapsto\alpha'/RR↦α′/R of T-duality), metric and BBB cross-terms trade places, and — because dualizing means doing a Gaussian path integral over a worldsheet gauge field — the leftover determinant shifts the dilaton: Φ~=Φ−12log⁡Gyy.\tilde\Phi=\Phi-\tfrac12\log G_{yy}.Φ~=Φ−21​logGyy​. For a plain rectangular circle with Gyy=R2/α′G_{yy}=R^2/\alpha'Gyy​=R2/α′ and no cross-terms this collapses to the familiar rules R↦α′R,gs↦α′ gsR,R\mapsto \frac{\alpha'}{R},\qquad g_s\mapsto \frac{\sqrt{\alpha'}\,g_s}{R},R↦Rα′​,gs​↦Rα′​gs​​, the second being just eΦ~e^{\tilde\Phi}eΦ~ with the shift above. The dilaton must move so that observers on the two dual circles measure the same low-energy physics — the R/gs2R/g_s^2R/gs2​ in front of the reduced action stays put. The full determinant/holonomy derivation lives in T-duality.

    Open questions

    • The dilaton's T-duality shift is quoted from the Gaussian-integral picture. The complete determinant/Jacobian and global-holonomy proof is deferred to T-duality.
    • Nothing in this tree-level bosonic action fixes Φ0\Phi_0Φ0​ (no dilaton potential). Stabilizing the string coupling requires ingredients — fluxes, superstring effects — outside this bosonic pass.

    Exercise

    The Einstein-frame rescaling G~μν=e−4Φ~/(D−2)Gμν\tilde G_{\mu\nu}=e^{-4\tilde\Phi/(D-2)}G_{\mu\nu}G~μν​=e−4Φ~/(D−2)Gμν​ is tuned to remove the dilaton from the coefficient of R~\tilde{\mathcal R}R~. Suppose a careless student instead used the exponent with the wrong sign, G~μν=e+4Φ~/(D−2)Gμν\tilde G_{\mu\nu}=e^{+4\tilde\Phi/(D-2)}G_{\mu\nu}G~μν​=e+4Φ~/(D−2)Gμν​. What exponential factor is then left multiplying −G~ R~\sqrt{-\tilde G}\,\tilde{\mathcal R}−G~​R~ in the action, and why does this fail to reach Einstein frame? Give the answer for general DDD, then evaluate at D=26D=26D=26.

    Solution

    With the wrong sign, the inverse relation is Gμν=e2ωG~μνG_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\omega}\tilde G_{\mu\nu}Gμν​=e2ωG~μν​ with ω=−2Φ~D−2\omega=-\dfrac{2\tilde\Phi}{D-2}ω=−D−22Φ~​ (opposite to the correct ω=+2Φ~D−2\omega=+\dfrac{2\tilde\Phi}{D-2}ω=+D−22Φ~​).

    Transforming the Einstein–Hilbert term exactly as in the note: −G=eDω−G~\sqrt{-G}=e^{D\omega}\sqrt{-\tilde G}−G​=eDω−G~​, R[G]=e−2ωR~+⋯\mathcal R[G]=e^{-2\omega}\tilde{\mathcal R}+\cdotsR[G]=e−2ωR~+⋯, and the prefactor gives e−2Φ~e^{-2\tilde\Phi}e−2Φ~. The total exponent multiplying −G~ R~\sqrt{-\tilde G}\,\tilde{\mathcal R}−G~​R~ is (D−2)ω−2Φ~=(D−2) ⁣(−2Φ~D−2)−2Φ~=−2Φ~−2Φ~=−4Φ~.(D-2)\omega-2\tilde\Phi = (D-2)\!\left(-\tfrac{2\tilde\Phi}{D-2}\right)-2\tilde\Phi = -2\tilde\Phi-2\tilde\Phi = -4\tilde\Phi.(D−2)ω−2Φ~=(D−2)(−D−22Φ~​)−2Φ~=−2Φ~−2Φ~=−4Φ~. So instead of cancelling, the dilaton dependence doubles: the gravity term becomes e−4Φ~ −G~ R~.e^{-4\tilde\Phi}\,\sqrt{-\tilde G}\,\tilde{\mathcal R}.e−4Φ~−G~​R~. This is worse than string frame (e−2Φe^{-2\Phi}e−2Φ), not better — the dilaton is still (more strongly) glued to the coefficient of the curvature, so the graviton is not canonically normalized and we are not in Einstein frame. Only the exponent that makes (D−2)ω−2Φ~=0(D-2)\omega-2\tilde\Phi=0(D−2)ω−2Φ~=0, i.e. the +++ sign, works. At D=26D=26D=26 the correct rescaling is G~μν=e−Φ~/6Gμν\tilde G_{\mu\nu}=e^{-\tilde\Phi/6}G_{\mu\nu}G~μν​=e−Φ~/6Gμν​ (since 4/(D−2)=4/24=1/64/(D-2)=4/24=1/64/(D−2)=4/24=1/6); the wrong-sign version e+Φ~/6Gμνe^{+\tilde\Phi/6}G_{\mu\nu}e+Φ~/6Gμν​ leaves the factor e−4Φ~e^{-4\tilde\Phi}e−4Φ~ above.
