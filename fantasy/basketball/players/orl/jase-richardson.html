Jase Richardson

Jase Richardson is a developmental combo guard whose encouraging July playmaking raises his dynasty ceiling more than it changes a crowded 2026-27 NBA role.

2025-26 baseline

Richardson played 54 games at 10.9 minutes, averaging 4.4 points, 1.2 rebounds, 1.1 assists, 0.4 steals, almost no blocks, 0.5 threes, and 0.5 turnovers. He shot .473 from the field and .717 from the line.

The efficient finishing was encouraging, but the sample did not yet establish a category-producing NBA role. Orlando's healthy guard queue begins with Suggs, Bane, and Black.

Summer League signal

Richardson recorded 15 points, four rebounds, and four assists on July 9; 15 points, five rebounds, and six assists on July 11; and 25 points on July 15. Orlando's July 16 feature emphasized what he was showing as a creator. The assist growth is the most fantasy-relevant signal because a pure scoring reserve would remain blocked.

Summer League opponents, pace, and roles are materially different from the NBA. The evidence increases confidence in long-term combo-guard utility but does not justify projecting 20 rotation minutes.

Role and pathway

Richardson is the fourth healthy guard and can enter behind either backcourt spot. The base gives him nine minutes across 60 appearances, with larger opportunities on Suggs maintenance nights or if Black moves into the starting lineup. Jevon Carter is the safer emergency organizer, but Richardson has the development incentive.

Category shape

  • Potential strengths: efficient interior guard scoring, improving playmaking, low turnovers in a modest role.
  • Needs proof: FT percentage, three-point volume, steals.
  • Weak: rebounds and blocks.

Without defensive events or high assist volume, he must become an efficient scoring guard to matter in 9-cat.

Career record

Pre-NBA: One season at Michigan State (2024-25), born October 16, 2005 and the youngest player in his draft class. Came off the bench for the first half of the year and started 15 of 36 games, finishing at 12.1 points, 3.3 rebounds and 1.9 assists in 25.3 minutes on .493/.412/.836 — a genuinely elite efficiency line for a freshman guard, with the shooting splits doing most of the draft work. Son of Jason Richardson, who won a national title at the same school. Draft: 2025, first round, 25th overall, Orlando.

Durability: 54 of 82 as a rookie. The missed games were rotation and assignment decisions on a deep playoff roster rather than a health story; no significant injury was reported.

Trajectory: One NBA season, and the efficiency held at a level rookies rarely sustain — .473 from the floor and .354 from three in 10.9 minutes, with a turnover rate low enough to be trusted. What is missing is volume in anything: 1.2 rebounds, 1.1 assists and essentially no blocks means there is no category he currently supplies. He enters 2026-27 as the fourth guard behind Suggs, Bane and Black, and the July Summer League passing — three straight games with real assist totals — is the only evidence that the ceiling is a combo guard rather than a scoring specialist. He is a dynasty stash on age and pedigree, not a redraft asset. The specific condition for relevance is an injury to Suggs or Bane that pushes him past 20 minutes for a multi-week stretch; short of that, nine minutes a night produces nothing rosterable.
