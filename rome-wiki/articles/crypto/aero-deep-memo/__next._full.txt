<h2 id="what-works">What Works</h2>
<p>Aerodrome is real Base infrastructure. It is the main Base liquidity hub, has visible fees, high lock participation, and better token liquidity than most smaller DeFi names.</p>
<p>The veAERO model routes fees and voting incentives to active lockers. That is a real value-capture mechanism if the investor is willing to lock, vote well, delegate to Relay, and accept strategy and duration risk.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-deep-pass-changed">What The Deep Pass Changed</h2>
<p>Liquid AERO is not the fee-capture asset. It is a dilutable option to become a veAERO operator. Passive liquid holders absorb emissions and only benefit indirectly if demand to lock AERO rises.</p>
<p>Emissions are still the core issue. On-chain reads put tail emissions around 21 bps/week, roughly 10.9% simple annualized to gauges, and roughly 12.7% simple annualized after rebase/team emissions. Current 30d incentives exceed holder revenue.</p>
<p>The Aero migration is not cosmetic. It combines Aerodrome and Velodrome under one token/governance/economic framework, changes NFTs and frontends, retires gauges/factories, and may alter voter participation mechanics. That is a near-term diligence gate.</p>
<h2 id="main-risks">Main Risks</h2>
<ul>
<li>Base concentration: Aerodrome is effectively a Base beta trade.</li>
<li>Emissions reflexivity: if AERO price falls, incentives buy less liquidity, which can reduce volume and fee capture.</li>
<li>Migration risk: users, LPs, veNFT holders, routers, and CEXs must transition cleanly.</li>
<li>Governance concentration: emergency council, factory, gauge, fee, managed veNFT, and Momentum Fund controls matter.</li>
<li>Front-end risk: Aerodrome had a prior DNS/front-end compromise with user losses, even though contracts were not the failure point.</li>
<li>Competition: Uniswap and PancakeSwap remain meaningful Base competitors.</li>
</ul>
