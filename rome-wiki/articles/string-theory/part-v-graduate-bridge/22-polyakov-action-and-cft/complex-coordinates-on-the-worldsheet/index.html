    Complex coordinates on the worldsheet

    Rotate the worldsheet to Euclidean signature and bundle its two coordinates into one complex number z=\sigma^1+i\sigma^2 . Then every 2D conformal transformation is just a holomorphic map z\to f(z) , every field splits into an independent left-moving (holomorphic) and right-moving (antiholomorphic) half, and the whole CFT toolkit — Laurent series, contour integrals, residues — becomes available. This is the working language of the worldsheet.

    Why complex coordinates

    The string sweeps out a 2D surface. In light-cone gauge you already saw that everything on it organizes into two independent flows: left-movers f(τ+σ)f(\tau+\sigma)f(τ+σ) and right-movers g(τ−σ)g(\tau-\sigma)g(τ−σ), riding the two null directions σ±=τ±σ\sigma^\pm=\tau\pm\sigmaσ±=τ±σ. Complex coordinates are the Euclidean reincarnation of exactly those two directions. Rotating time to be imaginary turns the two real null directions into one complex coordinate and its conjugate — zzz and zˉ\bar zzˉ — and the two flows become "depends only on zzz" (holomorphic) and "depends only on zˉ\bar zzˉ" (antiholomorphic).

    Why bother? Because on the complex plane, "conformal transformation" — a map that rescales the metric without shearing it — is the same thing as "holomorphic function." Angle-preserving maps of the plane are precisely z→f(z)z\to f(z)z→f(z). So the enormous, infinite-dimensional symmetry of a 2D CFT is nothing more exotic than complex analysis, and every calculation reduces to reading off a residue. The rest of this note makes that precise.

    Wick rotation and the complex coordinate

    Rotate the worldsheet time to imaginary values, σ0→−iσ2\sigma^0\to-i\sigma^2σ0→−iσ2, so the Lorentzian metric diag(−1,+1)\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1)diag(−1,+1) becomes the positive-definite diag(+1,+1)\mathrm{diag}(+1,+1)diag(+1,+1) in coordinates (σ1,σ2)(\sigma^1,\sigma^2)(σ1,σ2). Then bundle them:  z=σ1+iσ2,zˉ=σ1−iσ2 \boxed{\,z=\sigma^1+i\sigma^2,\qquad \bar z=\sigma^1-i\sigma^2\,}z=σ1+iσ2,zˉ=σ1−iσ2​ These are the Euclidean analogues of the light-cone coordinates σ±=τ±σ\sigma^\pm=\tau\pm\sigmaσ±=τ±σ that organized Mode expansions in light-cone gauge. That analogy is the whole reason holomorphic functions get called "left-moving" and antiholomorphic ones "right-moving."

    Conformal transformations = holomorphic maps

    In complex coordinates a conformal transformation of flat space is any holomorphic change of coordinate, z→z′=f(z),zˉ→zˉ′=fˉ(zˉ).z\to z'=f(z),\qquad \bar z\to\bar z'=\bar f(\bar z).z→z′=f(z),zˉ→zˉ′=fˉ​(zˉ). Under it ds2=dz dzˉ→∣f′(z)∣2 dz dzˉds^2=dz\,d\bar z\to|f'(z)|^2\,dz\,d\bar zds2=dzdzˉ→∣f′(z)∣2dzdzˉ — precisely the form g→Ω2gg\to\Omega^2 gg→Ω2g with Ω=∣f′(z)∣\Omega=|f'(z)|Ω=∣f′(z)∣, a local rescaling of the metric, which is a conformal transformation (cf. Weyl invariance). A whole function's worth of symmetry, f(z)f(z)f(z), is special to two dimensions; in d>2d>2d>2 the conformal group is finite-dimensional (SO(p+1,q+1)SO(p+1,q+1)SO(p+1,q+1)). This infinitude is the reason 2D CFT is so powerful — and the reason the string worldsheet is a tractable quantum theory at all.

    Two examples anchor it: z→z+az\to z+az→z+a is a translation, and z→ζzz\to\zeta zz→ζz is a rotation (for ∣ζ∣=1|\zeta|=1∣ζ∣=1) or a scaling/dilatation (for real ζ≠1\zeta\ne1ζ=1). These are generated by the Virasoro generators L−1L_{-1}L−1​ and L0L_0L0​ respectively — the two that, together with L1L_1L1​, form the anomaly-free global subalgebra.

    A practical convention: treat zzz and zˉ\bar zzˉ as independent variables, extending the worldsheet from R2\mathbb R^2R2 to C2\mathbb C^2C2, so Cauchy's theorem and residue calculus apply. At the end you restore the physical "real slice" zˉ=z∗\bar z=z^*zˉ=z∗.

    Holomorphic factorization

    The free-boson equation of motion in conformal gauge is the 2D wave equation; in complex coordinates it is simply ∂∂ˉ X=0.\partial\bar\partial\,X=0.∂∂ˉX=0. A function killed by ∂∂ˉ\partial\bar\partial∂∂ˉ is a holomorphic piece plus an antiholomorphic piece, so the general solution splits: X(z,zˉ)=X(z)+Xˉ(zˉ).X(z,\bar z)=X(z)+\bar X(\bar z).X(z,zˉ)=X(z)+Xˉ(zˉ). This is the Euclidean form of the left/right split Xμ=XL(τ+σ)+XR(τ−σ)X^\mu=X_L(\tau+\sigma)+X_R(\tau-\sigma)Xμ=XL​(τ+σ)+XR​(τ−σ) from Mode expansions in light-cone gauge. The two chiral halves of the string decouple, and from here on almost everything in worldsheet CFT factorizes into an independent holomorphic sector (functions of zzz) and antiholomorphic sector (functions of zˉ\bar zzˉ) — you solve one and copy the other by conjugation.

    The stress tensor goes (anti)holomorphic

    Factorization is sharpest for the Worldsheet stress tensor. Two facts about it, both derived elsewhere in this module, collapse dramatically in complex coordinates:

    • Tracelessness (from Weyl invariance), Tαα=0T^\alpha{}_\alpha=0Tαα​=0, becomes Tzzˉ=0T_{z\bar z}=0Tzzˉ​=0: the mixed component vanishes, killing the only piece that couples zzz and zˉ\bar zzˉ.
    • Conservation ∂αTαβ=0\partial^\alpha T_{\alpha\beta}=0∂αTαβ​=0 then reduces to ∂ˉ Tzz=0\bar\partial\,T_{zz}=0∂ˉTzz​=0 and ∂ Tzˉzˉ=0\partial\,T_{\bar z\bar z}=0∂Tzˉzˉ​=0.

    So the two surviving components are each chiral: ∂ˉ Tzz=0  ⇒  Tzz≡T(z) holomorphic,∂ Tzˉzˉ=0  ⇒  Tzˉzˉ≡Tˉ(zˉ) antiholomorphic.\bar\partial\,T_{zz}=0\;\Rightarrow\; T_{zz}\equiv T(z)\ \text{holomorphic},\qquad \partial\,T_{\bar z\bar z}=0\;\Rightarrow\; T_{\bar z\bar z}\equiv\bar T(\bar z)\ \text{antiholomorphic}.∂ˉTzz​=0⇒Tzz​≡T(z) holomorphic,∂Tzˉzˉ​=0⇒Tzˉzˉ​≡Tˉ(zˉ) antiholomorphic. Conservation plus tracelessness force the stress tensor to be a holomorphic function — and holomorphic functions are exactly what Cauchy's residue theorem handles. That is precisely why the Virasoro algebra can be extracted by a contour integral instead of a hard operator computation.

    Modes by contour integral

    Because T(z)T(z)T(z) is holomorphic it has a Laurent expansion on the plane, and the Virasoro generators are its modes: T(z)=∑m∈ZLmzm+2,Ln=12πi∮dz  zn+1 T(z),T(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb Z}\frac{L_m}{z^{m+2}},\qquad L_n=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dz\;z^{n+1}\,T(z),T(z)=∑m∈Z​zm+2Lm​​,Ln​=2πi1​∮dzzn+1T(z), and likewise Lˉn\bar L_nLˉn​ from Tˉ(zˉ)\bar T(\bar z)Tˉ(zˉ). The power zm+2z^{m+2}zm+2 (rather than zmz^mzm) is fixed by demanding TTT carry conformal weight 222: under z→λzz\to\lambda zz→λz, T→λ−2TT\to\lambda^{-2}TT→λ−2T, and the offset of 222 in the exponent is exactly what keeps each mode LmL_mLm​ scale-covariant. The second formula is just Cauchy's residue extraction of a Laurent coefficient. Inside correlators these operator products are radially ordered — larger ∣z∣|z|∣z∣ placed to the left, the complex-plane version of time ordering — and that ordering is what makes the contour deformation producing the Virasoro algebra legitimate.

    The cylinder–plane map

    The string worldsheet is naturally a cylinder: space σ\sigmaσ is periodic, time τ\tauτ runs along the tube. Give it a complex coordinate w=σ+iτw=\sigma+i\tauw=σ+iτ with σ∈[0,2π)\sigma\in[0,2\pi)σ∈[0,2π). The exponential map z=e−iwz=e^{-iw}z=e−iw unrolls the cylinder onto the complex plane: each constant-time slice τ=const\tau=\text{const}τ=const becomes a circle ∣z∣=eτ|z|=e^{\tau}∣z∣=eτ, and the far past τ→−∞\tau\to-\inftyτ→−∞ shrinks to the single point z=0z=0z=0. This is why CFT on the plane (radial quantization) computes the string spectrum — a state prepared in the infinite past is an operator inserted at the origin. The map is not free: the stress tensor, being non-primary, picks up a constant Schwarzian shift, Tcyl(w)=−z2 Tplane(z)+c24,T_{\text{cyl}}(w)=-z^2\,T_{\text{plane}}(z)+\frac{c}{24},Tcyl​(w)=−z2Tplane​(z)+24c​, and that +c/24+c/24+c/24 is one of the physical faces of the central charge — the shift that generates the Casimir energy of the ground state on the cylinder. It is the density-level source, not the energy itself: integrating TcylT_{\text{cyl}}Tcyl​ over the spatial circle turns this shift into the ground-state energy E=−2π24(c+cˉ)E=-\tfrac{2\pi}{24}(c+\bar c)E=−242π​(c+cˉ), negative and involving both chiral halves (see Virasoro algebra and central charge).

    Open questions

    • Reality slice. Treating z,zˉz,\bar zz,zˉ as independent is a calculational device. Physical Euclidean worldsheets sit on the real slice zˉ=z∗\bar z=z^*zˉ=z∗, restored after the contour/OPE manipulations.
    • Wick rotation. This note fixes the local Euclidean convention. A full Lorentzian-to-Euclidean path-integral derivation, including boundary phases, is useful but unnecessary for the local OPE algebra of this module.
