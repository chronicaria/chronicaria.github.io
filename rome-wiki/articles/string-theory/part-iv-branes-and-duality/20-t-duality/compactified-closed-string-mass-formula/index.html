    Compactified closed-string mass formula

    A closed string on a circle has two independent zero modes — one for momentum, one for winding — and its mass is the Pythagorean sum M^2 = p^2 + w^2 + \tfrac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp + \bar N^\perp - 2) .

    Two integers, two energies

    A closed string on a circle carries two integers: how much momentum it has going around the circle (nnn, spacing 1/R1/R1/R), and how many times it wraps the circle (mmm, winding). Both cost energy. Momentum costs p=n/Rp = n/Rp=n/R, cheap on a big circle. Winding costs w=mR/α′w = mR/\alpha'w=mR/α′; a wound string is a stretched rubber band, expensive on a big circle. The two energies pull in opposite directions in RRR, and that opposition is the entire engine of T-duality.

    The derivation turns on one fact: on a circle, the left-moving and right-moving halves of the string no longer share a single momentum. They split into two independent zero modes αˉ0\bar\alpha_0αˉ0​ (left) and α0\alpha_0α0​ (right). Their sum is momentum, their difference is winding. Everything below turns that split into a mass. Prerequisites: Momentum modes on a circle, Winding modes, Light-cone gauge.

    Step 0 — split into left- and right-movers

    The compact coordinate X(τ,σ)X(\tau,\sigma)X(τ,σ) satisfies the free wave equation ∂τ2X=∂σ2X\partial_\tau^2 X = \partial_\sigma^2 X∂τ2​X=∂σ2​X, so every solution is a left-moving wave plus a right-moving wave: X(τ,σ)=XL(u)+XR(v),u=τ+σ,v=τ−σ.X(\tau,\sigma) = X_L(u) + X_R(v), \qquad u = \tau+\sigma,\quad v = \tau-\sigma.X(τ,σ)=XL​(u)+XR​(v),u=τ+σ,v=τ−σ. This is just d'Alembert. It matters here because T-duality will turn out to be the operation of flipping the sign of XRX_RXR​ alone — which is only meaningful once the two halves are separated.

    Step 2 — sum is momentum, difference is winding

    The momentum around the circle is the total worldsheet current integrated over the string.     Step 5 — the formula in integer labels

    Substituting p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R, w=mR/α′w=mR/\alpha'w=mR/α′: \boxed{\,M^2 = \Big(\frac{n}{R}\Big)^2 + \Big(\frac{mR}{\alpha'}\Big)^2 + \frac{2}{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp + \bar N^\perp - 2\big).\,} \tag{8} Read term by term:

    • (n/R)2(n/R)^2(n/R)2 — momentum energy; falls as RRR grows (a big circle has finely-spaced momenta).
    • (mR/α′)2(mR/\alpha')^2(mR/α′)2 — winding/stretch energy; rises as RRR grows (a big circle is expensive to wrap).
    • 2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)\tfrac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2)α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2) — oscillator + zero-point energy; radius-independent. At n=m=N⊥=Nˉ⊥=0n=m=N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=0n=m=N⊥=Nˉ⊥=0 it gives the tachyon, M2=−4/α′M^2 = -4/\alpha'M2=−4/α′.

    The payoff is the shape of that first line. Compactification adds p2+w2p^2 + w^2p2+w2, not (p+w)2(p+w)^2(p+w)2: the cross term 2pw2pw2pw never enters the mass. It went instead into the separate level-matching constraint (5), N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nmN^\perp-\bar N^\perp = nmN⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm. That clean split is why the mass is symmetric in ppp and www even though the left and right excitation levels need not be equal. And because the momentum term falls while the winding term rises, their sum is left invariant by the swap R→α′/R, n↔mR\to\alpha'/R,\ n\leftrightarrow mR→α′/R, n↔m: a small circle and a big circle carry the identical list of masses, and a string cannot tell them apart. That is T-duality — see Spectrum invariance under R to alpha-prime over R.

    Exercise

    A vector (a would-be gauge boson) needs at least one oscillator carrying a non-compact Lorentz index.
