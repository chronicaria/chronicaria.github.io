    Bulk versus boundary observables

    If the two sides of AdS/CFT are really the same physics, every question has two answers that must agree — but the two calculators are switched on in opposite regimes, so the game is to find quantities you can compute on both sides and check.

    Hologram, made precise

    A hologram stores a 3D scene on a 2D film. AdS/CFT says gravity in a (d+1)(d+1)(d+1)-dimensional bulk is stored, without loss, on a ddd-dimensional boundary theory that has no gravity in it at all. This note is the dictionary: which bulk object is which boundary object, why you can seldom check a translation directly, and the one degree-of-freedom count that makes "the boundary has enough room" quantitative.

    The dictionary: what maps to what

    A duality is only content if it says what corresponds to what. Four entries, from firmest to softest:

    Bulk (AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5, gravity) Boundary (4D N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM, no gravity)
    isometry of AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​: the group SO(4,2)SO(4,2)SO(4,2) conformal symmetry (15 generators)
    isometry of S5S^5S5: the group SO(6)SO(6)SO(6) R-symmetry rotating the scalars and fermions
    geometry radius R/α′=λ1/4R/\sqrt{\alpha'}=\lambda^{1/4}R/α′​=λ1/4 't Hooft coupling λ=gYM2N\lambda=g_{YM}^2Nλ=gYM2​N
    string loop coupling gs=λ/Ng_s=\lambda/Ngs​=λ/N 1/N1/N1/N (loop counting at fixed λ\lambdaλ)
    a bulk field ϕ(x,z,Ω)\phi(x,z,\Omega)ϕ(x,z,Ω) a boundary operator O(x)\mathcal{O}(x)O(x)

    The first row is a clean group-theory equality: SO(4,2)SO(4,2)SO(4,2) is the 4D conformal group, so the bulk geometry already knows the boundary's symmetry (derived in Conformal boundary). The second row: harmonics on the S5S^5S5 carry SO(6)SO(6)SO(6) labels, which on the boundary become the internal quantum numbers rotating the six scalars and four fermions of N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM — the S5S^5S5 is not five more boundary dimensions. Rows three and four are the parameter dictionary gs=gYM2g_s=g_{YM}^2gs​=gYM2​, R4/α′2=λR^4/\alpha'^2=\lambdaR4/α′2=λ from Gauge-gravity duality, read off with the genus counting of Large-N gauge theory.

    The last row — bulk field ↔\leftrightarrow↔ boundary operator — is the operational heart of the duality and the one entry this module can only name, not use. Its precise form (the GKP–Witten relation) is below.

    Why you can rarely check a translation directly

    "Same physics, two descriptions" means any single number computed in the bulk must equal the same number computed on the boundary. The catch: each description is only tractable in the opposite regime.

    λ≫1⏟geometry weakly curved: gravity is easyλ≪1⏟coupling weak: gauge theory is easy\underbrace{\lambda \gg 1}_{\text{geometry weakly curved: gravity is easy}} \qquad\qquad \underbrace{\lambda \ll 1}_{\text{coupling weak: gauge theory is easy}}geometry weakly curved: gravity is easyλ≫1​​coupling weak: gauge theory is easyλ≪1​​

    Large λ\lambdaλ means R/α′=λ1/4≫1R/\sqrt{\alpha'}=\lambda^{1/4}\gg1R/α′​=λ1/4≫1, a gently curved space where classical supergravity is a good approximation — but then the gauge theory is strongly coupled and its perturbation theory is useless. Small λ\lambdaλ flips both. The two easy windows don't overlap, so you almost never get to compute the same quantity on both sides and watch it match. That is exactly what makes the duality powerful (it hands you the hard side's answer) and hard to test (you can't usually check the hard side).

    What rescues testability is supersymmetry. It forces certain quantities — protected observables — to be independent of λ\lambdaλ. You compute a protected quantity where it's easy (weak coupling, gauge side) and compare it to the strong-coupling gravity prediction; if they agree, that is a real cross-check spanning both regimes. Several such quantities have matched. This is strong evidence for the duality, not a proof for every observable.

    The holographic count: does the boundary have enough room?

    This is what makes "hologram" more than a slogan. If a 4D theory is to store all the physics of a 5D gravitational interior, it needs a comparable number of degrees of freedom. Do the counts match?

    Boundary side. An SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) gauge theory has fields in the adjoint representation, which has N2−1≈N2N^2-1\approx N^2N2−1≈N2 components. So the number of boundary degrees of freedom scales as nbdy∼N2.n_{\text{bdy}} \sim N^2.nbdy​∼N2.

    Bulk side. Gravitational "capacity" is measured by entropy, and a black hole's entropy is its horizon area in Planck units, not its volume (Entropy-area relation) — the first structural hint of holography. Measuring the AdS5_55​ horizon area in the appropriate Newton constant G(5)G^{(5)}G(5) gives a dimensionless bulk capacity nbulk∼R3G(5).n_{\text{bulk}} \sim \frac{R^3}{G^{(5)}}.nbulk​∼G(5)R3​. Reducing 10D gravity on the S5S^5S5 fixes G(5)G^{(5)}G(5). Using G(10)∼gs2α′4G^{(10)}\sim g_s^2\alpha'^4G(10)∼gs2​α′4, Vol(S5)∼R5\mathrm{Vol}(S^5)\sim R^5Vol(S5)∼R5, and R4/α′2=gsNR^4/\alpha'^2=g_s NR4/α′2=gs​N from the dictionary, \frac{R^3}{G^{(5)}}\sim \frac{R^3\,\mathrm{Vol}(S^5)}{G^{(10)}}\sim\frac{R^8}{g_s^2\alpha'^4}=\Big(\frac{R^4}{\alpha'^2}\Big)^2\frac{1}{g_s^2}=\frac{(g_s N)^2}{g_s^2}=N^2. \tag{1} The gsg_sgs​ cancels, leaving nbulk∼N2n_{\text{bulk}}\sim N^2nbulk​∼N2.

    Both sides scale as N2N^2N2. That commensurability is the minimum holography demands, and AdS/CFT passes it. The boundary really does have enough room. (This is a scaling check, not a coefficient match; the precise 1/41/41/4 area law needs the full dictionary.)

    The real dictionary, on record: GKP–Witten

    The quantitative bulk↔boundary statement is the GKP–Witten relation. A bulk field ϕ\phiϕ that approaches a boundary value ϕ0(x)\phi_0(x)ϕ0​(x) acts as a source for a boundary operator O(x)\mathcal{O}(x)O(x), and the two theories' generating functionals are equal: Z_{\text{gravity}}\big[\phi\to\phi_0\big]\;=\;\Big\langle \exp\!\int_{\partial\mathrm{AdS}}\phi_0\,\mathcal{O}\Big\rangle_{\text{CFT}}. \tag{2} In words: computing the bulk partition function as a function of the boundary data is the same as computing the boundary theory's correlators. In the classical-gravity limit the left side is e−Son-shell[ϕ0]e^{-S_{\text{on-shell}}[\phi_0]}e−Son-shell​[ϕ0​], so the practical rule becomes "differentiate the bulk on-shell action with respect to boundary sources to get CFT correlators." The relation also fixes how a bulk field's mass mmm sets the boundary operator's scaling dimension Δ\DeltaΔ: for a scalar in AdSd+1\mathrm{AdS}_{d+1}AdSd+1​, \Delta(\Delta-d)=m^2R^2, \tag{3} which is the indicial equation for a power-law fall-off ϕ∼zs\phi\sim z^{s}ϕ∼zs solving the AdS wave equation near the boundary (heavier bulk field →\to→ larger boundary dimension). Both roots sss satisfy (3); which one is the operator dimension is the subtlety flagged below.

    This note writes (2)–(3) only so the shape of the answer is on record. It is not derived here and cannot be — using it needs path-integral QFT, the on-shell gravitational action, and holographic renormalization, none of which this module builds. Recognize the relation when you meet it later; do not treat it as established here.

    Open questions

    • Eq. (1) is a scaling argument. Pinning the coefficient (and hence the exact 1/41/41/4 area law) requires the actual S5S^5S5 reduction of type IIB supergravity with all numerical factors, not the ∼\sim∼ estimate used here.
    • The ±\pm± roots of (3), Δ±=d2±d24+m2R2\Delta_\pm=\tfrac{d}{2}\pm\sqrt{\tfrac{d^2}{4}+m^2R^2}Δ±​=2d​±4d2​+m2R2​, both solve the equation. Which root is the operator dimension (and when "alternate quantization" uses the other) is a real subtlety — flag before using (3) quantitatively.
    • "Protected observables are λ\lambdaλ-independent" rests on supersymmetric nonrenormalization theorems only named here. Which observables (chiral primaries, certain anomaly coefficients)? Beyond scope.
    • Is the full field↔operator map known, or only for special operators? Known precisely for protected/chiral-primary operators and a handful of others; the generic map is hard and open.

    Exercise

    The dictionary gives gs=λ/Ng_s=\lambda/Ngs​=λ/N and R4/α′2=λR^4/\alpha'^2=\lambdaR4/α′2=λ. Using only these, show that the dimensionless bulk gravitational capacity R3/G(5)R^3/G^{(5)}R3/G(5) is independent of λ\lambdaλ at fixed NNN, and confirm it scales as N2N^2N2. Then state, in one sentence, what would have gone wrong with the holographic interpretation if the gsg_sgs​ had not cancelled in (1).

    Solution

    Start from R3/G(5)∼R8/(gs2α′4)R^3/G^{(5)}\sim R^8/(g_s^2\alpha'^4)R3/G(5)∼R8/(gs2​α′4) (from G(10)∼gs2α′4G^{(10)}\sim g_s^2\alpha'^4G(10)∼gs2​α′4 and Vol(S5)∼R5\mathrm{Vol}(S^5)\sim R^5Vol(S5)∼R5). Write R8=(R4/α′2)2 α′4=λ2α′4R^8=(R^4/\alpha'^2)^2\,\alpha'^4=\lambda^2\alpha'^4R8=(R4/α′2)2α′4=λ2α′4. Then R3G(5)∼λ2α′4gs2α′4=λ2gs2=λ2(λ/N)2=N2.\frac{R^3}{G^{(5)}}\sim\frac{\lambda^2\alpha'^4}{g_s^2\alpha'^4}=\frac{\lambda^2}{g_s^2}=\frac{\lambda^2}{(\lambda/N)^2}=N^2.G(5)R3​∼gs2​α′4λ2α′4​=gs2​λ2​=(λ/N)2λ2​=N2. The α′\alpha'α′ and λ\lambdaλ both cancel, leaving R3/G(5)∼N2R^3/G^{(5)}\sim N^2R3/G(5)∼N2 — no λ\lambdaλ dependence, as claimed.

    If gsg_sgs​ had not cancelled, the bulk capacity would depend on the coupling λ\lambdaλ while the boundary count stays fixed at N2−1≈N2N^2-1\approx N^2N2−1≈N2 (the adjoint dimension, which knows nothing about gYMg_{YM}gYM​). The two "number of degrees of freedom" counts would then disagree over most of parameter space, and the claim that a fixed 4D theory stores the whole bulk would fail. The cancellation is what lets a coupling-independent boundary field count match the bulk capacity.
