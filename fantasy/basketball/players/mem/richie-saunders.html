Range and risks

  • Strengths: shooting, steals, rebounding for a wing and experienced decision-making.
  • Liabilities: ACL recovery, age-adjusted upside and low projected assists/blocks.
  • Floor/base/ceiling: rehab extends and he misses most of the season / cleared in-season for limited development games / cleared near opening night and earns 18-20 minutes once the wing room thins, providing 1.3 threes and a steal.
  • Branches: rehab progression dominates, but roster consolidation is a real second lever — Memphis must cut six standard players, and a Grant buyout would open exactly his minutes.

Draft: 2026, second round, 32nd overall, Memphis — selected with the ACL tear already known.

Saunders has never appeared in an NBA game. His most recent season is the injury-shortened 2025-26 BYU line above.

Durability: The controlling fact of his profile. He tore his ACL 45 seconds into the February 15, 2026 game against Colorado, ending his college career after 25 games. No team-published return-to-play window has been located. He was not able to play Summer League. His own stated expectation, to John Hollinger, is that he will be ready near the start of the season; that is a player's expectation, not a clearance, and the two should not be merged.

Trajectory: The college arc is unusually clean — scoring, rebounds, assists and steals all rose in each of four seasons, and he shot .432 from three on real volume in his junior year before settling at .376 as the primary option. That is a 3-and-D wing profile with better steal production than most, and Memphis paying a 32nd pick for a player with a known ACL tear is a meaningful vote. The discount is age: he turns 25 in September 2026, so there is no long development runway and what you see is close to the finished product. He becomes fantasy-relevant only on a published clearance plus a wing-room opening — Memphis must cut six standard players, and a Jerami Grant buyout would clear precisely the minutes he projects into.
