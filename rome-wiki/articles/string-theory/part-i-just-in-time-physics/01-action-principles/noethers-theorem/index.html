    Noether's theorem

    Every continuous symmetry of the action hands you a conserved quantity — for free, and mechanically. A one-parameter symmetry produces a current j^\mu with \partial_\mu j^\mu = 0 , hence a charge Q=\int j^0\,d^dx that never changes.

    The entire argument fits in three sentences. A symmetry makes δS\delta SδS vanish; the equations of motion make the bulk of δS\delta SδS vanish; so the leftover boundary term must take the same value at the start and the end of the motion — and a quantity equal at every pair of endpoints is a constant. That constant is the conserved charge, and energy, momentum, angular momentum, and electric charge are all this same boundary term, read off for different symmetries.

    Why a mathematician should care: this is the theorem that turns conservation laws from a list of lucky accidents into a dictionary. You don't discover that momentum is conserved — you notice space looks the same everywhere and Noether hands you momentum. For string theory it is the machine that produces the string's spacetime momentum and the worldsheet stress tensor, so it must be airtight before we build strings.

    Two words of vocabulary. On-shell = "after imposing the equations of motion"; off-shell = "for any trial path, EOM not assumed." Noether's argument lives in the gap between them: an identity that holds off-shell, then read on-shell.

    Convention throughout: a continuous symmetry has a small constant parameter ϵ\epsilonϵ. Below, δq\delta qδq and δϕ\delta\phiδϕ denote the coefficient of ϵ\epsilonϵ — the parameter itself is stripped off.

    The mechanic's version: one coordinate, fixed time

    Start with S=∫titfL(q,q˙,t) dtS=\int_{t_i}^{t_f} L(q,\dot q,t)\,dtS=∫ti​tf​​L(q,q˙​,t)dt. Suppose the transformation q(t)→q(t)+ϵ δqq(t)\to q(t)+\epsilon\,\delta qq(t)→q(t)+ϵδq leaves the action invariant. The proof is two lines that pull in opposite directions and meet at the boundary.

    Line 1 (off-shell identity). For any variation whatsoever, integration by parts splits δS\delta SδS into a boundary piece and a bulk piece — this is the master formula from Euler-Lagrange equation: \delta S=\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\,\delta q\right]_{t_i}^{t_f}+\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial q}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\right)\delta q\,dt.\tag{1} This holds for every path, symmetry or not, on-shell or not. It is pure calculus.

    Line 2 (feed in the two facts).

    • Go on-shell. If q(t)q(t)q(t) solves Euler–Lagrange, the bulk bracket in (1) is exactly zero. Only the boundary term survives: δS=[∂L∂q˙ δq]titf.\delta S=\left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\,\delta q\right]_{t_i}^{t_f}.δS=[∂q˙​∂L​δq]ti​tf​​.
    • Use the symmetry. By assumption this δq\delta qδq leaves SSS invariant for every path — in particular for the solution — so δS=0\delta S=0δS=0.

    Setting the two equal, the boundary term itself vanishes: ∂L∂q˙δq\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\delta q∂q˙​∂L​δq takes the same value at tit_iti​ and tft_ftf​. But tft_ftf​ was arbitrary — slide it anywhere and equality with tit_iti​ still holds. A quantity equal at every instant is constant:  Q≡∂L∂q˙ δq=const. \boxed{\,Q\equiv\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\,\delta q=\text{const}.\,}Q≡∂q˙​∂L​δq=const.​

    That QQQ is the Noether charge: symmetry kills δS\delta SδS, the equations of motion kill the bulk, and the leftover boundary quantity is what remains conserved.

    This is the payoff of respecting boundary terms in Boundary terms and allowed variations. There the endpoint expression [p δq][p\,\delta q][pδq] looked like a nuisance to cancel; here the same expression, evaluated on a symmetry variation, is the conserved quantity. Same term, opposite attitude.

    Translation →\to→ momentum

    If LLL depends on q˙\dot qq˙​ but not qqq, the shift q→q+ϵq\to q+\epsilonq→q+ϵ (so δq=1\delta q=1δq=1) leaves LLL untouched. Then Q=∂L/∂q˙=pQ=\partial L/\partial\dot q=pQ=∂L/∂q˙​=p. Space looks the same everywhere ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ momentum is conserved. (Consistent with p˙=∂L/∂q=0\dot p=\partial L/\partial q=0p˙​=∂L/∂q=0 straight from Euler-Lagrange equation.)

    One formula that contains both

    Allow both a time shift t→t+ϵτt\to t+\epsilon\taut→t+ϵτ and a coordinate shift, with the transformed path obeying q′(t+ϵτ)=q(t)+ϵηq'(t+\epsilon\tau)=q(t)+\epsilon\etaq′(t+ϵτ)=q(t)+ϵη, and with the Lagrangian changing by at most a total derivative, L→L+ϵ dF/dtL\to L+\epsilon\,dF/dtL→L+ϵdF/dt. Running the fixed-time argument on the equivalent fixed-time variation δˉq=ϵ(η−q˙ τ)\bar\delta q=\epsilon(\eta-\dot q\,\tau)δˉq=ϵ(η−q˙​τ) — the shifted integration limits contribute the extra LτL\tauLτ — the single conserved boundary quantity is Q=p(\eta-\dot q\,\tau)+L\tau-F=p\eta-H\tau-F.\tag{2} The term −q˙ τ-\dot q\,\tau−q˙​τ appears because shifting ttt drags qqq along its own slope; grouping terms produces exactly the energy H=pq˙−LH=p\dot q-LH=pq˙​−L. Two checks: (τ,η,F)=(0,1,0)(\tau,\eta,F)=(0,1,0)(τ,η,F)=(0,1,0) gives Q=pQ=pQ=p (momentum), and (1,0,0)(1,0,0)(1,0,0) gives Q=−HQ=-HQ=−H (energy, up to the conventional sign). Momentum and energy are the same Noether charge, read for space- and time-translations respectively.

    The field version: from a charge to a current

    Promote q(t)q(t)q(t) to a field ϕ(x)\phi(x)ϕ(x) over DDD-dimensional spacetime with d=D−1d=D-1d=D−1 spatial dimensions, S=∫dDx L(ϕ,∂μϕ)S=\int d^Dx\,\mathcal{L}(\phi,\partial_\mu\phi)S=∫dDxL(ϕ,∂μ​ϕ), in units with c=1c=1c=1 so that x0=tx^0=tx0=t (see Euler-Lagrange equation). A continuous internal symmetry ϕ→ϕ+ϵ δϕ\phi\to\phi+\epsilon\,\delta\phiϕ→ϕ+ϵδϕ now buys you not one number but a conserved current: \boxed{\,j^\mu=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\delta\phi\,}\qquad\text{with}\qquad \partial_\mu j^\mu=0\ \ (\text{on-shell}).\tag{3}

    Read the current physically before the algebra: j0j^0j0 is the density of the conserved stuff, jij^iji is its flux. The statement ∂μjμ=0\partial_\mu j^\mu=0∂μ​jμ=0 is a continuity equation — the local promise that the stuff is never created or destroyed, only moved. Conservation is local first; a single global charge appears only after you integrate over space.

    Why ∂μjμ=0\partial_\mu j^\mu=0∂μ​jμ=0. The last equality is the assumption that L\mathcal{L}L is invariant; here δL\delta\mathcal LδL, like δϕ\delta\phiδϕ, has the ϵ\epsilonϵ stripped off. (If L\mathcal{L}L instead shifts by a total divergence, δL=∂μKμ\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\mu K^\muδL=∂μ​Kμ, the conserved current is jμ−Kμj^\mu-K^\mujμ−Kμ — needed just below.)

    Local law →\to→ global charge. Split ∂μjμ=0\partial_\mu j^\mu=0∂μ​jμ=0 into ∂tj0+∂iji=0\partial_t j^0+\partial_i j^i=0∂t​j0+∂i​ji=0, integrate over all space, and drop the spatial divergence as a boundary term at infinity (fields decay): ddt∫ddx j0⏟Q=−∫ddx ∂iji=0⟹Q=const.\frac{d}{dt}\underbrace{\int d^dx\,j^0}_{\displaystyle Q}=-\int d^dx\,\partial_i j^i=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Q=\text{const}.dtd​Q∫ddxj0​​=−∫ddx∂i​ji=0⟹Q=const. A region's charge can change only by flux leaking across its boundary; seal the boundary and QQQ is frozen — the field-theory echo of the mechanic's constant QQQ.

    Spacetime translations →\to→ the stress-energy tensor

    When the symmetry also moves coordinates, the current picks up an extra term — exactly the −Hτ-H\tau−Hτ move from formula (2). For a translation xν→xν+ϵνx^\nu\to x^\nu+\epsilon^\nuxν→xν+ϵν the parameter is a vector, so keep it explicit and strip it only at the end.     The same story, twice, in physics you already trust

    • The nonrelativistic string. Shifting the whole string sideways, y→y+ϵy\to y+\epsilony→y+ϵ, leaves the string Lagrangian invariant, and the current (3) with δy=1\delta y=1δy=1 has components Pt=∂L/∂y˙=μ0y˙\mathcal{P}^t=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot y=\mu_0\dot yPt=∂L/∂y˙​=μ0​y˙​ (transverse momentum density) and Px=∂L/∂y′=−T0 y′\mathcal{P}^x=\partial\mathcal L/\partial y'=-T_0\,y'Px=∂L/∂y′=−T0​y′ (its flux). No stress-tensor sign appears here because the symmetry shifts the field value, not the base coordinate. The equation of motion is literally the continuity equation ∂tPt+∂xPx=0\partial_t\mathcal{P}^t+\partial_x\mathcal{P}^x=0∂t​Pt+∂x​Px=0, and the charge py=∫0aPt dxp_y=\int_0^a\mathcal{P}^t\,dxpy​=∫0a​Ptdx is conserved iff no flux escapes the ends — free (Neumann) ends conserve it, a wall does not. That is Noether meeting Boundary terms and allowed variations: local conservation is automatic, but the global charge leaks if flux crosses a boundary (a wall, later a D-brane).
    • The relativistic particle. For S=−m∫dsS=-m\int dsS=−m∫ds (units with c=1c=1c=1 again), invariance under xμ→xμ+ϵμx^\mu\to x^\mu+\epsilon^\muxμ→xμ+ϵμ gives the conserved four-momentum pμ=m dxμ/dsp^\mu=m\,dx^\mu/dspμ=mdxμ/ds, and the equation of motion is precisely its conservation, dpμ/ds=0dp^\mu/ds=0dpμ/ds=0. Translation symmetry →\to→ four-momentum, word for word as the mechanic's version predicts (see Reparameterization invariance).

    Conservation laws are not gifts from nowhere; they are bookkeeping forced on us by the symmetries of the action.

    Crucial contrast: symmetry vs. redundancy

    Everything above is Noether's first theorem, and it applies only to global symmetries — finitely many constant parameters, relating physically distinct states (a constant translation moves you to a genuinely different place). A local gauge redundancy like reparameterization invariance instead relabels the same state. It yields no new charge; its Noether content (the second theorem) is a constraint and identities among the equations of motion. Getting a constant QQQ requires a real symmetry, not a relabeling — see Reparameterization invariance.

    Open questions

    • The canonical TμνT^\mu{}_\nuTμν​ in (4) need not be symmetric; the Belinfante procedure symmetrizes it once spin or gravity enters. Not needed for this module, but it is the tensor that couples to the metric later.
    • Gauge symmetries can carry genuine boundary charges in advanced settings; here the redundancy-to-constraint lesson is the first-pass fact strings require.

    Exercise

    A single free nonrelativistic particle in one dimension has L=12mq˙2L=\tfrac12 m\dot q^2L=21​mq˙​2. Under a Galilean boost q→q+ϵ tq\to q+\epsilon\, tq→q+ϵt (shift the trajectory by a uniform velocity ϵ\epsilonϵ, so δq=t\delta q=tδq=t), the velocity shifts as q˙→q˙+ϵ\dot q\to\dot q+\epsilonq˙​→q˙​+ϵ, and LLL is not invariant. Show that the boost is nonetheless a symmetry — the change in LLL is a total time derivative — and use the refined formula (2) to find the conserved Noether charge. Interpret it.

    Solution

    Under q→q+ϵtq\to q+\epsilon tq→q+ϵt we have τ=0\tau=0τ=0 and η=t\eta=tη=t, and the velocity shifts by a constant, q˙→q˙+ϵ\dot q\to\dot q+\epsilonq˙​→q˙​+ϵ. Expand the Lagrangian directly: L  →  12m(q˙+ϵ)2=L+ϵ mq˙+O(ϵ2),L\;\to\;\tfrac12 m(\dot q+\epsilon)^2=L+\epsilon\,m\dot q+O(\epsilon^2),L→21​m(q˙​+ϵ)2=L+ϵmq˙​+O(ϵ2), and mq˙=ddt(mq)m\dot q=\frac{d}{dt}(mq)mq˙​=dtd​(mq) identically, on any path. So L→L+ϵ dF/dtL\to L+\epsilon\,dF/dtL→L+ϵdF/dt with F=mqF=mqF=mq: not invariant, but off by a total derivative — exactly the case formula (2) covers. The boost is a legitimate symmetry.

    Apply Q=pη−Hτ−FQ=p\eta-H\tau-FQ=pη−Hτ−F with p=mq˙p=m\dot qp=mq˙​, η=t\eta=tη=t, τ=0\tau=0τ=0, F=mqF=mqF=mq: Q=(mq˙)(t)−0−mq=mq˙ t−mq=− m(q−q˙ t).Q=(m\dot q)(t)-0-mq=m\dot q\,t-mq=-\,m\big(q-\dot q\,t\big).Q=(mq˙​)(t)−0−mq=mq˙​t−mq=−m(q−q˙​t).     Interpretation. Up to the overall factor −m-m−m, the charge is q−q˙ t=q(0)q-\dot q\,t=q(0)q−q˙​t=q(0), the particle's initial position (equivalently, mmm times the position of the center of mass extrapolated back to t=0t=0t=0). Galilean-boost symmetry conserves the center-of-mass initial position — the statement that a free particle moves in a straight line at constant velocity. The charge depends explicitly on ttt, which is fine: Noether guarantees dQ/dt=0dQ/dt=0dQ/dt=0 along solutions, not that QQQ is built from q,q˙q,\dot qq,q˙​ alone. This is the one-particle shadow of the "center-of-energy motion" entry in the dictionary above.
