    Euler-Lagrange equation

    The Euler–Lagrange equation is the pointwise condition that makes the action stationary — it is what \delta S = 0 becomes once you integrate by parts and strip off the integral: \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} = 0 for a coordinate, \partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} = 0 for a field.

    Read it as p˙=F\dot p = Fp˙​=F: the rate of change of the momentum p=∂L/∂q˙p = \partial L/\partial\dot qp=∂L/∂q˙​ equals the force ∂L/∂q\partial L/\partial q∂L/∂q, the position-slope of LLL. The machinery below extracts this Newton-shaped law from "the action doesn't change to first order," then upgrades it from a particle to a field.

    Every derivation here runs the same four moves:

    1. Vary the unknown function, q→q+δqq \to q + \delta qq→q+δq.
    2. Linearize — keep only terms first-order in δq\delta qδq.
    3. Integrate by parts until no derivative touches δq\delta qδq.
    4. Split boundary from bulk. The bulk coefficient set to zero is the equation of motion; the boundary term is dealt with by boundary conditions.

    Part 1 — A coordinate q(t)q(t)q(t)

    One dynamical variable q(t)q(t)q(t), a general Lagrangian that may depend explicitly on time,

    S[q]=∫titfL(q(t),q˙(t);t) dt.S[q] = \int_{t_i}^{t_f} L\big(q(t), \dot q(t); t\big)\, dt .S[q]=∫ti​tf​​L(q(t),q˙​(t);t)dt.

    Vary q→q+δqq \to q + \delta qq→q+δq. Since δ\deltaδ (change the function) and ddt\tfrac{d}{dt}dtd​ (differentiate it) act on independent slots, they commute: δq˙=ddtδq\delta\dot q = \frac{d}{dt}\delta qδq˙​=dtd​δq. Expand LLL to first order in its first two slots:

    δS=∫titf(∂L∂q δq+∂L∂q˙ ddtδq)dt.\delta S = \int_{t_i}^{t_f}\left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial q}\,\delta q + \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q}\,\frac{d}{dt}\delta q \right) dt .δS=∫ti​tf​​(∂q∂L​δq+∂q˙​∂L​dtd​δq)dt.

    Two channels: LLL responds because the position moved (∂L/∂q\partial L/\partial q∂L/∂q) and because the velocity moved (∂L/∂q˙\partial L/\partial \dot q∂L/∂q˙​). The explicit-ttt slot contributes nothing — we vary qqq, not ttt.

    The second term has ddt\tfrac{d}{dt}dtd​ on δq\delta qδq, so it is not yet of the form ∫(⋯ ) δq dt\int(\cdots)\,\delta q\,dt∫(⋯)δqdt. Move the derivative off δq\delta qδq with integration by parts:

    ∂L∂q˙ddtδq=ddt ⁣(∂L∂q˙ δq)−(ddt∂L∂q˙)δq.\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q}\frac{d}{dt}\delta q = \frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q}\,\delta q\right) - \left(\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q}\right)\delta q .∂q˙​∂L​dtd​δq=dtd​(∂q˙​∂L​δq)−(dtd​∂q˙​∂L​)δq.

    The total-derivative piece integrates to an endpoint evaluation. Hence

    \boxed{\ \delta S = \left[\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q}\,\delta q\right]_{t_i}^{t_f} + \int_{t_i}^{t_f}\left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} - \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q} \right)\delta q\, dt .\ }\tag{1}

    Equation (1) is the variational method in miniature: endpoint physics (first term) plus bulk physics (integral), cleanly separated.

    Now impose Hamilton's principle, δS=0\delta S = 0δS=0, with endpoints held fixed: δq(ti)=δq(tf)=0\delta q(t_i) = \delta q(t_f) = 0δq(ti​)=δq(tf​)=0. That kills the boundary term. (Freeing the endpoints instead is the whole subject of Boundary terms and allowed variations.) The bulk integral must vanish for every allowed δq\delta qδq; by the fundamental lemma of the calculus of variations (see Functional derivative) the bracket vanishes pointwise:

    \boxed{\ \frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q} - \frac{\partial L}{\partial q} = 0 \ }\qquad \text{(Euler–Lagrange).}\tag{2}

    Sanity check with L=12mq˙2−V(q)L = \tfrac12 m\dot q^2 - V(q)L=21​mq˙​2−V(q): here ∂L∂q˙=mq˙\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q} = m\dot q∂q˙​∂L​=mq˙​ and ∂L∂q=−V′(q)\frac{\partial L}{\partial q} = -V'(q)∂q∂L​=−V′(q), so (2) gives mq¨=−V′(q)m\ddot q = -V'(q)mq¨​=−V′(q) — Newton's law, with the force pointing down the potential gradient as it must.

    Define the conjugate momentum p≡∂L/∂q˙p \equiv \partial L/\partial \dot qp≡∂L/∂q˙​. Then (2) is just

    p˙=∂L∂q,\dot p = \frac{\partial L}{\partial q},p˙​=∂q∂L​,

    "momentum changes at the rate set by the position-slope of LLL" — i.e. p˙=F\dot p = Fp˙​=F. And the payoff: if LLL does not depend on qqq (only on q˙\dot qq˙​), the right side is zero and ppp is conserved. A symmetry (invariance under shifting qqq) has produced a conservation law — the first taste of Noether's theorem.

    Part 2 — A scalar field ϕ(t,x)\phi(t, x)ϕ(t,x)

    Now the unknown is a field ϕ\phiϕ spread over spacetime. The Lagrangian becomes a Lagrangian density L\mathcal{L}L integrated over a DDD-dimensional spacetime, D=d+1D = d + 1D=d+1 (ddd space, one time):

    S[ϕ]=∫dDx  L(ϕ(x), ∂μϕ(x)),dDx=dt dx1⋯dxd,∂μϕ≡∂ϕ∂xμ.S[\phi] = \int d^D x\; \mathcal{L}\big(\phi(x),\, \partial_\mu\phi(x)\big),\qquad d^D x = dt\, dx^1\cdots dx^d,\quad \partial_\mu\phi \equiv \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x^\mu}.S[ϕ]=∫dDxL(ϕ(x),∂μ​ϕ(x)),dDx=dtdx1⋯dxd,∂μ​ϕ≡∂xμ∂ϕ​.

    Here μ=0,1,…,D−1\mu = 0,1,\dots,D-1μ=0,1,…,D−1 with x0=tx^0 = tx0=t (see Minkowski metric). If the index notation is new, read ∂μϕ\partial_\mu\phi∂μ​ϕ as a list of first derivatives (∂tϕ,∂x1ϕ,…,∂xdϕ)(\partial_t\phi, \partial_{x^1}\phi, \dots, \partial_{x^d}\phi)(∂t​ϕ,∂x1​ϕ,…,∂xd​ϕ) — one derivative channel per spacetime direction. The particle had a single velocity slot q˙\dot qq˙​; the field has DDD of them. That is the only new feature.

    Vary ϕ→ϕ+δϕ\phi \to \phi + \delta\phiϕ→ϕ+δϕ, so ∂μϕ→∂μϕ+∂μ(δϕ)\partial_\mu\phi \to \partial_\mu\phi + \partial_\mu(\delta\phi)∂μ​ϕ→∂μ​ϕ+∂μ​(δϕ). First order:

    δS=∫dDx(∂L∂ϕ δϕ+∂L∂(∂μϕ) ∂μ(δϕ)),\delta S = \int d^D x\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi}\,\delta\phi + \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\partial_\mu(\delta\phi) \right),δS=∫dDx(∂ϕ∂L​δϕ+∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂L​∂μ​(δϕ)),

    with the Einstein sum over μ\muμ implied. Integrate by parts in each direction — the multivariable analogue of the single IBP above, using ∂L∂(∂μϕ)∂μ(δϕ)=∂μ ⁣(∂L∂(∂μϕ)δϕ)−(∂μ∂L∂(∂μϕ))δϕ\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\partial_\mu(\delta\phi) = \partial_\mu\!\big(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\delta\phi\big) - \big(\partial_\mu\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\big)\delta\phi∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂L​∂μ​(δϕ)=∂μ​(∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂L​δϕ)−(∂μ​∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂L​)δϕ:

    \boxed{\ \delta S = \int d^D x\;\partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\,\delta\phi\right) + \int d^D x\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi} - \partial_\mu\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} \right)\delta\phi .\ }\tag{3}

    The first integral in (3) is a total spacetime divergence; by the divergence theorem it becomes a boundary term on the surface enclosing the region — one piece at the time edges ti,tft_i, t_fti​,tf​, plus pieces at the spatial edges. (The spatial-edge pieces are where the string's endpoint physics, and eventually D-branes, live; see Boundary terms and allowed variations.)

    Demand δS=0\delta S = 0δS=0 with δϕ\delta\phiδϕ vanishing on the boundary; the bulk vanishes for arbitrary δϕ\delta\phiδϕ, giving the field Euler–Lagrange equation:

    \boxed{\ \partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)}\right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \phi} = 0 .\ }\tag{4}

    This is the same law with one index more. The particle's momentum ppp becomes a current Πμ≡∂L/∂(∂μϕ)\Pi^\mu \equiv \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)Πμ≡∂L/∂(∂μ​ϕ), one component per spacetime direction, and p˙=F\dot p = Fp˙​=F becomes ∂μΠμ=∂L/∂ϕ\partial_\mu\Pi^\mu = \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\phi∂μ​Πμ=∂L/∂ϕ: the spacetime divergence of the current is sourced by the field-slope of L\mathcal{L}L. Where the particle had one bookkeeping equation for p(t)p(t)p(t), the field has a local balance at every spacetime point.

    For several fields ϕA\phi^AϕA, vary each independently:

    ∂μ ⁣(∂L∂(∂μϕA))−∂L∂ϕA=0for every A.\partial_\mu\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi^A)}\right)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi^A}=0\qquad\text{for every }A.∂μ​(∂(∂μ​ϕA)∂L​)−∂ϕA∂L​=0for every A.

    This is the rule later applied component-by-component to the string embedding fields Xμ(τ,σ)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)Xμ(τ,σ).

    Sanity check 1 — the vibrating string

    The nonrelativistic string is the field y(t,x)y(t,x)y(t,x) (transverse displacement) with density L=12μ0y˙2−12T0y′2\mathcal{L} = \tfrac12\mu_0\dot y^2 - \tfrac12 T_0 y'^2L=21​μ0​y˙​2−21​T0​y′2, where μ0\mu_0μ0​ is mass per length and T0T_0T0​ the tension. The two derivative slots are

    Π0=∂L∂y˙=μ0y˙,Π1=∂L∂y′=−T0 y′.\Pi^0 = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot y} = \mu_0\dot y,\qquad \Pi^1 = \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y'} = -T_0\, y'.Π0=∂y˙​∂L​=μ0​y˙​,Π1=∂y′∂L​=−T0​y′.

    Since L\mathcal{L}L has no bare yyy, the source ∂L/∂y=0\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial y = 0∂L/∂y=0, and (4) reads ∂tΠ0+∂xΠ1=0\partial_t\Pi^0 + \partial_x\Pi^1 = 0∂t​Π0+∂x​Π1=0:

    μ0 ∂2y∂t2−T0 ∂2y∂x2=0,\mu_0\,\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial t^2} - T_0\,\frac{\partial^2 y}{\partial x^2} = 0,μ0​∂t2∂2y​−T0​∂x2∂2y​=0,

    the wave equation with speed v0=T0/μ0v_0 = \sqrt{T_0/\mu_0}v0​=T0​/μ0​​. (Physically: Π0=μ0y˙\Pi^0 = \mu_0\dot yΠ0=μ0​y˙​ is the momentum density and Π1=−T0y′\Pi^1 = -T_0 y'Π1=−T0​y′ its flux; the EL equation is the statement that momentum flowing in equals momentum accumulating — a continuity equation.) See Wave equation.

    Sanity check 2 — the relativistic free scalar

    With mostly-plus signature ημν=diag(−1,+1,…,+1)\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,\dots,+1)ημν​=diag(−1,+1,…,+1) and natural units ℏ=c=1\hbar = c = 1ℏ=c=1,

    L=−12 ∂μϕ ∂μϕ−12m2ϕ2.\mathcal{L} = -\tfrac12\,\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi - \tfrac12 m^2\phi^2.L=−21​∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ−21​m2ϕ2.

    The slots are ∂L/∂(∂μϕ)=−∂μϕ\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi) = -\partial^\mu\phi∂L/∂(∂μ​ϕ)=−∂μϕ and ∂L/∂ϕ=−m2ϕ\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\phi = -m^2\phi∂L/∂ϕ=−m2ϕ, so (4) gives

    −∂μ∂μϕ+m2ϕ=0⟺(∂μ∂μ−m2)ϕ=0,-\partial_\mu\partial^\mu\phi + m^2\phi = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\partial_\mu\partial^\mu - m^2)\phi = 0,−∂μ​∂μϕ+m2ϕ=0⟺(∂μ​∂μ−m2)ϕ=0,

    the Klein–Gordon equation. In mostly-plus, ∂μ∂μ=−∂t2+∇2\partial_\mu\partial^\mu = -\partial_t^2 + \nabla^2∂μ​∂μ=−∂t2​+∇2, so a plane wave ei(k⃗⋅x⃗−ωt)e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec x - \omega t)}ei(k⋅x−ωt) obeys ω2=k⃗ 2+m2\omega^2 = \vec k^{\,2} + m^2ω2=k2+m2, i.e. E2=p⃗ 2+m2E^2 = \vec p^{\,2} + m^2E2=p​2+m2 — the Mass-shell relation. (This is why the overall minus sign on the kinetic term is not cosmetic: it is what makes the time-derivative term come out with the physical sign in mostly-plus.)

    Open questions

    • Higher-derivative Lagrangians (depending on q¨\ddot qq¨​ or ∂2ϕ\partial^2\phi∂2ϕ) give a modified EL equation with extra boundary data such as δq˙\delta\dot qδq˙​. The actions in this module are all first-derivative; higher-derivative effective actions are a later refinement.
    • Constrained or gauge fields need variations that respect a redundancy; that belongs with Gauge fixing as choosing a representative.

    Exercise

    Take the relativistic free scalar and add a cubic self-interaction, keeping mostly-plus signature:

    L=−12 ∂μϕ ∂μϕ−12m2ϕ2−λ3!ϕ3.\mathcal{L} = -\tfrac12\,\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi - \tfrac12 m^2\phi^2 - \tfrac{\lambda}{3!}\phi^3.L=−21​∂μ​ϕ∂μϕ−21​m2ϕ2−3!λ​ϕ3.

    Without re-deriving (4), use it to write down the equation of motion. Which of the three terms in L\mathcal{L}L feeds the "current" slot ∂L/∂(∂μϕ)\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)∂L/∂(∂μ​ϕ) and which feeds the "source" slot ∂L/∂ϕ\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\phi∂L/∂ϕ? Sketch in one line why the new term makes the equation nonlinear, and what breaks in Sanity check 2 as a result.

    Solution

    Only the kinetic term carries derivatives, so it alone feeds the current slot: ∂L∂(∂μϕ)=−∂μϕ,∂L∂ϕ=−m2ϕ−λ2ϕ2.\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)} = -\partial^\mu\phi,\qquad \frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi} = -m^2\phi - \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\phi^2.∂(∂μ​ϕ)∂L​=−∂μϕ,∂ϕ∂L​=−m2ϕ−2λ​ϕ2. (The mass and cubic terms have no derivatives, so they land entirely in the source slot, with ∂ϕ(λ3!ϕ3)=λ2ϕ2\partial_\phi(\tfrac{\lambda}{3!}\phi^3) = \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\phi^2∂ϕ​(3!λ​ϕ3)=2λ​ϕ2.) Plugging into ∂μ(∂L/∂(∂μϕ))−∂L/∂ϕ=0\partial_\mu\big(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial(\partial_\mu\phi)\big) - \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\phi = 0∂μ​(∂L/∂(∂μ​ϕ))−∂L/∂ϕ=0: −∂μ∂μϕ+m2ϕ+λ2ϕ2=0⟺(∂μ∂μ−m2)ϕ=λ2ϕ2.-\partial_\mu\partial^\mu\phi + m^2\phi + \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\phi^2 = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (\partial_\mu\partial^\mu - m^2)\phi = \tfrac{\lambda}{2}\phi^2.−∂μ​∂μϕ+m2ϕ+2λ​ϕ2=0⟺(∂μ​∂μ−m2)ϕ=2λ​ϕ2. The λ2ϕ2\tfrac{\lambda}{2}\phi^22λ​ϕ2 term is quadratic in ϕ\phiϕ, so the equation is nonlinear: sums of solutions are no longer solutions, and superposition fails. Consequently the plane-wave / mass-shell analysis of Sanity check 2 no longer applies — ei(k⃗⋅x⃗−ωt)e^{i(\vec k\cdot\vec x - \omega t)}ei(k⋅x−ωt) is not a solution, because ϕ2\phi^2ϕ2 generates new frequencies (the field's Fourier modes scatter off each other). Only the free (λ=0\lambda=0λ=0) theory has clean E2=p⃗ 2+m2E^2 = \vec p^{\,2} + m^2E2=p​2+m2 modes.
