<p>Now the one non-negotiable fact about the string: its worldsheet theory must be <strong>Weyl (conformal) invariant</strong>. That symmetry is what let us gauge away the unphysical modes, what forced <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>D</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>26</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">D=26</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">D</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">26</span></span></span></span>, and what put a massless graviton in the spectrum. Lose it and the whole quantization collapses.</p>
<p>Here is the tension. Coupling constants in a quantum field theory <strong>run</strong>: regulating the UV divergences introduces a scale, and the couplings change with it. The rate of change is the <strong>beta function</strong>. But a theory whose couplings run is <em>not</em> scale-invariant — running is the enemy of conformal symmetry. So conformal invariance survives quantization only at a <strong>fixed point of the renormalization-group flow</strong>, where every beta function vanishes.</p>

<h2 id="the-load-bearing-equation">The load-bearing equation</h2>
<p>At one loop the metric beta function is just the target-space Ricci tensor (derived in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-v-graduate-bridge/25-compactification-and-effective-actions/beta-functions-and-conformal-invariance/" class="wiki-link">Beta functions and conformal invariance</a>):
<span class="katex-error" title="ParseError: KaTeX parse error: \tag works only in display equations" style="color:#cc0000"> \beta_{\mu\nu}(G) = \alpha'\,\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}. \tag{1}</span>
Curvature is the obstruction to scale invariance: a curved target makes the couplings run. Demanding the flow stop, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>β</mi><mrow><mi>μ</mi><mi>ν</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>G</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta_{\mu\nu}(G)=0</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1.0361em;vertical-align:-0.2861em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0528em;">β</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t vlist-t2"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.1514em;"><span style="top:-2.55em;margin-left:-0.0528em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mord mtight"><span class="mord mathnormal mtight" style="margin-right:0.0637em;">μν</span></span></span></span></span><span class="vlist-s">​</span></span><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.2861em;"><span></span></span></span></span></span></span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord mathnormal">G</span><span class="mclose">)</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">0</span></span></span></span>, gives

the <strong>vacuum Einstein equation</strong>. Read the logic backwards to feel its force: we quantized a string in flat space, were pushed to <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>D</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>26</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">D=26</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">D</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">26</span></span></span></span>, then asked <em>nothing more</em> than that the string be allowed to move through a curved space consistently — and the string answered <em>only if that space is Ricci-flat.</em> We never invoked general relativity; the string handed it back to us.</p>
<p>Turn on the other two massless fields and (2) becomes Einstein's equation <strong>with sources</strong>: the 3-form <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>H</mi><mo>=</mo><mi>d</mi><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">H=dB</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0813em;">H</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span></span></span></span> and the dilaton gradients act as stress-energy. <h2 id="why-this-anchors-the-whole-module">Why this anchors the whole module</h2>
<p>The module has two faces and this is the hinge between them. <strong>Compactification</strong> (<a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-v-graduate-bridge/25-compactification-and-effective-actions/toroidal-compactification/" class="wiki-link">Toroidal compactification</a>, <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-v-graduate-bridge/25-compactification-and-effective-actions/t-duality/" class="wiki-link">T-duality</a>, <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-v-graduate-bridge/25-compactification-and-effective-actions/twisted-sectors-on-orbifolds/" class="wiki-link">Twisted sectors on orbifolds</a>) shows what <em>extra structure</em> the two-dimensional string detects in spacetime — winding, T-duality, orbifold twisted sectors — things invisible to a point particle. <strong>Effective actions</strong> show what <em>constraints</em> that same two-dimensional object imposes <em>back</em> on spacetime. Same worldsheet, both directions: it sees more than a point particle can, and it demands more than a point particle would. A single quantized string is so fussy about its own consistency that it dictates the laws of gravity for the entire universe it lives in.</p>
