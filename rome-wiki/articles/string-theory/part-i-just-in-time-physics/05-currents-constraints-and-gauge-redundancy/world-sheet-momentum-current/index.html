    A string's spacetime momentum is a conserved charge carried entirely on the two-dimensional worldsheet, like ink painted on a moving ribbon rather than smeared through the room.

    Momentum spread along the string

    A point particle carries momentum pμp_\mupμ​: one number per spacetime direction, located wherever the particle is. A string is extended, so its momentum is spread along it as a density. That density can slosh from one part of the string to another, but for a free string it never leaks away, and integrating it along the string at any instant recovers the total pμp_\mupμ​. The object that does this bookkeeping is a current with one leg in spacetime — which component of momentum — and one leg on the worldsheet — how it flows, sitting on a piece of string or streaming past a point of it. Conservation says momentum leaving one patch of the sheet must turn up in the adjacent patch.

    Why the current lives on the worldsheet

    The dynamical fields of a string are the embedding coordinates Xμ(τ,σ),X^\mu(\tau,\sigma),Xμ(τ,σ), functions of the two worldsheet parameters τ\tauτ (worldsheet time) and σ\sigmaσ (position along the string). The action integrates a Lagrangian density over (τ,σ)(\tau,\sigma)(τ,σ) — not over target spacetime. Noether's construction produces a current that flows through the integration domain, so here the flow index is a worldsheet index: Pμα,α∈{τ,σ}.\mathcal P^\alpha_\mu,\qquad \alpha\in\{\tau,\sigma\}.Pμα​,α∈{τ,σ}. Read the two indices separately:

    • the lower target-space index μ\muμ says which component of spacetime momentum is being tracked — the same role μ\muμ plays in pμp_\mupμ​;
    • the upper worldsheet index α\alphaα says how it flows: Pμτ\mathcal P^\tau_\muPμτ​ is the density of μ\muμ-momentum along the string, and Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ is the rate at which μ\muμ-momentum streams along the string past a point of fixed σ\sigmaσ.

    This is the same index-splitting as the stress tensor Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​ of Stress-energy tensor as momentum flow, with a twist: there the lower index labeled a coordinate translation of the world the field lives on; here it labels a target-space translation of where the string sits. That difference is what makes μ\muμ a spacetime index while α\alphaα stays two-valued.

    Target-space translation is the symmetry

    The Nambu–Goto density depends on the XμX^\muXμ only through their derivatives ∂αXμ\partial_\alpha X^\mu∂α​Xμ — the string cares about its shape and motion, not its absolute location. So a rigid shift of the whole string, δXμ(τ,σ)=ϵμ(ϵμ constant),\delta X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)=\epsilon^\mu \qquad(\epsilon^\mu \text{ constant}),δXμ(τ,σ)=ϵμ(ϵμ constant), changes nothing at all: δ(∂αXμ)=∂αϵμ=0  ⇒  δL=0.\delta(\partial_\alpha X^\mu)=\partial_\alpha\epsilon^\mu=0 \;\Rightarrow\; \delta\mathcal L=0.δ(∂α​Xμ)=∂α​ϵμ=0⇒δL=0. This is a strict invariance — the density is untouched, so no boundary-term subtraction is needed (contrast the coordinate-translation case, where δL\delta\mathcal LδL was a total derivative). The plain Noether rule then gives the current directly:

    Pμα=∂L∂(∂αXμ),(Pμτ,Pμσ)=(∂L∂X˙μ,  ∂L∂X′μ),\mathcal P^\alpha_\mu =\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha X^\mu)}, \qquad (\mathcal P^\tau_\mu,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu) =\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot X^\mu},\;\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial X'^\mu}\right),Pμα​=∂(∂α​Xμ)∂L​,(Pμτ​,Pμσ​)=(∂X˙μ∂L​,∂X′μ∂L​),

    writing X˙μ=∂τXμ\dot X^\mu=\partial_\tau X^\muX˙μ=∂τ​Xμ and X′μ=∂σXμX'^\mu=\partial_\sigma X^\muX′μ=∂σ​Xμ. The time component Pμτ=∂L/∂X˙μ\mathcal P^\tau_\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot X^\muPμτ​=∂L/∂X˙μ is the momentum canonically conjugate to XμX^\muXμ — a momentum density, exactly what the time slot of a momentum current ought to hold. Its explicit Nambu–Goto form is an unwieldy ratio of X˙⋅X′\dot X\cdot X'X˙⋅X′, X˙2\dot X^2X˙2, and X′2X'^2X′2; we need only its meaning, never the formula.

    Conservation is the statement

      ∂αPμα=∂τPμτ+∂σPμσ=0.  (1)\boxed{\;\partial_\alpha\mathcal P^\alpha_\mu =\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu+\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0.\;} \tag{1}∂α​Pμα​=∂τ​Pμτ​+∂σ​Pμσ​=0.​(1)

    This is not an extra assumption — it is the string equation of motion. Euler–Lagrange for XμX^\muXμ reads ∂α(∂L/∂(∂αXμ))−∂L/∂Xμ=0\partial_\alpha\big(\partial\mathcal L/\partial(\partial_\alpha X^\mu)\big)-\partial\mathcal L/\partial X^\mu=0∂α​(∂L/∂(∂α​Xμ))−∂L/∂Xμ=0, and since L\mathcal LL has no bare XμX^\muXμ the second term vanishes, leaving exactly (1). "The string obeys its equation of motion" and "spacetime momentum is locally conserved on the sheet" are the same sentence.

    Density, flux, and endpoints

    At fixed worldsheet time, the total spacetime momentum is the charge obtained by integrating the density along the string,

    pμ(τ)=∫0σ1Pμτ(τ,σ) dσ,(2)p_\mu(\tau)=\int_0^{\sigma_1}\mathcal P^\tau_\mu(\tau,\sigma)\,d\sigma, \tag{2}pμ​(τ)=∫0σ1​​Pμτ​(τ,σ)dσ,(2)

    with σ1=π\sigma_1=\piσ1​=π for the open string and σ1=2π\sigma_1=2\piσ1​=2π for the closed (our standing ranges). Is it actually conserved? Differentiate and feed in the local law (1):

    dpμdτ=∫0σ1∂τPμτ dσ=−∫0σ1∂σPμσ dσ=− Pμσ∣0σ1.(3)\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau} =\int_0^{\sigma_1}\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,d\sigma =-\int_0^{\sigma_1}\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,d\sigma =-\,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\Big|_{0}^{\sigma_1}. \tag{3}dτdpμ​​=∫0σ1​​∂τ​Pμτ​dσ=−∫0σ1​​∂σ​Pμσ​dσ=−Pμσ​​0σ1​​.(3)

    The interior contribution telescoped away; only the endpoints survive. This exposes Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ as the flux of μ\muμ-momentum out through the string ends — the rate at which the string leaks momentum through its boundary. Whether momentum is conserved is therefore entirely a boundary question:

    • Closed string: σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 and σ=σ1\sigma=\sigma_1σ=σ1​ are the same point, so the two endpoint terms cancel by periodicity.
    • Free open string: the free-endpoint (Neumann) boundary condition is precisely Pμσ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0Pμσ​=0 at each end — no flux can escape.

    Either way the right side of (3) vanishes and dpμdτ=0.\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau}=0.dτdpμ​​=0. The lone exception: an open string ending on a D-brane with a Dirichlet direction. There Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ need not vanish, so the string alone can gain or lose momentum through that end — the brane absorbs the difference, and only string-plus-brane momentum is conserved.

    Momentum as flux through any crossing curve

    Formula (2) used a constant-τ\tauτ slice, but nothing forced that choice. The stronger statement: the same charge is obtained by integrating the current across any curve γ\gammaγ that cuts all the way across the worldsheet.

    Along an infinitesimal oriented segment with tangent (dτ,dσ)(d\tau,d\sigma)(dτ,dσ), the normal is (dσ,−dτ)(d\sigma,-d\tau)(dσ,−dτ) — the tangent rotated a quarter turn clockwise, pointing outward when the segment belongs to a counterclockwise loop. Dotting the current (Pμτ,Pμσ)(\mathcal P^\tau_\mu,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu)(Pμτ​,Pμσ​) into that normal gives the flux crossing the segment:

    (Pμτ,Pμσ)⋅(dσ,−dτ)=Pμτ dσ−Pμσ dτ.(\mathcal P^\tau_\mu,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu)\cdot(d\sigma,-d\tau) =\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,d\sigma-\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,d\tau.(Pμτ​,Pμσ​)⋅(dσ,−dτ)=Pμτ​dσ−Pμσ​dτ.

    So the momentum crossing γ\gammaγ is

      pμ(γ)=∫γ ⁣(Pμτ dσ−Pμσ dτ).  (4)\boxed{\; p_\mu(\gamma)=\int_\gamma\!\big(\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,d\sigma-\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,d\tau\big). \;} \tag{4}pμ​(γ)=∫γ​(Pμτ​dσ−Pμσ​dτ).​(4)

    On a constant-τ\tauτ curve traversed toward increasing σ\sigmaσ, dτ=0d\tau=0dτ=0 and (4) collapses back to (2) — consistent.

    Why γ\gammaγ can be deformed freely. Take a contractible counterclockwise loop Γ\GammaΓ bounding a region RRR of the sheet. The two-dimensional divergence theorem plus conservation (1) gives

    ∮Γ ⁣(Pμτ dσ−Pμσ dτ)=∫R ⁣(∂τPμτ+∂σPμσ) dτ dσ=0.\oint_\Gamma\!\big(\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,d\sigma-\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,d\tau\big) =\int_R\!\big(\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu+\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\big)\,d\tau\,d\sigma =0.∮Γ​(Pμτ​dσ−Pμσ​dτ)=∫R​(∂τ​Pμτ​+∂σ​Pμσ​)dτdσ=0.

    No source inside means no net flux out. Consequently, sliding γ\gammaγ to a new curve γ′\gamma'γ′ changes pμp_\mupμ​ only by the flux through the enclosed strip — which is zero, provided you don't sweep across a momentum-leaking boundary (an open-string end with escaping flux). This is the worldsheet cousin of Cauchy's contour deformation: the value depends on the homotopy class of the curve, not its exact shape. For open strings γ\gammaγ must run from the σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 boundary to the σ=σ1\sigma=\sigma_1σ=σ1​ boundary; for closed strings it must wind once around the cylinder in the direction of increasing σ\sigmaσ. Those are precisely the curves that slice the whole string once.

    Why static gauge is a convenience, not the definition

    Conservation (3) is phrased in worldsheet time τ\tauτ, but a Lorentz observer wants conservation in their time t=X0/ct=X^0/ct=X0/c (we keep ccc explicit in this note). In static gauge we pin X0(τ,σ)=cτ,X^0(\tau,\sigma)=c\tau,X0(τ,σ)=cτ, so worldsheet time is observer time, t=τt=\taut=τ: a constant-τ\tauτ slice is the string photographed at one observer-instant, and dpμ/dτ=0dp_\mu/d\tau=0dpμ​/dτ=0 reads directly as dpμ/dt=0dp_\mu/dt=0dpμ​/dt=0.

    But we never needed that crutch. In an arbitrary parameterization a constant-τ\tauτ curve is not a snapshot at fixed ttt — yet curve independence saves us. The observer computes momentum on whatever curve γ\gammaγ corresponds to the string at time ttt; asking again at a later t′t't′ uses a different curve γ′\gamma'γ′; and since (4) is curve-independent, the two answers agree. Momentum conservation in real time falls out without committing to a gauge. That is the whole point of promoting (2) to (4).

    Exercise

    An open string has one end (σ=0\sigma=0σ=0) free and the other (σ=σ1\sigma=\sigma_1σ=σ1​) attached to a D-brane; μ=k\mu=kμ=k is a Dirichlet direction (normal to the brane), and there the endpoint flux is Pkσ(τ,σ1)=f(τ)≠0\mathcal P^\sigma_k(\tau,\sigma_1)=f(\tau)\neq 0Pkσ​(τ,σ1​)=f(τ)=0. Using only the local conservation law, find dpk/dτdp_k/d\taudpk​/dτ for the string, and state in one sentence what conservation law does still hold.

    Solution

    Start from the charge and differentiate, using ∂τPkτ=−∂σPkσ\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_k=-\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_k∂τ​Pkτ​=−∂σ​Pkσ​ from (1):

    dpkdτ=∫0σ1∂τPkτ dσ=− Pkσ∣0σ1=Pkσ(τ,0)−Pkσ(τ,σ1).\frac{dp_k}{d\tau}=\int_0^{\sigma_1}\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_k\,d\sigma =-\,\mathcal P^\sigma_k\Big|_0^{\sigma_1} =\mathcal P^\sigma_k(\tau,0)-\mathcal P^\sigma_k(\tau,\sigma_1).dτdpk​​=∫0σ1​​∂τ​Pkτ​dσ=−Pkσ​​0σ1​​=Pkσ​(τ,0)−Pkσ​(τ,σ1​).

    The free end at σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 contributes nothing (Pkσ(τ,0)=0\mathcal P^\sigma_k(\tau,0)=0Pkσ​(τ,0)=0), so

    dpkdτ=−f(τ)≠0.\frac{dp_k}{d\tau}=-f(\tau)\neq 0.dτdpk​​=−f(τ)=0.

    The string's kkk-momentum is not conserved: it drains through the D-brane end at rate f(τ)f(\tau)f(τ). What is conserved is the total momentum of string plus brane — the flux f(τ)f(\tau)f(τ) is exactly the momentum the string hands to (or takes from) the brane, so ddτ(pkstring+pkbrane)=0\tfrac{d}{d\tau}(p_k^{\text{string}}+p_k^{\text{brane}})=0dτd​(pkstring​+pkbrane​)=0. (In the Neumann directions μ≠k\mu\neq kμ=k, along the brane, both ends give zero flux and dpμ/dτ=0dp_\mu/d\tau=0dpμ​/dτ=0 as usual.)
