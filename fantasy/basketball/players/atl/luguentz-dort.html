Correction 2026-07-19: Oklahoma City traded Dort to Atlanta in a three-team deal completed this morning — OKC received three second-round picks (Chicago's 2027 via Dallas plus the two most favorable of Atlanta's 2031 and 2032 seconds) to get under the second tax apron, Dallas received Zaccharie Risacher, and Atlanta also received Ryan Nembhard from Dallas. The prior 22-minute reserve projection was an OKC-specific squeeze; in Atlanta he projects as the starting small forward in a Daniels–Alexander-Walker–Dort perimeter that beat coverage grades as a defense-first upgrade (SI's Hawks desk gave the deal an A-). Why the line looks like this

Dort averaged 8.3 points, 3.6 rebounds, 1.2 assists, 0.9 steals and 1.9 threes in 26.8 minutes over 69 starts, but shot 38.5% overall. The new line is essentially his 2025-26 rate profile restored to starter minutes: Atlanta acquired him to start on the wing, and 27 minutes sits just under his OKC starter workload because the Hawks rotation is deep and Quin Snyder staggers his perimeter defenders.

The fit is the point. Dort next to Dyson Daniels gives Atlanta arguably the league's best point-of-attack wing pair, with Nickeil Alexander-Walker as a third plus defender; SI's Hawks desk notes Dort ranked in the 99th percentile at drawing offensive fouls and frames the deal as the answer to the playoff loss to New York. His three-point volume (roughly 5 attempts per game at 34.4% last season) and matchup steals carry over; nothing about Atlanta raises his usage.

Sources: 2025-26 Thunder stats, SI Hawks trade grade, HoopsRumors trade breakdown, Hashtag dynasty.

Category shape

  • Wins: threes, low turnovers and matchup-driven steals.
  • Costs: FG%, scoring and assists; FT volume is too small to help.
  • Build fit: punt-FG streaming when three-point volume and a defensive assignment align.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: the wing crowd (Wiggins, Kispert, Hield survivors) pulls him toward 22 minutes on nights Atlanta wants shooting; still no fantasy consistency there.
  • Base: 27-minute starter, 1.9 threes and 0.9 steals with a material FG tax.
  • Ceiling: 30-plus minutes as the every-night stopper on a 50-win team; even then he is a 10-11 point specialist rather than a top-120 player.

Highlights along the way: NBA All-Defensive First Team in 2024-25 and a championship with the 2024-25 Thunder.

Durability: Two shortened early seasons (52 and 51 GP in the COVID-length and 2021-22 years — the latter ended by shoulder surgery), then four straight seasons at 69-79 GP with routine knee management. He is a dependable 70-game player, not an 82-game one.

Trajectory: The table shows a player whose scoring peaked at 22 (17.2 PPG in 2021-22) and has declined every season since as OKC's talent arrived — usage, not skill, drove the fall. The rate profile has been static for years: ~2 threes, ~1 steal, low turnovers, and a FG% that oscillates around .400. The Atlanta trade restores a 27-minute starting wing role, which makes the specialist line draftable in deep nine-category builds again. He becomes genuinely fantasy-relevant only if Atlanta's offense hands him closer to his 2021-22 shot volume — nothing in the trade logic suggests it will.
