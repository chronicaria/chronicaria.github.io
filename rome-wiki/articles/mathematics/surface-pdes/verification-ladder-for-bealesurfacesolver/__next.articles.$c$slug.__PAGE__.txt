<h2 id="why-a-ladder-is-necessary">Why a ladder is necessary</h2>
<p>A simulation that stays finite is not necessarily accurate, and an accurate PDE discretization does not automatically validate a scientific interpretation. The repository mixes package code, standalone research modules, result ledgers, manuscripts, and exploratory plans. Each layer answers a different question.</p>
<p>A useful evidence ladder is:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Code-path inspection</strong> — is the claimed equation actually implemented?</li>
<li><strong>Algebraic regression</strong> — does the general expression reduce to a known special case?</li>
<li><strong>Exact or manufactured solution</strong> — does the discrete error converge at the expected order?</li>
<li><strong>Conservation or budget closure</strong> — does the scheme respect the invariant or source balance relevant to the model?</li>
<li><strong>Nulls and ablations</strong> — does the signal vanish when its proposed mechanism is removed?</li>
<li><strong>Resolution and parameter robustness</strong> — does it persist beyond one grid and one tuning choice?</li>
<li><strong>Independent verification</strong> — can someone other than the result's producer recompute it from saved artifacts or a cold run?</li>
<li><strong>Scientific calibration</strong> — are units, physical parameters, observational degeneracies, and model limitations treated honestly?</li>
</ol>
<p>No later rung repairs a missing earlier one. A visually compelling flow field cannot substitute for a manufactured-solution test, and second-order convergence cannot establish that a one-layer atmosphere represents a real planet.</p>

<h2 id="manufactured-solutions-off-the-sphere">Manufactured solutions off the sphere</h2>
<p>When no natural exact solution exists on an ellipsoid, the repository uses the method of manufactured solutions: choose a smooth field <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>u</mi><mo>∗</mo></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">u^*</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6887em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal">u</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.6887em;"><span style="top:-3.063em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mbin mtight">∗</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span>, insert it into the continuous equation, and define the residual as a forcing so that <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>u</mi><mo>∗</mo></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">u^*</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6887em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal">u</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.6887em;"><span style="top:-3.063em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mbin mtight">∗</span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span> is exact by construction. Refining the cut-point grid then tests the operator independently of the scientific forcing.</p>
<p>For geometry-general shallow water, the research records approximately <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>1.94</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">1.94</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">1.94</span></span></span></span>–<span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2.00</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2.00</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">2.00</span></span></span></span> order in geopotential and momentum on sphere, oblate, and strongly triaxial test surfaces. Non-sphericity raises the error constant but does not erase the measured second-order trend in the tested range.</p>
<p>The day-night equilibrium <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>max</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>0</mn><mo separator="true">,</mo><mi>n</mi><mo>⋅</mo><mover accent="true"><mi>s</mi><mo>^</mo></mover><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\max(0,n\cdot\hat s)</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mop">max</span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord">0</span><span class="mpunct">,</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">⋅</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord accent"><span class="vlist-t"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.6944em;"><span style="top:-3em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:3em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">s</span></span><span style="top:-3em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:3em;"></span><span class="accent-body" style="left:-0.1944em;"><span class="mord">^</span></span></span></span></span></span></span><span class="mclose">)</span></span></span></span> is a deliberate counterexample to naive order claims: it has a kink at the terminator. <h2 id="conservation-and-structure">Conservation and structure</h2>
<p>Different equations require different budgets:</p>
<ul>
<li>diffusion should dissipate an appropriate norm;</li>
<li>unforced shallow water should control total layer mass and, depending on the formulation, energy or potential-vorticity quantities;</li>
<li>forced-damped flow should close the balance between resolved tendency and explicit source or sink;</li>
<li>reaction–diffusion should preserve physical bounds only if the numerical method actually has that property;</li>
<li>moving-surface problems need a geometric-conservation accounting as well as a field equation.</li>
</ul>
<p>The repository uses an Appendix-B cut-point quadrature to approximate surface integrals. Some standalone research modules report machine-level mass fixes or machine-level energy and enstrophy drift in specially constructed structure-preserving schemes. Those statements belong to those modules and their tested configurations; they should not be silently transferred to the default MacCormack solver.</p>
<h2 id="ablations-that-support-causal-interpretation">Ablations that support causal interpretation</h2>
<p>The most informative experiments change one lever at a time. Examples recorded in the repository include:</p>
<ul>
<li>sphere versus non-spherical geometry at fixed forcing;</li>
<li>one-gradient gravity-darkened forcing versus a flat-forcing control on the same oblate surface;</li>
<li><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>β</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\beta=0</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8889em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0528em;">β</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">0</span></span></span></span> in the brown-dwarf equilibrium target, which removes gravity-darkening contrast;</li>
<li>equal-area versus unequal-area oblate climate comparisons, which exposed and corrected a size confound;</li>
<li>cloud-off, still-atmosphere, and no-geometry controls in later brown-dwarf work;</li>
<li>stable states versus diverged triaxial Jacobi states, where a NaN result is explicitly not interpreted as a physical absence of superrotation.</li>
</ul>
<p>These controls are more valuable than a larger catalog of unablated simulations because they discriminate mechanism from correlation.</p>
<h2 id="failure-evidence-is-part-of-verification">Failure evidence is part of verification</h2>
<p>Several negative results define the method's boundary:</p>
<ul>
<li>a coarse distorted ellipsoid can lack enough admissible cut-point neighbors;</li>
<li>mixed-sign equilibration prevents a general M-matrix or positivity guarantee, and a reaction–diffusion path exhibited overshoot;</li>
<li>the default hyperbolic core needs numerical stabilization;</li>
<li>a viscosity-free centered wave step retains a checkerboard mode;</li>
<li>exact Jacobi-figure weather experiments can diverge even when the equilibrium figure itself is computed accurately;</li>
<li>the single-surface geometry mechanism does not explain WASP-43b's reported off-equatorial hotspot.</li>
</ul>
<p>Recording these failures prevents the success of one PDE family from being generalized to every surface, resolution, or physical regime.</p>
<h2 id="provenance-levels-for-rome">Provenance levels for Rome</h2>
<p>This import uses four practical labels when reading repository material:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Package capability:</strong> exported under <code>src/</code> and covered by <code>test/</code> or a package verification path.</li>
<li><strong>Validated research implementation:</strong> a standalone driver with a stated analytic, manufactured, conservation, or ablation gate and saved evidence.</li>
<li><strong>Working result:</strong> recorded in a capstone, ledger, or manuscript but still inside an active working tree.</li>
<li><strong>Proposal or frontier:</strong> a question, planned mechanism, or extrapolation not yet established by a passing computation.</li>
</ul>
<p>The source documents often report a green test suite and independent verifier/blesser workflow. This Rome import inspected those records but did not rerun the large Julia research campaign. Their numerical values should therefore be described as <strong>repository-recorded results</strong> until independently reproduced from a frozen revision.</p>

<h2 id="open-questions">Open questions</h2>
