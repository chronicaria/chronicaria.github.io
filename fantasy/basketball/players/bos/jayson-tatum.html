Nine-category: projected top 8-15 per game and top 10-18 in totals. Tatum supplies points, threes, rebounds and above-position assists without a categorical punt. Points: first-round volume anchor in common settings. Dynasty: top-12-ish win-now asset; the injury and age-28 horizon move him behind the very youngest elite tier without changing his franchise role.

Why the line looks like this

Tatum ruptured his right Achilles on May 12, 2025, returned on March 6, 2026, and played 16 regular-season games at 32.6 minutes: 21.8 points, 10.0 rebounds, 5.3 assists and 2.9 threes, but only 41.1% FG and 32.9% from three. That small sample is treated as a rehab-era floor on shooting, not his new talent level. NBA/Yahoo's early first-round review noted that he still ranked 26th in 9-cat and 19th in Yahoo High Score during the return stretch.

His season ended when left-knee stiffness kept him out of Game 7 after he had already reached 36-40 playoff minutes. The long offseason is positive, but no official July medical source declared him restriction-free. Boston also traded 28.7-point scorer Jaylen Brown for lower-volume Paul George, leaving Tatum with a clean path back to the largest offensive role.

Sources: Basketball Reference 2025-26 Celtics, NBA Achilles practice/return history, NBA May knee report, Yahoo interview after Game 7, NBA/Yahoo early first round.

Category shape

  • Wins: points, threes, rebounds, high-end assists for a forward, neutral-to-positive FT impact.
  • Stable but not elite: steals; field percentage should recover but remains high-volume sensitive.
  • Costs: turnovers and only modest blocks compared with modern first-round forwards.
  • Build fit: safe first pick for balanced, punt-blocks or punt-FG builds; less dominant in scarce defensive categories than Victor Wembanyama-type starts.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: 58-64 games, 24-25 points and low-40s FG if Achilles-era burst/shot quality lingers or knee maintenance grows. Still a top-30 per-game player, but a second-round totals outcome.
  • Base: 65 games and roughly 27-9-6 with three threes; near-pre-injury shooting rather than full career-best efficiency.
  • Ceiling: 76+ games, 28+ points and 6+ assists with 46-47% FG. Brown's departure turns him into a top-eight 9-cat asset and an MVP-level points option.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Duke, one season (2016-17). He played 29 games at 33.3 minutes for 16.8 points, 7.3 rebounds, 2.1 assists, 1.3 steals and 1.1 blocks on .452/.342/.849, taking ACC All-Freshman and third-team All-ACC honors plus a spot on the All-ACC Tournament team. He missed the season's opening stretch with a foot injury, which is part of why he was available at three. Draft: 2017, first round, third overall, Boston Celtics. Boston held the number one pick and traded it to Philadelphia before the draft for the third pick plus a future first, then used the third selection on Tatum; the pick moved, not the player.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks from a rater source could not be verified for any season and are not asserted. What the record supports without inference: from 2019-20 through 2024-25 Tatum was a six-year first- or early-second-round category asset built on points, threes and forward-scarce rebounds, with assists climbing steadily into a genuine positive by 2024-25 at 6.0. He has never helped in blocks and has never been better than neutral in steals. Field goal percentage is the swing category — .450 to .471 on 20-plus attempts is roughly neutral in most builds, which is why he fits punt-FG and punt-blocks constructions cleanly. The 2025-26 sample of 16 games is not rateable as a season; the dossier's note that he sat 26th in nine-cat and 19th in Yahoo High Score during the return stretch is the best available marker and should be read as a rehab-window rate, not a season finish. Points-league value has consistently run ahead of category value because the format rewards his scoring and rebounding volume and does not charge him for the missing defensive counting stats.

Durability: 80, 79, 66, 64, 76, 74, 74, 72, 16. Through 2024-25 this was one of the better availability records among high-usage stars — the 2019-20 and 2020-21 dips are largely schedule artifacts, since those seasons ran 72 and 72 games rather than 82, with a groin strain and a January 2021 COVID-19 absence accounting for most of the rest. He then played 76, 74, 74 and 72 in four consecutive full seasons. The record breaks in one place: a ruptured right Achilles suffered on May 12, 2025 in the conference semifinals against New York, followed by surgery and roughly ten months out. He returned on March 5, 2026 against Dallas, starting and playing 27 minutes; the "Why the line looks like this" section above dates the return to March 6, which contemporaneous reporting contradicts. He played 16 regular-season games before left-knee stiffness kept him out of Game 7 against Philadelphia. The knee event is separate from the Achilles and is the detail that keeps availability confidence below where a clean rehab would put it.

Trajectory: The arc through 2024-25 is a textbook usage-and-skill staircase rather than a minutes staircase: minutes went from 30.5 to a 36.9 plateau and then stayed there, while scoring tripled from 13.9 to a 30.1 peak on rising three-point volume, and assists more than tripled from 1.6 to 6.0 as Boston moved him from finisher to hub. The 2022-23 scoring peak was the usage high-water mark; the more valuable development came after it, when he traded roughly three points a game for an extra assist and a half and better shot selection. The 2025-26 sample is the only genuinely uncertain input. Three things in those 16 games point in different directions: rebounds jumped to a career-high 10.0 and steals to a career-high 1.4, which is not what a diminished athlete produces, while field goal percentage fell to .411 and blocks collapsed to 0.2, which is exactly what reduced vertical push looks like. The honest read is that ground-level production returned before explosion did. At 28 he is at the center of the aging curve, not past it, and the Achilles-return literature is thin enough that the second season back is where the answer arrives. For 2026-27, expect the shooting to recover most of the gap toward .450 given Jaylen Brown's departure clears defensive attention, expect blocks to stay near zero, and treat games played rather than per-game rate as the variable that decides his season.
