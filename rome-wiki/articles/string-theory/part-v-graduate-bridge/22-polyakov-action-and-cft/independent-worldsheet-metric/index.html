    Independent worldsheet metric

    Treating the worldsheet metric h_{\alpha\beta} as an independent field rather than as the geometry the string inherits is the single move that makes the string quantizable: its three components are eaten exactly by three local symmetries, so it propagates nothing, can be gauged flat, and its own equation of motion is the Virasoro constraints — the very constraints we used to impose by hand.

    A metric with no job of its own

    Start one dimension down, with a point particle. Its natural action is the arc length S=−m∫dτ−x˙2S=-m\int d\tau\sqrt{-\dot x^2}S=−m∫dτ−x˙2​ — a square root, awful to quantize. The trick that fixes it: bolt on an extra field, the einbein e(τ)e(\tau)e(τ), a "ruler" that measures proper time along the path, and write S=12∫dτ (e−1x˙2−m2e).S=\tfrac12\int d\tau\,\big(e^{-1}\dot x^2 - m^2 e\big).S=21​∫dτ(e−1x˙2−m2e). No square root, and eee is not really new. It has no derivatives in the action, so its equation of motion is algebraic: varying eee gives e=−x˙2/me=\sqrt{-\dot x^2}/me=−x˙2​/m, and putting that back reproduces the square-root action exactly. In momentum variables that same equation reads p2+m2=0p^2+m^2=0p2+m2=0 — the mass-shell constraint. The einbein's entire purpose is to be a non-dynamical field whose EOM enforces a constraint you would otherwise impose by hand.

    The worldsheet metric hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ is the einbein with one more dimension. Same three things happen: it kills the square root (making the Polyakov action quadratic in ∂X\partial X∂X), it is non-dynamical, and its algebraic EOM is a constraint — here, the Virasoro constraints. The rest of the note makes each of these three claims precise.

    Two metrics, kept separate

    The one confusion to avoid: there are two metrics on the worldsheet.

    • The induced metric γαβ=∂αX⋅∂βX\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta Xγαβ​=∂α​X⋅∂β​X — determined by the embedding XμX^\muXμ. It is the actual geometry the string sheet has, inherited from spacetime. (The dot is A⋅B=ημνAμBνA\cdot B=\eta_{\mu\nu}A^\mu B^\nuA⋅B=ημν​AμBν, mostly-plus.)
    • The intrinsic metric hαβ(τ,σ)h_{\alpha\beta}(\tau,\sigma)hαβ​(τ,σ) — an independent field, put into the Polyakov action by hand, with its own equation of motion. This is the kind of metric one writes down in 2D general relativity, a field on the surface rather than a consequence of it.

    In Nambu–Goto only γαβ\gamma_{\alpha\beta}γαβ​ exists. In Polyakov, hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ is a separate variable, and the relation hαβ∝γαβh_{\alpha\beta}\propto\gamma_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​∝γαβ​ is derived on-shell, not assumed — that derivation lives in Polyakov vs Nambu-Goto.

    Calling hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ auxiliary means something exact: it is varied in the action, but the action contains no derivatives of it. So its Euler–Lagrange equation is algebraic, not a wave equation. Unlike XμX^\muXμ, it does not propagate; it only ties together the gradients ∂αXμ\partial_\alpha X^\mu∂α​Xμ.

    Why it is pure gauge: the counting

    Here is the deep reason the metric can be thrown away entirely, not just simplified. A symmetric 2×22\times22×2 matrix has 3 independent components: hαβ=(hττhτσhτσhσσ).h_{\alpha\beta}=\begin{pmatrix} h_{\tau\tau} & h_{\tau\sigma}\\ h_{\tau\sigma} & h_{\sigma\sigma}\end{pmatrix}.hαβ​=(hττ​hτσ​​hτσ​hσσ​​). The Polyakov action has 3 local (position-dependent) symmetries acting on it:

    • 2 reparametrizations — worldsheet diffeomorphisms ξα→ξ~α(ξ)\xi^\alpha\to\tilde\xi^\alpha(\xi)ξα→ξ~​α(ξ), two arbitrary functions;
    • 1 Weyl rescaling hαβ→Ω2(ξ) hαβh_{\alpha\beta}\to\Omega^2(\xi)\,h_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​→Ω2(ξ)hαβ​, one arbitrary function (see Weyl invariance).

    A local symmetry is the license to change a field freely at every point without touching the physics. Three such licenses against three components: 3 components  −  3 local symmetries  =  0 propagating degrees of freedom.3\ \text{components}\;-\;3\ \text{local symmetries}\;=\;0\ \text{propagating degrees of freedom}.3 components−3 local symmetries=0 propagating degrees of freedom. The metric is pure gauge. Every value of hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ is reachable from every other by a symmetry, so choosing it costs nothing — which is exactly why we are allowed to gauge it flat.

    The 3-vs-2 subtlety: why only 2 constraints

    Varying a symmetric hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ with 3 entries looks like it should give 3 equations, yet there are only 2 Virasoro constraints. The resolution is that hhh enters the action only through the combination Mαβ≡−h hαβ,h≡det⁡hαβ,M^{\alpha\beta}\equiv\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta},\qquad h\equiv\det h_{\alpha\beta},Mαβ≡−h​hαβ,h≡dethαβ​, and in two dimensions this combination has a fixed determinant: det⁡Mαβ=(−h)2 det⁡(hαβ)=−hh=−1.\det M^{\alpha\beta}=(\sqrt{-h})^2\,\det(h^{\alpha\beta})=\frac{-h}{h}=-1.detMαβ=(−h​)2det(hαβ)=h−h​=−1. A symmetric 2×22\times22×2 matrix constrained to det⁡=−1\det=-1det=−1 has only 2 free parameters, not 3 — its overall scale is pinned. That missing scale is precisely the Weyl factor: the action never sees it, so varying it produces no equation. Hence 2 independent constraints, not 3.

    This is special to two dimensions. For an n×nn\times nn×n metric of signature (−,+,…,+)(-,+,\dots,+)(−,+,…,+) the same computation gives det⁡M=−(−h) n/2−1\det M=-(-h)^{\,n/2-1}detM=−(−h)n/2−1, which is a fixed constant only when n=2n=2n=2. In any other dimension MMM carries the scale and the metric is genuinely dynamical — the reason this whole story is unique to the worldsheet.

    Gauging it away: conformal gauge

    Because hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ is pure gauge, a standard fact of 2D geometry (the existence of isothermal coordinates) lets us bring any worldsheet metric locally to hαβ=ρ2(ξ) ηαβ,ηαβ=diag(−1,+1),h_{\alpha\beta}=\rho^2(\xi)\,\eta_{\alpha\beta},\qquad \eta_{\alpha\beta}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1),hαβ​=ρ2(ξ)ηαβ​,ηαβ​=diag(−1,+1), using up the 2 reparametrizations. This is conformal gauge: flat up to a position-dependent scale ρ2\rho^2ρ2. The leftover Weyl symmetry then erases ρ2\rho^2ρ2 from the combination that actually appears: −h hαβ=ηαβ(conformal gauge),\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\qquad\text{(conformal gauge)},−h​hαβ=ηαβ(conformal gauge), since the two powers of ρ\rhoρ from −h\sqrt{-h}−h​ and from hαβh^{\alpha\beta}hαβ cancel. After gauge-fixing the dynamical metric is entirely gone from the action — traded for a flat background plus the constraints it left behind.

    With −h hαβ=ηαβ\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}−h​hαβ=ηαβ, the XXX equation of motion collapses to the free 2D wave equation, \partial_\alpha(\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\beta X^\mu)=(-\partial_\tau^2+\partial_\sigma^2)X^\mu=0,\tag{1} and the metric's own EOM,   ∂αX⋅∂βX−12hαβ hγδ∂γX⋅∂δX=0  \;\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta X-\tfrac12 h_{\alpha\beta}\,h^{\gamma\delta}\partial_\gamma X\cdot\partial_\delta X=0\;∂α​X⋅∂β​X−21​hαβ​hγδ∂γ​X⋅∂δ​X=0 (the worldsheet stress tensor set to zero), reduces component by component to \dot X^2+X'^2=0\quad(\tau\tau),\qquad \dot X\cdot X'=0\quad(\tau\sigma),\tag{2} equivalently (X˙±X′)2=0(\dot X\pm X')^2=0(X˙±X′)2=0 — the Virasoro constraints, now derived rather than postulated. The remaining component (σσ\sigma\sigmaσσ) repeats the ττ\tau\tauττ equation, exactly matching the 3-vs-2 count above: the trace part carried no information.

    What gauge-fixing buys you

    Nambu–Goto hands you a square root and makes you impose (X˙±X′)2=0(\dot X\pm X')^2=0(X˙±X′)2=0 by hand. Polyakov's independent metric removes the square root, and because that metric is auxiliary and pure gauge, gauge-fixing it does two jobs at once: it turns the XXX dynamics into free waves (1), and its leftover equation of motion hands you the constraints for free (2). We added an auxiliary metric whose only purpose is to enforce a law, then gauged it away and kept the law.

    Open questions

    • Local versus global. hαβ=ρ2ηαβh_{\alpha\beta}=\rho^2\eta_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​=ρ2ηαβ​ is a local statement. On higher-genus worldsheets a finite number of metric parameters (moduli) survive gauge fixing; developed in Moduli spaces.
    • Quantum gauge fixing. Diffeomorphism + Weyl removes the metric classically. Quantum mechanically the gauge-fixing is legitimate only if the total central charge vanishes, which forces D=26D=26D=26; see Weyl invariance and the ghost module.
    • Geometry input. That every 2D metric is locally conformally flat (isothermal coordinates) is used here as a standard theorem, not proved.

    Exercise

    Show that the metric-EOM component along σσ\sigma\sigmaσσ is not an independent constraint — i.e. that in conformal gauge the σσ\sigma\sigmaσσ equation is identical to the ττ\tau\tauττ equation — and connect this to the counting argument. Then state what would go wrong with this reasoning in 333 worldsheet dimensions.

    Solution

    In conformal gauge −h hαβ=ηαβ=diag(−1,+1)\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1)−h​hαβ=ηαβ=diag(−1,+1), so hαβ=ηαβh_{\alpha\beta}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​=ηαβ​ (up to the Weyl factor, which cancels) and hγδ∂γX⋅∂δX=−X˙2+X′2h^{\gamma\delta}\partial_\gamma X\cdot\partial_\delta X=-\dot X^2+X'^2hγδ∂γ​X⋅∂δ​X=−X˙2+X′2. The stress tensor Tαβ=∂αX⋅∂βX−12ηαβ(−X˙2+X′2)T_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta X-\tfrac12\eta_{\alpha\beta}(-\dot X^2+X'^2)Tαβ​=∂α​X⋅∂β​X−21​ηαβ​(−X˙2+X′2) has components Tττ=X˙2−12(−1)(−X˙2+X′2)=12(X˙2+X′2),T_{\tau\tau}=\dot X^2-\tfrac12(-1)(-\dot X^2+X'^2)=\tfrac12(\dot X^2+X'^2),Tττ​=X˙2−21​(−1)(−X˙2+X′2)=21​(X˙2+X′2), Tσσ=X′2−12(+1)(−X˙2+X′2)=12(X˙2+X′2).T_{\sigma\sigma}=X'^2-\tfrac12(+1)(-\dot X^2+X'^2)=\tfrac12(\dot X^2+X'^2).Tσσ​=X′2−21​(+1)(−X˙2+X′2)=21​(X˙2+X′2). So Tττ=TσσT_{\tau\tau}=T_{\sigma\sigma}Tττ​=Tσσ​ identically: setting one to zero automatically sets the other. Only Tττ=0T_{\tau\tau}=0Tττ​=0 and Tτσ=X˙⋅X′=0T_{\tau\sigma}=\dot X\cdot X'=0Tτσ​=X˙⋅X′=0 are independent — 2 constraints, not 3.

    This is the local shadow of the counting. The redundant component is the trace ηαβTαβ\eta^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta}ηαβTαβ​; in 2D the stress tensor is automatically traceless (that is Weyl invariance), so its trace is not an equation. Equivalently, the scale of hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ dropped out of the action because det⁡(−h hαβ)=−1\det(\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta})=-1det(−h​hαβ)=−1 is fixed, leaving 2 free metric parameters and hence 2 constraints.

    In 3 dimensions the argument breaks: det⁡(−h hαβ)=−(−h)n/2−1=−(−h)1/2\det(\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta})=-(-h)^{n/2-1}=-(-h)^{1/2}det(−h​hαβ)=−(−h)n/2−1=−(−h)1/2 is not constant, so the Weyl factor is no longer invisible — the metric keeps a propagating scale, tracelessness fails, and there is no gauge in which the metric disappears. The trick is unique to the 2D worldsheet.
