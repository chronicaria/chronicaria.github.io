Norman Powell

Norman Powell is Chicago's veteran scoring and spacing stabilizer, valuable for points, threes, and FT% but carrying age-33 totals risk.

Role and baseline

Powell made the 2026 All-Star team and averaged 21.7 points, 2.7 threes, 3.5 rebounds, 2.5 assists, and 1.1 steals on .470/.827 shooting in 29.6 minutes across 58 games for Miami. Basketball-Reference

Chicago signed him to a reported two-year, $45 million contract. Elias Schuster's July 2 lineup projection placed him at starting shooting guard and argued that his spacing fits Giddey and the young forwards. That role is likely, not officially guaranteed. Fantasy fit

  • 9-cat: picks 65-95; clean points/threes/FT% profile with modest steals and limited rebounds/assists.
  • Points: picks 65-90; scoring keeps him useful, but he lacks peripheral-event upside.
  • Dynasty: win-now piece rather than a foundational asset at 33.
  • Best builds: teams needing efficient guard scoring without another high-turnover creator.

Range and triggers

  • Floor: 15 points and 2.2 threes in 50-58 games if age and minor injuries reduce his workload.
  • Ceiling: 22 points, three threes, and one steal with top-60 9-cat value if he remains the clear second scorer and Chicago leans on him early.
  • Risks: age curve, last season's 58 games, and the possibility that Chicago sells a veteran if its win trajectory collapses.
  • Watch: preseason minutes, second-unit staggering, and whether Caleb Wilson or Matas Buzelis becomes the No. 2 option.

Career record

Pre-NBA: UCLA, four seasons, 2011-12 through 2014-15. 4.6 points and 2.2 rebounds on .377/.347/.600 as a freshman; 6.1 and 2.2 on .434/.293/.675 as a sophomore; 11.4 and 2.8 on .533/.294/.780 as a junior; 16.4 points, 4.7 rebounds and 2.1 assists on .456/.319/.751 as a senior in 34.6 minutes. First-team All-Pac-12 in 2015 and an honorable-mention All-Defensive selection in 2014-15. The four-year arc is the relevant detail: he was a low-volume, poor-shooting reserve for two years and only became a primary scorer as a junior and senior, and his college three-point percentage never cleared .350. Draft: 2015, second round, 46th overall by Milwaukee; rights traded to Toronto on draft night with a 2017 first-round pick for Greivis Vásquez.

Split seasons: 2020-21 was 42 games in Toronto (30.4 MPG, 19.6 points, .498 FG, 2.8 threes) and 27 in Portland after the March 2021 trade (34.4 MPG, 17.0 points, .443 FG, 2.0 threes) — the efficiency dropped on a heavier off-ball role. 2021-22 was 40 games in Portland (33.3 MPG, 18.7 points) and only 5 with the Clippers after the February 2022 deal, because the foot fracture cost him the rest of the season.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not retrievable from open sources here, so no year-by-year finish is asserted. The box scores make the shape plain. Two distinct value tiers exist: a low-minutes bench era through 2018-19 that was never rosterable, and a starter era from 2019-20 onward in which he has been a points-threes-FT% specialist with a genuinely positive field goal percentage for a high-volume guard. The best raw seasons were 2024-25 (21.8 points, 3.0 threes, .484/.804) and 2025-26 (21.7 points, 2.7 threes, .470/.827); both are strong in four categories, neutral-to-negative in rebounds, assists and turnovers, and empty in blocks. Points leagues rate him slightly lower than categories because his production is concentrated in scoring alone with almost no peripheral accumulation, whereas categories reward the efficiency on both percentages.

Durability: 49, 76, 70, 60, 52, 69, 45, 60, 76, 60, 58. The rookie-year 49 was rotation and G-League assignment, not injury. 2018-19 was cut by a partial left shoulder dislocation on November 5, 2018 that cost roughly 21 games. 2019-20 involved a left shoulder problem in mid-December and a left hand metacarpal fracture on January 31, 2020, on top of a league season shortened by the March suspension. 2021-22 ended early with a medial sesamoid fracture in the left foot suffered February 10, 2022, roughly two months out. In 2025-26 he missed 24 games in total: an earlier stretch of 11 absences, eight of them injury and three for the birth of his daughter, then a Grade 1 right groin strain he aggravated against Philadelphia that was called week-to-week and lingered through the closing months — his scoring fell from about 22.5 per game across the first 49 games to 17.5 after the All-Star break. The causes of the 2022-23 and 2024-25 absences are not verified here. The pattern is not one catastrophic injury but a persistent inability to clear 65 games in seven of the last eight seasons.

Trajectory: Powell's career is one of the clearest minutes-driven breakouts of the last decade, and it happened late. Through age 25 he was a 15-to-19-minute reserve shooting .401 to .483 with under 1.1 threes a game; the 2019-20 jump to 28.4 minutes doubled his scoring to 16.0 without any efficiency cost, and every season since has simply tracked opportunity. The genuinely new development is the 2024-25 and 2025-26 pair — 21.8 and 21.7 points on .484 and .470 at ages 31 and 32, career-best volume arriving at an age when guards normally give it back, sustained by a shot diet that is roughly half threes and by an .824 career free throw stroke that has never wavered. There is no per-minute decline yet to point at. What there is instead is an availability curve moving the wrong way and a groin injury that visibly degraded his second half, and Chicago is projecting him at 30 minutes rather than the 32.6 he played in his last full-throttle season. The honest 2026-27 read is that the per-minute scoring is real and likely to hold for one more year, while the games played and the minutes are both more likely to fall than rise, which caps him as a strong-rate, moderate-total asset rather than the top-40 season the raw averages suggest.
