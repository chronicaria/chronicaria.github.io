Bennett Stirtz

Bennett Stirtz is an older rookie with shooting, decision-making and steals that should translate, but Oklahoma City's seven-deep guard room makes his one-minute base a redraft dead end.

That figure was a residual used to force the 240 sum rather than an achievable rotation number — no NBA player logs one or two minutes across 26 appearances — so he moves to the same appearance-card treatment already used for the two-ways, and the freed minutes go to Thomas Sorber, whose July knee procedure leaves him on track for training camp.

Nine-category / points: no 2026-27 draft value. Dynasty: Hashtag No. 282; low star ceiling but a plausible long-term rotation guard because shooting/feel can survive without high usage.

Why the line looks like this

Stirtz finished at Iowa with 19.8 points, 4.4 assists, 2.6 rebounds and 1.4 steals on 48/36/86 shooting, earning first-team All-Big Ten recognition. Oklahoma City traded up one spot to acquire his rights at No. 16, then signed him July 3.

Summer League began unevenly but improved as he handled more: he scored 18 against the Lakers, 13 on 5-for-10 against Golden State and 22 with six assists against Denver. That suggests NBA competence, not a role ahead of SGA, Williams, Mitchell, Wallace, McCain or Topic.

Sources: NBA prospect profile, official draft transaction, NBA Warriors recap, NBA Nuggets recap, Hashtag dynasty.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons across three levels, following coach Ben McCollum up the ladder. Northwest Missouri State (D2) 2022-24: MIAA Freshman of the Year, then 15.2 PTS / 3.5 REB / 3.5 AST / 1.4 STL as a sophomore, twice second-team All-MIAA. Drake 2024-25: 19.2 PTS / 5.7 AST while leading the MVC in scoring, steals (2.2) and assist-to-turnover ratio (2.86); MVC Player of the Year, Newcomer of the Year and Tournament MVP. Iowa 2025-26: 19.8 PTS / 2.6 REB / 4.4 AST / 1.4 STL on 48/36/86; first-team All-Big Ten, honorable-mention All-American. Draft: 2026, Round 1, Pick 16, Memphis Grizzlies; traded on draft night to Oklahoma City for Ebuka Okorie and two second-round picks.

No NBA record. Most recent season: 2025-26 at Iowa (19.8 PTS, 4.4 AST on 48/36/86).

Durability: No injury history of note across four college seasons.

Trajectory: The record is a rare three-level proof: the same efficient, low-turnover lead-guard game produced at D2, mid-major and Big Ten stops, which is why a 23-year-old rookie went 16th. Age caps the star outcome, but shooting, feel and steals are the skills that survive without usage. In 2026-27 he competes only for the developmental guard slot behind OKC's seven-deep room, likely splitting time with the Blue against Topic for creation reps. He becomes fantasy-relevant this season only via multiple guard injuries (a 14-minute, 5-point/3-assist/one-three line at best); the real case is dynasty, where a plausible long-term rotation-guard career justifies a late stash.
