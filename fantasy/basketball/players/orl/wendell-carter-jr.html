2025-26 baseline

Carter played 78 games at 29.3 minutes, averaging 11.8 points, 7.4 rebounds, 2.0 assists, 0.8 steals, 0.6 blocks, 0.9 threes, and 1.3 turnovers. He shot .512 on 8.2 field-goal attempts and .792 on 3.2 free throws.

The efficiency and availability were useful; the blocks were not. Carter's fantasy ceiling has long been lower than his real-life starting value because a center averaging well under one block needs either much larger scoring/rebounding volume or exceptional percentages.

Role and pathway

Carter is projected to start because he fits Orlando's core timeline, can defend stronger centers, and does not require Vucevic-level touches. The base removes four minutes after Vucevic's return while keeping Carter first in the center queue.

This is not secure enough to treat as reported fact. Sweeney could start Vucevic, use the two together, or close with Banchero at center. Carter's nightly range could span 20 to 30 minutes even when healthy. A Vucevic absence makes 28-plus easy; a healthy four-center rotation can push him below the base.

Category shape

  • Strong: field-goal percentage, rebounds, low turnovers.
  • Helpful: FT is acceptable for a center; occasional threes and steals.
  • Weak: blocks, scoring volume, standout category scarcity.

The lack of blocks makes Carter a poor fit for managers expecting their center slot to repair rim protection. He works better beside a high-block forward or another center.

Career record

Pre-NBA: One season at Duke (2017-18) alongside Marvin Bagley III — 37 games, 13.5 points, 9.1 rebounds, 2.0 assists and 2.1 blocks on .561 FG/.413 3PT/.738 FT, earning second-team All-ACC and the ACC All-Freshman Team. The 2.1 blocks and .413 from three at 18 are the two numbers worth remembering, because neither has translated. Draft: 2018, first round, seventh overall, Chicago Bulls; no draft-night trade. He was sent to Orlando with Otto Porter Jr. on March 25, 2021 in the deal that brought Nikola Vučević and Al-Farouq Aminu to Chicago — the same Vučević who is now his direct competition in Orlando.

The 2020-21 split: 32 games with Chicago at 24.8 minutes for 10.9 points, 7.8 rebounds on .512 FG, then 22 games with Orlando at 26.5 minutes for 11.7 points, 8.8 rebounds on .493 FG after the March 25, 2021 trade. Minutes and production barely moved, which is unusual for a midseason move and reflects that he was already a starter on both ends of the deal.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not verifiable from public archives for any of Carter's eight seasons, so no finish is asserted. The line supports this reading: 2021-22 and 2022-23 were his only seasons with a plausible top-100 category profile, because 15 points with 10.5 and 8.7 rebounds plus 1.1-1.4 threes on .525 shooting is a genuine four-category contributor even without blocks. Everything since 2023-24 has been late-round or waiver territory — the scoring collapsed to 9.1 in 2024-25 and only partly recovered, and the block rate has sat between 0.5 and 0.6 for four straight years, which is the structural problem for a fantasy center. Points-league value tracks the category value closely here, with one difference: his low turnovers are a category asset worth nothing in most points formats, so points leagues see him as a slightly worse version of the same player.

Durability: 44, 43, 54, 62, 57, 55, 68, 78. The first six years were a genuine availability problem; the last two are the opposite. Season by season: 2018-19 ended March 24, 2019 after left thumb surgery; 2019-20 was wrecked by a severe ankle sprain suffered against Dallas on January 6, 2020, initially projected at four to six weeks and ultimately costing 22 straight games; 2022-23 cost him 27 games, 20 of them to a broken left hand that required in-season surgery and a follow-up procedure that offseason; 2023-24 cost him 14 across four separate issues — right knee tendinitis (Nov. 1), a left plantar fascia strain (Nov. 4), plantar fasciitis (Nov. 6-25, 11 games), and a right hip contusion (Apr. 11). The specific causes of the 14 games missed in 2024-25 are not individually verified. The 78 games in 2025-26 is a career high by ten, with a neck sprain suffered against Detroit in April 2026 the only notable in-season exit. Two consecutive healthy seasons at ages 25 and 26 is the single most encouraging thing in his profile.

Trajectory: Carter's career is a peak-early-then-flatten curve, which is unusual for a center in his mid-twenties and matters for how much upside to price in. The leap was 2021-22, when Orlando handed a 22-year-old 29.9 minutes on a team with nothing else and he produced 15.0 and 10.5 with 1.1 threes — that is his ceiling season, and it happened four years ago on a 22-win roster. Everything since has been a controlled reduction as Banchero and Franz Wagner absorbed usage: his minutes have held between 25.6 and 29.9 the whole time, so the drop from 15.2 points to 11.8 is a genuine per-minute decline in role, not a rotation cut. The one real improvement in 2025-26 was the free-throw line, .792 on 3.2 attempts against a .740 career mark, plus the shooting recovering from a bad .460 in 2024-25 back to .512. At 27 he is at the top of the aging curve with the athletic peak behind him and the block rate never having arrived, so the 2026-27 case rests entirely on whether Vučević's return costs him four minutes or twelve — there is no development story left to bet on.
