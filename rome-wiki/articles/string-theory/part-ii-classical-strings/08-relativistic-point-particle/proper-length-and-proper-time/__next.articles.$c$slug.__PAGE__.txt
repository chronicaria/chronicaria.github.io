3:T24ee2,<h2 id="the-riding-stopwatch">The riding stopwatch</h2>
<p>Strap a stopwatch to a particle and let it fly. The number the stopwatch reads between two events on its path is the <strong>proper time</strong> — "proper" in the old sense of <em>belonging to it</em>. Two events, one clock riding between them: that reading is a fact about the trip, not about who is watching.</p>

<p>comes out the <em>same</em> for all of them: the invariant left over after the frame-dependent pieces cancel, and exactly what the riding stopwatch measures. What remains is to name this quantity and fix its units.</p>
<p>This is why the invariant matters. The <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-ii-classical-strings/08-relativistic-point-particle/proper-time-action/" class="wiki-link">Proper-time action</a> for a relativistic particle is built from exactly this quantity, because an action all observers compute identically gives physics all observers agree on. The four-momentum, the mass shell, the string generalization — everything downstream hangs on getting this one quantity and its units straight.</p>

<h2 id="unit-tangent-versus-four-velocity">Unit tangent versus four-velocity</h2>
<p>Once we have a scalar to parameterize by, we can differentiate position along the path. Two natural choices of parameter give two objects that differ only by a factor of <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">c</span></span></span></span>.</p>

<li><strong>Is straight-line motion really the maximum?</strong> Among nearby timelike worldlines between two fixed events, the straight one <em>maximizes</em> elapsed proper time (the twin who coasts ages most). Globally this can fail past conjugate points in curved spacetime — a caveat for the geodesic version, not needed here.</li>
<li><strong>One tick vs. a whole sheet.</strong> A string sweeps a 2D worldsheet with an induced <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mo>×</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2\times2</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7278em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">×</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span></span></span></span> metric, not a single proper-time element. That is why <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-ii-classical-strings/08-relativistic-point-particle/worldline-versus-worldsheet/" class="wiki-link">Worldline versus worldsheet</a> treats "proper time" as a particle-only convenience rather than a literal string gauge.</li>
</ul>
