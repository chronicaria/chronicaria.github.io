Cutoff and desk verdict

Research cutoff: 2026-07-18, America/New_York. “Latest” below means the latest publication, update, or live-page observation that the source desks could verify by that cutoff. Mutable pages can change after access.

No public source had yet supplied all of the following in one auditable product: a complete season-labelled 2026-27 player pool, current teams and 2026 rookies, projected games and minutes, primitive category statistics, explicit scoring settings, a reproducible method, and a row-level update history. Current evidence therefore has to remain separated into five classes:

  • Certified 2026-27 artifact — the season, horizon, format, and player pool agree. This can still be early, partial, or one analyst's opinion.
  • Forward opinion — genuinely about 2026-27, but a rank, mock, article, podcast, video, or social post rather than a statistical forecast.
  • Current display — a live roster, depth chart, news feed, crowd board, or platform value observed on a date; its season or methodology may be unstated.
  • Historical baseline — correctly describes 2025-26 or an earlier period and is useful only as an input or comparison.
  • Quarantined mixed vintage — the shell, update stamp, current team overlay, URL, or navigation looks current while the heading, player class, sample, or numerical payload is older.

The most important finding is that an update stamp is not a season stamp. RotoWire, CBS, FantasyPros, Hashtag Basketball, Basketball Monster, Yahoo, RTSports, and several official/G League pages all expose at least one surface where presentation freshness and data freshness diverge.

Import gates

A numerical ranking, projection, ADP, auction value, or depth/minute claim is not eligible for silent import until all applicable gates pass:

  1. Season: the visible heading or metadata says 2026-27, and the table content agrees.
  2. Population: the 2026 rookie class is present where relevant; withdrawn, unsigned, and non-NBA players are understood.
  3. Roster: several high-salience 2026 transactions are reflected. Current team badges alone do not prove the underlying values were regenerated.
  4. Objective: redraft versus dynasty, per-game versus totals, category versus points, league size, categories, punt treatment, and horizon are explicit.
  5. Provenance: author/model, publication or update date, access date, direct URL, and access state are retained.
  6. Method: ranks, projections, ADP, mock picks, crowd votes, actual results, and news are kept as different fields.
  7. Version: a dated snapshot is stored or the record is marked mutable. A live page with no version history must not overwrite an older observation without preserving it.

Operational rules

  • Keep source publication date, source update date, event date, and encyclopedia access date as separate fields.
  • A current team badge can be a mutable overlay on stale rankings. Validate the player pool and numerical season, not just the badge.
  • A missing rookie is a strong stale-season warning, not absolute proof by itself; inspect the source's stated universe.
  • Never average redraft, dynasty, category, points, per-game, totals, ADP, mocks, or crowd votes into one unlabeled consensus.
  • Deduplicate related families: Basketball Monster/Locked On/Josh Lloyd; RotoWire articles/tools/shows/social; SportsEthos shows/articles/social; Old Man Squad/Stuff It network; cross-platform mirrors; syndicated articles.
  • A paywall does not make a source unusable, but inaccessible rows must not be reconstructed from snippets, social promotion, or secondary citations. Store only authorized visible output and access state.
  • Historical 2025-26 data remain useful when explicitly labelled as a baseline. The error is not using old data; it is presenting old data as a new-season forecast.
  • Every refreshed source should record what changed, what it supersedes, the roster version it reflects, and which player/team notes must be recomputed.
