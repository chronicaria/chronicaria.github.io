    Fundamental string charge

    A fundamental string is electrically charged under the closed-string Kalb-Ramond field B_{\mu\nu} , the way a point particle is charged under the Maxwell field A_\mu . But the source is one-dimensional, so its charge density is a vector that flows along the string like current in a wire.

    Two charged objects side by side

    Set the particle and the string next to each other.

    • A point charge couples to AμA_\muAμ​ (one index); its charge density ρ=j0\rho=j^0ρ=j0 is a scalar sitting at a point; you measure it by enclosing it in a sphere.
    • A string couples to BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ (two indices); its charge density j⃗ 0\vec{j}^{\,0}j​0 is a vector pointing along the string; it behaves not like a static line of charge but like a steady current in a wire.

    The wire is the picture to carry through the note: a current cannot simply stop, and every result below follows from that.

    Where the charge comes from: the coupling term

    Quantizing the closed string produced a massless antisymmetric tensor state Bμν=−BνμB_{\mu\nu}=-B_{\nu\mu}Bμν​=−Bνμ​ (the Kalb–Ramond field), alongside the graviton gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​ and the dilaton. A point particle of charge qqq couples to AμA_\muAμ​ through the worldline term q∫Aμ dxμq\int A_\mu\,dx^\muq∫Aμ​dxμ. The string, sweeping out a two-dimensional worldsheet rather than a worldline, couples instead to the two-index field BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ through a worldsheet integral:

    S_B = -\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\,d\sigma\;\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_\alpha X^\mu\,\partial_\beta X^\nu\,B_{\mu\nu}\big(X(\tau,\sigma)\big).\tag{1}

    Here α,β=0,1\alpha,\beta=0,1α,β=0,1 are worldsheet indices (∂0=∂τ\partial_0=\partial_\tau∂0​=∂τ​, ∂1=∂σ\partial_1=\partial_\sigma∂1​=∂σ​) and ϵαβ\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}ϵαβ is the 2D Levi-Civita symbol with ϵ01=1\epsilon^{01}=1ϵ01=1. The object ϵαβ∂αXμ∂βXν\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha X^\mu\partial_\beta X^\nuϵαβ∂α​Xμ∂β​Xν is the antisymmetrized oriented area element the worldsheet sweeps through spacetime — the natural thing to contract with the antisymmetric BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​. Compare the two couplings directly: a particle's worldline drags a tangent vector dxμdx^\mudxμ to feed the one-index AμA_\muAμ​; a string's worldsheet drags an oriented area 2-form to feed the two-index BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​. One more index in the field, one more dimension in the source.

    The 12\tfrac1221​ is bookkeeping: because BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ is antisymmetric, only the antisymmetric part of ∂αXμ∂βXν\partial_\alpha X^\mu\partial_\beta X^\nu∂α​Xμ∂β​Xν survives the contraction, and the 12\tfrac1221​ keeps that antisymmetrization clean. It rides along into every equation below.

    This is a background coupling: we quantized with B=0B=0B=0, which was valid; turning on a classical BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ that solves its own field equations is what "a Kalb–Ramond background" means. The rest of this note treats BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ as such a fixed background and asks what the string looks like as a source for it.

    Reading off the string current jμνj^{\mu\nu}jμν

    The coupling (1) lives on the worldsheet; to see the string as a spacetime source we localize BBB with a delta function:

    B_{\mu\nu}\big(X(\tau,\sigma)\big)=\int d^D x\;\delta^D\big(x-X(\tau,\sigma)\big)\,B_{\mu\nu}(x).\tag{2}

    Substituting (2) into (1) turns the worldsheet coupling into a spacetime coupling −∫dDx Bμν(x) jμν(x)-\int d^D x\,B_{\mu\nu}(x)\,j^{\mu\nu}(x)−∫dDxBμν​(x)jμν(x), which defines the string current:

    j^{\mu\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau\,d\sigma\;\delta^D\big(x-X(\tau,\sigma)\big)\left(\frac{\partial X^\mu}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial X^\nu}{\partial\sigma}-\frac{\partial X^\nu}{\partial\tau}\frac{\partial X^\mu}{\partial\sigma}\right).\tag{3}

    Two features are immediate and load-bearing. First, jμν=−jνμj^{\mu\nu}=-j^{\nu\mu}jμν=−jνμ by construction — it is antisymmetric like the field it sources. Second, the delta function fires only on the worldsheet: off the sheet, x≠X(τ,σ)x\neq X(\tau,\sigma)x=X(τ,σ) always, and jμν=0j^{\mu\nu}=0jμν=0. So the string is a source for BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ exactly as the Maxwell current jμj^\mujμ is a source for AμA_\muAμ​ — except this source also remembers an oriented direction in spacetime.

    It is a conserved current

    To get the equation of motion for BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ we need its kinetic term. The gauge-invariant field strength is the exterior derivative of BBB (see Kalb-Ramond field),

    Hμνρ=∂μBνρ+∂νBρμ+∂ρBμν,H_{\mu\nu\rho}=\partial_\mu B_{\nu\rho}+\partial_\nu B_{\rho\mu}+\partial_\rho B_{\mu\nu},Hμνρ​=∂μ​Bνρ​+∂ν​Bρμ​+∂ρ​Bμν​,

    the two-form analogue of Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμF_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\muFμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​: the combination built from the potential that survives gauge transformations. Its kinetic term is

    SH=−16κ2∫dDx  HμνρHμνρ,S_H=-\frac{1}{6\kappa^2}\int d^D x\;H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho},SH​=−6κ21​∫dDxHμνρ​Hμνρ,

    with κ\kappaκ a coupling constant fixed later by the string coupling and α′\alpha'α′. Rather than quote the field equation, vary SHS_HSH​. Varying H2H^2H2 pulls down a factor of 222 (16→13\tfrac16\to\tfrac1361​→31​); then, because HHH is totally antisymmetric, the three terms in δHμνρ=∂μδBνρ+∂νδBρμ+∂ρδBμν\delta H_{\mu\nu\rho}=\partial_\mu\delta B_{\nu\rho}+\partial_\nu\delta B_{\rho\mu}+\partial_\rho\delta B_{\mu\nu}δHμνρ​=∂μ​δBνρ​+∂ν​δBρμ​+∂ρ​δBμν​ each contract with HμνρH^{\mu\nu\rho}Hμνρ to give the same thing after relabeling dummy indices, a factor of 333 that clears the prefactor entirely (13→1\tfrac13\to131​→1):

    δSH=−13κ2∫dDx  Hμνρ δHμνρ=−1κ2∫dDx  Hμνρ ∂ρ(δBμν)=1κ2∫dDx  δBμν ∂ρHμνρ,\begin{aligned} \delta S_H &=-\frac{1}{3\kappa^2}\int d^D x\;H^{\mu\nu\rho}\,\delta H_{\mu\nu\rho}\\ &=-\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\int d^D x\;H^{\mu\nu\rho}\,\partial_\rho(\delta B_{\mu\nu})\\ &=\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\int d^D x\;\delta B_{\mu\nu}\,\partial_\rho H^{\mu\nu\rho}, \end{aligned}δSH​​=−3κ21​∫dDxHμνρδHμνρ​=−κ21​∫dDxHμνρ∂ρ​(δBμν​)=κ21​∫dDxδBμν​∂ρ​Hμνρ,​

    the last line integrating by parts and dropping a spacetime boundary term. The coupling (1), rewritten via (2), contributes δScoup=−∫dDx δBμν jμν\delta S_{\rm coup}=-\int d^D x\,\delta B_{\mu\nu}\,j^{\mu\nu}δScoup​=−∫dDxδBμν​jμν. Requiring δSH+δScoup=0\delta S_H+\delta S_{\rm coup}=0δSH​+δScoup​=0 for arbitrary antisymmetric δBμν\delta B_{\mu\nu}δBμν​ gives the field equation

    \frac{1}{\kappa^2}\frac{\partial H^{\mu\nu\rho}}{\partial x^\rho}=j^{\mu\nu},\tag{4}

    the exact analogue of Maxwell's ∂νFνμ=jμ\partial_\nu F^{\nu\mu}=j^\mu∂ν​Fνμ=jμ. Take one more divergence and use that HHH is totally antisymmetric in (μ,ν,ρ)(\mu,\nu,\rho)(μ,ν,ρ) while partial derivatives commute:

    \frac{\partial j^{\mu\nu}}{\partial x^\mu}=\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\frac{\partial^2 H^{\mu\nu\rho}}{\partial x^\mu\,\partial x^\rho}=0.\tag{5}

    The antisymmetric object ∂μ∂ρ\partial_\mu\partial_\rho∂μ​∂ρ​ contracted against the antisymmetric-in-μρ\mu\rhoμρ tensor HμνρH^{\mu\nu\rho}Hμνρ vanishes identically — the same one-line argument that makes the Maxwell current conserved. The string current is conserved on its first index.

    The charge is a vector along the string

    Equation (5) conserves jμνj^{\mu\nu}jμν on μ\muμ and leaves ν\nuν free. So ν\nuν labels a whole family of conserved currents, one for each value. Their charge densities are the time components j0νj^{0\nu}j0ν; but j00=0j^{00}=0j00=0 by antisymmetry, so the survivors are the spatial ones j0kj^{0k}j0k. Hence

    the Kalb–Ramond charge density is a spatial vector j⃗ 0 with components j0k.\text{the Kalb–Ramond charge density is a spatial vector }\vec{j}^{\,0}\text{ with components }j^{0k}.the Kalb–Ramond charge density is a spatial vector j​0 with components j0k.

    This is why it is a vector and not a scalar: antisymmetry kills the 000000 component that a scalar density would have lived in. Setting ν=0\nu=0ν=0 in the conservation law (5) gives ∂μjμ0=−∂kj0k=0\partial_\mu j^{\mu0}=-\partial_k j^{0k}=0∂μ​jμ0=−∂k​j0k=0, i.e.

    \nabla\cdot\vec{j}^{\,0}=0.\tag{6}

    This is the local balance law for string charge. In ordinary vector calculus ∇⋅j⃗=0\nabla\cdot\vec j=0∇⋅j​=0 says current is neither created nor destroyed: whatever flows into a small region flows back out. Here it says string-charge current lines have no loose ends — a string cannot stop in empty space, any more than a wire's current can. It can only close into a loop, run to infinity, or hand its current off to another system at a boundary. That handoff is what Endpoint charge and Maxwell fields on branes supply. The total vector charge is the space integral

    \vec{Q}=\int d^d x\;\vec{j}^{\,0}.\tag{7}

    Now pin down the direction of j⃗ 0\vec{j}^{\,0}j​0. Work in static gauge X0=τX^0=\tauX0=τ: the delta function in (3) factorizes as δ(t−τ) δ(x⃗−X⃗)\delta(t-\tau)\,\delta(\vec x-\vec X)δ(t−τ)δ(x−X), the τ\tauτ-integral collapses, and the second term drops (it carries ∂X0/∂σ=0\partial X^0/\partial\sigma=0∂X0/∂σ=0), leaving

    \vec{j}^{\,0}(\vec x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\int d\sigma\;\delta\big(\vec x-\vec X(t,\sigma)\big)\,\vec{X}'(t,\sigma),\qquad \vec X'=\partial\vec X/\partial\sigma.\tag{8}

    At every point of the string the charge density points along X⃗′\vec X'X′ — the tangent to the string, in the direction of increasing σ\sigmaσ. Kalb–Ramond charge is not a scalar smeared on the string; it is a vector that flows along it. That is the wire.

    Worked example: an infinite static string

    Lay a static string along the x1x^1x1 axis: X1=f(σ)X^1=f(\sigma)X1=f(σ) with fff strictly monotonic, X2=⋯=Xd=0X^2=\dots=X^d=0X2=⋯=Xd=0. Only j01j^{01}j01 survives, and (8) becomes

    =\frac12\,\mathrm{sgn}(f')\,\delta(\vec x_\perp),$$ using the delta-function identity $\int d\sigma\,\delta(x^1-f(\sigma))\,f'(\sigma)=f'/|f'|=\mathrm{sgn}(f')$ at the unique root. The charge is localized transverse to the string, and its **sign tracks the orientation**: increasing $f$ ($\mathrm{sgn}\,f'=+1$) versus decreasing ($-1$) are strings pointing opposite ways. This is why $B$-charged strings must be **oriented** curves. The Nambu–Goto action is reparameterization-invariant, but the coupling (1) is *not*: sending $\sigma\to\pi-\sigma$ leaves the measure $d\tau\,d\sigma$ unchanged but flips the sign of $X'$, so $S_B$ changes sign. Orientation is therefore physical. Consistently, unoriented string theories carry no $B_{\mu\nu}$ in their spectrum — there would be no direction for the charge vector to point. ## Open questions - The absolute normalization of $\kappa$ in terms of $g_s$ and $\alpha'$ is not fixed here; it belongs with the later string-coupling / Born–Infeld normalization. - For an *open* string the conservation law (5) fails by endpoint terms unless brane fields are included; the delta-function calculation then produces boundary contributions at $\sigma=0,\pi$. That failure, and its cure, is the subject of [[Endpoint charge]]. - Whether a given $B_{\mu\nu}$ configuration is a legitimate background can only be settled by re-quantizing the string in that background; the full nonlinear consistency condition is deferred to later Polyakov/CFT material. ## Exercise Take an infinite static string along $x^1$ described by $X^1=f(\sigma)$, and consider two parameterizations of the *same* curve in space:

    \text{(a)}\quad f(\sigma)=\sigma,\qquad\qquad \text{(b)}\quad f(\sigma)=-\sigma.

    For each, compute $j^{01}$ from equation (8), and state the resulting total charge per unit transverse area. Then explain in one sentence why the two answers differ even though the *set of points* occupied by the string is identical — and what physical quantity the difference is tracking. > [!success]- Solution > Use $j^{01}=\tfrac12\int d\sigma\,\delta(x^1-f(\sigma))\,f'(\sigma)\,\delta(\vec x_\perp)$ from (8). > > **(a)** $f(\sigma)=\sigma$ gives $f'=+1$, so $\int d\sigma\,\delta(x^1-\sigma)\cdot 1 = 1$ and > $$j^{01}=+\tfrac12\,\delta(\vec x_\perp).$$ > The charge per unit transverse area (integrate $\delta(\vec x_\perp)$ over the transverse plane) is $+\tfrac12$. > > **(b)** $f(\sigma)=-\sigma$ gives $f'=-1$. Substituting $u=-\sigma$, $\int d\sigma\,\delta(x^1+\sigma)\cdot(-1)=-1$, so > $$j^{01}=-\tfrac12\,\delta(\vec x_\perp),$$ > charge per unit transverse area $-\tfrac12$. Equivalently, both cases collapse to $\tfrac12\,\mathrm{sgn}(f')\,\delta(\vec x_\perp)$, which is $\pm\tfrac12$. > > **Why they differ:** the coupling $S_B$ is not reparameterization-invariant, and (b) is (a) with orientation reversed ($\sigma\to-\sigma$ flips the sign of the tangent $\vec X'$). The current $\vec j^{\,0}\propto\vec X'$ points *along* the string, so reversing the string's orientation flips the sign of its charge. The difference is tracking **orientation**: (a) and (b) are the same wire carrying current in opposite directions, hence carrying opposite $B$-charge. A string and its orientation-reverse are charge conjugates, which is exactly why $B$-charged strings must be oriented curves.
