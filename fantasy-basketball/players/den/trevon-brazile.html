Trevon Brazile

Trevon Brazile is Denver's most interesting signed deep-dynasty rookie because college steals and blocks pair with real shooting upside, but a second-round pedigree and crowded frontcourt keep the base projection small.

Team: Denver Nuggets · Position: PF/C · Rookie season · Pick No. 35

Reported baseline

Brazile averaged 13.0 points, 7.3 rebounds, 1.6 assists, 1.5 steals, 1.6 blocks and 1.3 threes in 31.5 minutes over 36 games for Arkansas, shooting 52.5% from the field and 71.7% at the line.1 Denver traded down from No. 26 to draft him 35th while acquiring two future second-rounders, then officially signed him July 10.2 3

The strongest July flash was 32 points on 11-of-19 shooting with six threes and six rebounds against Oklahoma City; he also posted 19 points and eight rebounds against Portland.4 Those games support the high tail of the shooting projection but are not NBA-role evidence.

9-cat per-game band: 250-plus. Points: deep-league watch list. Dynasty: Denver's preferred rookie stash; blocks, steals and threes offer a path to future category relevance.

Fantasy profile

  • Helps: blocks, rebounds and transition finishing; theoretical threes plus steals are the differentiators.
  • Hurts: FT%, playmaking and rookie efficiency/turnovers if asked to create.
  • Floor: G League/developmental season below 8 MPG, especially if Watson returns or another center signs.
  • Ceiling: wins a 20-MPG reserve frontcourt role and approaches one three plus one block per game, a top-160 category profile.

Role, health, and confidence

Brazile can compete at both reserve frontcourt spots, but Bagley, Nnaji and Holmes are signed and Aaron Gordon absorbs small-ball center minutes. His Arkansas record also includes a prior season-ending right ACL injury, relevant background but not evidence of a current restriction.1 Roster confidence high, minutes low, rate low-medium, availability medium.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Five college seasons — Missouri 2021-22 (25 GP: 6.6 PTS / 5.1 REB / 1.9 BLK on .534 FG), then Arkansas 2022-26. His 2022-23 season ended at 9 games (11.8 PTS / 6.0 REB) with a torn ACL and a medical redshirt; 2023-24: 26 GP, 8.6 / 5.9 / 1.2 BLK (.488/.353/.695); 2024-25: 34 GP, 6.8 / 5.4 / 1.1 BLK (.600 FG); 2025-26 (senior): 36 GP / 35 GS, 31.5 MPG, 13.0 PTS / 7.3 REB / 1.6 AST / 1.5 STL / 1.6 BLK / 1.3 3PM on .525/.341/.717 — SEC All-Tournament Team, two-time SEC Player of the Week, only Razorback ever with 40+ threes, 40+ blocks and 40+ steals in one season. College career: 130 GP, 9.2 PTS / 6.0 REB on .528/.351/.662. Draft: 2026, Round 2, Pick 35 (selected by San Antonio, rights traded to Denver, which moved down from No. 26 for two future seconds).

No NBA regular-season record — his most recent competitive season is 2025-26 at Arkansas, plus a 2026 Summer League flash (32 points with six threes vs. Oklahoma City).

Durability: One structural red flag: the 2022-23 ACL tear cost him 27-plus games, and he missed the first 8 games of 2021-22 with a medical issue plus 8-plus games in 2023-24. But he answered it — 34 of 36 and then 36 of 37 games the last two seasons, including a 46:37 double-OT outing, so the knee is demonstrated healthy under a full-starter load.

Trajectory: The college record shows a rare stocks-plus-threes big: his senior year combined 1.6 blocks, 1.5 steals and 1.3 threes on 34% deep volume, exactly the category cocktail that makes fantasy centers interesting. It took him five college years and an ACL rehab to put a full season together, which is why he lasted to pick 35. In 2026-27 he competes for the fourth or fifth big-man spot in Denver behind Gordon, Bagley, Nnaji and Holmes (plus Watson's wing-forward minutes); he becomes fantasy-relevant only if he cracks roughly 18-20 MPG — via a Watson departure, an injury, or beating Nnaji/Holmes outright in camp — at which point the block-steal-three profile could return top-160 value at near-zero cost.
