f:T41326,<h2 id="the-anomaly-in-one-number">The anomaly in one number</h2>
<p>The conformal transformations of the worldsheet form a symmetry, and every symmetry has generators with an algebra. Classically that algebra is rigid and well known (the <strong>Witt algebra</strong>). Quantizing adds exactly one new term — a c-number that no rearrangement of operators can remove. That term is an <strong>anomaly</strong>: a classical symmetry that the quantum theory cannot quite keep. Its strength is a single number <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">c</span></span></span></span>, and the whole drama of the bosonic string — <em>why 26 dimensions?</em> — is the demand that this anomaly cancel.</p>
<p>One way to picture <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">c</span></span></span></span>: it is the "zero-point noise" the vacuum makes when you try to run a conformal transformation on it. Empty space has more structure quantum-mechanically than classically, and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">c</span></span></span></span> measures how much. You cannot make it go away in any single sector; you can only arrange for the sectors to cancel.</p>

<p>Faces 3 and 4 are why the string cares. On the string worldsheet, conformal (Weyl) invariance is not a bonus symmetry — it is a <em>gauge</em> symmetry, the leftover freedom after fixing the auxiliary metric to conformal gauge. A gauge symmetry with an anomaly is fatal: the theory would depend on the unphysical Weyl factor. So consistency demands the <strong>total</strong> central charge vanish.</p>
