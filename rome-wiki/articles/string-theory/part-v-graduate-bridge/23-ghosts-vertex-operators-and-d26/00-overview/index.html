    Gauge-fixing forces D=26D = 26D=26

    Summing the string's path integral over all worldsheet metrics massively overcounts, because diffeomorphisms and Weyl rescalings relate metrics that describe the same physics — like keeping every duplicate photo of the same person instead of one per person. Faddeev–Popov gauge-fixing keeps one metric per equivalence class, and a change of variables always carries a Jacobian; here that Jacobian is a determinant we rewrite as a path integral over two new fields, bbb and ccc. These are the ghosts: not particles, just the determinant written in field language. The redundancy we divided out then refuses to stay divided out. Its would-be anomaly is proportional to the total central charge, so the only way to kill it is ctotal=0c_{\text{total}}=0ctotal​=0. The DDD coordinates XμX^\muXμ supply c=Dc=Dc=D; the ghosts supply exactly −26-26−26; balancing them forces D=26D=26D=26.

    Why bother — we already have D=26D=26D=26

    In Light-cone gauge (Modules 14–16) we got D=26D=26D=26 by a brute-force route: fix a gauge that breaks manifest Lorentz invariance, quantize, then demand Lorentz invariance back. The dimension fell out as the price of consistency. It works, but it hides the thing you most want to keep visible — Lorentz symmetry.

    This module takes the covariant road. Keep all DDD coordinates XμX^\muXμ; fix the worldsheet gauge symmetries (diffeomorphisms + Weyl) instead of the spacetime ones. The price is different: gauge-fixing spits out the bcbcbc ghosts. Their negative central charge subtracts the unphysical gauge directions without choosing a spacetime frame, so Lorentz invariance stays manifest the whole way. Same answer, cleaner logic, and along the road we pick up the machinery — the state–operator map, vertex operators — that runs the interacting theory in Module 24.

    The logical spine

    Four links, each derived in a sublesson:

    1. Gauge-fixing → ghosts. The Polyakov integral sums over metrics gαβg_{\alpha\beta}gαβ​, but diffeos + Weyl are redundancies. Faddeev–Popov turns "divide by the gauge volume" into a determinant, and that determinant becomes a path integral over anticommuting bαβb_{\alpha\beta}bαβ​ (symmetric traceless, from the metric-variation multiplier) and cαc^\alphacα (a vector, from the diffeomorphism parameter). → 23.1 Faddeev-Popov ghosts

    2. Ghosts → cgh=−26c_{\text{gh}}=-26cgh​=−26. In conformal gauge the ghost action becomes a free holomorphic CFT. The index structure bzz,czb_{zz},c^zbzz​,cz forces weights (hb,hc)=(2,−1)(h_b,h_c)=(2,-1)(hb​,hc​)=(2,−1), and the resulting TTTTTT OPE has quartic pole −13/(z−w)4-13/(z-w)^4−13/(z−w)4, i.e. cgh=−26.c_{\text{gh}}=-26.cgh​=−26. It is negative because anticommuting integer-spin fields violate unitarity — exactly what a non-physical bookkeeping sector should do. → 23.2 The bc ghost CFT

    3. Anomaly cancellation → D=26D=26D=26. The Weyl (trace) anomaly of any 2d CFT is ⟨Tαα⟩=−c12 R,\langle T^\alpha{}_\alpha\rangle=-\frac{c}{12}\,R,⟨Tαα​⟩=−12c​R, with ccc the total central charge. Weyl symmetry is a gauge symmetry, and a gauge anomaly is fatal — it means the redundancy you divided out has come back to haunt you. So ctotal=0c_{\text{total}}=0ctotal​=0 is not optional. Each scalar XμX^\muXμ gives c=1c=1c=1, so c_{\text{total}}=\underbrace{D}_{\text{matter}}+\underbrace{(-26)}_{\text{ghosts}}=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D=26. \tag{1} → 23.3 Total central charge and D = 26

    4. The reusable machinery. In a CFT every local operator is a state (radial quantization maps the infinite past of the cylinder to the point z=0z=0z=0). Physical string states become vertex operators — integrated local operators whose Weyl-invariance condition, weight (1,1)(1,1)(1,1), is exactly the mass-shell condition. → 23.4 State-operator correspondence · 23.5 Vertex operators and physical-state conditions

    Critical central charge, not critical dimension

    Equation (1) never actually required DDD free scalars. The real consistency condition is just cmatter=26c_{\text{matter}}=26cmatter​=26. Any CFT with c=26c=26c=26 is a valid string background:

    • 262626 free scalars → flat 26d Minkowski space (the case where 262626 looks like a dimension).
    • D<26D<26D<26 flat scalars ×\times× an "internal" CFT with c=26−Dc=26-Dc=26−D → strings on R1,D−1×(internal)\mathbb{R}^{1,D-1}\times(\text{internal})R1,D−1×(internal). For D=4D=4D=4 observable spacetime dimensions you need an internal c=22c=22c=22 CFT. That internal sector — geometric or abstract — is what "extra hidden dimensions" really means.

    So "critical dimension" is a misnomer for a critical central charge. Only when the c=26c=26c=26 CFT happens to be free scalars does 262626 get to masquerade as a spacetime dimension. This reframing is the single most important idea in the module: the string cares about a number (ccc), and geometry is one of many ways to supply it.

    How this connects to the light-cone road

    Both roads compute the same anomaly in different gauges. The light cone fixes the Weyl anomaly away, so the inconsistency resurfaces as a Lorentz anomaly [M−I,M−J]≠0[M^{-I},M^{-J}]\neq 0[M−I,M−J]=0, cured only at D=26D=26D=26 with mass shift alc=−124(D−2)=−1a_{\text{lc}}=-\tfrac{1}{24}(D-2)=-1alc​=−241​(D−2)=−1. The covariant road keeps Lorentz manifest, so the same disease surfaces as the Weyl anomaly, cured by cmatter+cgh=D−26=0c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}=D-26=0cmatter​+cgh​=D−26=0. One disease, two symptoms, one cure. The term-by-term match — 242424 transverse oscillators of the light cone ↔\leftrightarrow↔ 262626 covariant coordinates minus the two gauge directions the ghosts remove — is worked out in 23.6 Two roads to D = 26.

    Open questions

    • BRST is the honest way to carve physical states out of the ghost-enlarged Hilbert space. This module uses the "poor man's" spectrum instead: ignore ghost excitations, keep the matter primary of weight 111 or (1,1)(1,1)(1,1). That suffices on-shell at tree level. Off-shell and at loops, ghost zero modes and BRST cohomology become load-bearing — deferred to Module 24, where amplitudes and worldsheet moduli appear.
    • The timelike X0X^0X0 contributes c=+1c=+1c=+1 but a negative-norm Fock space; the subtlety lives in the inner product, not the central charge. What ultimately removes those negative-norm states is again BRST.

    Exercise

    State, from memory, where each piece of the equation cmatter+cgh=0c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}=0cmatter​+cgh​=0 comes from, and use it to answer: what is the critical dimension of a hypothetical bosonic string whose ghost system had weights (hb,hc)=(3,−2)(h_b,h_c)=(3,-2)(hb​,hc​)=(3,−2) instead of (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1)?

    Solution

    The chain. Weyl symmetry is a gauge symmetry of the Polyakov string, so its anomaly must vanish. That anomaly is ⟨Tαα⟩=−c12R\langle T^\alpha{}_\alpha\rangle=-\tfrac{c}{12}R⟨Tαα​⟩=−12c​R, fatal unless the total central charge is zero. The matter is DDD free scalars at c=1c=1c=1 each, giving cmatter=Dc_{\text{matter}}=Dcmatter​=D. The FP ghosts — which replaced a symmetric-traceless tensor and a vector, hence carry weights (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) — form a bcbcbc CFT whose TTTTTT OPE has quartic pole −13/(z−w)4-13/(z-w)^4−13/(z−w)4, so cgh=2×(−13)=−26c_{\text{gh}}=2\times(-13)=-26cgh​=2×(−13)=−26. Cancellation: D−26=0⇒D=26D-26=0\Rightarrow D=26D−26=0⇒D=26.

    The extension. A first-order bcbcbc system of weight λ\lambdaλ (here bbb has weight λ\lambdaλ, ccc has weight 1−λ1-\lambda1−λ) has central charge cbc=1−3(2λ−1)2.c_{bc}=1-3(2\lambda-1)^2.cbc​=1−3(2λ−1)2. Check: λ=2\lambda=2λ=2 gives 1−3(3)2=1−27=−26  ✓1-3(3)^2=1-27=-26\;\checkmark1−3(3)2=1−27=−26✓. For the hypothetical (hb,hc)=(3,−2)(h_b,h_c)=(3,-2)(hb​,hc​)=(3,−2), i.e. λ=3\lambda=3λ=3: cbc=1−3(2⋅3−1)2=1−3⋅25=−74.c_{bc}=1-3(2\cdot3-1)^2=1-3\cdot25=-74.cbc​=1−3(2⋅3−1)2=1−3⋅25=−74. Anomaly cancellation would then require cmatter=74c_{\text{matter}}=74cmatter​=74, so a free-scalar background would need D=74\boxed{D=74}D=74​. This is exactly the module's central lesson: the critical dimension is dictated entirely by the ghost central charge, which is dictated by the weights, which are dictated by the tensor structure of the gauge symmetry being fixed. Change the gauge structure, change the number.
