1c:T189e5,<h2 id="the-consistency-meter">The consistency meter</h2>
<p>The Polyakov theory has a redundancy — Weyl rescaling of the worldsheet metric — that we divided out. Quantum mechanically that division leaves a residue, the Weyl anomaly, and its size is a single number: the <strong>total central charge</strong>. If that number is nonzero, the redundancy we thought we removed is still secretly present and the theory is broken. Making it vanish costs exactly one thing — the spacetime dimension must be <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>26</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">26</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">26</span></span></span></span>.</p>
<p>The total central charge is the theory's consistency meter. Every worldsheet field shifts the reading: matter fields push it positive, the gauge-fixing ghosts push it negative, and the theory is consistent only when the reading is zero. With <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>D</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">D</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">D</span></span></span></span> free scalars contributing <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>+</mo><mi>D</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">+D</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">+</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">D</span></span></span></span> and the ghosts contributing <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo><mn>26</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-26</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7278em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">−</span><span class="mord">26</span></span></span></span>, zero means <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>D</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>26</mn></mrow><annotation <h2 id="why-the-total-c-must-be-zero">Why the total <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">c</span></span></span></span> must be zero</h2>
<p>The Polyakov string has two worldsheet gauge symmetries: diffeomorphisms and Weyl rescalings <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>g</mi><mrow><mi>α</mi><mi>β</mi></mrow></msub><mo>→</mo><msup><mi>e</mi><mrow><mn>2</mn><mi>ω</mi></mrow></msup><msub><mi>g</mi><mrow><mi>α</mi><mi>β</mi></mrow></msub></mrow><annotation A gauge symmetry is a <strong>redundancy of description</strong>, not a physical symmetry — configurations related by it are literally the same state, and we divided by the gauge volume to avoid overcounting (see <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-v-graduate-bridge/23-ghosts-vertex-operators-and-d26/faddeev-popov-ghosts/" class="wiki-link">Faddeev-Popov ghosts</a>).</p>
<p>Global and gauge anomalies differ sharply. An anomaly in a <em>global</em> symmetry is merely interesting — the symmetry is broken by quantum effects, and that is a physical fact about the theory. An anomaly in a <em>gauge</em> symmetry is <strong>fatal</strong>: the states we tried to remove as redundant refuse to decouple, negative-norm ghosts leak into physical amplitudes, and the theory has no consistent Hilbert space. A gauge anomaly cannot be lived with the way a global one can; it must be cancelled.</p>
<p>The Weyl symmetry is anomalous. On a curved worldsheet the trace of the stress tensor — which classically vanishes, that vanishing <em>being</em> Weyl invariance — acquires a quantum piece:

<h2 id="forcing-d--26">Forcing D = 26</h2>
