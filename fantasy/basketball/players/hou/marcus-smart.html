Marcus Smart

Marcus Smart improves Houston's real rotation more than its fantasy rotation; his steals and assists remain useful, but too many guards stand between him and standard-league volume.

Role and evidence

Houston signed Smart on July 10 to a reported two-year, $13 million deal with a second-year player option. He averaged 9.3 points, 3.0 assists, 2.8 rebounds, 1.40 steals, and 1.6 threes in 28.5 minutes over 62 games for the Lakers. His stronger 10-game playoff line included 12.9 points and 2.40 steals, evidence that the ceiling is not gone.

The Rockets do not need to reproduce that workload. VanVleet, Thompson, and Sheppard are ahead in the creation hierarchy; Bogdanović and Thornton also need guard minutes. Smart's clearest nightly value is changing defensive matchups and stabilizing lineups, contributions that fantasy scoring captures incompletely.

Range, confidence, and reversals

  • Ceiling: VanVleet is limited and Smart reaches 23-26 minutes, returning 8/3/3 with 1.3 steals and late-round value.
  • Floor: 8-12 specialist minutes, sporadic DNPs, and damaging FG% whenever shot volume rises.
  • Confidence: roster high; minutes low; steal rate medium-high; efficiency medium-low.
  • Draft trigger: he becomes a deep-league buy if Houston consistently uses him as the second point guard rather than as a matchup wing.

Durability: Only three 70+ game seasons in twelve years. The Boston years were dotted with hand, oblique and ankle absences; 2023-24 collapsed to 20 games (finger surgery), and 2024-25 to 34 across Memphis and Washington. The 62 games of 2025-26 were his most since 2021-22 — a genuine health rebound, but at 32 the multi-year base rate still says plan for 55-65.

Trajectory: The record shows a decade-long elite defensive guard — 2022 DPOY, three All-Defensive First Teams, career 1.6 steals — whose offense never rose above league-average efficiency and whose peak assist years (5.7-6.3 in 2020-23) depended on Boston handing him the offense. The two post-Boston years cratered; the Lakers season re-established him as a viable 28-minute rotation guard with intact steal rate. In 2026-27 he competes for Houston's backup guard minutes behind Fred VanVleet, Amen Thompson and Reed Sheppard. He becomes fantasy-relevant on one trigger: a VanVleet absence or workload cap that makes Smart the second point guard — at 23+ minutes his steals-plus-assists floor returns immediately, as the 2025-26 line shows.
