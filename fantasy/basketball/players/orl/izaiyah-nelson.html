College and transaction baseline

Orlando acquired pick 51 and signed Nelson to a two-way contract. The official release lists 34 starts at South Florida with 15.9 points, 9.6 rebounds, 1.2 assists, 1.65 steals, and 1.41 blocks in 27.2 minutes on .561 shooting. He earned AAC Player of the Year, Defensive Player of the Year, and Newcomer of the Year honors.

That combination of finishing, rebounding, steals, and blocks is fantasy-relevant. The unanswered questions are NBA size translation, shooting range, FT, and decision-making.

Injury

Imaging on July 12 revealed a left ankle fracture. Orlando announced surgery and an expected return to basketball activities in three to four months. That timetable reaches roughly October or November for activity, after which conditioning, contact work, and assignment decisions may still delay games.

The projection therefore assumes no opening rotation and only eight conditional NBA appearances. It should move down if rehabilitation slips and up only after documented clearance.

Role and pathway

Even when healthy, Nelson begins behind Carter, Vucevic, Bitadze, Isaac, and two-way teammate Castleton at center, while Banchero/Penda/da Silva occupy forward minutes. His best 2026-27 developmental outcome may be sustained G League work after recovery rather than NBA cameos.

Longer term, the center room can change quickly: Vucevic is on a one-year deal and the standard roster is expensive. Nelson's dynasty appeal is therefore more about 2027-28 pathways than opening night.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons, three at Arkansas State and a transfer year at South Florida. As a freshman in 2022-23 he started 18 of 33 games, averaging 4.6 points and 5.0 rebounds in 17.5 minutes on .525 shooting, and led the team with 44 blocks. He grew into 9.8 points and 7.6 rebounds as a sophomore, then 10.6 points, 8.9 rebounds, 0.7 steals and 1.3 blocks as a junior, earning third-team All-Sun Belt. Transferring up to South Florida for 2025-26 produced the breakout: 34 starts with 15.9 points, 9.6 rebounds, 1.2 assists, 1.65 steals and 1.41 blocks in 27.2 minutes on .561 shooting, and a sweep of American Conference Player of the Year, Defensive Player of the Year and Newcomer of the Year. Wikipedia lists that season slightly differently at 15.8 points and 9.8 rebounds; the figures above are Orlando's official release. Draft: 2026, second round, 51st overall, Washington; traded to Orlando and signed to a two-way contract with the Osceola Magic.

Nelson has never appeared in an NBA game. His most recent season is the 2025-26 South Florida line above.

Durability: Imaging on July 12, 2026 revealed a left ankle fracture. Orlando announced surgery and a projected three-to-four-month return to basketball activities — roughly October or November — which is a return to activity, not a return to games. Conditioning, contact clearance and assignment decisions sit after that. No prior injury history located across four college seasons.

Trajectory: The production is real and the shape is fantasy-friendly: a rebounder who finished at .561 while producing both steals and blocks, and who swept his conference's top individual awards in his one season at the higher level. The open questions are the ones that always attach to a 51st pick — whether the size holds against NBA centers, whether there is any shooting range, and whether the free-throw line is playable. All of that is now deferred by the ankle. Even healthy he sits behind Wendell Carter, Vucevic, Bitadze, Isaac and fellow two-way Castleton, so his best 2026-27 outcome is sustained Osceola work after clearance rather than NBA cameos. The dynasty case is 2027-28: Vucevic is on a one-year deal and Orlando's standard roster is expensive, so the center room can turn over quickly.
