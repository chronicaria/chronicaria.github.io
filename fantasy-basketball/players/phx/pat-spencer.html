Pat Spencer

Pat Spencer moves from a meaningful Golden State reserve role to a Phoenix two-way, making him a capable emergency playmaker whose prior assists should not be projected into a crowded Suns guard room.

Role and evidence

Phoenix signed Spencer to a two-way contract on July 3, and the current roster lists him alongside Koby Brea and CJ Huntley in that bucket. Sources: RealGM transaction history, accessed 2026-07-18, RealGM current roster, accessed 2026-07-18. Confidence: high.

Spencer's 2025-26 Golden State line was 7.2 points, 2.4 rebounds, 3.5 assists, and 0.7 steals in 18.6 minutes across 66 games, with .427 FG and .772 FT. It demonstrates NBA competence but came in a different system and under a standard rotation context. Source: Hashtag Basketball, updated 2026-07-02. Confidence: medium-high on history, low on Phoenix translation.

Phoenix has Devin Booker, Jalen Green, Collin Gillespie, Jordan Goodwin, and standard-contract reserve Jamaree Bouyea ahead of him. Two-way availability rules and roster activations further reduce the base.

Category shape

  • Wins: Assists and low-to-moderate turnovers when used as a true point guard.
  • Neutral: FT% and steals can be playable.
  • Costs: FG%, threes, rebounds, blocks, and minutes.
  • Build fit: Deep points/assists formats only after multiple guard absences.

Points and dynasty outlook

Hashtag ranked Spencer No. 304 in dynasty, while its keeper crowd still showed No. 176 on July 18. That crowd number likely reflects his prior Golden State role and is stale relative to the new two-way context. Sources: Hashtag dynasty, updated 2026-07-02, Hashtag keeper, updated 2026-07-18. Confidence: low for the crowd signal.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: Mostly G League or inactive nights behind five standard guards.
  • Base: 18 NBA appearances at seven minutes, distributing second-unit possessions.
  • Ceiling: A guard injury cluster creates 20 minutes, four-plus assists, and temporary deep points-league value.

Draft verdict

Spencer is better than his two-way label implies but worse positioned than his 2025-26 box score suggests. Treat him as a transaction-wire name: a standard conversion or a confirmed backup-point role would be the signal to reassess.

Career record

Pre-NBA: The most unusual résumé in the league: NCAA lacrosse superstar at Loyola Maryland (2016-19, Tewaaraton Award winner, DI-record 231 career assists), then one grad basketball season at Northwestern (2019-20): 10.4 PTS / 4.1 REB / 3.9 AST in 31 games. Pro path: Hamburg Towers (Germany, 2021: 7.0 PPG in 8 games), Capital City Go-Go (2021-22: 6.0 PPG in 26 G League games), Santa Cruz (2022-23). Draft: undrafted (2020).

Durability: No injury flags in the public record; the GP climb (6 → 39 → 66) tracks contract status, not health — two-way in 2023-24, converted to standard March 2025, re-signed two-way September 2025, converted again February 2026.

Trajectory: The record shows a late-starting, twice-converted guard whose role expanded every season and who delivered genuine backup-point production (3.5 assists in 18.6 minutes) at 29. Phoenix nevertheless signed him to a two-way in July 2026, resetting him behind five standard-contract guards; in 2026-27 he competes with Bouyea for the emergency ball-handler call-up. He becomes fantasy-relevant only through a third contract conversion or a Suns guard injury cluster — his Golden State season proves the assist rate is real when minutes exist, which is exactly why he is a transaction-wire watch rather than a draftable player.
