Why this shape

  • Miami officially exercised Larsson's option after he started 54 games, confirming a real place in the core.
  • Tim Hardaway Jr. supplies more shooting; Larsson supplies more defense, driving and passing. The model starts Hardaway but gives Larsson only one fewer minute because closing groups can reverse that order.
  • Giannis and Bam reduce open driving lanes, so Larsson's 32.3% three-point baseline must improve for the role to grow substantially.
  • Herro/Jaquez/Jakučionis departures leave secondary handling available, partly offsetting the spacing concern.

Format outlook

  • Nine-category: late-round watchlist in 14-team leagues; waiver player in most 12-team formats until the threes or stocks rise.
  • LeBron fork: James was unsigned on 2026-07-18 with Miami a reported finalist (ESPN). A signing pulls Larsson from 24 MPG toward 18 and off the watchlist outside 14-team formats. His defensive value gives him a better claim on surviving minutes than Hardaway, but not on the 24 modeled here.
  • Points: useful only if the role returns to 27–30 minutes and assists approach four.
  • Dynasty: a reasonable deep stash at age 25, especially for managers betting on a starter outcome; not a high-upside star archetype.
  • Real-life versus fantasy: his defense, connective passing and low-mistake play can earn more NBA trust than fantasy rank.

Range and watchpoints

  • Ceiling: wins the starting job, plays 29 MPG and reaches about 13/4/4 with 1.2 threes and one steal.
  • Median: first wing off the bench and frequent closer, but a low-ceiling fantasy line.
  • Floor: 18–20 minutes if Hardaway starts, Wiggins stays healthy and Conwell/Fontecchio win the shooting minutes.
  • Market sentiment: a high-engagement r/heat thread strongly favored Larsson as the starter; that is fan preference, not coaching evidence.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Swedish, with a professional record before college. He grew up in Stockholm playing for Skuru IK, moved to BC Luleå at 16, and spent two seasons in the Swedish Basketball League; in his debut season he averaged 7.6 points, 2.8 rebounds, 1.9 assists and 0.8 steals and was named the league's star of the year. He then played four US college seasons. Utah (2020-21): 25 games with 18 starts, 26.6 minutes, 8.2 points, 3.2 rebounds, 2.8 assists on .467/.463/.883 — the .463 from three on a real freshman role is the outlier number in his whole shooting history. Arizona (2021-22 through 2023-24): 7.2 points, 3.4 rebounds, 1.8 assists across 37 games as Pac-12 Sixth Man of the Year; 9.9, 4.3 and 3.1 across 35 games with 18 starts; then 12.8, 4.1 and 3.7 across 36 starts as a senior, earning second-team All-Pac-12 and second-team Academic All-American. He has represented Sweden at youth and senior level and averaged 18.8 points at EuroBasket 2025 as Sweden reached the round of 16. Draft: 2024, second round, 44th overall, Houston — traded to Miami on draft night.

Three-point accuracy: .337 in 2024-25, .323 in 2025-26. He made eight starts as a rookie and 54 in 2025-26, and appeared in four 2025 playoff games at 5.0 points. The 2025-26 line in the snapshot table above matches this record exactly.

Fantasy history: 2024-25: unrostered in standard formats; 14.2 minutes and no anchor category put him well outside the top 250. 2025-26: a real but modest jump — a 26.4-minute starter with 11.4 points, 3.4 assists and .496 shooting is roughly a late-round or deep-league profile rather than a standard-league one. A verified season-end 9-cat rank was not locatable for either season and is not worth estimating. The category problem is structural: he clears the bar in field-goal percentage and turnovers, is neutral in assists and rebounds for a wing, and provides almost nothing in threes (0.9), steals (0.7) or blocks (0.2). Points leagues rate him slightly better than categories because his efficiency and assist volume accumulate cleanly and he is not penalized for lacking a defensive-stat anchor, but the gap is small — he is a low-volume connector in both formats.

Durability: GP by season — 55 of 82 as a rookie (mostly rotation decisions on a deep roster rather than injury), 70 of 82 in 2025-26. The 2025-26 misses were two identifiable issues: an ankle injury sustained against Orlando on 2025-12-09 that cost him roughly five games across more than two weeks before he returned against Atlanta, and a left lower-leg contusion late in the year that cost him the regular-season finale and left him questionable for the play-in against Charlotte. Neither is a structural concern. Twelve missed games in a first full starting season, from two discrete minor lower-leg injuries, is a clean availability record.

Trajectory: The leap from 2024-25 to 2025-26 is the whole story, and it was almost entirely opportunity rather than skill development: minutes went from 14.2 to 26.4, an 86 percent increase, and scoring went from 4.6 to 11.4, a 148 percent increase — so there was some genuine per-minute gain, mostly from efficiency (.438 to .496 from the field) and from a much larger playmaking role (1.2 assists to 3.4). The free-throw improvement from .672 to .797 is real and matches his college baseline, which suggests the rookie number was noise. What did not improve is the thing that matters most for his ceiling: three-point accuracy fell from .337 to .323 on higher volume, and that is now two NBA seasons of below-average shooting against one anomalous .463 season at Utah five years ago. He turns 26 in February 2027, so he is at the age where the current shape is close to the finished product rather than a waypoint. That argues the 2026-27 base is a repeat of 2025-26 with a modest role bump from Miami's guard departures, not another leap. For fantasy the ceiling is capped by the same thing that caps the projection card at 24 minutes: a player who does five things adequately and nothing exceptionally is more valuable to an NBA coach than to a category-league roster.
