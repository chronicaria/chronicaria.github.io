Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: rank 115-165; blocks and threes play, while poor FG% for a center and low rebounds are real costs.
  • Points: matchup streamer unless the league heavily rewards blocks.
  • Dynasty: short-horizon contender piece only. Hashtag ranked him 637th in July. Hashtag

Role and scenarios

  • Low: age-related mobility decline or injury pushes him below 18 minutes and outside the rotation by spring.
  • Base: starts, plays 22 minutes and anchors drop coverage while Isaiah Jackson handles the high-energy share.
  • High: Niederhauser's return lags and Lopez reaches 25 minutes with 1.5-plus blocks.

LA picked up the option because stretch-center competence has real lineup value. It did not recreate Zubac's defensive rebounding, and Lopez should not be projected for a vintage Milwaukee workload.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Stanford, two seasons. As a freshman in 2006-07 he went 12.6 points, 6.0 rebounds, 1.7 blocks on .496 shooting in 25.2 minutes, making the All-Pac-10 Freshman team. As a sophomore in 2007-08 he was 19.3 points, 8.2 rebounds, 2.1 blocks on .468/.789 in 30.8 minutes across 27 games, earning first-team All-Pac-10 and third-team All-American honors from the AP and NABC. He attempted essentially no college threes — 0-for-something as a sophomore, .200 as a freshman — which makes the later stretch-five conversion one of the more complete skill reinventions of the era. A McDonald's All-American in 2006. Draft: 2008, first round, 10th overall, New Jersey Nets.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not verifiable from the sources available here, so specific finishes are not asserted. The career splits into three distinct fantasy identities. Through 2016-17 he was a high-volume scoring center whose value came from points, field-goal percentage and blocks with zero threes — a top-40-type category asset in his best Brooklyn years. From 2018-19 through 2024-25 he was the archetypal blocks-plus-threes big, a combination scarce enough that he held mid-round category value on modest scoring; 2022-23 (15.9 points, 2.5 blocks, 1.7 threes, .531) was the peak of that version. 2025-26 was the first season the formula stopped working: 1.2 blocks and .428 shooting from a center are actively negative in two categories. Points-league value has always trailed the category value badly, because the profile leans on blocks and threes rather than raw volume, and 2025-26 left him a matchup streamer.

Durability: 82, 82, 82, 5, 74, 17, 72, 73, 75, 74, 81, 68, 70, 13, 78, 79, 80, 75. Three seasons account for nearly all of the missed time. In 2011-12 he broke his right foot in the preseason, missed the first 32 games, then sprained his right ankle five games after returning and was shut down. In 2013-14 a foot injury on December 19 ended his season after 17 games. In 2021-22 he had back surgery in December following the season opener and did not return until March 14, 2022. Outside those three, he has been remarkably available for a seven-footer — twelve seasons of 68 or more games, including 75 last year at 37. The foot problems are more than a decade old and have not recurred.

Trajectory: Two full career reinventions sit inside this table. The first was 2016-17, when a player who had attempted essentially zero threes in eight NBA seasons and two college seasons started making 1.8 a game — that single change extended his career by roughly a decade. The second was Milwaukee's drop-coverage system from 2018-19 onward, which converted him into a two-plus-blocks rim protector while the shooting kept the floor open. The 2025-26 line is the first evidence that the reinventions have run out: minutes fell ten per game from 31.8 to 21.8, blocks halved from 1.9 to 1.2, and field-goal percentage collapsed from .509 to .428, which is the worst mark of his career by a wide margin. Per-36 the decline is real rather than a minutes artifact — roughly 14.0 points and 2.0 blocks per 36 versus 14.7 and 2.2 the prior year, with the efficiency drop unexplained by role. At 38 with a shrinking rim-protection signal and no rebounding base to fall back on, the base case for 2026-27 is continued erosion, and the reasonable projection is that last season's rates are the ceiling rather than a floor to bounce off.
