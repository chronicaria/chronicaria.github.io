What to eat by city

Milan

Anchor dinner · 22 Dec

Order: risotto alla milanese, ossobuco, cotoletta, mondeghili, winter cassoeula, panettone, Campari drinks, Franciacorta, or a Negroni sbagliato.

Best areas: Brera for convenience, Porta Romana for serious restaurants with less visitor pressure, Navigli/Darsena for a lively aperitivo, and Isola/Porta Venezia for bars.

Plan a proper old-school Milanese dinner on 22 December. Aperitivo can replace dinner only when the food offering is substantial.

Lake Como

Anchor lunch · 23 Dec

Order: risotto con pesce persico, lake fish if the source is clear, polenta, pizzoccheri or other Lombard mountain dishes, and a local white wine.

Book

Reserve Seta in Bellagio at 12:30 on 23 December. Its current menu lists perch risotto at €25; budget €40–55 per person for a main, water, and shared wine. Nando is the €25–40 practical fallback. Both require written Christmas-week confirmation in late November.

  • Seta dining
  • Seta menu
  • Nando reservation request

The long indoor lunch is the day’s leisure block, not something to compress for another closed villa. See the Lake Como winter day.

Venice

Holiday meals · 24–25 Dec

Order: baccalà mantecato, sarde in saor, polpette, mozzarella in carrozza, bigoli in salsa, seppie in nero, fritto misto, lagoon seafood, an ombra of wine, or a Select spritz.

Best areas: Cannaregio’s Misericordia/Ormesini, Dorsoduro around San Trovaso, and San Polo behind Rialto. Avoid making San Marco frontage the default.

Good current leads to reconfirm for the exact dates:

  • Cantine del Vino già Schiavi and Osteria al Squero for a Dorsoduro cicchetti pair.
  • Vino Vero for natural wine and creative cicchetti in Cannaregio.
  • Osteria al Cicheto for a tiny reserved dinner near Santa Lucia.
  • Nevodi for a convenient Castello dinner.
  • Antiche Carampane for the seafood splurge.

Florence

Arrival lunch · 26 Dec

Order: ribollita, lampredotto, schiacciata, crostini neri, bistecca alla fiorentina, cantucci with vin santo, and Chianti Classico.

Best areas: Sant’Ambrogio for a local market lunch; Oltrarno, Santo Spirito, and San Frediano for wine bars and dinner; Mercato Centrale for travel-day convenience.

Current leads:

  • Le Volpi e l’Uva for wine and an Oltrarno aperitivo.
  • Il Santo Bevitore for a destination dinner.
  • Mercato Centrale for an easy arrival lunch, subject to its hours on 26 December.

For bistecca, confirm the price per kilogram, likely cut size, and that everyone accepts the traditional rare preparation. Share one steak; do not order one per person.

Rome

Anchor dinner · 29 Dec

Order: carbonara, cacio e pepe, amatriciana, gricia, supplì, pizza al taglio, puntarelle, artichokes, saltimbocca, coda alla vaccinara, Frascati, or Cesanese.

Best areas: Testaccio for Roman cooking, Monti after the Colosseum, selected Trastevere streets for atmosphere, and Ostiense/Garbatella for a less visitor-heavy evening.

Current leads:

  • Armando al Pantheon for the 29 December anchor dinner if its holiday week is confirmed.
  • Flavio al Velavevodetto in Testaccio for classic pasta/secondi.
  • Bonci Pizzarium near the Vatican for a flexible pizza lunch.

Rome’s New Year’s Eve plan now uses the official 18:00 Swan Lake at Teatro Costanzi followed by a reserved late dinner. Wait for the ballet runtime, then secure a table around 21:30 near Repubblica/Monti or at the hotel, ideally through midnight. There is no safe assumption of a normal walk-in meal that night.

Holiday food plan

24 December, Venice

  • Book a seafood-centered dinner within walking distance of the lodging.
  • Confirm the menu and price in writing by early November.
  • Buy water, breakfast food, fruit, and emergency sandwiches before late afternoon.

25 December, Venice

  • Reserve a long lunch, ideally at the hotel or a restaurant with a written holiday confirmation.
  • Keep dinner light and prearranged; do not rely on grocery shopping.

31 December, Rome

  • Use lunch and a 16:30 snack to avoid rushing dinner before the 18:00 opera.
  • Reserve a post-performance table only after the runtime is known, or with a restaurant willing to hold a flexible arrival window.
  • Ask whether wine, water, service, midnight toast, and table duration are included.
  • Target €60–100 for a strong late dinner. Skip the official €580-per-person opera/hotel dinner bundle; it is unnecessary for a festive evening.

1 January, Rome

  • Reserve lunch and confirm dinner. Treat open cafés as welcome extras.

Drinking at age 20

Everyone may legally buy and be served alcohol; Italian law prohibits sale and service to people under 18. Carry accepted physical photo ID, and remember that a venue may apply a stricter entry policy.

  • Venice currently prohibits public-street alcohol consumption from 20:00–08:00 outside licensed venues and terraces. Finish the drink at the bacaro rather than carrying it to the next stop.
  • Rome and other cities may publish stricter glass and container rules for New Year’s Eve only days before the event.
  • Drivers under 21 in Italy must maintain 0.0 BAC. The trip should remain car-free.
  • Watch drinks being made, leave together, share live locations, and never leave an intoxicated friend alone.

Bills and etiquette

  • Coperto is a disclosed per-person cover charge in much of Italy; servizio is a service charge. Check the menu.
  • Tipping is optional. Round up, leave €2–5, or roughly 5–10% for genuinely excellent service; do not add another large tip when service is included.
  • Free tap water is not guaranteed. Order acqua naturale or frizzante.
  • Coffee al banco is usually cheaper than sitting at a landmark café.
  • No one must order antipasto, primo, secondo, and dessert. Shared starters plus one main each are normal.
  • Ask for the check: “Il conto, per favore.”
  • Restaurants may resist splitting across three cards. One person can pay and settle through Splitwise.

Entry requirements

This section assumes U.S. citizens; anyone with another passport must check separately.

  • U.S. tourists may enter Schengen visa-free for fewer than 90 days in a rolling 180-day period.
  • Passport must have been issued within the previous 10 years, remain valid at least three months after the Schengen departure, and have at least two blank pages. For a 2 January 2027 departure, the legal expiry threshold is after 2 April; after 2 July follows the U.S. six-month safety recommendation.
  • The EU Entry/Exit System became fully operational 10 April 2026. First entry may include passport data, face image, and fingerprints; there is no separate application or fee.
ETIAS · moving target

As of 21 July, applications were not open and the exact launch date had not been announced, but launch was expected in the final quarter of 2026. Once live, the official fee is €20 for ages 18–70. Check only the official EU ETIAS site in September and again in November; do not pay an intermediary now.

Keep offline copies of flight, lodging, insurance, and return-ticket details. Hotels and licensed rentals will inspect passports for registration.

Insurance and health

Travel insurance is not legally required for U.S. tourists, but winter weather plus multiple prepaid bookings make it sensible. Compare policies for:

  • at least $100,000 emergency medical;
  • at least $250,000 evacuation/repatriation;
  • trip cancellation/interruption and missed connection;
  • delay coverage beginning after 6–12 hours;
  • baggage delay/loss and 24-hour assistance.

Buy soon after the first nonrefundable deposit if a pre-existing-condition waiver or CFAR option matters. Credit-card trip protection often does not provide serious overseas medical coverage.

Carry prescription medicine in original labeled containers, its generic name, and several extra days’ supply.

Phones and money

Phone

  • Confirm every phone is unlocked.
  • Install an eSIM at home; 10GB each is plenty for maps, messages, and bookings with hotel Wi-Fi.
  • Keep the U.S. line only for 2FA if the carrier confirms roaming-text terms.
  • Download offline Google/Apple maps, Italian language data, tickets, and hotel addresses.
  • WhatsApp keeps the U.S. number while using a data eSIM.

Money

  • Bring two no-foreign-transaction-fee cards stored separately and one low-fee debit card.
  • Carry €50–100 each initially, or a shared €100–150 cash kitty.
  • Use bank ATMs marked Bancomat; decline currency conversion and choose euros.
  • Keep a physical card even if using a phone wallet.

Safety

The everyday risk is petty theft around stations, crowded transport, landmarks, bars, and cafés.

  • Wear a zipped crossbody toward the front; no phone or wallet in an outer pocket.
  • One person watches bags while others board.
  • Ignore unsolicited ticket-machine help, petitions, bracelets, and distraction games.
  • Use official taxi ranks or established apps; ignore solicitors inside stations and airports.
  • Keep passports, medicine, cash, and electronics on the body or in the personal item — not an unattended train rack.
  • Save passport scans and reservation copies in a secure shared folder.

The U.S. State Department currently rates Italy Level 2 and specifically warns about pickpocketing on public transport and at major tourist sites.

Winter packing list

Expected planning normals range from roughly 34–47°F in Milan and Venice to 40–58°F in Rome and Naples. Dampness and wind can feel colder, and daylight is only about nine hours.

  • Waterproof hooded shell
  • Packable insulated jacket or wool coat
  • Two warm midlayers and long-sleeve base layers
  • Scarf, gloves, and beanie
  • Waterproof, broken-in walking shoes with traction
  • Warm socks, compact umbrella, and quick-dry clothes
  • One overhead carry-on no larger than 22 × 14 × 9 inches, plus a small personal item
  • Type C/F/L-compatible adapter; Italy is about 230V/50Hz
  • AirTag, small day bag, reusable bottle, and portable battery
Two rules

Do laundry in Florence. Do not use a simple plug adapter with a U.S.-only 120V hair tool.

For Venice, check the official tide forecast starting 72 hours before arrival. Waterproof ankle shoes are usually more useful than hauling oversized rain boots.
