One equation, two readings.</p>
<p>This module takes that single template and asks two questions physics forces on you, which applied math usually lets you skip:</p>
<ol>
<li>
<p><strong>Where did the balance law come from?</strong> Answer: a continuous <em>symmetry</em>. Noether's theorem is a machine — feed it a symmetry, out comes a current that is automatically divergence-free on-shell. Translation symmetry feeds it, out comes the stress-energy tensor: the balance sheet for energy and momentum themselves.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Which symbols in my equations are real, and which are just my choice of labels?</strong> Some equations do not evolve anything; they <em>forbid</em> illegal initial data (<strong>constraints</strong>). And some of your variables were never physical to begin with — they are surplus notation, and picking convenient values for them is <strong>gauge fixing.</strong></p>
</li>
</ol>
<p>One claim ties the module together: <strong>symmetries and redundancies are the same machine run in opposite directions.</strong> A symmetry moves you between genuinely <em>different</em> physical states and hands you a conserved charge. A redundancy moves you between <em>identical</em> descriptions of <em>one</em> state and hands you a constraint. Same invariance principle; the charge and the constraint are two faces of it.</p>
<p>The three objects, stated once each:</p>
<ul>
<li>A conserved current is a local balance sheet; Noether's theorem prints one for every continuous symmetry, and translation symmetry prints the stress-energy tensor.</li>
<li>A constraint is not a law of motion but a filter that rejects illegal states, and it appears precisely when your description carries extra, physically meaningless labels.</li>
<li>Gauge fixing spends those meaningless labels on convenience; done right it changes nothing physical, and checking that "nothing physical changed" survives quantization is what later forces <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>D</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>26</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">D=26</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">D</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">26</span></span></span></span>.</li>
</ul>

<p>So this module is the last structural layer before quantization: it names what flows, what is forbidden, and what was only a coordinate choice.</p>
<h2 id="the-chain-of-ideas">The chain of ideas</h2>
