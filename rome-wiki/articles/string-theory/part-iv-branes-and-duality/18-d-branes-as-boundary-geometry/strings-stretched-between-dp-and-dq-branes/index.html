    Strings stretched between Dp- and Dq-branes

    A string running between two branes of different dimension has directions that are Neumann at one end and Dirichlet at the other; these ND directions carry half-integer modes, which shift the ground-state energy by +\tfrac{1}{16} per direction and remove the massless gauge boson.

    One end of the string lives on the big brane (Dppp) and slides freely; the other is pinned to the small brane (Dqqq). A direction that runs along the big brane but across the small one leaves that string free at one end and frozen at the other. A string clamped on one side and loose on the other rings at half-integer frequencies — an organ pipe open at one end, whose lowest note is a quarter wavelength rather than a half. Half-integer instead of integer modes is the entire content of this note; the shifted vacuum energy and the missing photon both follow from it.

    Builds on Boundary conditions as geometry and Strings between parallel branes.

    Three kinds of direction, not two

    For one brane (or two parallel ones of the same dimension) every direction is either tangent (Neumann, free endpoint) or normal (Dirichlet, pinned endpoint). Put down a Dppp- and a Dqqq-brane with q<pq<pq<p, parallel and nested, and a third possibility opens up: a direction can be tangent to the big brane but normal to the small one. So directions split into three classes.

    class along Dppp? along Dqqq? b.c. for a Dp ⁣→ ⁣p\!\to\!p→Dqqq string count
    common tangential yes yes NN (free–free) q+1q+1q+1
    mixed yes no ND (free–pinned) p−qp-qp−q
    common normal no no DD (pinned–pinned) d−pd-pd−p

    The counts sum to d+1d+1d+1, one per spacetime direction. For a concrete case, take a D2 filling the (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) plane and a D1 lying along xxx. Then xxx is common tangential (NN), zzz is common normal (DD), and yyy is the mixed one — along the D2 sheet but normal to the D1 line, hence ND.

    The light-cone gauge fixes X±X^\pmX± using two of the common tangential (NN) directions, leaving three index types:

    \underbrace{X^+,X^-,\{X^i\}}_{\text{NN}}\qquad \underbrace{\{X^r\}}_{\text{ND}}\qquad\underbrace{\{X^a\}}_{\text{DD}}, \qquad i=2,\dots,q,\;\; r=q{+}1,\dots,p,\;\; a=p{+}1,\dots,d. \tag{1}

    Watch the upper limit qqq on the transverse NN index iii: only directions common to both branes are NN. The directions q+1,…,pq{+}1,\dots,pq+1,…,p are precisely the mixed ones. If you ever forget which class a direction is in, ask the endpoint question: can this endpoint move in this direction? Free means Neumann, pinned means Dirichlet — one answer per end, per direction.

    The ND coordinate: free at σ=0\sigma=0σ=0, pinned at σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π

    Orient the string from the Dppp (at σ=0\sigma=0σ=0) to the Dqqq (at σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π). A mixed coordinate XrX^rXr is Neumann at σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 (its endpoint slides along the Dppp) and Dirichlet at σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π (its endpoint is nailed to the Dqqq's position xˉ2r\bar x_2^rxˉ2r​):

    \frac{\partial X^r}{\partial\sigma}(\tau,\sigma)\Big|_{\sigma=0} = 0, \qquad X^r(\tau,\sigma)\big|_{\sigma=\pi} = \bar x_2^r. \tag{2}

    Solve the wave equation as a left- plus right-mover, Xr=12(fr(τ+σ)+gr(τ−σ))X^r=\tfrac12(f^r(\tau+\sigma)+g^r(\tau-\sigma))Xr=21​(fr(τ+σ)+gr(τ−σ)). The σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 Neumann condition sets gr′=fr′g^r{}'=f^r{}'gr′=fr′, so gr=fr+g^r=f^r+gr=fr+const, and absorbing the constant into xˉ2r\bar x_2^rxˉ2r​ gives

    X^r(\tau,\sigma) = \bar x_2^r + \tfrac12\big(f^r(\tau+\sigma)+f^r(\tau-\sigma)\big). \tag{3}

    Now impose Dirichlet at σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π: Xr(τ,π)=xˉ2rX^r(\tau,\pi)=\bar x_2^rXr(τ,π)=xˉ2r​ requires fr(τ+π)=−fr(τ−π)f^r(\tau+\pi)=-f^r(\tau-\pi)fr(τ+π)=−fr(τ−π), i.e.

    f^r(u+2\pi) = -\,f^r(u). \tag{4}

    This sign is the crux of the note. Compare it with the two familiar cases:

    • NN or DD (free–free or pinned–pinned): fff is periodic, f(u+2π)=f(u)f(u+2\pi)=f(u)f(u+2π)=f(u) → integer harmonics.
    • ND (free–pinned): fff is anti-periodic, it flips sign after 2π2\pi2π and only returns after 4π4\pi4π → half-integer harmonics.

    The minus sign kills every even harmonic (they satisfy the plus sign) and keeps only the odd ones. This is the standard Sturm–Liouville fact that a mode with a Neumann end and a Dirichlet end is a cos⁡ ⁣((n+12)σ)\cos\!\big((n{+}\tfrac12)\sigma\big)cos((n+21​)σ), not a cos⁡(nσ)\cos(n\sigma)cos(nσ) or sin⁡(nσ)\sin(n\sigma)sin(nσ) — the clamped-loose organ pipe, whose fundamental is a quarter wave.

    Half-integer modes — the fingerprint of ND

    Expanding the anti-periodic frf^rfr over odd harmonics and passing to oscillators gives

    X^r(\tau,\sigma) = \bar x_2^r + i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z_{\text{odd}}}\frac2n\,\alpha_{n/2}^r\,e^{-i\frac n2\tau}\cos\!\Big(\frac{n\sigma}{2}\Big), \tag{5}

    so the oscillator labels are 12,32,52,…\tfrac12,\tfrac32,\tfrac52,\dots21​,23​,25​,… — half-integers. (The factor iii makes XrX^rXr real given the reality condition (αn/2r)†=α−n/2r(\alpha^r_{n/2})^\dagger=\alpha^r_{-n/2}(αn/2r​)†=α−n/2r​; the factor 2n\tfrac2nn2​ fixes the standard commutator below.) Two consequences to read straight off:

    • No zero mode, no momentum. There is no n=0n=0n=0 term — odd nnn excludes it — so an ND coordinate carries no average momentum. That fits: one end is pinned, so the string can't drift in this direction. (Contrast the DD case in Strings between parallel branes, which also has no momentum but does get a nonzero α0\alpha_0α0​ from brane separation. ND has neither.)
    • Half-integer moding is exactly the NS-sector worldsheet fermion. When you meet the superstring, the anti-periodic (NS) fermion carries this same half-integer expansion: same boundary algebra, different field.

    The commutator inherits the label:

    [\alpha_{m/2}^r,\alpha_{n/2}^s] = \frac{m}{2}\,\delta^{rs}\delta_{m+n,0}, \qquad m,n\in\mathbb Z_{\text{odd}}, \tag{6}

    the half-integer echo of the usual [αm,αn]=m δm+n,0[\alpha_m,\alpha_n]=m\,\delta_{m+n,0}[αm​,αn​]=mδm+n,0​.

    Mass formula

    Only common DD separations create a taut stretched segment (the mechanism of Strings between parallel branes); a displacement along an ND direction can always be absorbed by sliding the free Dppp endpoint, so it costs no tension energy. Putting the shifted intercept (8) together with the stretch term:

      M2=(∣ΔxˉDD∣2πα′)2+1α′(N⊥−1+p−q16).  (9)\boxed{\; M^2 = \left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x_{\mathrm{DD}}|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2 + \frac{1}{\alpha'}\left(N^\perp -1+\frac{p-q}{16}\right). \;} \tag{9}M2=(2πα′∣ΔxˉDD​∣​)2+α′1​(N⊥−1+16p−q​).​(9)

    The first term is (tension×stretched length)2(\text{tension}\times\text{stretched length})^2(tension×stretched length)2; the second is oscillators plus the ND-shifted vacuum.

    Spectrum: a scalar, and no photon

    Take the branes coincident in the common-DD directions so the stretch term vanishes. The ground state (N⊥=0N^\perp=0N⊥=0) is a single scalar living on the (q+1)(q{+}1)(q+1)-dimensional Dqqq world-volume (its momentum labels p⃗=(p2,…,pq)\vec p=(p^2,\dots,p^q)p​=(p2,…,pq) span only the NN directions):

    M^2 = \frac{1}{\alpha'}\left(-1+\frac{p-q}{16}\right). \tag{10}

    So it is tachyonic for p−q<16p-q<16p−q<16, massless exactly at p−q=16p-q=16p−q=16, massive for p−q>16p-q>16p−q>16 (bosonic string). The first excited states use one ND oscillator, N⊥=12N^\perp=\tfrac12N⊥=21​:

    a_{1/2}^{r\dagger}\,|p^+,\vec p\,;[pq]\rangle, \qquad r=q{+}1,\dots,p, \tag{11}

    giving (p−q)(p-q)(p−q) scalar fields on the world-volume — one per mixed direction (the label rrr is not a Dqqq world-volume index, so these are scalars, not a vector).

    Unlike the same-dimension case, this sector has no massless gauge boson. A would-be photon needs an NN oscillator, a1i†a_1^{i\dagger}a1i†​, at level N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1; but with p>qp>qp>q the intercept obeys −1+p−q16>−1-1+\tfrac{p-q}{16}>-1−1+16p−q​>−1, so N⊥≥1N^\perp\ge1N⊥≥1 forces M2>0M^2>0M2>0. Every candidate vector is massive. Gauge symmetry lives where strings can end on identical branes; a Dppp–Dqqq string spans a genuine dimensional mismatch, and that mismatch is exactly what the half-integer modes encode. The lightest thing the sector offers is a scalar, not a photon.

    Open questions

    • DN (a string from Dqqq to Dppp) simply swaps the two endpoints; the moding, intercept, and spectrum are identical — a mirror image, aDN=aND=+148a_{\mathrm{DN}}=a_{\mathrm{ND}}=+\tfrac{1}{48}aDN​=aND​=+481​.
    • The bosonic ground state is tachyonic unless p−q≥16p-q\ge16p−q≥16; in the superstring the same ND counting produces the phenomenologically central case of D-branes differing by 4 (e.g. D0 inside D4), where the shift lands the sector on a supersymmetric, tachyon-free spectrum. That belongs to a later superstring layer.

    Exercise

    A string runs from a D5 to a D1 (so p=5p=5p=5, q=1q=1q=1), both coincident in all common normal directions, in the bosonic d=25d=25d=25 theory.

    (a) How many NN, ND, and DD transverse directions does this sector have? (Total transverse =24=24=24.) (b) Compute the ground-state M2M^2M2 from the shifted intercept.
