Jerami Grant

Jerami Grant is coming off a bounce-back 18.6-point season in 29.7 minutes, and Memphis's development priorities — not decline — are the reason his projected role is smaller than his 2025-26 line.

Player card

  • Team/status: officially acquired from Portland in the Ja Morant trade; standard roster, with later trade or role compression still plausible.
  • Position: official roster label F; check SF/PF eligibility.
  • Age/experience: 32 during the season; entering NBA Year 13.
  • Depth role: veteran sixth man and second-unit scoring hub behind Boozer and the young wings, 24-minute base role. Published Grizzlies rotation projections treat him as the likely sixth man and the veteran offensive anchor on a team that shed Morant, Jackson, Bane and Aldama inside 13 months.
  • 2025-26: 57 GP/38 starts for Portland, 29.7 MPG; .453 FG, .814 FT, 2.4 3PM, 18.6 PTS, 3.5 REB, 2.1 AST, 0.7 STL, 0.6 BLK, 2.1 TO. ESPN stats, accessed 2026-07-18

The prior version described Grant as having "declining minutes," which the record does not support: he played 29.7 MPG across 57 games for Portland in 2025-26 and averaged 18.6 points on .453/.389 shooting with 2.4 threes, up from 14.4 points the prior year. The remaining haircut is real but is now attributed to its actual cause — a rebuilding team's development priorities, plus genuine deadline-trade or buyout risk on a $32 million contract with a player option to follow — rather than to a decline that did not happen. Nine-category: top 110-150 per game; scoring, threes and FT impact are useful, but rebounds and defensive events are weak for a forward. Points: more playable because shot volume matters and category scarcity does not. Dynasty: sell/contender depth at age 32; a trade to a larger role could restore short-term value but not long-horizon insulation.

Range, strengths and risks

  • Strengths: points, threes, free throws and position-flexible real-life defense.
  • Liabilities: low rebounds/assists, age curve, reduced volume relative to Portland, and ordinary FG%.
  • Floor: Memphis prioritizes youth or shuts him down after the deadline; 16-minute role, a February buyout, or uncertain new-team fit. Grant is a textbook in-season buyout candidate — a $32 million veteran sixth man on a tanking roster — and his departure would release minutes to Wells, GG Jackson, Prosper and Lopez.
  • Base: first veteran forward off the bench, preserving scoring efficiency in fewer minutes than Portland gave him.
  • Ceiling: starts alongside Boozer at 28-30 minutes or is traded to a contender needing offense, restoring 17-18 points and top-100 value.
  • Injury/transaction branches: Boozer's readiness lowers his Memphis usage; a Grant trade opens nine minutes each for GG/Prosper/Hendricks/Lopez in different lineups.

Durability: Iron-man early (77-81 GP three times through 2018-19), but he has not topped 63 games in any of the last six seasons: 54 in the shortened 2020-21, 47 in 2021-22 (thumb UCL surgery, then a season-ending calf strain), then 63 / 54 / 47 / 57 in Portland. Plan for 55-60 games, not 70.

Trajectory: The record shows a defense-first energy forward who reinvented himself as a 19-22 PPG scorer in Detroit and Portland, cratered to 14.4 on .373 FG in 2024-25, and then bounced back convincingly in 2025-26 (18.6 PTS, 2.4 3PM, .453/.814). The scoring skill is intact; what changed is context — Memphis acquired him in the Morant deal as a veteran sixth man on a rebuilding roster, so the 2026-27 projection cuts minutes for development reasons, not performance ones. He is fantasy-relevant now as a points/threes/FT source around the top-110-150 line, and becomes clearly draftable again if he starts at 28-30 minutes or is rerouted to a contender that needs his 17-18 points.
