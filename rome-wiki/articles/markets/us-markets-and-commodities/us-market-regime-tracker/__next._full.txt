<h2 id="data-state">Data state</h2>
<p>The June employment report, published July 2, showed payrolls up 57,000, unemployment at 4.2%, participation at 61.5%, and average hourly earnings up 3.5% year over year; April and May payrolls were revised down by 74,000 combined.</p>
<p>May CPI, published June 10, rose 0.5% month over month and 4.2% year over year, while core CPI was 2.9% year over year. June CPI is scheduled for July 14.</p>

<h2 id="cross-asset-confirmation-panel">Cross-asset confirmation panel</h2>
<p>The latest aligned daily observations available during this scan were mostly July 8. The 2-year Treasury yield was 4.21%, the 10-year nominal yield 4.56%, the 10-year real yield 2.31%, and the 10-year breakeven 2.25%. Relative to July 6, nominal yields rose 8 basis points at both maturities, while the real yield rose 7 basis points and the breakeven only 1 basis point. That decomposition fits a real-rate or term-premium move better than a fresh inflation-expectations shock, though two days are not a regime.</p>
<p>High-yield option-adjusted spreads were 2.70% on July 8, versus 2.72% July 6, and VIX closed at 16.90 versus 15.57. Credit did not confirm acute risk-off stress even as equity volatility firmed. The Fed's broad dollar index was 120.69 on July 2, below 121.15 on July 1; its slower publication cadence prevents a same-day causal comparison.</p>

<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why it matters</h2>
<p>The next step is cross-asset confirmation across nominal and real yields, breakevens, equities and breadth, the dollar, credit, volatility, and commodity curves. Causality should not be assigned until event timing and confounders are checked.</p>
