Kon Knueppel

Kon Knueppel combines elite three-point volume, two-category efficiency, and a new creation runway, giving him Charlotte's safest redraft floor and strongest long-term fantasy profile.

Snapshot

  • Role: starting SG/SF; secondary initiator and potential co-primary creator.
  • 2025-26: 81 GP, 31.5 MPG; .475 FG · .863 FT · 3.4 3PM · 18.5 PTS · 5.3 REB · 3.4 AST · 0.7 STL · 0.2 BLK · 2.0 TO.
  • Rookie résumé: All-Rookie First Team, narrow Rookie of the Year runner-up, and NBA leader with 273 made threes.
  • Prior value: roughly No. 66 per game in standard nine-category formats and 30.9 ESPN fantasy points per game.

Nine-category: top 45-60; threes and percentages provide the floor, while sub-one steals-plus-blocks cap the ceiling. Points: top 30-45 because assists and scoring should climb. Dynasty: top 20-35; arguably Charlotte's best blend of age, health, and scalable skill.

Development thesis

Knueppel's rookie 22% usage can plausibly reach 24-26% after Ball and Bridges departed. The Hornets should expand his on-ball reps instead of using him only as a movement shooter. More self-created possessions support 20-plus points and four-to-five assists, but .475 FG may be difficult to repeat against set defenses without Ball's passing.

His fantasy ceiling is unusually sensitive to defense. If he stays near 0.9 combined steals and blocks, even a 22/6/5 season can land outside the category top 30. A modest jump to 1.3-1.5 stocks would change the valuation materially.

Health and range

Knueppel played 81 games and missed only one after a December ankle sprain; X-rays were negative. That is the safest availability record among Charlotte's three lead creators.

  • Ceiling: 23-24 PTS, four threes, 5.5-6 REB and about five assists with neutral-to-positive percentages; top 30-40 redraft and eventual top-20 dynasty value.
  • Median: elite threes, solid scoring/assists, and merely adequate defense—the projection above.
  • Floor: efficiency regresses under greater defensive attention while stocks remain scarce; 18-19 PTS and a rank around 70-90.

Market check

ESPN ranked him No. 33 in April points leagues before Charlotte opened this role. Barutha's July 14 redraft list put him No. 54. Hashtag had him No. 28 in dynasty and No. 29 on its keeper board; Dynatyze displayed No. 31; RotoWire placed him No. 39 in a three-year dynasty window. Public redraft evidence therefore spans roughly rounds three through six depending on format.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Wisconsin Lutheran High School in Milwaukee, where he averaged 26.4 points, 8.8 rebounds and 5.1 assists as a senior, won Wisconsin Mr. Basketball, and led a 30-0 team to the 2024 Division 2 state title. Then one season at Duke (2024-25): 39 games, all starts, 30.5 minutes, 14.4 points, 4.0 rebounds, 2.7 assists on .479/.406/.914. ACC Tournament MVP, second-team All-ACC, ACC All-Freshman Team. The .914 college free-throw mark and .406 on high college three-point volume are the two numbers that translated most directly. Draft: 2025, first round, fourth overall by Charlotte; no draft-night trade. He then won the 2025 Summer League title with the Hornets and was named Summer League Championship MVP after 21 points, five rebounds and two assists in the final against Sacramento.

He shot .425 from three on that volume. His 273 made threes led the NBA outright — the first rookie ever to lead the league in made threes in a season — and he set league records for fastest to 50, 100 and 200 career threes. Rookie honors: Eastern Conference Rookie of the Month for November, All-Rookie First Team, and second in Rookie of the Year voting behind Cooper Flagg. Career high of 34 points with eight threes against Dallas.

Fantasy history: One season, and no independently verifiable season-end nine-category table was found; the roughly No. 66 per-game figure and 30.9 ESPN fantasy points per game cited in this dossier's snapshot could not be confirmed against a public source, so treat both as estimates. The category shape is unambiguous and does not depend on the rank. He was a large positive in threes — league-leading volume at .425 — a positive in field-goal percentage at .475 for a high-volume perimeter player, a positive in free-throw percentage at .863, and useful in points and rebounds for a guard. He was close to worthless in blocks at 0.2 and a genuine negative in steals at 0.7. Points leagues rated him better than categories did in every public board found, because that format ignores how thin his defensive counting stats are.

Durability: 81 of 82 games. The one absence followed a right ankle sprain in late December 2025; X-rays were negative and he returned immediately. No other reported injury, no load management pattern, no minutes restriction. For a 20-year-old rookie carrying a starter's workload, this is the cleanest availability record on the Charlotte roster and one of the cleaner rookie seasons in the league.

Trajectory: With one season there is no arc yet, only a baseline and a set of leading indicators. The strongest of those is that his shooting did not regress from college to the NBA — .406 to .425 from three and .914 to .863 at the line, on roughly triple the three-point volume, which is the opposite of what usually happens to a freshman shooter facing NBA closeouts. That argues the efficiency is a skill rather than a favorable-rookie-year artifact, and it is the main reason his 2026-27 projection can survive a usage increase. The real uncertainty is defensive counting stats: 0.9 combined stocks is not a rate that typically improves much with age, and it caps his category ceiling regardless of how the scoring develops. He plays 2026-27 at age 21 with LaMelo Ball and Miles Bridges gone, so more on-ball reps are close to certain; the question is whether the .475 field-goal percentage survives a shot diet with fewer created catch-and-shoot looks. The likeliest 2026-27 outcome is more points and assists at slightly worse efficiency, which is a modest net gain in categories and a larger one in points formats.
