<p>Separate three buckets:</p>
<ul>
<li>Small/mid-cap AI-adjacent companies: real businesses, still volatile, usually not "penny stocks."</li>
<li>Exchange-listed microcaps: more disclosure and listing standards, but often fragile.</li>
<li>OTC microcaps/nanocaps: highest information risk, liquidity risk, and promotion risk.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="source-stack">Source stack</h2>
<p>Use screeners only to discover names. The actual research should come from primary documents:</p>
<ul>
<li>SEC EDGAR: 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K, S-1/S-3, DEF 14A, Form 4, 13D/G.</li>
<li>OTC Markets page: market tier, disclosure status, warnings, quote/liquidity context.</li>
<li>Company materials: investor deck, website, product docs, customer case studies, pricing, demos.</li>
<li>Independent checks: customer references, patents, GitHub activity if relevant, job postings, litigation search, management history.</li>
<li>Promotion checks: paid newsletters, social media spikes, press-release mills, unexplained volume.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="ai-reality-test">AI reality test</h2>
<p>For every company claiming "AI," answer:</p>
<ul>
<li>What is the actual product?</li>
<li>Is the model core to the business, or a marketing adjective?</li>
<li>What proprietary data, distribution, workflow integration, regulation, or switching cost could create a moat?</li>
<li>Are there named customers, recurring revenue, deferred revenue, retention data, or paid pilots?</li>
<li>What are the inference/training costs, and do gross margins survive real usage?</li>
<li>Why would this company beat hyperscalers, incumbents, open-source models, or better-funded startups?</li>
</ul>
<p>If the answer is mostly press-release language, assume the market is selling theme exposure rather than business quality.</p>
<h2 id="financial-survival-test">Financial survival test</h2>
<p>The core question is not "could this 10x?" It is "can this company survive long enough without destroying shareholders?"</p>
<p>Check:</p>
<ul>
<li>Cash runway: cash divided by recent operating cash burn.</li>
<li>Gross margin and whether AI usage makes it better or worse.</li>
<li>Share count history over several years.</li>
<li>Warrants, convertibles, toxic financing, ATM programs, equity lines, and preferred shares.</li>
<li>Auditor language: going-concern warnings, internal-control weaknesses, auditor changes.</li>
<li>Related-party transactions and executive compensation.</li>
<li>Reverse splits, name changes, and repeated pivots into hot themes.</li>
</ul>
<p>Fully diluted market cap matters more than share price. A stock at $0.50 can be expensive if the capital structure is a mess.</p>
<h2 id="market-microstructure-test">Market microstructure test</h2>
<p>For a speculative position, the ability to exit matters.</p>
<ul>
<li>Use average daily dollar volume, not just share volume.</li>
<li>Check bid-ask spread as a percentage of price.</li>
<li>Use limit orders.</li>
<li>Avoid positions that would take multiple normal trading days to exit without becoming the market.</li>
<li>Treat sudden volume on no fundamental news as a warning until proven otherwise.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="hard-no-filters">Hard-no filters</h2>
<p>Immediate rejection is reasonable if any of these show up:</p>
<ul>
<li>No current, credible public financials.</li>
<li>The AI claim cannot be tied to a product, revenue line, or technical asset.</li>
<li>Paid promotion is louder than business execution.</li>
<li>Insider or promoter selling is the main visible activity.</li>
<li>Liquidity is too thin for the intended position size.</li>
<li>Dilution is structurally built into the financing.</li>
<li>Management has a pattern of failed reverse mergers, name changes, sanctions, or promotional pivots.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="before-buy-memo">Before-buy memo</h2>
<p>Every buy should fit on one page:</p>
<ul>
<li>Ticker, market cap, fully diluted market cap, cash, debt, burn, runway.</li>
<li>What the company actually sells.</li>
<li>Why the market might be mispricing it.</li>
<li>The next two catalysts.</li>
<li>What would falsify the thesis.</li>
<li>Maximum dollar loss.</li>
<li>Exit rule for success and failure.</li>
<li>Date for review.</li>
</ul>
<p>This turns the speculation into a small research lab, which fits the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/quant-research/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Quant research +</a> path better than impulse trading does.</p>
