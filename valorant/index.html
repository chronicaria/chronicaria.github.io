Four players, every round of an act, and a line that stops when you weren't there.

Every competitive match in E11A4 that at least one of the four played — 65 matches, 1,368 rounds. The chart below narrows to the 29 matches where at least two of them were on the same server, which is the only place their lines mean anything against each other, and which happens to begin at Martin's first match of the act. Every dot is one round's win probability added. The line through them is a decayed mean: every round a player has ever played counts, but its weight halves every 50 rounds, so recent form leads without older play being thrown away. Where somebody sat out, their line stops and resumes when they return.

The walk

    Same model as before, not re-solved: these are the same cross-fitted, out-of-fold credits from a 5,137-match corpus, re-sliced to whoever was on the server. The decay is fed by every round a player has played this act, including the solo queues the chart does not draw — a round still says something about form even when nobody was there to be compared against. At a 50-round half-life the most recent 50 rounds carry half the weight, the most recent 100 carry three quarters.

    Read the level, not the wiggles. A single round's RWPA has a standard deviation of 0.211 against a mean of +0.010 — round-level noise runs about twenty times the season signal. Decay helps: a 50-round half-life carries the precision of about 144 rounds, where a flat 50-round window carries 50, so the standard error is ±1.75 per 100 rather than ±2.98. It is still larger than the 0.72 spread between the highest and lowest season rate, so no crossing of two lines here settles anything. The figure at the end of each line is where its decayed mean finished, which is a different number from the season rate on the cards below — Martin ends at +0.2 here and averages +1.2 across the act, one being recent form and the other the whole act.     Season rates, and where they came from

    Each card carries its own decomposition. The net is small because it is the residue of two much larger flows: kill credit and death debit each run an order of magnitude above everything else and very nearly cancel, which is why they are drawn as one duel bar with both sides printed rather than as two bars that would set the scale twenty times too wide. Plant, defuse and clock are what is left. The steps sum to the net exactly.

    These are not a leaderboard, and the page will not sort them. They cover different matches, different opponents, and exposure differing by more than ten to one. Trzzcko's rate is the clearest warning: five matches and ninety-three rounds is enough to produce a large number and nowhere near enough to mean one.

    There is a deeper reason they don't compare. Every non-cohort player in this dataset is re-pseudonymised in each match, so opponents and teammates cannot be linked across games. Lineup quality and player skill are therefore not separable — a structural limit, not something more matches would fix.
