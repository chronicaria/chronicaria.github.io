Milwaukee Bucks 2026-27 fantasy basketball team outlook

Milwaukee begins a volatile post-Giannis transition in which opportunity is abundant, the roster is unfinished, and Tyler Herro, Ryan Rollins, Kel'el Ware, and Myles Turner are the clearest fantasy anchors.

Up: 2026-27 Fantasy Basketball Encyclopedia

Executive fantasy read

The Bucks are no longer a Giannis-centered contender. The July 6 trade sent Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis to Miami for Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr, Kasparas Jakučionis, Nate Ament, future draft capital, and a swap. Milwaukee then added Caris LeVert, re-signed Gary Trent Jr and Ousmane Dieng, and filled its third two-way slot with Rafael Castro. That leaves a young core, several overlapping veteran guards and wings, and an unusually wide range of plausible outcomes.

Giannis and Portis together took 53 minutes and 41.3 points a night out of the rotation — Giannis 27.6 points in 28.9 minutes across 36 games, Portis 13.7 points in 24.2. That vacated volume is the whole reason the minute model below runs above 2025-26 workloads for the returning core rather than below them.

Dieng's new money matters to the roster-cut math below: he is no longer a minimum-salary cut candidate. Trent's deal is fully guaranteed and effectively untradeable while under investigation, which argues against — not for — his being one of the two contracts that disappear.

  • Best redraft bets: Herro's scoring, Ryan Rollins' assists and steals, Ware's rebounds, and Myles Turner' threes plus blocks.
  • Best dynasty bets: Ware, Brayden Burries, Herro, Turner, and Ament, with Jakučionis as the patient guard-development play.
  • Best category specialists: Turner for blocks and threes, Rollins for steals, AJ Green and Trent for threes, Ware for rebounds and field-goal percentage. Green is the cheapest source of two-plus makes a night on the board after setting the franchise single-season three-point record.
  • Biggest traps: paying for last season's minutes from any veteran wing, assuming Kevin Porter Jr. is healthy, or treating the current 17-standard-player roster as opening-night final.
  • Preferred team builds: Ware fits punt-FT% or rebounding builds; Turner fits scarce-center-stats builds; Herro fits points/threes/FT%; Rollins fits steals/assists but carries turnover risk.

Milwaukee finished 32-50 in 2025-26 and was 13-33 without Giannis, according to Brew Hoop's July 16 review. The base case is roughly the same record with a very different possession tree: Herro becomes the main scorer, Rollins and Jaquez handle more creation, and Ware gains starter-level usage. The high case requires Herro and Porter to be healthy, the useful veterans to remain past the deadline, and the young frontcourt to defend. The low case is a deliberate veteran sell-off followed by developmental lineups.

Roster construction warning

As of the July 18 cutoff, Milwaukee carries 17 standard contracts plus three two-way players (Cormac Ryan, Kam Jones, Rafael Castro). The regular-season limit is 15 standard contracts, so at least two standard players must be traded, waived, or otherwise removed before opening night. All three two-way slots are filled, so there is no open developmental spot to absorb a squeezed standard player.

Likely pressure points, not reported decisions:

  • Nine players can credibly claim guard or wing minutes: Herro, Rollins, Porter, Jakučionis, Burries, Green, Trent, LeVert, and Jaquez.
  • Turner and Ware are both starting-caliber centers; using Ware at power forward can raise their shared ceiling but creates spacing and matchup questions.
  • Kyle Kuzma, Herro, Turner, Porter, LeVert, Green, and Trent could all draw trade interest during a rebuild.
  • Pete Nance, Jericho Sims, Bogoljub Marković, and the veteran-wing cluster are the clearest opening-night squeeze points, but contract economics can matter more than basketball fit.

Public projections disagree on the center order. Yahoo's post-trade lineup piece starts Ware at center with Kuzma at power forward and Turner outside the starting five; SI's Bucks site and Roundtable both start Turner at center, with Ware at power forward (Roundtable) or off the bench (SI). Turner is simultaneously the most-cited Milwaukee trade candidate — Toronto (a Poeltl/Gradey Dick framework), Atlanta and Charlotte have all been linked, though reporting says Milwaukee is fielding calls rather than actively shopping him. If Turner moves, the backup-center minutes go to Jericho Sims and two-way Rafael Castro; Castro is the roster's most actionable waiver contingency.

Evidence and source disagreements

  • Public depth charts are provisional. RealGM's July 18 ordering is useful for roster status, but it cannot resolve the mandatory two-player standard-roster reduction.
  • Herro's outside rank varies materially. FantasyPros expert rows viewed July 18 ranged roughly from the mid-teens to the mid-50s; this projection is lower than the most aggressive ranks because his 2025-26 season ended at 33 games and Milwaukee has little incentive to overextend him.
  • Hashtag Basketball's July 18 crowd dynasty page placed Herro around No. 60, Ware No. 69, Burries No. 106, Turner No. 134, and Ament No. 170. Deeper Milwaukee entries appeared unstable, so they are treated as market signals rather than forecasts.
  • The NBA's July 15 transition analysis explicitly frames Herro as the main scorer and expects starter-level minutes for Ware and Jaquez. That supports opportunity but does not announce a lineup.
  • Update 2026-07-19: Brayden Burries closed Las Vegas at 23.5 points, 4.5 rebounds, 4.2 assists, 2.0 steals, and 1.0 blocks per game on .500/.364/.667 with three total turnovers across four games, including a 27-point game with the game-winning layup against Philadelphia on July 18; Nate Ament started slowly but scored in double figures in his last two games. Learning notes

The important edge is not guessing all 15 opening-night roster spots in July. It is pricing the workload elasticity created by a rebuilding team with two excess contracts. Herro, Rollins, Ware, and Turner have enough independent category production to draft now; nearly everyone else should be valued as a scenario option.

Open questions

  • Which two standard contracts disappear before opening night?
  • Will Jenkins start Turner and Ware together or stagger them as nominal centers?
  • Is Herro fully ready for camp after the toe/foot and rib problems that limited him to 33 games?
  • How much of Porter's pre-surgery burst and playmaking returns?
  • Does Milwaukee keep its veterans through the trade deadline or prioritize developmental minutes immediately?
