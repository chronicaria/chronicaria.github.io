<h2 id="the-apparent-endpoint-is-really-a-clock-stack">The apparent endpoint is really a clock stack</h2>
<p>A political contract can look finished on election night while remaining unresolved for days, or it can stop trading before the decisive information arrives. A venue can determine an outcome but delay final payout during a challenge window. An onchain winner can become redeemable without the holder actually redeeming. A research dataset can then label the same forecast against a corrected outcome months later.</p>
<p>Calling all of these moments “settlement” hides the mechanism. A useful minimum schema separates seven operational timestamps and one analytical timestamp:</p>

<h2 id="1-information-cutoff-what-state-of-the-world-counts">1. Information cutoff: what state of the world counts</h2>
<p>The information cutoff is part of the proposition itself. “Will candidate X concede by 11:59 p.m. on election day?” asks about conduct before a deadline even if the source is checked the next morning. “Who will win the state?” may depend on certified results rather than the first media call. A contract can therefore have a cutoff for qualifying facts, a separate official-source publication time, and a later decision time.</p>
<p>This distinction prevents hindsight from changing the event. Information learned after <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>t</mi><mi>i</mi></msub></mrow><annotation For example, a timestamped statement published just after midnight should not satisfy a by-midnight contract merely because it was available before adjudication. Conversely, a later official canvass may be the best evidence of votes cast before the cutoff.</p>
<p>Researchers need the cutoff for forecast evaluation. A price at 8 p.m. cannot be described as a forecast of an event defined using information through midnight unless the target is stated carefully. The contract’s semantic horizon, not the download date, determines what probability the price expressed.</p>
<h2 id="2-market-close-the-end-of-executability">2. Market close: the end of executability</h2>
<p>Market close is when trading stops, not necessarily when the answer becomes known. Kalshi’s lifecycle documentation defines <code>close_time</code> as the point after which trading stops; markets then enter a closed state awaiting determination. The documentation distinguishes this from expected and latest expiration fields. Kalshi also says close time may move, including reopening a closed market when the close time is moved into the future, and that a market can close early when permitted.</p>
<p>For measurement, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>t</mi><mi>c</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">t_c</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7651em;vertical-align:-0.15em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal">t</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t vlist-t2"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.1514em;"><span style="top:-2.55em;margin-left:0em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mord mathnormal mtight">c</span></span></span></span><span class="vlist-s">​</span></span><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.15em;"><span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span> should normally be represented by both the venue field and the last <em>executable</em> market observation. A last-trade price can be hours old. A displayed midpoint can combine stale or tiny orders. If a research question asks for the “closing forecast,” the defensible object is often the last bid and ask available immediately before close, with timestamp and size, rather than a single unqualified last price.</p>
<p>Close is also a policy choice. Early closure reduces the chance that traders transact on nearly certain information, but it removes late information aggregation. Later closure can improve price discovery while exposing participants to operational pauses or a race between unofficial knowledge and official confirmation. Neither design makes the closing price automatically superior; it changes what that price measures.</p>
<h2 id="3-determination-the-first-assigned-result">3. Determination: the first assigned result</h2>
<p>Determination is the first formal assignment of a payout-relevant outcome. In a centralized lifecycle, venue staff apply the contract rules and named source. Kalshi describes a <code>determined</code> status in which the result is known and a settlement timer is running. Kalshi’s current help material says most markets settle within a few hours after the outcome is known, often about three hours, but may take longer while official source-agency data is pending. It advises users of standard single-outcome markets to check the rules and contact support if the event ended more than roughly twelve hours earlier with no update; neither statement is a guarantee for a political market.</p>
<p>In an optimistic-oracle design, the analogous moment may be a bonded proposal rather than a terminal judgment. Polymarket documents a process in which a proposer selects an outcome and posts a bond. The proposal then enters a challenge period. Calling proposal time “resolution time” would erase the possibility that the proposal is disputed or found too early.</p>
<p>Political determination often waits for a legally or contractually authoritative event. Reported votes, recount windows, certifications, litigation, concessions, and the market’s specified settlement source can fall on different dates. That explains why a television call and a venue determination can be far apart without either timestamp being erroneous: they answer different questions under different standards.</p>
<h2 id="4-dispute-provisional-truth-under-review">4. Dispute: provisional truth under review</h2>
<p>A dispute interval is economically real. Kalshi exposes a <code>settlement_timer_seconds</code> field; while a market is determined, the result may be disputed, after which the market can be amended and the timer restarted. Its documented terminal REST state is <code>finalized</code>, even though the corresponding WebSocket event is named <code>settled</code>.</p>
<p>Polymarket documents a two-hour challenge period after a proposal. An undisputed proposal is the fast path. A dispute triggers another proposal round; a second dispute escalates to UMA’s Data Verification Mechanism. Polymarket estimates disputed resolution at four to six days, including debate and voting. UMA’s own documentation explains the general mechanism: data is accepted after a configurable liveness period if undisputed, while disputed claims go to token-holder voting and can take days.</p>
<p>The analyst should record each proposal and amendment rather than overwrite history. A market can be economically near-certain yet legally provisional. Treating the first determination as the final label creates label leakage when the researcher later knows whether it survived challenge.</p>
<h2 id="5-finalization-the-outcome-becomes-terminal-for-the-venue">5. Finalization: the outcome becomes terminal for the venue</h2>
<p>Finalization is the state transition after adjudication is complete and the venue has operationalized the payout. On Kalshi, the lifecycle ends at <code>finalized</code>; <code>settlement_ts</code> is populated and positions have been paid. On Polymarket, the oracle report leads the Conditional Token Framework to record a payout vector, after which winning tokens can be redeemed.</p>
<p>Finalization is distinct from epistemic certainty. An official correction can still arrive. A court may later describe events differently. But the contract needs a terminal rule so collateral is not locked forever. Finalization therefore means “terminal under this mechanism and rule set,” not “incapable of revision anywhere in the world.” This is one reason <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/markets/prediction-markets/political-event-contract-resolution-designs/" class="wiki-link">Political event-contract resolution designs</a> must be compared by their named sources, fallbacks, and amendment rules rather than just by market titles.</p>
<h2 id="6-redemption-or-payment-when-value-becomes-usable">6. Redemption or payment: when value becomes usable</h2>
<p>Custodial and onchain venues expose different last-mile clocks. Kalshi says winning contracts are paid into the cash balance once the market settles, so finalization and internal payment can be nearly the same system event even if bank withdrawal occurs later. Polymarket requires a redemption operation after resolution: the holder redeems through the CTF collateral adapter, which burns the ERC-1155 outcome tokens through the CTF contract, receives released USDC.e collateral, wraps it into pUSD, and returns pUSD to the wallet. Its documentation says redemption is available after resolution and has no deadline.</p>

A study of venue operations might use earliest technical redeemability. A study of user cash flow might use the trader-specific redemption transaction. <p>The first term is venue or oracle finalization latency after close. The second is payment-rail or user-action latency. Fees, gas, withdrawal holds, weekends, and manual inaction belong in the second term only when relevant.</p>
<h2 id="7-corrections-version-the-label-instead-of-rewriting-history">7. Corrections: version the label instead of rewriting history</h2>
<p>Official political data can be corrected after initial publication. Venues may also clarify a rule, amend a determination during a dispute, or leave a finalized result unchanged because the contract’s finality rule has already operated. These are different events.</p>
<p>A correction ledger should preserve at least: original outcome, original source value, original timestamp, corrected value, correction timestamp, venue response, and the dataset version that incorporated it. Never silently replace the historical label. Silent replacement makes an old forecast appear to have targeted a label that did not exist when the forecast or payout occurred.</p>
<p>Three labels may coexist legitimately:</p>
<ul>
<li>the <strong>world-state label</strong>, based on the best later account of what happened;</li>
<li>the <strong>contract label</strong>, based on the venue’s rules and final outcome;</li>
<li>the <strong>as-known label</strong>, based on information available at a specified evaluation time.</li>
</ul>
<p>Calibration of tradable contracts normally uses the contract label. Political-science analysis may prefer the world-state label. A real-time forecasting audit may require the as-known label. Reporting which label is used is more important than pretending there is one universally correct timestamp.</p>

<h2 id="why-it-matters-latency-changes-the-economics">Why it matters: latency changes the economics</h2>
<h3 id="capital-lockup-and-realized-return">Capital lockup and realized return</h3>
<p>A winning contract’s nominal payoff omits the time collateral remains unavailable. <p>Expected latency matters before close. A price below apparent fair value may compensate for disputed-source risk, slow certification, or awkward redemption. Thus the observed price can mix event probability with a settlement-liquidity discount. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/markets/prediction-markets/from-event-contract-prices-to-probabilities/" class="wiki-link">From event-contract prices to probabilities</a> should not treat that discount as political belief.</p>
<h3 id="cross-venue-comparison">Cross-venue comparison</h3>
<p>Comparing venues by “closing price” requires aligned propositions and clocks. Venue A may close before polls close; Venue B may trade through election-night returns. Venue A may determine from certification; Venue B may finalize after an oracle proposal. A comparison that samples each venue at its native close gives each forecast a different information set.</p>
<p>Use at least two panels:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Common-clock forecasts:</strong> sample all venues at the same UTC timestamp before the shared information cutoff, using executable quotes.</li>
<li><strong>Native-lifecycle outcomes:</strong> report each venue’s own close, determination, finalization, and payment latencies.</li>
</ol>
<p>The first panel compares forecasting information. The second compares market design and operational performance. Combining them rewards venues that remain open longer for having seen more information, or penalizes them for slow finalization, depending on the metric.</p>
<h3 id="backtests-and-leakage">Backtests and leakage</h3>
<p>Backtests fail when current API fields are mistaken for historical state. A present-day record marked <code>closed</code> or <code>finalized</code> does not prove when that status changed. Polymarket’s event API exposes fields such as <code>endDate</code>, <code>closedTime</code>, <code>umaEndDate</code>, <code>umaResolutionStatus</code>, and <code>finishedTimestamp</code>; those names should be interpreted from documentation and validated against event or onchain history, not collapsed into one endpoint. Kalshi’s API separately exposes close-time filters and settled-time filters, plus lifecycle events.</p>
<p>A robust backtest stores raw snapshots or event streams. It should reconstruct what was visible at each simulated decision time, exclude later outcome fields from feature generation, and document stale-quote rules. It should also avoid selecting only markets that eventually finalized cleanly, because unresolved, voided, or disputed markets are part of the ex ante opportunity set. See <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/markets/prediction-markets/backtesting-political-forecasts-without-leakage/" class="wiki-link">Backtesting political forecasts without leakage</a>.</p>
<h3 id="calibration-and-operational-measurement">Calibration and operational measurement</h3>
<p>Calibration bins answer whether events priced near <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>q</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">q</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span></span></span></span> occur about <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>q</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">q</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span></span></span></span> of the time. Settlement latency can bias that test through selection and horizon mismatch. Long-disputed markets may be dropped from a dataset frozen too early. Markets with fast, obvious outcomes may be overrepresented. Late closing prices incorporate more realized information than early ones. A fair study reports unresolved censoring and either waits for a fixed maturity window or uses a prespecified treatment of pending labels.</p>

<p>Report medians, upper quantiles, disputed and undisputed strata, and missing-timestamp rates. Heavy tails are the story: routine markets may settle quickly while a few legally ambiguous elections immobilize capital for much longer.</p>
<h2 id="a-practical-data-contract">A practical data contract</h2>
<p>For each market, preserve the venue ID, canonical question, full rules version, named resolution source, timezone, cutoff definition, all lifecycle timestamps, all determination proposals, dispute status, final payout vector, payment mechanism, correction history, and raw provenance URL. Store timestamps in UTC while retaining the contract’s local-time wording. Record whether a time is scheduled, observed, inferred, or missing.</p>
<p>For each price observation, preserve bid, ask, last trade, size, timestamp, trading status, and collection time. For each analytical result, preserve the evaluation rule and dataset version. This makes it possible to reproduce both a political forecast study and a settlement-operations study without pretending they share one clock.</p>
<p>The governing rule is simple: <strong>name the interval you measured</strong>. “Settled in three hours” could mean close to first determination, proposal to unchallenged acceptance, finalization to wallet redemption, or event occurrence to cash credit. Without endpoints, the number is not comparable.</p>
