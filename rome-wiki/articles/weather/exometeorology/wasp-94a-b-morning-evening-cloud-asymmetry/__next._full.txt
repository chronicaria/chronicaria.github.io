<h2 id="evidence-ladder">Evidence ladder</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Measured:</strong> the team reports a <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>6</mn><mi>σ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">6\sigma</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">6</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">σ</span></span></span></span> difference between the two limb-resolved transmission spectra.</li>
<li><strong>Retrieved:</strong> the morning limb is cloud-covered at reported <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>11</mn><mi>σ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">11\sigma</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">11</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">σ</span></span></span></span> significance, while the evening limb is hotter and has strong water absorption, reported at <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>10</mn><mi>σ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">10\sigma</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">10</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">σ</span></span></span></span>.</li>
<li><strong>Interpreted:</strong> condensates form on the cooler morning side and evaporate as circulation carries them toward the hotter evening side.</li>
</ul>
<p>The last step is a physical explanation of the spectra, not direct tracking of individual clouds. The result also shows why a one-dimensional, limb-averaged retrieval can bias inferred abundances when the terminators differ.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why it matters</h2>
<p>The paper turns spatially resolved transit spectroscopy into a test of cloud microphysics and circulation. It is stronger than a generic “weather detected” claim because the measured asymmetry and the inferred cloud cycle remain explicitly separate.</p>
