Where do the extra components go?</p>
<p>Two places, and neither is a physical particle:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Gauge redundancy</strong> — some components are not new physics, just the <em>same</em> physical configuration described in more than one way. Shifting <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>A</mi><mi>μ</mi></msub></mrow><annotation This is over-description, and we are free to delete it.</li>
<li><strong>Constraints</strong> — once we delete the redundancy, the field equation <em>solves</em> some of the remaining components in terms of the others. Those are not independent data; they are determined.</li>
</ul>
<p>A rigid rod in 3D makes the constraint concrete: its two endpoints carry <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>6</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">6</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">6</span></span></span></span> coordinates, but the fixed length ties one combination down, leaving <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>5</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">5</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">5</span></span></span></span> genuine degrees of freedom. Light-cone gauge is the coordinate choice that exposes both mechanisms at once: the redundancy is spent deleting the <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>+</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">+</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">+</span></span></span></span> components, the constraints then determine the <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>−</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">-</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">−</span></span></span></span> components, and what survives is the <strong>transverse</strong> field. The physical content of a massless field lives entirely in its transverse directions, so counting polarizations is tensor combinatorics on <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>D</mi><mo>−</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">D-2</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">D</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">−</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span></span></span></span> slots. <h2 id="what-spin-2-is-gravity-does-and-does-not-mean-here">What "spin-2 is gravity" does and does not mean here</h2>
<p>This note proves a <strong>free-field dictionary</strong> entry: a massless symmetric tensor with gauge symmetry (8) carries exactly the polarization content of a graviton. That is enough to <em>identify</em> the string's massless closed-string tensor state as "the graviton" at the free level. It is <strong>not</strong> a derivation of Einstein's equations.</p>
<p>The interacting statement "massless spin-2 forces general relativity" needs more input: a massless spin-2 field couples consistently only to a conserved symmetric source (the <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-i-just-in-time-physics/05-currents-constraints-and-gauge-redundancy/stress-energy-tensor-as-momentum-flow/" class="wiki-link">Stress-energy tensor as momentum flow</a>); it carries energy-momentum so it must couple to its own source; iterating that self-coupling turns the linear gauge symmetry (8) into full nonlinear coordinate invariance, whose unique two-derivative completion is Einstein gravity (up to field redefinitions and a cosmological term). That argument uses locality, Lorentz invariance, and a low-energy two-derivative truncation — it is a signpost, not a theorem provable by polarization counting. We return to it via the closed-string massless spectrum (Module 16) and worldsheet consistency (Module 25).</p>
