Jalen Green

Player card

  • Team / role: Phoenix Suns starting scoring guard, secondary transition and half-court attacker
  • Healthy rotation: 30 MPG
  • Season projection: 61 GP, 30 MPG
  • 9-cat line: .435 FG (7.0/16.1), .780 FT (2.3/2.9), 2.4 3PM, 18.7 PTS, 3.9 REB, 3.1 AST, 1.0 STL, 0.3 BLK, 2.3 TO
  • Projection confidence: Medium-low
  • Formats: Better points-league than nine-cat bet; buy-low dynasty talent with material soft-tissue risk

Role and evidence

Green played only 32 games in 2025-26 after a right-hamstring strain and later tightness/recurrence. His abbreviated line—17.8 points, 3.6 rebounds, 2.8 assists, 2.2 threes, .422 FG, and .747 FT—should not be treated as his settled level. Source: Hashtag Basketball dynasty database, updated 2026-07-02. Confidence: medium-high on the historical line.

The official November diagnosis called for a four-to-six-week reevaluation, but he ultimately missed far more time and exited a January return game with tightness. He had played every game in earlier seasons, which makes the range unusually wide: the hamstring may be a one-season cluster or a new recurring concern. Sources: NBA injury update, 2025-11-16, NBA report on January return/tightness, 2026-01-24. Confidence: high on history, low-medium on recurrence probability.

Phoenix's projected lineup puts Green next to Devin Booker, Dillon Brooks, Miles Bridges, and Mark Williams. Booker owns primary creation; Green's best path is early-offense volume, second-unit staggering, and improved catch-and-shoot efficiency. A sixth-man deployment would reduce nominal status but could improve usage. The base keeps him starting and at 30 minutes.

Category shape

  • Wins: Points and threes, with some steals upside.
  • Neutral: Rebounds and assists are useful but not build-defining.
  • Costs: High-volume field-goal variance, turnovers, and uncertain free-throw rate.
  • Build fit: Points leagues, punt-FG% category teams, and rosters already strong in big-man stats.

Points and dynasty outlook

ESPN ranked Green No. 81 in its April points-league list. That predated the Bridges/Kennard moves but already incorporated a disastrous availability season. Source: ESPN way-too-early rankings, 2026-04-14. Confidence: medium as a dated market anchor.

Hashtag's July board placed him No. 95 in dynasty and its July 18 keeper crowd placed him No. 93. At age 24, a full-health season can beat both, but the crowded team prevents projecting a clean return to Houston-era volume. Sources: Hashtag dynasty rankings, updated 2026-07-02, Hashtag keeper crowd, updated 2026-07-18. Confidence: medium-low; market inputs are not medical evidence.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: Fewer than 50 games or repeated ramp-ups, with 16-17 points and damaging efficiency.
  • Base: 61 games, 18.7 points, 2.4 threes, and fringe top-100 nine-cat value.
  • Ceiling: 70 games at 32-plus minutes, 22 points, 3.5 assists, and league-average guard efficiency; top-60 points value is reachable.

Draft verdict

Green is a price-sensitive upside pick, not a safe rebound candidate. Draft rooms that punish the 2025-26 injury can create value, but any top-70 nine-cat cost assumes both health and a role Phoenix has not yet demonstrated. Camp reports on hamstring restrictions and second-unit staggering matter more than preseason scoring totals.

Career record

Pre-NBA: No college. Green was ESPN's No. 1 overall recruit in the 2020 class and announced on April 16, 2020 that he would sign with NBA G League Ignite — a one-year, $500,000 deal that made him the first player to join that team — over offers from Auburn, Oregon and Memphis. Ignite (2020-21): 15 games, 32.0 minutes, 17.9 points, 4.1 rebounds, 2.8 assists, 1.5 steals; shooting splits for that G League season are not verified here and are omitted rather than estimated. High school at San Joaquin Memorial (Fresno) for three years, then Prolific Prep (Napa) as a senior: 27.9 points as a sophomore and MaxPreps National Sophomore of the Year, 30.1 as a junior, 31.5 as a senior with Sports Illustrated All-American Player of the Year honors. Draft: 2021, first round, 2nd overall, Houston Rockets — the first player drafted directly out of G League Ignite. Not traded on draft night.

Green moved to Phoenix in the June 2025 Kevin Durant trade — a record seven-team deal in which Houston sent Green, Dillon Brooks and the No. 10 pick in the 2025 draft plus second-rounders for Durant. The deal could not process until July 6, 2025 because of a restriction in the rookie extension Green signed the previous October.

Fantasy history: No season-end 9-cat rank from Basketball Monster, Hashtag, or the ESPN Player Rater is verifiable for any individual season here, so no ranks are asserted. The shape is unambiguous from the raw lines, though: Green has been a points-league asset and a category-league drag for his entire career. A .422 career field goal percentage on roughly 16 attempts per game and 2.4 turnovers are two active negatives that his scoring and 2.5 threes must overcome, and he has never averaged a full steal in a healthy season or delivered blocks. Free throw percentage in the .780-.813 range is his one quiet positive. The two market anchors the dossier already cites — ESPN's No. 81 points-league rank in April 2026 and Hashtag's No. 95 dynasty / No. 93 keeper placements in July — are forward-looking projections, not season-end results, and should be read as sentiment rather than history.

Durability: GP by season: 67, 76, 82, 82, 32. The first four years are among the best availability records of any recent lottery guard — back-to-back 82-game seasons at ages 21 and 22, with the 67 as a rookie reflecting rest and a shortened role rather than a chronic problem. 2025-26 broke that cleanly: a right hamstring strain in November 2025 carried an official four-to-six-week reevaluation window, he missed substantially more time than that, and he exited a January return game with recurrent tightness. Total: 32 games and a career-low 25.9 minutes. The interpretive question is whether that is a single-season cluster against four years of durability or the first instance of a recurring soft-tissue pattern, and one season does not resolve it. Hamstring reinjury rates are high enough that the four clean years should not fully discount the risk.

Trajectory: Green's per-game production has been remarkably static across five seasons — 17.3, 22.1, 19.6, 21.0, 17.8 points on a field goal percentage that has sat between .416 and .426 every single year. That flatness is the story. He arrived as a finished-shape high-volume scorer at 19 and has not meaningfully changed the shape since: the rebounding ticked up in 2023-24 and back down, the assists peaked at 3.7 in his second year, and the efficiency has refused to move despite four different roster contexts and a coaching change. The one genuine improvement was three-point volume and accuracy in 2024-25 (2.9 makes at .354), which was reversed in the injury-shortened Phoenix year at .313 on a 32-game sample too small to trust in either direction. Entering his age-24 season he is at the point where a scorer either converts athleticism into efficiency or settles as a volume specialist, and five seasons of identical FG% is evidence for the latter. The realistic 2026-27 upside is availability-driven rather than skill-driven: the same player over 65-70 games alongside Booker is worth considerably more than what 32 games produced, but nothing in the history supports projecting an efficiency leap on top of it.
