3:T1e94,<h2 id="judgment">Judgment</h2>
<p>The answer depends on the date.</p>
<p>Before the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/russo-japanese-war/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Russo-Japanese War +</a>, annexation was a Japanese goal and a serious danger, but not inevitable. <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/korea/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Korea +</a> still had some room to maneuver through court reform, balance-of-power diplomacy, and rivalry among Japan, Russia, Britain, China, and the United States.</p>
<p>After Japan defeated Russia in 1905, the situation changed. Russia accepted Japan's paramount interests in Korea through the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/treaty-of-portsmouth/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Treaty of Portsmouth +</a>, the United States signaled acceptance through the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/taft-katsura-agreement/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Taft-Katsura Agreement +</a>, and Britain was already aligned with Japan through the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/anglo-japanese-alliance/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Anglo-Japanese Alliance +</a>. The <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/eulsa-treaty/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Eulsa Treaty +</a> then stripped the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/korean-empire/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Korean Empire +</a> of diplomatic sovereignty and closed off the normal path of foreign appeal.</p>
<p>After 1907, annexation was close to a fait accompli. The failure of the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/hague-secret-emissary-affair/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Hague Secret Emissary Affair +</a> gave Japan a pretext to force <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/gojong/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Gojong +</a> from the throne. The <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/japan-korea-treaty-of-1907/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Japan-Korea Treaty of 1907 +</a> placed Korean administration under Japanese direction, Japanese officials entered the bureaucracy, and the Korean army was disbanded. From that point, only a major external shock could realistically have stopped formal annexation.</p>
<h2 id="what-was-contingent">What Was Contingent</h2>
<p>The exact form and timing were still contingent. Japan might have maintained Korea as a protectorate longer. Annexation might have happened later, or under a different legal pretext. Japanese leaders did not always agree on the best administrative form of control.</p>
<p>The deeper loss of sovereignty, however, had already happened before 1910. The treaty of August 22, 1910, publicly announced on August 29, was the formal legal wrapper around a political reality Japan had built step by step.</p>
<h2 id="why-korea-was-vulnerable">Why Korea Was Vulnerable</h2>
<p>Several structural factors made annexation increasingly likely:</p>
<ul>
<li>Korea lacked a strong army and modern fiscal base.</li>
<li>Court politics remained divided and dependent on outside patrons.</li>
<li>Reform efforts such as the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/gwangmu-reform/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Gwangmu Reform +</a> were real but too late, underfunded, and politically narrow.</li>
<li>Japan treated Korea as vital to its own security and empire.</li>
<li>Britain, the United States, and Russia had stronger reasons to accommodate Japan than to defend Korean sovereignty.</li>
<li>Anti-Japanese resistance was widespread, but fragmented and unable to reverse control of diplomacy, police, army, and bureaucracy.</li>
</ul>
<p>This does not mean Korea was doomed by weakness or culture. It means Korea faced an imperial system in which sovereignty depended on military capacity and great-power recognition, and by 1905 both had turned decisively against it.</p>
<h2 id="counterfactual-openings">Counterfactual Openings</h2>
<p>The plausible escape routes were narrow:</p>
<ul>
<li>Japan loses or stalemates in the <a href="/rome-wiki/wanted/russo-japanese-war/" class="wiki-link wiki-link-missing" title="Unwritten article — a research thread waiting to be followed">Russo-Japanese War +</a>.</li>
<li>Britain or the United States refuses to accept Japan's Korean predominance.</li>
<li>Russia remains strong enough in East Asia to deter full Japanese control.</li>
<li>Korean reforms build a credible army, tax base, and bureaucratic state before 1904.</li>
<li>A domestic coalition unites court, reformers, army, and popular resistance before Japan controls the state machinery.</li>
</ul>
<p>None of these was impossible. But after 1905, each required more than Korean will; it required a change in the international balance.</p>
<h2 id="verdict">Verdict</h2>
<p>Annexation in 1910 was not metaphysically inevitable. It was historically contingent.</p>
<p>But it became progressively more locked in:</p>
<ul>
<li>After 1895: likely enough to be a central danger.</li>
<li>After 1905: highly likely.</li>
<li>After 1907: nearly inevitable without a great-power rupture.</li>
<li>By 1910: formal annexation was the last step in an already operating colonial takeover.</li>
</ul>
