    Born-Infeld action

    Born–Infeld is the nonlinear electrodynamics with a built-in maximal field b=1/(2\pi\alpha') that reduces to Maxwell when the field is weak; it packs into a square-root determinant, gives a point charge finite self-energy, and with the brane tension out front is the leading local abelian gauge action on a D-brane: \mathcal L = -T_p\sqrt{-\det(\eta_{mn}+2\pi\alpha' F_{mn})} .

    The relativistic particle Lagrangian L=−mc21−v2/c2L=-mc^2\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}L=−mc21−v2/c2​ has a square root that forbids v>cv>cv>c. Born–Infeld puts the field inside a square root the same way, so the field can never exceed bbb — and that one move cures the infinite self-energy of the Maxwell electron and turns out to be exactly what a D-brane's gauge field does.

    Builds on Nonlinear electrodynamics (the L(s,p)\mathcal L(s,p)L(s,p) framework) and Electric and magnetic fields on D-branes (where the bound Ecrit=bE_{\rm crit}=bEcrit​=b comes from).

    Building the Lagrangian from two demands

    From Nonlinear electrodynamics we know any Lorentz- and gauge-invariant local theory is a function L(s,p)\mathcal L(s,p)L(s,p) of the two invariants s=−14FμνFμν=12(E2−B2),p=E⃗⋅B⃗.s=-\tfrac14 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12(E^2-B^2), \qquad p=\vec E\cdot\vec B.s=−41​Fμν​Fμν=21​(E2−B2),p=E⋅B. We want the one that (i) →s\to s→s at small field, so it looks Maxwellian, and (ii) has a maximal electric field, because string theory bounds EEE on a brane.

    The particle analogy tells us how: put the field invariant inside a square root. The simplest candidate, \mathcal L = -b^2\sqrt{1 - \frac{E^2-B^2}{b^2}} + b^2 = -b^2\sqrt{1 - \frac{2s}{b^2}} + b^2, \tag{1} does both. With B=0B=0B=0 it is −b21−E2/b2+b2-b^2\sqrt{1-E^2/b^2}+b^2−b21−E2/b2​+b2, real only for E≤bE\le bE≤b — the maximal field. For s≪b2s\ll b^2s≪b2 the root expands as −b2(1−s/b2)+b2=s+O(s2)-b^2(1-s/b^2)+b^2 = s+\mathcal O(s^2)−b2(1−s/b2)+b2=s+O(s2), whose leading term is exactly Maxwell. The additive +b2+b^2+b2 enforces L(0)=0\mathcal L(0)=0L(0)=0, cancelling the constant the root leaves behind.

    Genuine Born–Infeld also keeps the second invariant ppp: \boxed{\;\mathcal L = -b^2\sqrt{1 - \frac{2s}{b^2} - \frac{p^2}{b^4}} + b^2\;}. \tag{2} The p2p^2p2 term is what makes Born–Infeld non-birefringent: every polarization propagates at the same speed even in a strong background. Generic nonlinear theories split polarizations, so the vacuum acts like a birefringent crystal; Born–Infeld is the unique one that does not. That is its signature virtue.

    The determinant form

    One rewrite makes everything portable across dimensions: \boxed{\;\mathcal L = -b^2\sqrt{-\det\!\Big(\eta_{\mu\nu} + \tfrac{1}{b}F_{\mu\nu}\Big)} + b^2\;}. \tag{3}

    Why it equals (2) in 4D is pure algebra. Since η+F/b=η (1+ηF/b)\eta+F/b = \eta\,(1 + \eta F/b)η+F/b=η(1+ηF/b) and det⁡η=−1\det\eta=-1detη=−1, −det⁡ ⁣(η+1bF)=det⁡ ⁣(1+1b ηF),-\det\!\Big(\eta+\tfrac1b F\Big) = \det\!\Big(1+\tfrac1b\,\eta F\Big),−det(η+b1​F)=det(1+b1​ηF), and (setting b=1b=1b=1 for readability) the matrix 1+ηF1+\eta F1+ηF in mostly-plus signature is 1+ηF=(1ExEyEzEx1Bz−ByEy−Bz1BxEzBy−Bx1).1+\eta F = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & E_x & E_y & E_z\\ E_x & 1 & B_z & -B_y\\ E_y & -B_z & 1 & B_x\\ E_z & B_y & -B_x & 1\end{pmatrix}.1+ηF=​1Ex​Ey​Ez​​Ex​1−Bz​By​​Ey​Bz​1−Bx​​Ez​−By​Bx​1​​. Its determinant collects into two pieces. The terms quadratic in the field (one off-diagonal factor from each of the symmetric pairs) give −E2+B2=−2s-E^2+B^2=-2s−E2+B2=−2s. The purely quartic terms (permutations touching no diagonal entry) assemble into −∑iEi2Bi2−2∑i<jEiEjBiBj=−(E⃗⋅B⃗)2=−p2.-\sum_i E_i^2 B_i^2 - 2\sum_{i<j}E_iE_jB_iB_j = -(\vec E\cdot\vec B)^2 = -p^2.−∑i​Ei2​Bi2​−2∑i<j​Ei​Ej​Bi​Bj​=−(E⋅B)2=−p2. So det⁡(1+ηF)=1−2s−p2\det(1+\eta F)=1-2s-p^2det(1+ηF)=1−2s−p2, i.e. with bbb restored 1−2s/b2−p2/b41-2s/b^2-p^2/b^41−2s/b2−p2/b4 — exactly the argument of the root in (2). This is an exact identity, not an expansion.

    The determinant form buys generality. Lorentz invariance is manifest because det⁡(η+F/b)\det(\eta+F/b)det(η+F/b) is a scalar, and the determinant makes sense in any number of dimensions, whereas the "sss and ppp" form is special to 4D — only there is ppp a single scalar. This is why (3), not (2), is what travels to the brane.

    Finite self-energy of a point charge

    Born and Infeld built this in 1934 for a specific reason: in Maxwell theory a point charge has infinite self-energy, and they wanted a classical electron with a finite one. Set B⃗=0\vec B=0B=0, so L=−b21−E2/b2+b2\mathcal L=-b^2\sqrt{1-E^2/b^2}+b^2L=−b21−E2/b2​+b2. The constitutive relation (from D⃗=∂L/∂E⃗\vec D=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\vec ED=∂L/∂E, see Nonlinear electrodynamics) is \vec D = \frac{\vec E}{\sqrt{1-E^2/b^2}}, \qquad\text{invert:}\qquad \vec E = \frac{\vec D}{\sqrt{1+D^2/b^2}}. \tag{4} This carries two bounds: E≤bE\le bE≤b (the maximal field) and E≤DE\le DE≤D, with E→bE\to bE→b only as D→∞D\to\inftyD→∞. The energy density is the Legendre transform H=D⃗⋅E⃗−L\mathcal H=\vec D\cdot\vec E-\mathcal LH=D⋅E−L, which in terms of DDD is \mathcal H = b^2\sqrt{1+\frac{D^2}{b^2}} - b^2. \tag{5}

    The divergence lives entirely in the energy, not the field. Gauss's law ∇⋅D⃗=ρ\nabla\cdot\vec D=\rho∇⋅D=ρ is linear, so D⃗\vec DD around a point charge QQQ is the ordinary Coulomb field D∼Q/(4πr2)D\sim Q/(4\pi r^2)D∼Q/(4πr2), same as Maxwell. What changes is how the energy density behaves where DDD is huge:

    • Maxwell (D=ED=ED=E): H∼12D2∼r−4\mathcal H\sim\tfrac12 D^2\sim r^{-4}H∼21​D2∼r−4, so ∫ ⁣H d3x∼∫r−4⋅r2 dr=∫r−2 dr\int\!\mathcal H\,d^3x \sim \int r^{-4}\cdot r^2\,dr=\int r^{-2}\,dr∫Hd3x∼∫r−4⋅r2dr=∫r−2dr diverges at r=0r=0r=0.
    • Born–Infeld (large DDD): EEE is capped at bbb, so H≃bD∼r−2\mathcal H\simeq bD\sim r^{-2}H≃bD∼r−2 grows only linearly in DDD. Then ∫ ⁣H d3x∼∫r−2⋅r2 dr=∫dr\int\!\mathcal H\,d^3x\sim\int r^{-2}\cdot r^2\,dr=\int dr∫Hd3x∼∫r−2⋅r2dr=∫dr converges.

    The cap on EEE softens r−4r^{-4}r−4 to r−2r^{-2}r−2 and saves the integral. Substituting the Coulomb DDD into (5) and integrating over all space, the radial integral is ∫0∞(1+x4−x2) dx=Γ(14)2/(6π)\int_0^\infty(\sqrt{1+x^4}-x^2)\,dx=\Gamma(\tfrac14)^2/(6\sqrt\pi)∫0∞​(1+x4​−x2)dx=Γ(41​)2/(6π​), and U_Q = \frac{1}{4\pi}\cdot\frac13\,\Gamma(\tfrac14)^2\, b^{1/2}\,Q^{3/2} \approx \frac{1}{4\pi}\,(4.382)\, b^{1/2}Q^{3/2}. \tag{6} The tell-tale is the power: Q3/2Q^{3/2}Q3/2, not the Maxwell Q2Q^2Q2. A nonlinear theory does not scale quadratically with charge. With b=1/(2πα′)b=1/(2\pi\alpha')b=1/(2πα′) and ℓs=α′\ell_s=\sqrt{\alpha'}ℓs​=α′​, the finite radius that regulates the charge is the string length — the string smears the point out over ℓs\ell_sℓs​, the cutoff Maxwell always needed but never had.

    The D-brane action

    This is where the module lands. Electric and magnetic fields on D-branes showed, by T-duality, that a constant electric field on a brane matches a moving dual brane with Lagrangian ∝−1−(2πα′E)2\propto -\sqrt{1-(2\pi\alpha' E)^2}∝−1−(2πα′E)2​, and a constant magnetic field matches a tilted dual brane with ∝−1+(2πα′B)2\propto -\sqrt{1+(2\pi\alpha' B)^2}∝−1+(2πα′B)2​. Both are the electric and magnetic slots of one determinant. Identifying b=1/(2πα′)b=1/(2\pi\alpha')b=1/(2πα′), so that F/b=2πα′FF/b = 2\pi\alpha' FF/b=2πα′F, the determinant form (3) becomes the local abelian Born–Infeld action on a Dppp-brane: \boxed{\;\mathcal L = -T_p\sqrt{-\det\!\big(\eta_{mn} + 2\pi\alpha'\,F_{mn}\big)}\;}. \tag{7} Here m,n=0,…,pm,n=0,\dots,pm,n=0,…,p run over the brane world-volume and TpT_pTp​ is the brane tension. Two things happened to the constants of (3):

    • The overall b2b^2b2 and the additive +b2+b^2+b2 were free normalizations there; on the brane the scale is fixed to TpT_pTp​, and the constant is now physical — at F=0F=0F=0, L→−Tp\mathcal L\to -T_pL→−Tp​, i.e. minus the tension, the rest energy of a static brane.
    • The scale bbb survives only inside the root, as 1/b=2πα′1/b=2\pi\alpha'1/b=2πα′. The dimensionless combination 2πα′F2\pi\alpha' F2πα′F is the same one that entered the open-string boundary condition in Maxwell fields coupled to open strings — the field turns nonlinear precisely when 2πα′F∼12\pi\alpha' F\sim 12πα′F∼1.

    Maxwell is the low-field limit. Expand the root to second order in FFF (using the determinant identity above with F→2πα′FF\to 2\pi\alpha' FF→2πα′F): L=−Tp(1+(2πα′)24FmnFmn+⋯).\mathcal L = -T_p\Big(1 + \frac{(2\pi\alpha')^2}{4}F_{mn}F^{mn} + \cdots\Big).L=−Tp​(1+4(2πα′)2​Fmn​Fmn+⋯). The constant −Tp-T_p−Tp​ is the rest energy; the F2F^2F2 term is −Tp(2πα′)24FmnFmn-T_p\tfrac{(2\pi\alpha')^2}{4}F_{mn}F^{mn}−Tp​4(2πα′)2​Fmn​Fmn, the Maxwell Lagrangian, with the prefactor Tp(2πα′)2T_p(2\pi\alpha')^2Tp​(2πα′)2 setting the gauge coupling. The remaining terms are higher powers of 2πα′F2\pi\alpha' F2πα′F. The brane's photon obeys Maxwell only because we usually sit at F≪1/α′F\ll 1/\alpha'F≪1/α′; push the field toward the string scale and the square root's nonlinearities take over.

    Three senses of "exact" (don't conflate them)

    This note uses "exact" three times, meaning three different things:

    1. The determinant identity (2)↔\leftrightarrow↔(3) is exact algebra.
    2. The point-charge energy (6) is exact within classical 3+1 Born–Infeld electrodynamics.
    3. The brane action (7) is exact in FFF for constant abelian fields at disk (tree) level, and is the leading term for slowly varying fields. Derivative corrections (∂F\partial F∂F), non-abelian ordering, and string loops all live outside the determinant.

    Open questions

    • The brane action (7) is fixed here by matching the pure-electric and pure-magnetic cases separately. That the same determinant handles simultaneous constant E⃗\vec EE and B⃗\vec BB is strongly motivated but not re-derived from a full disk partition function (the beta-function route in DBI action as a preview confirms it).
    • The Maxwell prefactor Tp(2πα′)2T_p(2\pi\alpha')^2Tp​(2πα′)2 is the brane gauge coupling gYM−2g_{YM}^{-2}gYM−2​; its exact normalization (factors of gsg_sgs​) is not tracked here.
    • Higher-derivative (∂F\partial F∂F) corrections are not inside the determinant; Born–Infeld is the leading term of an α′\alpha'α′ expansion (see DBI action as a preview).

    Exercise

    The maximal field caps EEE; does the same square root cap BBB? Take the pure-magnetic branch E⃗=0\vec E=0E=0 in (2), L=−b21+B2/b2+b2\mathcal L=-b^2\sqrt{1+B^2/b^2}+b^2L=−b21+B2/b2​+b2, where the magnetic term enters with a plus sign — the opposite signature sign from electric. (a) Is there any bound on BBB? (b) Compute the magnetic response H⃗=−∂L/∂B⃗\vec H=-\partial\mathcal L/\partial\vec BH=−∂L/∂B and its large-BBB limit, and contrast the electric–magnetic asymmetry with the symmetric-looking determinant (7). (c) Why does the brane nonetheless treat EEE and BBB so differently physically?

    Solution

    (a) No bound on BBB. With E⃗=0\vec E=0E=0, s=−12B2s=-\tfrac12 B^2s=−21​B2 and p=0p=0p=0, so the root is 1+B2/b2\sqrt{1+B^2/b^2}1+B2/b2​, real for all BBB. Only the electric field is capped (1−E2/b2\sqrt{1-E^2/b^2}1−E2/b2​ needs E≤bE\le bE≤b); the magnetic root only grows. The maximal field is electric, not magnetic.

    (b) Magnetic response. H⃗=−∂∂B⃗(−b21+B2/b2+b2)=B⃗1+B2/b2.\displaystyle \vec H = -\frac{\partial}{\partial\vec B}\Big(-b^2\sqrt{1+B^2/b^2}+b^2\Big) = \frac{\vec B}{\sqrt{1+B^2/b^2}}.H=−∂B∂​(−b21+B2/b2​+b2)=1+B2/b2​B​. At small BBB, H⃗→B⃗\vec H\to\vec BH→B (Maxwell). At large BBB, H→bH\to bH→b — the response saturates at bbb even though BBB itself is unbounded. Compare the electric relation (4): there D⃗=E⃗/1−E2/b2\vec D=\vec E/\sqrt{1-E^2/b^2}D=E/1−E2/b2​ blows up as E→bE\to bE→b while EEE is capped. The roles of "field" and "response" swap between the electric and magnetic sectors, which is exactly the ±\pm± inside the root — the signature of spacetime, entering the determinant through the ηmn\eta_{mn}ηmn​ with one timelike and ppp spacelike directions.

    (c) Physically, from Electric and magnetic fields on D-branes: an electric field is T-dual to a moving brane, and its speed cannot exceed ccc, which caps EEE at bbb. A magnetic field is T-dual to a tilted brane, and a brane can tilt by any angle — so BBB is unbounded. The 1−E2/b21-E^2/b^21−E2/b2 versus 1+B2/b21+B^2/b^21+B2/b2 asymmetry is the string-theory statement that "you can't beat the speed of light, but you can rotate as much as you like," written inside one Lorentz-invariant determinant.
