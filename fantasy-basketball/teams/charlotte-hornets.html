Charlotte Hornets

Charlotte enters 2026-27 with three enticing fantasy initiators, a crowded frontcourt, and an unfinished 18-player standard roster that must still lose three contracts before opening night.

Desk verdict

As of 2026-07-18. Charlotte won 44 games in 2025-26, then traded LaMelo Ball, Josh Green, and Miles Bridges for Naz Reid, Grayson Allen, Royce O'Neale, Mouhamadou Gueye, and a large draft-pick haul. The fantasy result is concentrated creation: Coby White becomes the starting point guard, while Brandon Miller and Kon Knueppel should absorb much of the vacated usage. Reid has his first clear path to a 30-minute starting role, but the center minutes behind and beside him are a genuine three-player contest.

The best draft bets are Knueppel and Miller at a reasonable price, then White if calf-risk and lead-guard skepticism are already discounted. Naz Reid is a role-upside pick rather than a proven early-round category profile. Moussa Diabaté is the specialist target; Ryan Kalkbrenner and Hannes Steinbach are contingent-value bigs.

2025-26 team baseline

Charlotte finished 44-38 and ninth in the East, beat Miami in the first Play-In game, and lost 121-90 to Orlando. Basketball-Reference's possession model graded the Hornets at a 96.8 pace (26th), 119.4 offensive rating (fifth), 114.4 defensive rating (12th), +5.0 net rating (eighth), and +4.48 SRS (eighth). The team made 16.4 threes per game at 37.8% and ranked second in rebound percentage and offensive-rebound percentage in the official game notes.

The split is central to the forecast: Charlotte began 16-28 and closed 28-10. The Lee-extension release says the Hornets led the NBA in net rating after January 1, with the No. 1 offense, fifth-ranked defense, league-best rebound percentage, 17.3 threes, and 19.0 second-chance points per game. Ball and Bridges were major pieces of that surge, so the system signal is real but not directly portable.

Role battles and fantasy consequences

  • Lead guard: White is the starter, but his career has been more combo guard than table-setter. Miller and Knueppel should share initiation; Anderson's path is clearer in dynasty than redraft.
  • Usage: Ball and Bridges vacated roughly 37 points and 10 assists per game from last year's roster. Miller has the clearest scoring ceiling; Knueppel's percentages and threes make his fantasy floor safer.
  • Frontcourt: Reid can play both frontcourt spots. Diabaté supplies rebounding and switchability, Kalkbrenner supplies rim protection and elite finishing, and Steinbach supplies the most connective offense. Foul trouble and matchup-based starts may keep all three volatile.
  • Veteran wings: Allen has the best fantasy per-minute game, but he missed 31 regular-season games last year. O'Neale is the steadier real-life reserve. Finney-Smith is not being projected into the rotation.
  • Development crunch: Salaün, McNeeley, Anderson, and the two-ways need NBA reps, yet Charlotte also added veterans. A deadline sell-off would radically improve their March outlook.
