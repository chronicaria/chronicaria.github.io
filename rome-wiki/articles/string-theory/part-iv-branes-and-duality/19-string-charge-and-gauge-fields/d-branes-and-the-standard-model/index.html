    D-branes and the Standard Model

    Stack N D-branes on top of each other and the open strings living on them assemble into a U(N) gauge theory — non-abelian force fields of exactly the kind that runs the Standard Model — while strings stretched from one stack to another become charged matter. This is why D-branes turned string theory into a candidate description of particle physics, and it all follows from one fact: an open string endpoint remembers which brane it sits on.

    This is an orientation note, not a derivation of the Standard Model. Everything below rests on one idea: endpoint labels are gauge data. Once an endpoint behaves like a charge (Endpoint charge), labeling which brane it touches becomes a gauge representation.

    Two endpoints, two labels

    An open string has two endpoints. Put a stack of NNN coincident branes in front of it and each endpoint lands on one of the NNN branes. So every open string state carries two labels — one per endpoint:

    ∣ i,j ⟩,i,j=1,…,N,|\,i,j\,\rangle,\qquad i,j = 1,\dots,N,∣i,j⟩,i,j=1,…,N,

    where iii is the brane the first endpoint touches and jjj the brane the second endpoint touches. These are the Chan–Paton labels. There are N2N^2N2 such label pairs, hence N2N^2N2 open-string states at each level of the spectrum.

    Collect the N2N^2N2 states of the massless vector — the brane photon of Maxwell fields on branes, now one for each label pair — into an N×NN\times NN×N matrix MijM^i{}_jMij​. Relabeling the NNN identical branes among themselves is a unitary rotation U∈U(N)U\in U(N)U∈U(N); because each endpoint carries a brane label, the two ends rotate oppositely and the matrix transforms in the adjoint:

    M⟼U M U−1,U∈U(N).(1)M \longmapsto U\,M\,U^{-1},\qquad U\in U(N). \tag{1}M⟼UMU−1,U∈U(N).(1)

    That is the entire mechanism. A single brane gives one photon, a U(1)U(1)U(1) Maxwell field (N=1N=1N=1: MMM is a 1×11\times11×1 number, UMU−1=MUMU^{-1}=MUMU−1=M). A stack of NNN gives N2N^2N2 photons packaged as a matrix that mixes under U(N)U(N)U(N) — a non-abelian Yang–Mills gauge field. The gauge index that field theory hands you with no explanation is, here, just the bookkeeping of endpoint labels when several identical branes coincide.

    Why coincidence is essential: strings that go massless

    The diagonal entries MiiM^i{}_iMii​ are strings with both ends on the same brane. The off-diagonal MijM^i{}_jMij​ (i≠ji\ne ji=j) are strings stretched between two different branes in the stack. A stretched string has minimum length equal to the brane separation, and a string of length ℓ\ellℓ carries energy ∼ℓ/α′\sim \ell/\alpha'∼ℓ/α′ (tension times length). So an off-diagonal state has mass

    m  ∼  separationα′.(2)m \;\sim\; \frac{\text{separation}}{\alpha'}. \tag{2}m∼α′separation​.(2)

    Pull the branes apart and the off-diagonal strings turn massive; only the NNN diagonal photons stay massless, leaving U(1)NU(1)^NU(1)N. Bring the branes together until they coincide and the separation goes to zero, so all N2N^2N2 strings become massless at once — the moment the full U(N)U(N)U(N) switches on.

    Brane separation is therefore a Higgs field. Coincident branes give unbroken U(N)U(N)U(N); separated branes give the symmetry Higgsed down to U(1)NU(1)^NU(1)N by the masses (2). Non-abelian symmetry is restored by coincidence, and geometry — how far apart the branes sit — controls which gauge bosons stay light. In a mathematician's terms: turning on the brane-separation modulus is exactly the Higgs mechanism, with the W-boson mass set by the modulus.

    Why this looks like the Standard Model

    The Standard Model gauge group is

    SU(3)c×SU(2)w×U(1)Y.SU(3)_c \times SU(2)_w \times U(1)_Y.SU(3)c​×SU(2)w​×U(1)Y​.

    A stack of 3 branes gives U(3)U(3)U(3) and a stack of 2 gives U(2)U(2)U(2), and since

    U(N)=SU(N)×U(1)(infinitesimally, u(N)≅su(N)⊕u(1)),U(N) = SU(N)\times U(1) \quad\text{(infinitesimally, } \mathfrak{u}(N)\cong\mathfrak{su}(N)\oplus\mathfrak{u}(1)\text{)},U(N)=SU(N)×U(1)(infinitesimally, u(N)≅su(N)⊕u(1)),

    those stacks contain the SU(3)SU(3)SU(3) of color and the SU(2)SU(2)SU(2) of the weak force. Three color branes and two "weak" branes reproduce the non-abelian skeleton of particle physics for free.

    The word "contain" is carrying weight, because the bare stack gives too much:

    U(3)×U(2)  =  SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)×U(1).U(3)\times U(2) \;=\; SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)\times U(1).U(3)×U(2)=SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1)×U(1).

    There are two leftover U(1)U(1)U(1) factors, not one. A realistic model must pick which single combination is the observed hypercharge U(1)YU(1)_YU(1)Y​ and make the other U(1)U(1)U(1) gauge bosons massive or decoupled. That takes extra branes and anomaly bookkeeping beyond this module — but the point stands: the gauge group of nature falls out of stacking a handful of branes.

    Where matter comes from: strings between stacks

    The gauge bosons above are strings with both ends on the same stack. Matter is the other case — a string with one endpoint on stack AAA and the other on stack BBB. It carries a charge under both gauge groups, one from each endpoint, so it transforms in the bifundamental representation:

    (NA, N‾B),i.e.M⟼UA M UB−1.(3)(\mathbf{N}_A,\ \overline{\mathbf{N}}_B),\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad M \longmapsto U_A\,M\,U_B^{-1}. \tag{3}(NA​, NB​),i.e.M⟼UA​MUB−1​.(3)

    Compare with the adjoint (1): a single-stack string sees UMU−1U M U^{-1}UMU−1, the same group at both ends; a two-stack string sees UAMUB−1U_A M U_B^{-1}UA​MUB−1​, different groups — one endpoint a fundamental of AAA, the other an antifundamental of BBB. Matter charges are once again nothing but endpoint data: each end of the string knows which stack it touches.

    Concretely, a colored quark is an open string with one endpoint on the color stack — the endpoint carrying the color index — and the other on a "weak" stack carrying the SU(2)SU(2)SU(2) index. The three colors are the three choices of which color brane that endpoint lands on: three parallel branes, three colors. Reversing the string's orientation swaps its endpoints, hence swaps fundamental ↔\leftrightarrow↔ antifundamental, and an oppositely oriented string is the antiquark. This is the "flip the endpoints, flip the charge" of a single endpoint in Endpoint charge, now promoted to representation theory: N→N‾\mathbf{N}\to\overline{\mathbf{N}}N→N in place of q→−qq\to -qq→−q.

    Honest scope

    This is model-building orientation, not a derivation of the Standard Model from this vault. A realistic construction needs ingredients not developed here: compactification geometry; intersecting or orientifold branes to get chiral matter (a bare parallel stack gives left- and right-handed quarks with equal charges — non-chiral, wrong); the extra branes that fix hypercharge; anomaly cancellation; supersymmetry breaking.

    The takeaway is narrower but strong enough for this course. A D-brane does not merely host a single Maxwell field: a stack hosts matrix-valued non-abelian gauge fields, and strings stretched between stacks are automatically charged matter in bifundamental representations. Both facts are forced by the one principle of this module — the endpoint carries a charge and remembers its brane — which is why D-branes became a serious language for building particle physics.

    Open questions

    • Which compactifications produce exactly the Standard Model spectrum, with the right chirality and no unwanted light fields?
    • How do the extra U(1)U(1)U(1) factors from U(3)×U(2)U(3)\times U(2)U(3)×U(2) become massive, and which combination survives as hypercharge U(1)YU(1)_YU(1)Y​?
    • How are Yukawa couplings and the three generations computed — from brane intersection numbers, worldsheet instantons, or geometry?

    Exercise

    A stack of NNN coincident branes gives U(N)U(N)U(N), with N2N^2N2 massless gauge bosons. Suppose you slide one brane a small distance ddd away from a coincident stack of NNN, leaving N−1N-1N−1 branes together and 1 apart.

    (a) Which gauge symmetry survives massless, and how many massless gauge bosons is that? (b) How many gauge bosons acquire mass, and roughly what is their mass? (c) Interpret the result as a Higgs mechanism: what plays the role of the Higgs vacuum expectation value?

    Solution

    Split the NNN brane labels into a block of N−1N-1N−1 coincident branes and a single separated brane. The N2N^2N2 open-string states organize by where their two endpoints sit.

    (a) Strings with both endpoints on the N−1N-1N−1 coincident branes are unaffected and stay massless: that block is a coincident stack of N−1N-1N−1, so it supports U(N−1)U(N-1)U(N−1), with (N−1)2(N-1)^2(N−1)2 massless gauge bosons. The lone separated brane still has strings with both ends on itself — one more massless photon, a U(1)U(1)U(1). So the surviving massless gauge group is U(N) ⟶ U(N−1)×U(1),U(N)\ \longrightarrow\ U(N-1)\times U(1),U(N) ⟶ U(N−1)×U(1), with (N−1)2+1=N2−2N+2(N-1)^2 + 1 = N^2 - 2N + 2(N−1)2+1=N2−2N+2 massless gauge bosons.

    (b) The remaining states are the off-diagonal strings with one endpoint on the N−1N-1N−1 block and the other on the separated brane. These fill the two off-diagonal blocks of the N×NN\times NN×N matrix — N−1N-1N−1 states with the separated brane as the second label, N−1N-1N−1 with it as the first — so 2(N−1)2(N-1)2(N−1) in all, one block the orientation reversal of the other. Each is now stretched a distance ddd, so by (2) each acquires mass m∼dα′.m \sim \frac{d}{\alpha'}.m∼α′d​. Check the count: [(N−1)2+1]+2(N−1)=N2\big[(N-1)^2 + 1\big] + 2(N-1) = N^2[(N−1)2+1]+2(N−1)=N2. All N2N^2N2 states accounted for.

    (c) These 2(N−1)2(N-1)2(N−1) massive vectors are exactly the "W-bosons" of the broken symmetry: U(N)→U(N−1)×U(1)U(N)\to U(N-1)\times U(1)U(N)→U(N−1)×U(1) is a Higgsing, and the brane separation ddd is the Higgs expectation value. At d=0d=0d=0 (coincident) the symmetry is restored and all N2N^2N2 bosons are massless; turning on d≠0d\ne 0d=0 is turning on the Higgs vev, giving the off-diagonal gauge bosons mass ∝d\propto d∝d. Geometry is the Higgs field.
