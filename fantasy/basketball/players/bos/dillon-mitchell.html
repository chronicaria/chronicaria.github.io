Dillon Mitchell

Dillon Mitchell is a defense-and-athleticism dynasty flier whose six-steal Summer League eruption showed the fantasy hook, but his unsigned status and near-absent jumper make even a two-way projection conditional.

Mitchell has zero simultaneous minutes in the Boston Celtics 240-minute baseline. The first line is a conditional appearance projection if Boston awards him one of its open two-way slots; it is not a claim that the contract already exists.

Nine-category: no redraft value without a contract and activation, but steals/rebounds can stream in a surprise role. Points: low scoring and FT risk reduce appeal. Dynasty: deep stash for managers betting that elite athletic defense earns an NBA floor and skill development later unlocks minutes.

Why the line looks like this

At St. John's, Mitchell averaged 8.3 points, 7.0 rebounds, 3.0 assists and 1.3 steals in 28.2 minutes. He shot 55.9% overall but 6.7% from three and 49.4% at the line. NBA's official draft profile framed defense as the most translatable skill and highlighted a 38.5-inch vertical, lob/cut finishing and connective passing while identifying the jumper as the central obstacle.

Mitchell's 24-point, eight-rebound, six-steal Summer League game against Charlotte displayed the best-case fantasy template. It did not answer how a non-shooter fits beside Boston's centers in an NBA half court. At the July 18 cutoff, roster sources still listed Boston holding his draft rights without a signed standard or two-way contract. Calling him the favorite for an open two-way is an inference from draft investment and summer role.

Sources: NBA official 2026 draft profile, CelticsBlog Summer League Charlotte recap, Celtics community event with Mitchell and Cenac, RealGM roster-state cross-check.

Category shape

  • Projectable strengths: steals, rebounds, cuts, transition finishes and low-usage passing.
  • Swing skill: any credible corner three changes his lineup viability.
  • Costs: FT% could be severe; half-court scoring and blocks are uncertain.
  • Build fit: deep punt-FT/punt-points dynasty; not a conventional 2026-27 redraft selection.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: no Boston contract, with rights retained while he develops elsewhere.
  • Base conditional: wins a two-way, appears in 15 NBA games for five minutes and spends most of the season in Maine.
  • Ceiling: becomes Boston's preferred defensive reserve, reaches 15-18 minutes after injuries and supplies roughly 1.5 steals plus strong wing rebounds; deep 9-cat value despite poor FT/threes.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons — Texas 2022-23 (38 GP, 4.3 PPG, 3.9 RPG, .636 FG%, .405 FT%) and 2023-24 (34 GP, 9.6 PPG, 7.5 RPG, 1.4 APG, 1.0 SPG, .585 FG%, .598 FT%); Cincinnati 2024-25 (35 GP, all starts, 9.9 PPG, 6.9 RPG, 1.7 APG, 1.4 SPG, .614 FG%, .294 3P%, .395 FT%); St. John's 2025-26 (37 GP, 25 starts, 8.3 PPG, 7.0 RPG, 3.0 APG, 1.3 SPG in 28.2 MPG, .559 FG%, .067 3P%, .494 FT%). Third-team All-Big East, inaugural Big East All-Defensive Team, and Big East All-Tournament in 2025-26. Draft: 2026, Round 2, Pick 40, Boston Celtics.

No NBA record — his most recent season is 2025-26 at St. John's, and as of July 18, 2026 he remains unsigned with Boston holding his draft rights.

Durability: Never a problem in college — 38, 34, 35 and 37 games across four seasons with no significant injury absences on record. Trajectory: The record shows a hyper-athletic forward who kept adding pieces — rebounding as a sophomore, steals at Cincinnati, playmaking (3.0 APG) at St. John's — while the jumper and free-throw stroke never arrived (career-worst .067 3P% and .494 FT% as a senior). In 2026-27 he competes for one of Boston's open two-way slots and a Maine-first developmental role behind the standard-roster wings. He becomes fantasy-relevant only if he signs, injuries open 15+ NBA minutes, and his steals-plus-rebounds defensive profile survives the spacing cost he imposes — a deep 9-cat/dynasty scenario, not a redraft one.
