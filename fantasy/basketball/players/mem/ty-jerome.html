Ty Jerome

Correction 2026-07-18: base GP lowered from 58 to 45. The injury ledger described Jerome as having "missed 57 games, including the first 46" — that was wrong in both halves. He played 15 games in 2025-26 and missed 67. An October calf sprain cost him the first half of the season, his debut came January 31, he never played more than three consecutive games all year, and he ended the season on an eleven-game absence with a left-ankle sprain. A 58-game base was close to a four-fold jump off the actual sample. The pattern that matters is not one long absence but the failure to string appearances together once back, which is the harder thing to project out of. A crowded Memphis guard rotation and unresolved roster status compound rather than offset it.

Correction 2026-07-18 Trimmed from 24 to 22 base minutes to make room for Walter Clayton Jr., who was previously carried at zero base minutes despite averaging 25.0 MPG with 5.7 assists across 24 Memphis games after the February trade. Jerome keeps the lead-guard label; the two now share the backcourt rather than Jerome holding it alone.

Nine-category: top 70-115 per game if retained, driven by efficient guard shooting, assists, threes and manageable turnovers. Points: assists and scoring make him a clear standard-league option when starting. Dynasty: useful contender guard but limited long-term insulation at age 29 with recurrent role/health uncertainty.

Range, strengths and risks

  • Strengths: assists, threes, both percentages and relatively low turnovers for a lead guard.
  • Liabilities: low rebounds/blocks, modest minutes ceiling and fragile availability.
  • Floor: traded/waived into a smaller role, or calf trouble recurs; no Memphis fantasy value.
  • Base: survives consolidation and starts at 24 minutes with Pippen/Spencer sharing creation.
  • Ceiling: fully healthy 28-minute starter approaching 18 points and six assists, a top-60-to-80 category outcome.
  • Injury/transaction branches: July roster reporting expects one of Jerome/Pippen/Clayton to be moved; a Jerome departure makes Pippen, Spencer and Clayton the primary beneficiaries.

The raw 19.7-point baseline is a 15-game sample and should not be carried forward without regression. Conversely, his .474/.420/.875 shooting and 5.7 assists show a fantasy-friendly rate profile. The principal disagreement is not skill but whether Memphis retains him and trusts a normal workload. Sports Illustrated / Memphis On SI, 2026-07-09

Career record

Pre-NBA: Virginia, three seasons (2016-17 through 2018-19). 4.3 points in 13.9 minutes on .473/.397 as a freshman; 10.6 points, 3.1 rebounds and 3.9 assists in 30.8 minutes on .421/.379/.905 as a sophomore; 13.6 points, 4.2 rebounds and 5.5 assists in 33.9 minutes on .435/.399/.736 as a junior on the 2019 national championship team. Second-team All-ACC in 2019 and third-team in 2018; Bob Cousy Award finalist. Draft: 2019, first round, 24th overall, Philadelphia — traded on draft night, first to Boston (with the 33rd pick, for the rights to Matisse Thybulle), then immediately from Boston to Phoenix alongside Aron Baynes for a future first.

No season involved a mid-year trade; the team changes all happened in the offseason, and the October 2022 Houston stop lasted one day before he was waived and signed a two-way deal with Golden State.

Fantasy history: 2024-25 was his first fantasy-relevant season and the only one with a verifiable public finish — roughly top-100 in eight-category leagues, produced entirely in 19.9 minutes off the bench, alongside a third-place finish in Sixth Man of the Year voting. Earlier seasons were too small or too inefficient to rank meaningfully, and 2025-26's 15 games make any season-end rank meaningless regardless of the per-game line. Points-league shape is worse than category shape at every stage of his career: he has never carried the minutes or the rebounding base to accumulate, while the category build gets paid for elite free-throw percentage on low volume, high three-point makes, and a turnover rate low for a lead guard.

Durability: 31, 33, 48, 45, 2, 70, 15. The pattern is the concern, not any single event. A right ankle sprain in October 2019 opened his rookie year; a severe left ankle sprain carried into 2020-21 and pushed his return to a February G League assignment; groin surgery ended 2021-22 on March 8, 2022; 2022-23's 45 games were partly a two-way roster designation rather than health. 2023-24 is the outlier — a right ankle impingement first reported October 27, 2023 led to surgery on January 25, 2024 and cost him all but two games. 2024-25's 70 games is the only genuinely clean season of his career. 2025-26 broke immediately: a high-grade right calf strain in the October 17 preseason game at Miami, initially projected at four weeks and then re-set at six to nine, kept him out until a January 31 debut against Minnesota on a strict minutes cap; he then never played more than three straight games, missed time in early March with a left thigh contusion, and finished the year having missed the last 11 in a row with a sprained left ankle.

Trajectory: Jerome's arc is a late, real skill jump sitting on an availability record that has never supported it. Through age 25 he was a marginal shooter (.336, .446, .378 from the field) surviving on passing and free-throw touch; the change came in Cleveland at 27, when .516 from the field and .872 at the line in a defined bench-creator role turned him into a genuinely efficient scorer, and the 2025-26 Memphis sample — 19.7 points and 5.7 assists in 22.6 minutes, with 2.8 threes — extended that on a bigger usage share. Per-minute this is now roughly a 31-point, 9-assist per-36 player, which is not a minutes illusion; the production per possession is legitimately good. What the history does not support is projecting it across a full season, because he has played 70-plus games once in seven years and the 2025-26 calf strain was high-grade and recurred into secondary soft-tissue absences. At 29 in a crowded Memphis guard room with active trade chatter, the rate profile argues for a top-70 per-game player and the durability record argues for pricing him as a 50-to-60 game asset — those two facts are the entire investment case, and they point in opposite directions.
