Drake Powell

Drake Powell is a defense-first Year 2 wing whose real-life pathway remains clearer than his fantasy profile after a low-efficiency rookie season.

Team
Brooklyn Nets

Outside the 262-player projection pool — see the dossier for status.

Snapshot

  • Team / role: Brooklyn Nets developmental SG/SF, initially behind Keon Ellis, Josh Minott and Terance Mann.
  • 2025-26 baseline: 63 games, 21.0 MPG, 6.5 PTS, 1.8 REB, 1.4 AST, 0.6 STL and 0.2 BLK on .402/.280/.896 shooting. Basketball-Reference, accessed 2026-07-18
  • Team context: Brooklyn added another lottery guard in Mikel Brown Jr. and veteran perimeter defense in Ellis, making Powell's route to on-ball volume especially narrow.

Fantasy translation

  • Redraft: outside the standard-league pool; only a 20-team or dynasty watchlist.
  • Points: limited by low usage, rebounds and assists even if his defense earns spot starts.
  • Dynasty: hold only in deep formats where an athletic 3-and-D wing can be given multiple development years.

Role, floor and ceiling

Powell's floor is regular Long Island time and fewer than 10 NBA minutes on healthy nights. His ceiling is a 20-minute defensive-wing role with roughly seven points, one three and one combined steal/block, but that still may not create standard nine-category value.

Risk monitor

Powell began his rookie season after right-knee surgery and appeared on the official October injury report with a right-ankle sprain; later left-knee management also interrupted his season. The bigger fantasy problem is skill translation: .280 from three and modest event production did not compensate for a low-usage role. NBA injury report, 2025-10-26

Career record

Pre-NBA: One season at North Carolina (2024-25), 37 games with 24 starts, 25.6 MPG, 7.4 PTS, 3.4 REB, 1.1 AST, 0.7 STL, 0.7 BLK, 1.0 3PM on .483/.379/.648 shooting. A 2024 McDonald's All-American out of Northwood High School (Pittsboro, NC), he was recruited as an elite athlete and defender rather than a scorer, and his freshman usage reflected that. Draft: 2025, round 1, pick 22 by Atlanta; draft rights traded to Brooklyn.

Durability: 63 of 82 games as a rookie. He entered the season coming off right-knee surgery and appeared on the October injury report with a right-ankle sprain; left-knee management cost additional time later in the year. No single absence was long, but the knee history is now two-sided.

Trajectory: One NBA season establishes him as a rotation-caliber defender with no offensive base — 21 minutes a night produced 6.5 points on 40 percent shooting, and the .280 from three is the number that decides his career. The .896 free-throw mark is the only evidence his stroke is fixable. In 2026-27 he is competing for the same wing minutes as Keon Ellis, Josh Minott and Terance Mann on a roster that keeps adding guards, so his path is subtraction: a Mann or Ellis trade at the deadline plus a jump to league-average three-point volume would put him back near 25 minutes. Absent that, he is a Long Island shuttle with spot-start defensive assignments.
