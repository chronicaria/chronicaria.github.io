    Critical dimension D=26

    Requiring that the quantized string still respect Lorentz symmetry forces the normal-ordering constant to a=-1 and the spacetime dimension to D=26 . Neither number is put in by hand; both are the unique price of consistency.

    Why Lorentz invariance is the constraint

    Light-cone gauge is a bargain with a hidden cost. To make the theory tractable we single out two spacetime directions (X±X^\pmX±) and keep only the D−2D-2D−2 transverse oscillators — but that choice secretly picks a preferred frame, so Lorentz invariance is no longer manifest and has to be checked by hand. The check is whether the rotation generators that mix a transverse direction with a light-cone direction still close into the Lorentz algebra. Classically they do. Quantum mechanically, normal-ordering the oscillators leaves a residue — an anomaly — and it vanishes for exactly one value of DDD and one value of aaa. Rotating your head in 262626 dimensions is fine; in 252525 or 272727 it quietly breaks the theory.

    Two numbers fall out of one condition. Picture two dials, the dimension DDD and the constant aaa, and a single gauge that must read zero. The anomaly has two functionally independent pieces — one scaling like the mode number nnn, one like 1/n1/n1/n — so demanding zero pins both dials at once.

    Convention

    We work in natural units (ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1), mostly-plus signature, with the normal-ordering constant added to the level: \alpha' M^2 = N^\perp + a\quad(\text{open}),\qquad \frac{\alpha'}{4}M^2 = N^\perp + a = \bar N^\perp + a\quad(\text{closed}). \tag{1} Stacking oscillators raises M2M^2M2 in integer steps of the level N⊥N^\perpN⊥; the constant aaa (which will come out negative) slides the whole tower down. The factor of 444 between open and closed is the squared zero-mode normalization, not a second effect (see Open versus closed spectra). Some texts subtract a positive intercept instead, writing α′M2=N⊥−a+\alpha'M^2=N^\perp-a_+α′M2=N⊥−a+​ with a+=−aa_+=-aa+​=−a; then a=−1⇔a+=+1a=-1\Leftrightarrow a_+=+1a=−1⇔a+​=+1. Same physics, sign moved to the other side — always check which form a formula uses before comparing factors.

    Step 1 — the constant as a function of D

    Normal-ordering the D−2D-2D−2 transverse oscillators leaves a finite zero-point (Casimir) energy. Each free harmonic mode of frequency nnn contributes 12n\tfrac12 n21​n, and there are D−2D-2D−2 transverse fields, so the bare shift is E_0=\frac{D-2}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n. \tag{2} This sum diverges; it is regularized honestly with a short-distance cutoff (full derivation in Normal ordering constant). Damping high modes by e−ϵne^{-\epsilon n}e−ϵn and expanding, \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,e^{-\epsilon n}=\frac{e^{-\epsilon}}{(1-e^{-\epsilon})^2}=\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}-\frac{1}{12}+O(\epsilon^2). \tag{3} The 1/ϵ21/\epsilon^21/ϵ2 piece is a local UV vacuum energy, subtracted once and for all by the normal-ordering prescription; the frame-independent finite remnant is −112-\tfrac1{12}−121​ — the number people summarize as "ζ(−1)=−112\zeta(-1)=-\tfrac1{12}ζ(−1)=−121​," but obtained here from a cutoff, never from naively summing the integers. The constant aaa is this energy added to the level, so a=E_0^{\text{fin}}=\frac{D-2}{2}\left(-\frac1{12}\right)=\boxed{\,-\frac{D-2}{24}\,}. \tag{4} Regularization alone ties aaa to DDD, but it does not yet select DDD: it gives a one-parameter family of candidate theories, one per dimension. Something else must pick the member.

    Step 2 — the quick argument: masslessness forces D=26

    Look at the first excited level, built on the light-cone momentum vacuum ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ (shorthand for ∣p+,p⃗T⟩|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∣p+,p​T​⟩). It carries only transverse indices:

    string first excited state little-group content
    open $\alpha_{-1}^{I}, 0;p\rangle$
    closed $\bar\alpha_{-1}^{I}\alpha_{-1}^{J}, 0;p\rangle$

    The key fact from Little group and Wigner classification: a massive particle in DDD dimensions can be boosted to rest, so its polarizations must fill a representation of the rest-frame rotation group SO(D−1)SO(D-1)SO(D−1); a massless particle cannot be brought to rest, and its polarizations only need to fill SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2).

    Here is the squeeze. The open first-excited state supplies exactly D−2D-2D−2 states transforming as an SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2) vector. There is no way to assemble D−2D-2D−2 states into a representation of the larger group SO(D−1)SO(D-1)SO(D−1) — a massive vector needs D−1D-1D−1. So if this level were massive, it could not form a Lorentz multiplet at all, and Lorentz invariance would be lost. The only escape is that the level is massless, where SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2) is the whole requirement and the count matches. (Same logic for the closed level, whose (D−2)⊗(D−2)(D-2)\otimes(D-2)(D−2)⊗(D−2) is precisely the transverse polarization content of massless two-index fields.)

    Impose masslessness on the open formula (1) at N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1: 0 = \alpha' M_1^2 = 1+a \;\Longrightarrow\; a=-1. \tag{5} Combine with (4): -\frac{D-2}{24}=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; \boxed{\,D=26\,}. \tag{6} The closed string gives the identical condition: masslessness at N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=1N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1 again needs D−224=1\tfrac{D-2}{24}=124D−2​=1. The photon and the graviton come out massless in exactly the dimension where the zero-point shift equals one unit of oscillator level. Bump DDD off 262626 and the "photon" acquires a mass it has no room to carry, and Lorentz invariance breaks.

    This argument creaks. It is a necessary condition dressed as a derivation: it assumes the level should fit into a Lorentz multiplet and reads off the dimension that allows it. It never shows Lorentz invariance genuinely survives quantization at all levels. For that we need the algebra itself.

    Step 3 — the honest check: the Lorentz algebra must close

    The sharp statement is operatorial. The Lorentz generators MμνM^{\mu\nu}Mμν must satisfy the Lorentz algebra as quantum operators. Every commutator is automatic except the dangerous one, [M^{-I},M^{-J}]\overset{!}{=}0, \tag{7} which mixes a transverse rotation with a light-cone boost. It looks trivially zero — but in light-cone gauge M−IM^{-I}M−I contains αn−\alpha^-_nαn−​, which the constraints solve as a quadratic expression in the transverse oscillators. Reordering that product picks up commutator terms, so (7) is a real quantum computation. It is the operator that asks, after quantizing in a preferred frame, whether the theory still rotates correctly.

    Carrying out the long, standard contraction with DDD and aaa left free gives, for the closed string, [M^{-I},M^{-J}]=\frac{\text{const}}{(p^+)^2}\sum_{n>0}\left[\left(\frac{D-2}{24}-1\right)n-\frac1n\left(a+\frac{D-2}{24}\right)\right]\left(\alpha^I_{-n}\alpha^J_n-\alpha^J_{-n}\alpha^I_n\right)+(\alpha\leftrightarrow\bar\alpha). \tag{8} Each mode nnn contributes an antisymmetric transverse-rotation operator α−nIαnJ−α−nJαnI\alpha^I_{-n}\alpha^J_n-\alpha^J_{-n}\alpha^I_nα−nI​αnJ​−α−nJ​αnI​; these are nonzero and independent for different nnn, so Lorentz invariance demands the coefficient vanish at every nnn separately. (The open string gives the same bracket with one tower instead of two.) The overall prefactor const/(p+)2\text{const}/(p^+)^2const/(p+)2 depends on the oscillator normalization and drops out of the argument: any nonzero prefactor leaves the per-nnn conditions untouched, since only the bracket's nnn-part and 1/n1/n1/n-part carry the physics.

    A single pair of numbers cannot make α n+β/n\alpha\, n+\beta/nαn+β/n vanish for all positive integers nnn unless α=β=0\alpha=\beta=0α=β=0, so the two pieces must vanish independently: \frac{D-2}{24}-1=0,\qquad a+\frac{D-2}{24}=0. \tag{9} The first equation gives D=26D=26D=26; feeding that into the second gives a=−1a=-1a=−1: \boxed{\,D=26,\qquad a=-1\,}. \tag{10} This is why the two dials are pinned simultaneously: the anomaly has two functionally independent pieces, so the one equation [M−I,M−J]=0[M^{-I},M^{-J}]=0[M−I,M−J]=0 delivers two conditions. Lorentz invariance is genuinely anomalous in every dimension except 262626 — the classical symmetry survives quantization at one value of DDD and nowhere else. The quick argument's masslessness condition (5) is contained in (9): the second equation is just the Casimir relation (4), a=−D−224a=-\tfrac{D-2}{24}a=−24D−2​, while the first forces D=26D=26D=26, and together they give a=−1a=-1a=−1, reproducing (5). So the algebra does not assume masslessness; it derives the D=26D=26D=26 condition the quick argument had to put in by hand, upgrading a plausibility argument into a theorem.

    The same fact, three faces

    • Light-cone / no-ghost: at D=26, a=−1D=26,\ a=-1D=26, a=−1 the would-be negative-norm timelike states decouple; away from it they leak into the physical spectrum. This is the covariant restatement of the anomaly.
    • CFT / Weyl anomaly: the covariant route replaces "[M−I,M−J]=0[M^{-I},M^{-J}]=0[M−I,M−J]=0" with "total central charge =0=0=0," giving cmatter+cgh=D−26=0c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}=D-26=0cmatter​+cgh​=D−26=0. Same 262626, no broken Lorentz symmetry along the way. See Total central charge and D = 26.

    One disease, three symptoms: a finite Casimir subtraction, a closing Lorentz algebra, and a vanishing Weyl anomaly are the same consistency condition wearing different clothes.

    Open questions

    • Only one input is imported rather than derived here: the term-by-term contraction that turns the normal-ordered M−IM^{-I}M−I into the coefficient in (8). It is standard and tedious; this note displays the result and completes the logical argument from it.
    • The cutoff-regularized constant (2)–(4) is summarized; the reusable derivation lives in Normal ordering constant.
    • The superstring analogue is D=10D=10D=10: worldsheet fermions change the zero-point sum and the GSO projection removes the tachyon. That belongs to a superstring note, not here.

    Exercise

    Two parts, both answerable from this note alone.

    (a) Suppose a careless regularization of the Casimir sum in (3) had produced −16-\tfrac16−61​ instead of −112-\tfrac1{12}−121​ (someone kept a spurious factor of 222). Redo Step 1 to get the constant a(D)a(D)a(D), then use the masslessness condition (5) to find the "critical dimension" this mistake predicts.

    (b) Sanity-check the sign. In the correct theory, is the ground state ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ (level N⊥=0N^\perp=0N⊥=0) above, at, or below M2=0M^2=0M2=0 at D=26D=26D=26? Use (1) and (6). What is this state called, and why is the constant aaa the thing that puts it there?

    Solution

    (a) With finite part −16-\tfrac16−61​, equation (4) becomes a=D−22(−16)=−D−212.a=\frac{D-2}{2}\left(-\frac16\right)=-\frac{D-2}{12}.a=2D−2​(−61​)=−12D−2​. Masslessness of the first level still demands a=−1a=-1a=−1 — that condition comes from the mass formula (1) at N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1, which the Casimir error does not touch. So −D−212=−1  ⟹  D=14.-\frac{D-2}{12}=-1 \;\Longrightarrow\; D=14.−12D−2​=−1⟹D=14. Here 141414 is a perfectly good integer, so the "must be a whole number" reflex would not catch this slip: an integer dimension is necessary but not sufficient. The value 262626 is inseparable from the specific number 112\tfrac1{12}121​; the whole edifice rides on getting that regularized constant exactly right, which is why Step 1 insists on an honest cutoff rather than a memorized −112-\tfrac1{12}−121​.

    (b) Set N⊥=0N^\perp=0N⊥=0 and a=−1a=-1a=−1 in the open formula (1): α′M2=0+(−1)=−1\alpha'M^2 = 0+(-1) = -1α′M2=0+(−1)=−1, so M2=−1/α′<0M^2=-1/\alpha' < 0M2=−1/α′<0. The ground state sits below M2=0M^2=0M2=0: it is the tachyon (see Tachyon as instability). The constant puts it there — with no oscillators excited, the state carries only the zero-point shift aaa, so the same a=−1a=-1a=−1 that makes the first level massless drags the ground level to negative M2M^2M2. The masslessness of the photon/graviton and the tachyonic ground state are two readings of one number.
