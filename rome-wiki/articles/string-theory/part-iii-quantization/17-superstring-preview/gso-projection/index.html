    GSO projection

    The GSO projection throws away half the string states — the half with the "wrong" number of worldsheet fermions — and the half that survives has no tachyon and exactly as many bosons as fermions.

    The problem, and the cheap fix

    After building the Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond sectors the spectrum is sick in two ways: the NS ground state is a tachyon (α′M2=−12<0\alpha'M^2=-\tfrac12<0α′M2=−21​<0, an unstable vacuum), and bosons and fermions do not come in equal numbers, so there is no supersymmetry.

    The fix is startlingly cheap. Every state is built by hitting a vacuum with some number of fermionic oscillators. Count that number and ask whether it is even or odd; keep one parity, delete the other. That single yes/no cut deletes the tachyon and, once applied to both sectors, lines the surviving bosonic (NS) and fermionic (R) towers up on a common mass grid with equal counts at every level.

    A fermionic oscillator is a light switch: each mode is either off (absent) or on (present, at most once, since b−rb−r=0b_{-r}b_{-r}=0b−r​b−r​=0). A state is a room full of switches, and its parity is whether an even or odd number are on. GSO keeps only rooms of one parity. The projected NS tower (a tower of spacetime bosons) then matches the projected R tower (a tower of spacetime fermions) state for state at each mass level — but that cross-sector balance is a separate fact, proved below by direct count, not a consequence of the parity cut alone.

    The rule

    Define worldsheet-fermion number FFF = the number of fermionic oscillators (b−rIb^I_{-r}b−rI​ in NS, d−nId^I_{-n}d−nI​ in R) acting on the ground state. The parity operator (−1)F(-1)^F(−1)F has eigenvalue +1+1+1 on even states, −1-1−1 on odd states. GSO keeps a definite parity with the projector

    P_\pm = \tfrac12\bigl(1 \pm (-1)^F\bigr), \qquad P_\pm^2 = P_\pm. \tag{1}

    The word projection is literal: P±P_\pmP±​ is idempotent, so it splits the Hilbert space into a kept image and a discarded kernel. P+P_+P+​ keeps even parity, P−P_-P−​ keeps odd. The superstring's choice:

    • NS sector: keep odd FFF — apply P−P_-P−​. The NS vacuum has F=0F=0F=0 (even), so it is thrown out; the first survivors carry one bbb.
    • R sector: here FFF counts the nonzero modes d−nId^I_{-n}d−nI​ (n≥1n\ge1n≥1) built above a ground family. GSO keeps even FFF on the ∣R1a⟩|R_1^a\rangle∣R1a​⟩ family plus odd FFF on ∣R2a⟩|R_2^a\rangle∣R2a​⟩. Since ∣R1a⟩|R_1^a\rangle∣R1a​⟩ and ∣R2a⟩|R_2^a\rangle∣R2a​⟩ themselves differ by odd zero-mode parity, this is a single uniform parity once the two are counted together.

    The R rule looks lopsided only because the eight zero modes d0Id_0^Id0I​ flip parity "for free" — they do not change M2M^2M2, so they merely relabel the ground family. Which family counts as "kept" is a choice of chirality; see the last section.

    Why the tachyon dies

    The would-be tachyon is the bare NS vacuum ∣NS⟩⊗∣p+,p⃗T⟩|\mathrm{NS}\rangle\otimes|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∣NS⟩⊗∣p+,p​T​⟩ with zero bbb-oscillators — an even number. NS keeps only odd FFF, so it is deleted outright. The lightest survivors are the one-bbb states

    b^I_{-1/2}|\mathrm{NS}\rangle, \qquad \alpha'M^2 = -\tfrac12 + \underbrace{\tfrac12}_{N^\perp} = 0. \tag{2}

    The oscillator b−1/2Ib^I_{-1/2}b−1/2I​ contributes N⊥=12N^\perp=\tfrac12N⊥=21​, exactly cancelling the intercept in α′M2=−12+N⊥\alpha'M^2=-\tfrac12+N^\perpα′M2=−21​+N⊥. GSO has promoted the lightest state from a tachyon to a massless one. The spectrum now starts at M2=0M^2=0M2=0, with the eight transverse polarizations (I=2,…,9I=2,\dots,9I=2,…,9) of a massless photon — a Maxwell field. No instability remains.

    The parity cut hides a second bonus. In the kept NS tower an odd count of half-integer modes forces N⊥N^\perpN⊥ to be half-integer, so α′M2=−12+N⊥\alpha'M^2=-\tfrac12+N^\perpα′M2=−21​+N⊥ lands on integers. The R tower is integer-moded, so its α′M2=N⊥\alpha'M^2=N^\perpα′M2=N⊥ is already integer. The two towers now sit on the same integer mass grid — a prerequisite for pairing them level by level.

    Why the counts balance

    The balance is between the two sectors, not inside either one. All NS states are spacetime bosons; all R states are spacetime fermions. So "bosons === fermions at each level" means the projected NS count equals the projected R count level by level — a match across the NS/R divide. Check it by hand at the massless level.

    states count
    NS (bosons) b−1/2I∣NS⟩b^I_{-1/2}\lvert\mathrm{NS}\rangleb−1/2I​∣NS⟩, I=2,…,9I=2,\dots,9I=2,…,9 8
    R (fermions) GSO-kept ground states ∣R1a⟩\lvert R_1^a\rangle∣R1a​⟩, a=1,…,8a=1,\dots,8a=1,…,8 8

    8 \text{ bosons} \;=\; 8 \text{ fermions}. \tag{3}

    The R sector started with 16=2416=2^416=24 degenerate ground states; the parity cut keeps exactly one eight-state family. So the massless open superstring is a Maxwell field (8 boson polarizations) plus one massless spacetime fermion (8 fermion polarizations) — a supersymmetric multiplet.

    The equality persists at every higher level too — 8,128,1152,…8, 128, 1152, \dots8,128,1152,… on both sides — and that is spacetime supersymmetry, seen through its bookkeeping. What actually does the work is worth separating out: the parity cut fixes which states survive in each sector, but the surviving NS count and the surviving R count then agreeing is a genuine coincidence of two different combinatorial towers. At the massless level it is the statement that the vector and spinor of the transverse rotation group SO(8)SO(8)SO(8) both have dimension 888. That SO(8)SO(8)SO(8) vector/spinor coincidence, and its promotion to every level, is exactly the deep fact this preview quotes rather than proves.

    What we are not proving

    The rule above is exactly right, but why this particular parity cut — and not some other — is the consistent, supersymmetric one is a deeper story. It rests on modular invariance of the one-loop amplitude and mutual locality of the sectors, which need the full RNS/superconformal machinery this preview does not build. Other parity truncations are also self-consistent, but they are not supersymmetric; GSO is the one that is.

    Chirality: the R-sector choice

    Picking ∣R1a⟩|R_1^a\rangle∣R1a​⟩ over ∣R2a⟩|R_2^a\rangle∣R2a​⟩ as the "kept" family is a genuine choice of spacetime chirality. For the open string it is a convention — nothing physical depends on it. For the closed string, where left- and right-movers are projected separately, the relative choice between the two sides is exactly what splits type IIA (opposite chirality) from type IIB (same chirality) — see Type II closed-string orientation.

    Open questions

    • The consistency proof of GSO — modular invariance + mutual locality — is deferred to a full RNS treatment; this vault teaches central-charge/anomaly logic but not the RNS modular-invariance derivation.
    • The Ramond chirality operator is represented here only by the ∣R1a⟩|R_1^a\rangle∣R1a​⟩ vs ∣R2a⟩|R_2^a\rangle∣R2a​⟩ parity split inherited from Neveu-Schwarz and Ramond sectors; its embedding into ten-dimensional Lorentz spinors is not built.

    Exercise

    The GSO parity cut is supposed to be a genuine projector. Take FFF = worldsheet-fermion number and P±=12(1±(−1)F)P_\pm=\tfrac12(1\pm(-1)^F)P±​=21​(1±(−1)F). (a) Verify P+2=P+P_+^2=P_+P+2​=P+​ and that P++P−=1P_++P_-=1P+​+P−​=1, P+P−=0P_+P_-=0P+​P−​=0. (b) In the NS sector, show that the parity (−1)F(-1)^F(−1)F of a state is fixed by its level: integer N⊥N^\perpN⊥ forces even FFF, half-integer N⊥N^\perpN⊥ forces odd FFF. Conclude that the odd-FFF projection keeps exactly the half-integer-N⊥N^\perpN⊥ levels, and hence that every surviving NS state has integer α′M2\alpha'M^2α′M2. (Warn the "switch-flip" tempter off here: flipping one switch adds weight rrr, so it moves you to a different level — it cannot pair states within a level, and indeed each NS level is purely one parity.) (c) Apply the NS rule to the two states ∣NS⟩|\mathrm{NS}\rangle∣NS⟩ and b−1/2I∣NS⟩b^I_{-1/2}|\mathrm{NS}\rangleb−1/2I​∣NS⟩: which survives, and what is its α′M2\alpha'M^2α′M2?

    Solution

    (a) Since (−1)F(-1)^F(−1)F has eigenvalues ±1\pm1±1, it squares to the identity: ((−1)F)2=1\bigl((-1)^F\bigr)^2=1((−1)F)2=1. Then P±2=14(1±(−1)F)2=14(1±2(−1)F+1)=12(1±(−1)F)=P±.P_\pm^2=\tfrac14\bigl(1\pm(-1)^F\bigr)^2=\tfrac14\bigl(1\pm2(-1)^F+1\bigr)=\tfrac12\bigl(1\pm(-1)^F\bigr)=P_\pm.P±2​=41​(1±(−1)F)2=41​(1±2(−1)F+1)=21​(1±(−1)F)=P±​. Adding, P++P−=12(1+(−1)F)+12(1−(−1)F)=1P_++P_-=\tfrac12(1+(-1)^F)+\tfrac12(1-(-1)^F)=1P+​+P−​=21​(1+(−1)F)+21​(1−(−1)F)=1 (they exhaust the space). Multiplying, P+P−=14(1+(−1)F)(1−(−1)F)=14(1−((−1)F)2)=0P_+P_-=\tfrac14\bigl(1+(-1)^F\bigr)\bigl(1-(-1)^F\bigr)=\tfrac14\bigl(1-((-1)^F)^2\bigr)=0P+​P−​=41​(1+(−1)F)(1−(−1)F)=41​(1−((−1)F)2)=0 (the two images are disjoint). So P±P_\pmP±​ are complementary orthogonal projectors — a clean split of the Hilbert space.

    (b) In the NS sector N⊥N^\perpN⊥ collects two contributions: the bosonic modes α−nI\alpha^I_{-n}α−nI​ each add an integer nnn, and the fermionic modes b−rIb^I_{-r}b−rI​ each add a half-integer rrr. With FFF fermionic modes switched on (each used at most once), the fermionic part of N⊥N^\perpN⊥ is a sum of FFF half-integers, which is a half-integer when FFF is odd and an integer when FFF is even. Adding the always-integer bosonic part: N⊥N^\perpN⊥ is half-integer   ⟺  F\iff F⟺F is odd, and integer   ⟺  F\iff F⟺F is even. So (−1)F(-1)^F(−1)F is not free to vary at a fixed level — the level pins it. The odd-FFF (that is, P−P_-P−​) projection therefore keeps precisely the half-integer-N⊥N^\perpN⊥ levels and deletes the integer ones. On the survivors α′M2=−12+N⊥=−12+(half-integer)\alpha'M^2=-\tfrac12+N^\perp=-\tfrac12+(\text{half-integer})α′M2=−21​+N⊥=−21​+(half-integer) is an integer — the NS tower lands on the same grid as the integer-moded R tower.

    This also disarms the tempting "switch-flip" argument for balance. Acting with a fermionic mode b−rIb^I_{-r}b−rI​ does flip parity, but it raises N⊥N^\perpN⊥ by r>0r>0r>0, so it maps a level to a different level — it cannot pair even against odd within a level. In fact each NS level is entirely one parity (all even or all odd), the opposite of a 50/5050/5050/50 split. The true boson/fermion balance is the cross-sector match NS ↔\leftrightarrow↔ R, level by level; the parity cut only selects which NS and R states enter that match.

    (c) ∣NS⟩|\mathrm{NS}\rangle∣NS⟩ has F=0F=0F=0 (even) — deleted by the odd-keeping NS rule. b−1/2I∣NS⟩b^I_{-1/2}|\mathrm{NS}\rangleb−1/2I​∣NS⟩ has F=1F=1F=1 (odd) — kept, with N⊥=12N^\perp=\tfrac12N⊥=21​ and α′M2=−12+12=0,\alpha'M^2=-\tfrac12+\tfrac12=0,α′M2=−21​+21​=0, a massless state. The tachyon dies and a photon takes its place.
