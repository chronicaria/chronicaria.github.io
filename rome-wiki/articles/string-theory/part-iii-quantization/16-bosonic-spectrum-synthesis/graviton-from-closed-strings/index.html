    Graviton from closed strings

    The first excited closed-string state \bar\alpha_{-1}^I\alpha_{-1}^J|0;p\rangle is massless at D=26 and splits into a graviton , a B -field , and a dilaton . A massless spin-2 particle can only interact as gravity, so string theory contains general relativity without ever being told about it.

    Two waves, two indices

    A closed string carries two independent vibrations: a wave running left around the loop and a wave running right, each with its own transverse polarization. The mildest excitation wiggles each wave once, picking up one polarization index from each. Two indices glued together form a rank-2 tensor, and a massless rank-2 tensor field is a metric perturbation. Everything after that is bookkeeping: splitting the tensor into its irreducible pieces.

    The open string, by contrast, supports a single wave: one index, one massless vector, a photon (Photon from open strings). Same machine, one more wave, and gravity drops out.

    The state and its mass

    The closed string carries two oscillator towers — left-movers αˉnI\bar\alpha_n^IαˉnI​ and right-movers αnI\alpha_n^IαnI​, with transverse indices I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1, i.e. D−2D-2D−2 values. In natural units (ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1) the mass formula is symmetric in the two sectors (Open versus closed spectra): M^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}\Bigl(N^\perp-\tfrac{D-2}{24}\Bigr)=\frac{4}{\alpha'}\Bigl(\bar N^\perp-\tfrac{D-2}{24}\Bigr).\tag{1} These are two independent mass-shell conditions — one from the right-mover L0L_0L0​, one from the left-mover Lˉ0\bar L_0Lˉ0​ — and both must hold, so N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥: level matching. This is not optional. A closed loop has no marked starting point, so states must be invariant under rigid shifts of σ\sigmaσ; the generator of those shifts is L0−Lˉ0L_0-\bar L_0L0​−Lˉ0​, and demanding it annihilate physical states is exactly N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥. It forbids exciting one sector alone.

    The lowest state above the ground state must therefore put one oscillator in each tower:   ∣ΩIJ⟩≡αˉ−1I α−1J ∣0;p⟩  I,J=2,…,D−1,\boxed{\;|\Omega^{IJ}\rangle \equiv \bar\alpha_{-1}^I\,\alpha_{-1}^J\,|0;p\rangle\;}\qquad I,J=2,\dots,D-1,∣ΩIJ⟩≡αˉ−1I​α−1J​∣0;p⟩​I,J=2,…,D−1, where ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ is shorthand for the light-cone Fock ground state ∣p+,p⃗T⟩|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∣p+,p​T​⟩. It has N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=1N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1, so (1) gives M2=4α′(1−D−224),M^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{D-2}{24}\Bigr),M2=α′4​(1−24D−2​), which is generically nonzero and vanishes exactly when D−224=1\tfrac{D-2}{24}=124D−2​=1, i.e. D=26D=26D=26 — the Critical dimension D=26 step.

    Masslessness here is forced, not convenient. The states ∣ΩIJ⟩|\Omega^{IJ}\rangle∣ΩIJ⟩ carry two transverse indices, so they fill representations of SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2) — the little group of a massless particle (Little group and Wigner classification). Were this level massive, its states would have to assemble into complete multiplets of the larger massive little group SO(D−1)SO(D-1)SO(D−1), and they cannot: a massive spin-2 is the symmetric traceless tensor of SO(D−1)SO(D-1)SO(D−1) with D(D−1)2−1\tfrac{D(D-1)}{2}-12D(D−1)​−1 components, while the symmetric part of ΩIJ\Omega^{IJ}ΩIJ supplies only (D−1)(D−2)2−1\tfrac{(D-1)(D-2)}{2}-12(D−1)(D−2)​−1 — at D=26D=26D=26, 324324324 needed versus 299299299 available. The only Lorentz-consistent reading is M2=0M^2=0M2=0, and (1) then pins D=26D=26D=26.

    So at D=26D=26D=26 this level consists of 242=57624^2=576242=576 massless states ∣ΩIJ⟩|\Omega^{IJ}\rangle∣ΩIJ⟩, each index running over the 242424 transverse directions.

    Splitting the two-index object

    Each index of ΩIJ\Omega^{IJ}ΩIJ is a vector of SO(24)SO(24)SO(24), so the state lives in 24⊗24\mathbf{24}\otimes\mathbf{24}24⊗24. Any rank-2 tensor splits into three pieces that do not mix under rotations — symmetrize, antisymmetrize, and pull out the trace: ΩIJ=(Ω(IJ)−δIJD−2 ΩKK)⏟symmetric traceless  +  Ω[IJ]⏟antisymmetric  +  δIJD−2 ΩKK⏟trace.\Omega^{IJ}=\underbrace{\Bigl(\Omega^{(IJ)}-\tfrac{\delta^{IJ}}{D-2}\,\Omega^{KK}\Bigr)}_{\text{symmetric traceless}}\;+\;\underbrace{\Omega^{[IJ]}}_{\text{antisymmetric}}\;+\;\underbrace{\tfrac{\delta^{IJ}}{D-2}\,\Omega^{KK}}_{\text{trace}}.ΩIJ=symmetric traceless(Ω(IJ)−D−2δIJ​ΩKK)​​+antisymmetricΩ[IJ]​​+traceD−2δIJ​ΩKK​​. Here Ω(IJ)=12(ΩIJ+ΩJI)\Omega^{(IJ)}=\tfrac12(\Omega^{IJ}+\Omega^{JI})Ω(IJ)=21​(ΩIJ+ΩJI) and Ω[IJ]=12(ΩIJ−ΩJI)\Omega^{[IJ]}=\tfrac12(\Omega^{IJ}-\Omega^{JI})Ω[IJ]=21​(ΩIJ−ΩJI). Each piece is the transverse content of a spacetime field:

    • Symmetric traceless GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ — a massless spin-2 field: the graviton (24⋅252−1=299\tfrac{24\cdot25}{2}-1=299224⋅25​−1=299 states).
    • Antisymmetric BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ — the Kalb–Ramond field, a 2-form: it couples to the string's worldsheet the way the 1-form photon AμA_\muAμ​ couples to a charged particle's worldline (24⋅232=276\tfrac{24\cdot23}{2}=276224⋅23​=276 states).
    • Trace Φ\PhiΦ — a single scalar, the dilaton, whose expectation value sets the string coupling gs=e⟨Φ⟩g_s=e^{\langle\Phi\rangle}gs​=e⟨Φ⟩; the derivation from the worldsheet Euler character is in String coupling and the dilaton (111 state).

    The count closes: 299+276+1=576=242299+276+1=576=24^2299+276+1=576=242, every component of ΩIJ\Omega^{IJ}ΩIJ accounted for. These three massless fields are universal — they appear in every string theory. Only the graviton concerns us here.

    Why a massless spin-2 field must be gravity

    That GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ "looks like" a metric is not enough. The load-bearing claim is stronger: any consistent interacting massless spin-2 field is forced to be general relativity — the Feynman–Weinberg argument, in three steps.

    Step 1 — expand around flat space. Write the field as a fluctuation Gμν=ημν+hμν.G_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}.Gμν​=ημν​+hμν​. At this stage hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ is a free spin-2 field, not yet a dynamical metric.

    Step 2 — the free theory needs a gauge symmetry. Canonically quantizing hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ covariantly hits the same trouble as covariant electromagnetism: the mostly-plus metric has a minus sign in the time direction, so time-like polarizations create negative-norm states — a state like a0i†∣0⟩a^\dagger_{0i}|0\ranglea0i†​∣0⟩ has norm proportional to η00=−1\eta_{00}=-1η00​=−1. The only cure is a gauge symmetry that decouples them: h_{\mu\nu}\longrightarrow h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\mu.\tag{2} This is the linearized diffeomorphism. It removes the time-like and longitudinal polarizations, leaving exactly the transverse-traceless states counted above — the same 24⋅252−1=299\tfrac{24\cdot25}{2}-1=299224⋅25​−1=299 physical polarizations light-cone gauge produced directly. A healthy free massless spin-2 field must carry gauge symmetry (2).

    Step 3 — turn on interactions and bootstrap. Couple hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ to matter. Lorentz invariance plus (2) force the leading coupling to be to the conserved stress tensor, Sint∼∫dDx hμν Tμν,S_{\text{int}}\sim\int d^Dx\,h_{\mu\nu}\,T^{\mu\nu},Sint​∼∫dDxhμν​Tμν, so the field's charge is energy-momentum itself. But hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ carries energy, so its own stress tensor must appear on the right-hand side too; that correction shifts TμνT^{\mu\nu}Tμν again, and iterating the self-consistency bootstraps the linear symmetry (2) into full nonlinear diffeomorphism invariance. The unique two-derivative theory with that symmetry is the Einstein–Hilbert action, SEH∼∫dDx −G R,S_{\rm EH}\sim\int d^Dx\,\sqrt{-G}\,R,SEH​∼∫dDx−G​R, up to higher-derivative corrections suppressed at low energy (prefactors are schematic; the structure is the point). The string-internal route to Einstein's equations — demanding that worldsheet conformal invariance survive quantization — comes later, in Einstein equations from conformal invariance.

    String theory was never handed a metric. We extremized the area of a worldsheet, gauge-fixed, quantized free transverse oscillators, and the first excited closed level automatically contained a massless spin-2 particle. The Feynman–Weinberg logic then says that particle can only interact as general relativity. Gravity is not an input; it is the mildest closed-string vibration.

    Open questions

    • Light-cone gauge displays only physical polarizations. The covariant gauge redundancies of GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ (diffeomorphisms) and BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ (2-form gauge symmetry) are invisible here; they reappear in covariant/BRST language.
    • The Feynman–Weinberg bootstrap is sketched to checkpoint level. A full proof that the nonlinear completion is uniquely Einstein–Hilbert is a field-theory result beyond this vault; the string-internal derivation is Einstein equations from conformal invariance.
    • The dilaton–coupling relation gs=e⟨Φ⟩g_s=e^{\langle\Phi\rangle}gs​=e⟨Φ⟩ is stated at spectrum level; its derivation from the worldsheet Euler character lives in String coupling and the dilaton.

    Exercise

    The photon (Photon from open strings) is the open-string analogue of this note. Use the open mass formula α′M2=N⊥−1\alpha'M^2=N^\perp-1α′M2=N⊥−1 and the closed formula (1).

    1. The open string's first massless state α−1I∣0;p⟩\alpha_{-1}^I|0;p\rangleα−1I​∣0;p⟩ carries one transverse index. Why does the closed string's first massless state carry two? Answer using level matching, in one sentence.
    2. The open massless level has D−2=24D-2=24D−2=24 states (one massless vector). Show the closed massless level has (D−2)2=576(D-2)^2=576(D−2)2=576 states, and check that the graviton, BBB-field, and dilaton dimensions sum to that number.
    Solution

    Part 1. The two towers are tied by level matching N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥: you cannot raise the right-mover level without matching it in the left-mover. The lowest excited state therefore needs N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=1N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1, i.e. one oscillator in each tower, contributing one transverse index each — two indices total. (The open string has a single tower, so one oscillator, one index.)

    Part 2. Both closed indices run over the D−2=24D-2=24D−2=24 transverse directions, so ΩIJ\Omega^{IJ}ΩIJ has 24×24=57624\times24=57624×24=576 components. The irreducible pieces of a rank-2 tensor of SO(n)SO(n)SO(n), with n=24n=24n=24: n(n+1)2−1⏟sym. traceless+n(n−1)2⏟antisym.+1⏟trace=299+276+1=576=242. ✓\underbrace{\tfrac{n(n+1)}{2}-1}_{\text{sym. traceless}}+\underbrace{\tfrac{n(n-1)}{2}}_{\text{antisym.}}+\underbrace{1}_{\text{trace}}=299+276+1=576=24^2.\ \checkmarksym. traceless2n(n+1)​−1​​+antisym.2n(n−1)​​​+trace1​​=299+276+1=576=242. ✓ The −1-1−1 in the symmetric part is the trace, moved into its own singlet — that is exactly the dilaton. So graviton (299)(299)(299), BBB-field (276)(276)(276), dilaton (1)(1)(1) account for every state, with no double-counting.
