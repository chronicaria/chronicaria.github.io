Projection logic

Vanderbilt averaged 4.4 points, 4.5 rebounds, 1.3 assists, 0.8 steals, and 0.3 blocks in 17.4 minutes over 65 games in 2025-26. The new roster has more shooting and on-ball depth, while Mamukelashvili, LaRavia, Williams, and Carr all compete for forward minutes. The desk therefore assigns Vanderbilt a small healthy-night defensive shift rather than assuming his prior workload survives. His projected appearance average equals those eight base minutes. Durability: Feet are the career-long theme: they shortened his Kentucky year, kept him buried in Denver, ended 2023-24 at 29 games (midfoot sprain in February), and delayed his 2024-25 debut to midseason after offseason surgery. The 65 games of 2025-26 were his most since 2022-23, but he has exactly three seasons of 64+ games in eight years.

Trajectory: The record shows a genuine three-year prime (2020-23): a starting energy forward giving 6-8 points, 6-8 rebounds and 1.1-1.3 steals on elite FG%, peaking with the 2022-23 UTA/LAL year. Since then minutes, rebound rate and finishing efficiency have all declined together — 2025-26's 4.4/4.5 in 17 minutes is a specialist's line, not a starter's. In 2026-27 he competes with Mamukelashvili, LaRavia and Williams for situational forward minutes. He becomes fantasy-relevant only in the narrow scenario where injuries or a defensive-closer role push him back to ~24 minutes, where his steals-plus-rebounds combination has historically produced deep-league value; nothing in the last three seasons suggests LA plans that role for him.
