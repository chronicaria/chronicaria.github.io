Methodology and interpretation

Rankings and projections are different products

Basketball Monster's Help and FAQ, undated and accessed 2026-07-18, defines Player Rankings as ranks from games already played and Projections as forecasted player ranks under the user's settings. The distinction is reinforced by Josh Lloyd's “Welcome” methodology article, published 2022-08-15 and accessed 2026-07-18:

  • Lloyd and Kyle McKeown project individual NBA stat lines; the site calculates values under the active league parameters.
  • The staff do not manually arrange a universal draft list.
  • A projection page's numerical order is not automatically the order in which players should be drafted.
  • Injury risk, positional and category scarcity, platform ADP/pre-rank, and team-build fit remain separate decisions.

The article is old but still consistent with the live 2026 help pages. It is methodology, not evidence about any 2026-27 player.

Value construction

The FAQ says Basketball Monster values are z-scores, with 0.00 representing the league average. Standard eight-category settings use points, threes, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, FG%, and FT%; standard nine-category adds turnovers. Category weights and punts alter the values. Columns ending in V are single-category values.

The user can also change:

  • league size and player pool;
  • active and bench roster spots;
  • category versus points scoring and category weights;
  • fantasy-provider position eligibility;
  • per-game, per-36, or total-stat treatment;
  • date range or past-game sample;
  • home/away and team/position filters;
  • auction settings, game limits, and H2H playoff weeks.

Total Value penalizes missed games. Per Game Value ignores games played. The FAQ recommends a third view, Total Value with Added Replacement Games, which fills missed games with estimated free-agent production. These three units should never be compared without labeling them.

Projection maintenance and uncertainty

The home page says the normal product maintains full-season and daily/short-term projections. It describes full-season projections as updated multiple times per day and short-term projections as updated immediately after news, with Josh Lloyd maintaining the primary set and a Kyle McKeown set available as a second opinion. The FAQ additionally exposes a 1-to-5 projection-confidence field.

Those are descriptions of the normal service, not timestamp proof for a particular row. A Basketball Monster projection citation should capture the access time, analyst set, categories, weights, punts, value unit, games and minutes assumptions, and relevant league settings. Without those fields, a rank is not reproducible.

Draft-room values

The undated Draft Tracker tutorial, accessed 2026-07-18, documents three distinct concepts:

  • Value: player value against the original league player pool; the tutorial's example uses 156 players in a 12-team by 13-player league.
  • Dynamic Value: recalculated against the players and category supply still available as a draft progresses.
  • Positional Value: adjusted against the remaining supply at the player's eligible positions.

The tool also shows categorical scarcity, team summaries, projected standings, statuses, injuries, playoff games, quality games, and a Monster draft-board rank. Dynamic and positional values change with every refresh and with punt settings. They are decision aids, not stable published ranks.

Access and preservation limits

  • Subscription/login: the projection, draft, dynasty, projected-standings, advanced-ownership, depth-chart, tier, and Monster tools are member-oriented. The current 2026-27 projection output was not visible without access and the site had not opened the season product.
  • No July price claim: no stable current 2026-27 membership price was visible. Older tier prices or search snippets must not be carried forward.
  • Dynamic state: ranks, projections, ADP, ownership, injuries, teams, and navigation update in place without a public changelog for each row. A URL alone is not a reproducible citation.
  • Personalization: outputs depend on league settings. Even two users opening the same page may see different value ranks.
  • Search-index contamination: search engines can wrap an older table or article in the site's newer shell. The season label attached to the data, not the footer year or current navigation, controls classification.
  • Rights: Basketball Monster's terms prohibit scraping and republication of its database. This note records a limited set of selected claims for source evaluation; it does not reproduce a full projection or rankings dataset.
  • Analyst health/coverage: the site showed fundraising and health notices concerning Kyle McKeown during this period. No unsupported inference is made about the future availability of his projection set; the only safe claim is that the normal product advertises a Kyle alternative and the current home page named Josh Lloyd as lead analyst.

Encyclopedia use

  1. Keep Basketball Monster at specialist-analysis evidence grade C for projections and ranks. Use its public results as statistical context, not as the source of record for rosters, injuries, or transactions.
  2. Schedule a new extraction after the site's announced mid-August opening. Capture both Josh and Kyle sets if legitimately accessible, with settings and units.
  3. Normalize per-game and total value separately. Preserve estimated games, minutes, punts, category weights, and replacement-game treatment.
  4. Treat the Summer League table as a player-development clue only. Do not let Thomas's No. 1 or Tomlin's No. 5 event rank become an NBA redraft claim.
  5. Compare public 2025-26 ranks only against other completed-season results. They are useful for identifying category mechanisms, not setting a 2026-27 board.
  6. For Cleveland, the first future refresh should capture Mitchell, Mobley, Harden, Allen, Tyson, Thomas, and Tomlin, plus the effect of any unresolved roster move on minutes and usage.
