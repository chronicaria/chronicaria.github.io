    00 — Overview — Quantum Closed Strings

    Quantized in light-cone gauge, a closed loop of string carries two independent oscillator towers — waves running left and right around the loop — tied together by the single constraint that survives because an unmarked loop has no place to pin \sigma=0 . Out of the spectrum falls a massless spin-2 particle, the graviton: nobody put gravity in, and it came anyway.

    Circulating waves, not standing waves

    A guitar string clamped at both ends only has standing waves. A closed loop has no clamps, so waves can circulate — clockwise and counterclockwise, independently. Those are the two sectors. Quantum-mechanically each sector is a copy of the harmonic-oscillator tower you already met for the open string, and the closed string is almost their tensor product. That "almost" carries all the physics: one leftover symmetry glues the two towers level by level, and the lowest glued excitation is a graviton.

    Gravity falls out

    The open string gave a photon (Photon from open strings). The closed string gives gravity. At its first excited level sits a symmetric, traceless, transverse rank-2 tensor — exactly the polarization data of a linearized metric hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​. We never wrote a dynamical metric into the action and never invoked general covariance; a graviton dropped out of quantizing a loop. Riding alongside it are two more massless fields: the antisymmetric Kalb–Ramond tensor BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ and the scalar dilaton ϕ\phiϕ. The dilaton is the sleeper: its background value sets the string coupling, gs∼e⟨ϕ⟩g_s\sim e^{\langle\phi\rangle}gs​∼e⟨ϕ⟩, so the strength of every interaction — gravity included — is a dynamical field, not a dialed-in constant. Closed-string theory is therefore a quantum theory of gravity in which the coupling could, in principle, be computed rather than measured.

    The logical spine (five moves)

    Read the sublessons in this order; each is one link in the chain.

    1. Two towers. The wave equation solution splits as Xμ=XLμ(τ+σ)+XRμ(τ−σ)X^\mu=X_L^\mu(\tau+\sigma)+X_R^\mu(\tau-\sigma)Xμ=XLμ​(τ+σ)+XRμ​(τ−σ). An open string's endpoints glue these together; a closed loop has only periodicity Xμ(τ,σ)=Xμ(τ,σ+2π)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)=X^\mu(\tau,\sigma+2\pi)Xμ(τ,σ)=Xμ(τ,σ+2π), which is too weak to relate them. So αnμ\alpha_n^\muαnμ​ (right) and αˉnμ\bar\alpha_n^\muαˉnμ​ (left) survive as two commuting oscillator algebras. They touch in exactly one place: the shared zero mode, α0μ=αˉ0μ=α′/2 pμ\alpha_0^\mu=\bar\alpha_0^\mu=\sqrt{\alpha'/2}\,p^\muα0μ​=αˉ0μ​=α′/2​pμ — one momentum, not two. See 15.1 Left and right oscillator algebras.
    2. Level matching. You can rigidly rotate the loop, σ↦σ+σ0\sigma\mapsto\sigma+\sigma_0σ↦σ+σ0​, without changing anything physical — there's no marked point to anchor σ=0\sigma=0σ=0.     3. Mass formula. Solving the light-cone constraint for M2=−p2M^2=-p^2M2=−p2 (mostly-plus signature, ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1) gives M2=2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2) → N⊥=Nˉ⊥≡N  4α′(N−1),M^2=\frac{2}{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2\big)\ \xrightarrow{\ N^\perp=\bar N^\perp\equiv N\ }\ \frac{4}{\alpha'}(N-1),M2=α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)  N⊥=Nˉ⊥≡N ​ α′4​(N−1), with a normal-ordering constant −1-1−1 per sector (hence −2-2−2).     4. The massless level. At N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=1N⊥=Nˉ⊥=1 the states are α−1Iαˉ−1J∣p+,p⃗T⟩\alpha_{-1}^I\bar\alpha_{-1}^J|p^+,\vec p_T\rangleα−1I​αˉ−1J​∣p+,p​T​⟩, indexed by a (D−2)×(D−2)(D-2)\times(D-2)(D−2)×(D−2) matrix.     5. The coupling. The dilaton's expectation value fixes gs∼e⟨ϕ⟩g_s\sim e^{\langle\phi\rangle}gs​∼e⟨ϕ⟩, making the coupling a field. See 15.5 String coupling and the dilaton.

    The three key numbers, and where they come from

    • Ground state is a tachyon, α′M2=−4\alpha'M^2=-4α′M2=−4. Plug N=0N=0N=0 into M2=4α′(N−1)M^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}(N-1)M2=α′4​(N−1). This is four times more negative than the open-string tachyon (α′M2=−1\alpha'M^2=-1α′M2=−1) — a signal of vacuum instability, not a real particle.
    • Level spacing is 4/α′4/\alpha'4/α′, not 1/α′1/\alpha'1/α′. Two factors of 2 multiply. The shared zero mode is half the open one (α′/2 pμ\sqrt{\alpha'/2}\,p^\muα′/2​pμ against the open 2α′ pμ\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^\mu2α′​pμ), which puts 2/α′2/\alpha'2/α′ in front of the levels instead of 1/α′1/\alpha'1/α′. And level matching lets you climb only diagonally, (N⊥,Nˉ⊥)→(N⊥+1,Nˉ⊥+1)(N^\perp,\bar N^\perp)\to(N^\perp+1,\bar N^\perp+1)(N⊥,Nˉ⊥)→(N⊥+1,Nˉ⊥+1), so each physical step raises N⊥+Nˉ⊥N^\perp+\bar N^\perpN⊥+Nˉ⊥ by 2. The product 2/α′×22/\alpha'\times22/α′×2 is the 4/α′4/\alpha'4/α′ spacing shared by the two forms 2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)\frac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2)α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2) and 4α′(N−1)\frac{4}{\alpha'}(N-1)α′4​(N−1).
    • Massless at level 1 forces D=26D=26D=26. The excited states carry only transverse SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2) indices, so they cannot fill out a massive SO(D−1)SO(D-1)SO(D−1) multiplet — Lorentz invariance forces them massless. Masslessness means 4α′(1−D−224)=0\frac{4}{\alpha'}\big(1-\frac{D-2}{24}\big)=0α′4​(1−24D−2​)=0, i.e. D=26D=26D=26, equivalently a normal-ordering constant a=−D−224=−1a=-\frac{D-2}{24}=-1a=−24D−2​=−1 per sector. The full anomaly-based proof is in Critical dimension D=26.

    Why "massless spin-2" is gravity

    Not a coincidence, not merely graviton-like. A free symmetric tensor hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ carries unphysical timelike components; consistency demands the gauge redundancy hμν→hμν+∂μξν+∂νξμh_{\mu\nu}\to h_{\mu\nu}+\partial_\mu\xi_\nu+\partial_\nu\xi_\muhμν​→hμν​+∂μ​ξν​+∂ν​ξμ​, and any matter this field couples to must then carry a conserved source. But once hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ interacts it carries energy-momentum itself, so it must source itself; iterating that self-consistency (the Feynman–Weinberg bootstrap) turns the linear gauge symmetry into the full nonlinear diffeomorphism invariance of Einstein gravity. A consistent interacting massless spin-2 particle has no choice but to be a graviton. Details in 15.4 Graviton antisymmetric tensor and dilaton.

    Open questions

    • We take on faith here that Lorentz invariance fixes D=26D=26D=26 and a=−1a=-1a=−1. The zeta/cutoff computation ∑n→−112\sum n \to -\tfrac1{12}∑n→−121​ gives the right number, but the honest reason is that the light-cone Lorentz algebra fails to close otherwise — flagged for Critical dimension D=26.
    • The closed-string tachyon (M2=−4/α′M^2=-4/\alpha'M2=−4/α′) signals that the bosonic vacuum is a maximum, not a minimum, of some potential. Its fate is genuinely not understood; the superstring removes it.

    Exercise

    Starting from the two facts you're allowed to assume — the closed-string mass formula M2=2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)M^2=\frac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2)M2=α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2) and the level-matching constraint N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥ — do all three:

    (a) Write the lowest two allowed levels and their M2M^2M2, and explain why there is no physical state with 12α′M2=−1\tfrac12\alpha'M^2=-121​α′M2=−1. (b) Recast the formula in the single-level form M2=4α′(N−1)M^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}(N-1)M2=α′4​(N−1) and confirm it agrees with (a). (c) At the first excited level, the states carry a (D−2)×(D−2)(D-2)\times(D-2)(D−2)×(D−2) index matrix. Decompose that matrix into pieces that transform independently under transverse rotations, name the field each piece becomes, and count the states for D=26D=26D=26.

    Solution

    (a) Lowest two allowed levels. Level matching forces N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥, so only diagonal levels (N⊥,Nˉ⊥)=(0,0),(1,1),…(N^\perp,\bar N^\perp)=(0,0),(1,1),\dots(N⊥,Nˉ⊥)=(0,0),(1,1),… survive.

    • (0,0)(0,0)(0,0): 12α′M2=0+0−2=−2\tfrac12\alpha'M^2=0+0-2=-221​α′M2=0+0−2=−2, so M2=−4/α′M^2=-4/\alpha'M2=−4/α′ — a scalar tachyon.
    • (1,1)(1,1)(1,1): 12α′M2=1+1−2=0\tfrac12\alpha'M^2=1+1-2=021​α′M2=1+1−2=0, so M2=0M^2=0M2=0 — the massless level.

    A state with 12α′M2=−1\tfrac12\alpha'M^2=-121​α′M2=−1 would need N⊥+Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp+\bar N^\perp=1N⊥+Nˉ⊥=1, i.e. (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) or (0,1)(0,1)(0,1). Both violate N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥, so they are unphysical: you cannot excite one sector without the other. There is no physical closed-string state between the tachyon and the massless level.

    (b) Single-level form. Set N⊥=Nˉ⊥≡NN^\perp=\bar N^\perp\equiv NN⊥=Nˉ⊥≡N. Then N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2=2N−2=2(N−1)N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2=2N-2=2(N-1)N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2=2N−2=2(N−1), so M2=2α′⋅2(N−1)=4α′(N−1).M^2=\frac{2}{\alpha'}\cdot 2(N-1)=\frac{4}{\alpha'}(N-1).M2=α′2​⋅2(N−1)=α′4​(N−1). Check: N=0⇒M2=−4/α′N=0\Rightarrow M^2=-4/\alpha'N=0⇒M2=−4/α′ (the tachyon) and N=1⇒M2=0N=1\Rightarrow M^2=0N=1⇒M2=0 (massless). Agrees with (a), and the level spacing is 4/α′4/\alpha'4/α′.

    (c) Decomposing the massless matrix. The states are ∑I,JRIJ α−1Iαˉ−1J∣p+,p⃗T⟩\sum_{I,J}R_{IJ}\,\alpha_{-1}^I\bar\alpha_{-1}^J|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∑I,J​RIJ​α−1I​αˉ−1J​∣p+,p​T​⟩ with RIJR_{IJ}RIJ​ an arbitrary (D−2)×(D−2)(D-2)\times(D-2)(D−2)×(D−2) matrix. Any square matrix splits into three pieces that don't mix under rotations: RIJ=S^IJ⏟sym., traceless+AIJ⏟antisym.+S′ δIJ⏟trace.R_{IJ}=\underbrace{\hat S_{IJ}}_{\text{sym., traceless}}+\underbrace{A_{IJ}}_{\text{antisym.}}+\underbrace{S'\,\delta_{IJ}}_{\text{trace}}.RIJ​=sym., tracelessS^IJ​​​+antisym.AIJ​​​+traceS′δIJ​​​.

    • S^IJ\hat S_{IJ}S^IJ​ (symmetric traceless) →\to→ graviton GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​: (D−2)(D−1)2−1=24⋅252−1=299\tfrac{(D-2)(D-1)}{2}-1=\tfrac{24\cdot25}{2}-1=\mathbf{299}2(D−2)(D−1)​−1=224⋅25​−1=299 states.
    • AIJA_{IJ}AIJ​ (antisymmetric) →\to→ Kalb–Ramond field BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​: (D−2)(D−3)2=24⋅232=276\tfrac{(D-2)(D-3)}{2}=\tfrac{24\cdot23}{2}=\mathbf{276}2(D−2)(D−3)​=224⋅23​=276 states.
    • S′ δIJS'\,\delta_{IJ}S′δIJ​ (trace) →\to→ dilaton ϕ\phiϕ: 1\mathbf{1}1 state.

    Total 299+276+1=576=242=(D−2)2299+276+1=576=24^2=(D-2)^2299+276+1=576=242=(D−2)2. The count checks, and the symmetric-traceless piece — transverse, traceless, massless, rank-2 — is precisely a graviton's polarizations. It emerged from quantizing the loop; no metric was ever put in.
