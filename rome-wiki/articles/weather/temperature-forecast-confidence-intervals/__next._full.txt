17:T3160,<h2 id="the-concrete-answer">The concrete answer</h2>
<aside class="callout callout-example">
<p class="callout-title">Example</p>
<p>"I see 80 °F, 3 days out. What's the 95% interval?"
<strong>≈ [72, 88] °F</strong> typically (conservative edge [71, 89] °F). Half-width ~±8 °F. On a calm summer high it's more like ±4 °F; ahead of a front or a heat-wave onset it can blow out to ±15 °F+.</p>
</aside>

<h2 id="why-a-single--band-is-a-lie-the-average-tells">Why a single ± band is a lie the average tells</h2>
<p>The ±8 °F is an annual, all-region mean. The real error distribution is <strong>not</strong> a fixed Gaussian:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Regime-dependent.</strong> Stable/quiescent (summer high, well-mixed afternoon): ±3–5 °F. Active (fronts, convection, heat-wave onset, winter inversions): ±12–18 °F+.</li>
<li><strong>Non-Gaussian — peaked core, fat tails.</strong> More forecasts land dead-on than a Gaussian predicts, <em>and</em> the misses are worse (occasional busts). During heat waves 2 m errors jump to 5–10 °C (Ennis et al. 2025).</li>
<li><strong>Seasonal / geographic.</strong> Winter > summer; coasts and mountains worse than flatland.</li>
</ul>
<p>This is precisely why real forecasts come from an <strong><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/ensemble-forecasting/" class="wiki-link">ensemble</a></strong>, which gives a <em>flow-dependent</em> spread instead of a fixed rule of thumb: the band should be narrow on a boring day and wide on a volatile one.</p>
<h2 id="calibration-footnote">Calibration footnote</h2>
<p>NWS <strong>temperature</strong> forecasts are found to be well-calibrated. Commercial <strong>precipitation</strong> probabilities show a deliberate "wet bias" — predict 20%, it rains ~5% (Bickel &#x26; Kim 2008; Silver 2012) — so trust temperature bands more than a low PoP.</p>

<h2 id="exercise">Exercise</h2>
<details class="callout callout-question">
<summary class="callout-title">If a day-5 forecast says 60 °F, what's a reasonable 95% band, and when would you distrust it?</summary>
<p>Day-5 MAE ≈ 4.5 °F → ±2.46×4.5 ≈ <strong>±11 °F → [49, 71] °F</strong> as an all-regime average. Distrust (widen it) if a front, convection, or a temperature swing is in play near that day, or in winter/coastal/mountain settings — check the actual <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/ensemble-forecasting/" class="wiki-link">ensemble spread</a> rather than the rule of thumb.</p>
