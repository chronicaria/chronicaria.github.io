18:T1f61e,<h2 id="loops-do-two-things-points-cannot">Loops do two things points cannot</h2>
<p>A string is a <em>loop</em>, not a <em>point</em>, and a loop can do two things a point cannot.</p>
<p><strong>It can wrap.</strong> Put a point particle on a circle and the only new thing that happens is that its momentum around the loop becomes quantized, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi><mo>=</mo><mi>n</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">/</mi><mi>R</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p=n/R</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">n</span><span class="mord">/</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0077em;">R</span></span></span></span> — a ladder of states (the Kaluza–Klein tower) whose spacing grows as the circle shrinks. A closed string does all that <em>and</em> can wind around the circle like a rubber band around a pole, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">m</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">m</span></span></span></span> times, and it costs energy to stretch it. Winding is topological: you cannot unwrap without cutting the string. This one extra integer <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><annotation Momentum and winding trade places; the string sees a symmetry a point particle has no access to.</p>
<p><strong>It sweeps a surface.</strong> As it moves, a point particle traces a line; a string traces a 2d sheet, the <em>worldsheet</em>. That sheet carries its own quantum field theory, and the geometry of spacetime enters as the <em>coupling constants</em> of that theory. Demanding that this 2d theory stay quantum-mechanically consistent — specifically, that it keep its conformal (Weyl) symmetry, which we cannot give up — forces the spacetime fields to obey equations of motion. Those equations turn out to be <strong>Einstein's equations</strong>. Gravity is not put in by hand; it is the price of keeping the worldsheet a CFT.</p>
<p>So the module has two threads, and they are the same thread seen from two sides:</p>
