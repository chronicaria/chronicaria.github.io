Thompson averaged 11.7 points and 2.9 threes in 21.7 minutes across 69 games in 2025-26, but shot 39.3% overall and started only eight times.

This is the Dallas branch of a two-branch projection. Traded or bought out: zero Dallas value; the shooting minutes go to Biberović and Christie with a marginal lift to Sasser. His value in a new uniform depends entirely on that team's spacing needs and is not modelled here.

Correction 2026-07-18: cut from 58 GP × 18 MPG to 38 × 15 and split into branches. Marc Stein reports Dallas is actively seeking a Thompson trade, lists him with Gafford and Washington as the players Dallas is most open to moving, and does not rule out a buyout if nothing materialises by August or September (Yahoo Sports, July 2026). The prior line gave a near-full Dallas season to a player the front office is trying to remove. Miami has been linked only in a buyout scenario; Golden State and Denver have also been floated. His $17.4M expiring is Dallas's cleanest matching salary.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: three-point streamer; negative FG% can erase much of the benefit. Low turnovers and low-volume FT% are minor positives.
  • Points: matchup-only streamer because rebounds, assists, and defensive events are scarce.
  • Dynasty: minimal value at age 36; contenders can hold only if threes are a weekly need.

Role, floor, ceiling, uncertainty

  • Median role: 15-minute movement-shooting reserve, if he is still on the roster at opening night.
  • Floor: traded or bought out before camp ends; no Dallas games at all.
  • Ceiling: 22–24 MPG and three-plus threes if Biberović struggles, Dallas finds no trade, and the team needs maximum spacing.
  • Main swing: roster status, not role. Dallas is shopping him. Secondarily, new coach Dusty May has no obligation to preserve a reputation role, and Biberović's signing provides a younger version of the same scarce team function.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Three seasons at Washington State — 12.5 points as a freshman in 2008-09, 19.6 as a sophomore, 21.6 points with 5.2 rebounds and 3.7 assists as a junior in 2010-11. College splits of .424/.390/.827. Two-time first-team All-Pac-10; set the WSU single-season scoring record with 733 points and finished third on the school's all-time list. Number retired by Washington State in 2020. Draft: 2011, first round, 11th overall, Golden State.

Accolades: four championships (2015, 2017, 2018, 2022), five All-Star selections (2015-2019), two All-NBA Third Teams (2015, 2016), All-Defensive Second Team (2019), All-Rookie First Team (2012), 2016 Three-Point Contest winner. Holds the record for points in a quarter (37) and for three-pointers in a game (14).

Durability: Available almost every night for his first eight seasons — never below 66 games. He then missed two full seasons: a torn left ACL in Game 6 of the 2019 Finals wiped out 2019-20, and a torn right Achilles in a November 2020 workout wiped out 2020-21. He returned in January 2022, which is why 2021-22 shows only 32 games. Since returning he has been durable again — 69, 77, 72 and 69 games — so the recent decline is age and role, not availability.

Trajectory: The scoring curve has fallen in a straight line since the Achilles: 21.9 to 17.9 to 14.0 to 11.7, with minutes down to 21.7 and field-goal percentage to a career-low .393. What survives is the one elite skill — he still made 2.9 threes a game in under 22 minutes, a rate almost nobody sustains off the bench — but everything around it is gone, with rebounds, assists and steals all at career lows. At 36 he is a movement shooter and nothing else. The complication for 2026-27 is roster status rather than role: Dallas is shopping him and has not ruled out a buyout, and Tarik Biberović was signed to do the same job younger. He returns fantasy value only as a three-point streamer, and only if he stays somewhere that needs spacing badly enough to give him 22-plus minutes.
