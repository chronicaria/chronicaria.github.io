Donovan Mitchell

Donovan Mitchell is Cleveland's safest fantasy star: a durable prime-age source of points, threes, free throws, and steals whose first-round ceiling depends on how many other ball-dominant stars Cleveland adds.

Mitchell's 2025-26 baseline was 70 games and 33.5 minutes with .483/.865 percentages, 3.2 threes, 27.9 points, 4.5 rebounds, 5.7 assists, 1.5 steals, 0.3 blocks, and 2.8 turnovers. The projection trims efficiency and assists for a full-season James Harden partnership. 2025-26 Cavaliers statistics

Role and category profile

  • Role: primary scorer, late-clock closer, and secondary initiator. Cleveland announced a four-year extension July 7, eliminating contract uncertainty. NBA offseason deals tracker
  • 9-cat: strongest in PTS, 3PM, FT% volume, and STL; useful AST from shooting guard; modest rebound/block output. High-volume FG% is neutral-to-positive when he repeats 2025-26, but .483 is an aggressive repeat assumption.
  • Points leagues: top-20-to-30 profile because the scoring/assist base is sturdy. Turnover penalties and LeBron/Harden overlap matter more than in 9-cat.
  • Dynasty: Dynatyze ranked him 20th on July 18; Hashtag Basketball listed him 19th in dynasty and 26th in keeper. He is a top-25 core asset, with age and extension security offset by a crowded creation ecosystem. In an April 9 RotoWire nine-category roto discussion, Adam King argued Mitchell belonged in his personal top 12; that is an early analyst take, not consensus ADP. Dynatyze · Hashtag dynasty · RotoWire podcast

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: top 30-40 per game if Harden returns and LeBron arrives, pushing assists closer to four and minutes toward 31.
  • Base: picks 15-28 in 9-cat; approximately 27/4/5 with elite threes and strong steals.
  • Ceiling: top 10-15 if Cleveland adds no other high-usage creator and Mitchell sustains the 2025-26 efficiency/steal spike.

Health and uncertainty

Mitchell played 70 games in 2025-26. He dealt with a right-groin strain and a bruised left eye late in the season but carried no public long-term restriction at the cutoff. The larger risk is structural: a LeBron signing would improve team outcomes while reducing touches for Mitchell, Harden, and Evan Mobley.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Louisville, two seasons. 2015-16 as a freshman: 31 games, 19.1 minutes, 7.4 points, 3.4 rebounds, 1.7 assists, 0.8 steals on .442/.250/.754. 2016-17 as a sophomore: 34 games, 32.3 minutes, 15.6 points, 4.9 rebounds, 2.7 assists, 2.1 steals on .408/.354/.806, first-team All-ACC. The sophomore steal rate is the tell that the NBA steal production was a real skill and not a fluke. Draft: 2017, first round, 13th overall by Denver, traded on draft night to Utah for Trey Lyles and the 24th pick (Tyler Lydon).

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-category ranks from Basketball Monster, Hashtag, or the ESPN Player Rater were not retrievable for any season, so no specific finishes are asserted. The verified table shows a stable four-category engine — points, threes, free-throw percentage on high volume, steals — with rebounds and assists at useful-not-difference-making levels for the position and blocks at zero. The variable across nine years is field-goal percentage: .432 to .449 in Utah, .484 and .483 in his two best Cleveland seasons, .443 in 2024-25. That swing is the difference between a mid-first-round nine-cat season and a third-round one, because he shoots enough volume for the percentage to move a whole roster. Points leagues rate him more highly and more stably than categories do, since the turnovers and absent blocks matter far less there and the scoring base has never dropped below 20 per game.

Durability: 79, 77, 69, 53, 67, 68, 55, 71, 70. Never a season-ending structural injury; the missed time is a recurring pattern of soft-tissue and lower-body problems that cost two to four weeks at a time. 2020-21 was a right ankle sprain in mid-April that cost the final 16 regular-season games. 2023-24 was the worst stretch: a fractured nose on 3 March, a surgical repair on 19 March, and a left knee bone bruise in the same month. 2024-25 ended with a left ankle injury suffered in a Conference Semifinals warm-up. 2025-26 cost him 12 games — a right groin strain that took out at least three consecutive games in late February and early March, plus a bruised left eye late — and he again finished the postseason dealing with a left ankle problem from the Conference Semifinals. The floor is reliable (67-plus games in seven of nine years); the 82-game season has never happened.

Trajectory: The Cleveland arc is the interesting part. His Utah peak was volume-driven — 26 points on .438 shooting is a scoring title bid that costs a fantasy roster real efficiency. The 2022-23 move to Cleveland changed his shot diet, not his usage: field-goal percentage jumped from .448 to .484 and stayed materially above his Utah baseline in three of four Cleveland years. 2024-25 looks like the outlier now rather than a decline signal, because it was Atkinson's minutes reduction (31.4, a career low) plus a cold three-point year, and 2025-26 reversed both. On a rate basis 2025-26 was the best offensive season of his career: 30.0 points per 36 minutes against 28.5 per 36 in the 28.3-point 2022-23 season, on nearly identical efficiency, while adding 1.3 assists. Entering his age-30 season that is a player still at his peak, not past it, and the honest risk is not decline but touch compression — if Cleveland's creation hierarchy gets more crowded, the assists and usage compress first while the percentages and steals hold.
