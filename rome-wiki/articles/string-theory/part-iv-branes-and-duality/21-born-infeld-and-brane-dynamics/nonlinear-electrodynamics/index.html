    Nonlinear electrodynamics

    Nonlinear electrodynamics is Maxwell's theory with one rule loosened: instead of forcing the response fields \vec D=\vec E , \vec H=\vec B , you let a single function — the Lagrangian \mathcal L — decide how the vacuum responds. The vacuum stops being empty and starts acting like a polarizable material. This is the framework Born–Infeld lives in; here we build the machinery, and Born-Infeld action picks the one \mathcal L that string theory demands.

    In a medium, Maxwell's equations come in two families: the ones about (E⃗,B⃗)(\vec E,\vec B)(E,B) and the ones about (D⃗,H⃗)(\vec D,\vec H)(D,H). What glues the families together is a constitutive law D⃗(E⃗,B⃗)\vec D(\vec E,\vec B)D(E,B) fixing how strongly the material polarizes. Ordinary electromagnetism sets that law to the trivial D⃗=E⃗\vec D=\vec ED=E. Nonlinear electrodynamics keeps every Maxwell equation but replaces the law by a free function L\mathcal LL — and applies it to the vacuum itself. Everything below unpacks that one move.

    The split: (E⃗,B⃗)(\vec E,\vec B)(E,B) vs (D⃗,H⃗)(\vec D,\vec H)(D,H)

    In a medium Maxwell's equations split into two groups (we set c=1c=1c=1 throughout). The source-free pair:

    ∇×E⃗=−∂B⃗∂t,∇⋅B⃗=0,\nabla\times\vec E = -\frac{\partial\vec B}{\partial t}, \qquad \nabla\cdot\vec B = 0,∇×E=−∂t∂B​,∇⋅B=0,

    and the pair carrying the free sources ρ,j⃗\rho,\vec jρ,j​:

    ∇⋅D⃗=ρ,∇×H⃗=j⃗+∂D⃗∂t.\nabla\cdot\vec D = \rho, \qquad \nabla\times\vec H = \vec j + \frac{\partial\vec D}{\partial t}.∇⋅D=ρ,∇×H=j​+∂t∂D​.

    The first pair holds automatically once E⃗,B⃗\vec E,\vec BE,B descend from potentials (E⃗=−∇ϕ−∂tA⃗\vec E=-\nabla\phi-\partial_t\vec AE=−∇ϕ−∂t​A, B⃗=∇×A⃗\vec B=\nabla\times\vec AB=∇×A) — it is the Bianchi identity, geometry, no physics. All the physics is in the second pair, inert until you supply a rule connecting D⃗,H⃗\vec D,\vec HD,H back to E⃗,B⃗\vec E,\vec BE,B:

    D⃗=D⃗(E⃗,B⃗),H⃗=H⃗(E⃗,B⃗).\vec D = \vec D(\vec E,\vec B), \qquad \vec H = \vec H(\vec E,\vec B).D=D(E,B),H=H(E,B).

    In a linear dielectric D⃗=ϵE⃗\vec D=\epsilon\vec ED=ϵE; in a linear magnetic material B⃗=μH⃗\vec B=\mu\vec HB=μH. Nonlinear electrodynamics is the case where this rule is nonlinear, and applied to the vacuum. The conceptual leap is that the (E⃗,B⃗)(\vec E,\vec B)(E,B) equations are not more fundamental than the (D⃗,H⃗)(\vec D,\vec H)(D,H) ones: the nonlinear constitutive law is not an add-on describing some material, it is the definition of the theory.

    Covariant form: the tensor GμνG^{\mu\nu}Gμν

    Package E⃗,B⃗\vec E,\vec BE,B into Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμF_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\muFμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​ as usual, with Fi0=EiF_{i0}=E_iFi0​=Ei​, Fij=ϵijkBkF_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}B_kFij​=ϵijk​Bk​ (mostly-plus signature throughout). The source-free pair is the automatic Bianchi identity. The sourced pair is written with a new antisymmetric tensor GμνG^{\mu\nu}Gμν:

    ∂Gμν∂xν=jμ,\frac{\partial G^{\mu\nu}}{\partial x^\nu} = j^\mu,∂xν∂Gμν​=jμ,

    where GμνG^{\mu\nu}Gμν is the same matrix as FμνF^{\mu\nu}Fμν but with E⃗→D⃗, B⃗→H⃗\vec E\to\vec D,\ \vec B\to\vec HE→D, B→H:

    Gμν=(0DxDyDz−Dx0Hz−Hy−Dy−Hz0Hx−DzHy−Hx0).G^{\mu\nu} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & D_x & D_y & D_z\\ -D_x & 0 & H_z & -H_y\\ -D_y & -H_z & 0 & H_x\\ -D_z & H_y & -H_x & 0\end{pmatrix}.Gμν=​0−Dx​−Dy​−Dz​​Dx​0−Hz​Hy​​Dy​Hz​0−Hx​​Dz​−Hy​Hx​0​​.

    In Maxwell theory G=FG=FG=F and this is just ∂νFμν=jμ\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu}=j^\mu∂ν​Fμν=jμ. In a nonlinear theory G≠FG\ne FG=F, and the entire content of the nonlinearity sits in the function G(F)G(F)G(F). The rest of the note produces that function from a Lagrangian.

    The master formula: Gμν=−∂L/∂FμνG^{\mu\nu} = -\partial\mathcal L/\partial F_{\mu\nu}Gμν=−∂L/∂Fμν​

    Where does GGG come from? From an action. Take a Lagrangian density L(Fμν)\mathcal L(F_{\mu\nu})L(Fμν​) — gauge invariant because it is built from FFF, and local because it uses FFF itself and not derivatives like ∂ρFμν\partial_\rho F_{\mu\nu}∂ρ​Fμν​ — plus the source coupling:

    S=∫dDx L(Fμν)+∫dDx Aμjμ.S = \int d^Dx\, \mathcal L(F_{\mu\nu}) + \int d^Dx\, A_\mu j^\mu.S=∫dDxL(Fμν​)+∫dDxAμ​jμ.

    Varying L\mathcal LL means differentiating with respect to the antisymmetric object FμνF_{\mu\nu}Fμν​, which needs one convention. For any function MMM of the field strengths,

    \delta M = \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial M}{\partial F_{\mu\nu}}\,\delta F_{\mu\nu}, \qquad \frac{\partial M}{\partial F_{\mu\nu}} = -\frac{\partial M}{\partial F_{\nu\mu}}. \tag{1}

    The 12\tfrac1221​ undoes the double-counting from summing over both orderings (μν)(\mu\nu)(μν) and (νμ)(\nu\mu)(νμ); the antisymmetry of ∂M/∂Fμν\partial M/\partial F_{\mu\nu}∂M/∂Fμν​ is imposed, matching that of FFF. Now plug in δFμν=∂μδAν−∂νδAμ\delta F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu-\partial_\nu\delta A_\muδFμν​=∂μ​δAν​−∂ν​δAμ​, use the antisymmetry to fold both terms into one, and integrate by parts:

    δ ⁣∫dDx L=∫dDx 12∂L∂Fμν(∂μδAν−∂νδAμ)=∫dDx δAμ ∂ν ⁣(∂L∂Fμν).\delta\!\int d^Dx\,\mathcal L = \int d^Dx\,\frac12\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial F_{\mu\nu}}(\partial_\mu\delta A_\nu-\partial_\nu\delta A_\mu) = \int d^Dx\,\delta A_\mu\,\partial_\nu\!\Big(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial F_{\mu\nu}}\Big).δ∫dDxL=∫dDx21​∂Fμν​∂L​(∂μ​δAν​−∂ν​δAμ​)=∫dDxδAμ​∂ν​(∂Fμν​∂L​).

    Adding the source term, δS=0\delta S=0δS=0 for all δAμ\delta A_\muδAμ​ gives ∂ν(∂L/∂Fμν)+jμ=0\partial_\nu(\partial\mathcal L/\partial F_{\mu\nu}) + j^\mu = 0∂ν​(∂L/∂Fμν​)+jμ=0. Comparing to ∂νGμν=jμ\partial_\nu G^{\mu\nu}=j^\mu∂ν​Gμν=jμ forces the identification

    \boxed{\;G^{\mu\nu} = -\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial F_{\mu\nu}}\;}. \tag{2}

    This is the engine of the framework: name any L(F)\mathcal L(F)L(F) and (2) hands you GGG, hence the constitutive law. Choosing L\mathcal LL is choosing a theory.

    Reading off D⃗\vec DD and H⃗\vec HH

    Apply (2) componentwise. With Ei=Fi0=−F0iE_i=F_{i0}=-F_{0i}Ei​=Fi0​=−F0i​, so ∂/∂Ei=−∂/∂F0i\partial/\partial E_i = -\partial/\partial F_{0i}∂/∂Ei​=−∂/∂F0i​:

    Di=G0i=−∂L∂F0i=∂L∂Ei,i.e.  D⃗=∂L∂E⃗  .D_i = G^{0i} = -\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial F_{0i}} = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial E_i}, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad \boxed{\;\vec D = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\vec E}\;}.Di​=G0i=−∂F0i​∂L​=∂Ei​∂L​,i.e.D=∂E∂L​​.

    Here is the reading that makes the rest inevitable: E⃗∼A⃗˙\vec E\sim\dot{\vec A}E∼A˙ plays the role of a velocity, so D⃗=∂L/∂E⃗\vec D=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\vec ED=∂L/∂E is the canonical momentum conjugate to A⃗\vec AA. The same computation on the magnetic side gives

      H⃗=−∂L∂B⃗  .\boxed{\;\vec H = -\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\vec B}\;}.H=−∂B∂L​​.

    Because D⃗\vec DD is a momentum, the energy density is a Legendre transform of L\mathcal LL — exactly H=pq˙−LH=p\dot q - LH=pq˙​−L from mechanics, with D⃗↔p\vec D\leftrightarrow pD↔p and E⃗↔q˙\vec E\leftrightarrow\dot qE↔q˙​:

    \mathcal H = \vec D\cdot\vec E - \mathcal L. \tag{3}

    Maxwell is the sanity check. With LM=−14FμνFμν=12(E2−B2)\mathcal L_M = -\tfrac14 F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12(E^2-B^2)LM​=−41​FμνFμν​=21​(E2−B2), differentiation gives D⃗=E⃗\vec D=\vec ED=E, H⃗=B⃗\vec H=\vec BH=B — the trivial linear law — and (3) gives H=D⃗⋅E⃗−12(E2−B2)=12(E2+B2)\mathcal H = \vec D\cdot\vec E-\tfrac12(E^2-B^2)=\tfrac12(E^2+B^2)H=D⋅E−21​(E2−B2)=21​(E2+B2), the familiar field energy. Maxwell is the one case where the vacuum is not polarizable; any nonlinear L\mathcal LL makes D⃗≠E⃗\vec D\ne\vec ED=E, and the vacuum responds.

    What can L\mathcal LL depend on? The two invariants

    Gauge invariance is free. Lorentz invariance is the real constraint: L\mathcal LL must be a Lorentz scalar built from FμνF_{\mu\nu}Fμν​ with no free indices. In four dimensions there are exactly two independent such scalars:

    s≡−14FμνFμν=12(E2−B2),p≡−14F~μνFμν=E⃗⋅B⃗,s \equiv -\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2}(E^2 - B^2), \qquad p \equiv -\frac{1}{4}\tilde F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu} = \vec E\cdot\vec B,s≡−41​FμνFμν​=21​(E2−B2),p≡−41​F~μνFμν​=E⋅B,

    where F~μν=12ϵμνρσFρσ\tilde F^{\mu\nu}=\tfrac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}F~μν=21​ϵμνρσFρσ​ is the dual field strength (it swaps E⃗↔B⃗\vec E\leftrightarrow\vec BE↔B up to signs). So in four dimensions the whole freedom of the theory is one function of two numbers:

      L=L(s,p)  .\boxed{\;\mathcal L = \mathcal L(s,p)\;}.L=L(s,p)​.

    Maxwell is simply L=s\mathcal L = sL=s. Everything else — Born–Infeld included — is a different choice of this function. Note sss is a true scalar but p=E⃗⋅B⃗p=\vec E\cdot\vec Bp=E⋅B is a pseudoscalar (it flips sign under parity), so a parity-invariant theory can use ppp only through p2p^2p2.

    Why the list stops at two. Given a generic (non-null) electromagnetic field, you can always find a frame where E⃗\vec EE and B⃗\vec BB are parallel — boosting mixes them until the perpendicular parts cancel. In that frame the field is described by just two magnitudes, E∥E_\parallelE∥​ and B∥B_\parallelB∥​, and

    E∥2−B∥2=2s,E∥B∥=p.E_\parallel^2 - B_\parallel^2 = 2s, \qquad E_\parallel B_\parallel = p.E∥2​−B∥2​=2s,E∥​B∥​=p.

    Any Lorentz scalar built algebraically from FFF is a frame-independent number, so it can depend only on these two frame-independent magnitudes — on sss and ppp. There is nothing else. (Degenerate cases s=0s=0s=0 or p=0p=0p=0 follow by continuity.) That is why sss and ppp are the only invariants in four dimensions.

    One caveat on locality. All of this assumes L\mathcal LL is algebraic in FFF. Derivative terms like (∂ρFμν)(∂ρFμν)(\partial_\rho F_{\mu\nu})(\partial^\rho F^{\mu\nu})(∂ρ​Fμν​)(∂ρFμν) are also legal, gauge-invariant, and Lorentz-invariant, but they live outside this framework. Born–Infeld and its neighbors are the local, algebraic L(s,p)\mathcal L(s,p)L(s,p); the ∂F\partial F∂F corrections are a separate story, and the first thing this framework misses.

    Why this is the right home for the brane gauge field

    Two demands pick out the brane theory from the infinite space of L(s,p)\mathcal L(s,p)L(s,p):

    1. Reduce to Maxwell at weak field — because quantizing open strings already produced a genuine Maxwell photon on the brane (module 19). So L→s\mathcal L \to sL→s as F→0F\to 0F→0.
    2. A maximal electric field Ecrit=1/(2πα′)≡bE_{\rm crit}=1/(2\pi\alpha')\equiv bEcrit​=1/(2πα′)≡b — because a charged string endpoint cannot be pulled harder than the string tension (Electric and magnetic fields on D-branes). Maxwell has no such scale, so the theory must be nonlinear.

    These two conditions nearly pin down L\mathcal LL, and the simplest function meeting them is the Born–Infeld Lagrangian — the next note, Born-Infeld action.

    Open questions

    • General nonlinear theories exhibit birefringence: different polarizations travel at different speeds through a background field. Born–Infeld is the special member with no birefringence — a distinguishing virtue not derived here (it comes from the specific p2p^2p2 term in the BI Lagrangian).
    • In D≠4D\ne 4D=4 the counting of invariants changes — there is no single dual scalar ppp — yet the determinant form of Born–Infeld still makes sense in any dimension. The clean "function of s,ps,ps,p" statement is special to four dimensions.

    Exercise

    Show that the Maxwell Lagrangian LM=12(E2−B2)\mathcal L_M = \tfrac12(E^2-B^2)LM​=21​(E2−B2) gives back the trivial constitutive law and the standard field energy. Concretely, use D⃗=∂L/∂E⃗\vec D=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\vec ED=∂L/∂E, H⃗=−∂L/∂B⃗\vec H=-\partial\mathcal L/\partial\vec BH=−∂L/∂B, and H=D⃗⋅E⃗−L\mathcal H=\vec D\cdot\vec E-\mathcal LH=D⋅E−L to compute D⃗\vec DD, H⃗\vec HH, and H\mathcal HH. Then, as a one-line sign check, confirm that a Lagrangian with the wrong relative sign, L=12(E2+B2)\mathcal L=\tfrac12(E^2+B^2)L=21​(E2+B2), would give the unphysical H⃗=−B⃗\vec H=-\vec BH=−B — showing why the E2−B2E^2-B^2E2−B2 combination (i.e. sss) is forced.

    Solution

    Differentiate componentwise. Since LM=12(Ex2+Ey2+Ez2)−12(Bx2+By2+Bz2)\mathcal L_M=\tfrac12(E_x^2+E_y^2+E_z^2) - \tfrac12(B_x^2+B_y^2+B_z^2)LM​=21​(Ex2​+Ey2​+Ez2​)−21​(Bx2​+By2​+Bz2​), Di=∂LM∂Ei=Ei  ⇒  D⃗=E⃗,Hi=−∂LM∂Bi=−(−Bi)=Bi  ⇒  H⃗=B⃗.D_i = \frac{\partial\mathcal L_M}{\partial E_i} = E_i \;\Rightarrow\; \vec D=\vec E, \qquad H_i = -\frac{\partial\mathcal L_M}{\partial B_i} = -(-B_i) = B_i \;\Rightarrow\; \vec H=\vec B.Di​=∂Ei​∂LM​​=Ei​⇒D=E,Hi​=−∂Bi​∂LM​​=−(−Bi​)=Bi​⇒H=B. The two minus signs on the magnetic side — one from the definition H⃗=−∂L/∂B⃗\vec H=-\partial\mathcal L/\partial\vec BH=−∂L/∂B, one from the −B2-B^2−B2 in LM\mathcal L_MLM​ — cancel to give H⃗=+B⃗\vec H=+\vec BH=+B. This is the trivial linear law: the Maxwell vacuum is not polarizable.

    For the energy, use D⃗=E⃗\vec D=\vec ED=E: H=D⃗⋅E⃗−LM=E2−12(E2−B2)=12(E2+B2),\mathcal H = \vec D\cdot\vec E - \mathcal L_M = E^2 - \tfrac12(E^2-B^2) = \tfrac12(E^2+B^2),H=D⋅E−LM​=E2−21​(E2−B2)=21​(E2+B2), the familiar electromagnetic field energy — manifestly positive, as an energy must be.

    Sign check. Take L=12(E2+B2)\mathcal L=\tfrac12(E^2+B^2)L=21​(E2+B2) instead. Now Hi=−∂L/∂Bi=−BiH_i=-\partial\mathcal L/\partial B_i=-B_iHi​=−∂L/∂Bi​=−Bi​, so H⃗=−B⃗\vec H=-\vec BH=−B: the magnetic response points backwards. Equivalently the energy would come out H=12(E2−B2)\mathcal H=\tfrac12(E^2-B^2)H=21​(E2−B2), which is negative for strong magnetic fields — unbounded below, an unstable theory. The relativistically correct scalar is s=12(E2−B2)s=\tfrac12(E^2-B^2)s=21​(E2−B2) (from −14F2-\tfrac14 F^2−41​F2); its sign is fixed by Lorentz invariance, and it is exactly the sign that makes the energy positive. The apparent sign flip between L\mathcal LL and H\mathcal HH on the BBB terms is the Legendre transform doing its job.
