Role and scenarios

  • Low: veteran/transaction congestion holds him near 20-22 minutes and he is only a three-point streamer.
  • Base: starts beside Darius Garland, plays 28 minutes and alternates on-ball reps with off-ball shooting.
  • High: Leonard leaves, Mathurin does not return and Wagler reaches 31-33 minutes with an 18/5/5 line.

Wagler scored 23 points with four threes and four assists against Utah in Summer League, but that is role evidence rather than an NBA rate forecast. Career record

Pre-NBA: Illinois, one season (2025-26). Wagler started all 37 games as a freshman and averaged 17.9 points, 5.1 rebounds, 4.2 assists, 0.9 steals and 0.4 blocks in 33.9 minutes on .445/.397/.796. Three-point makes per game and turnovers per game are not verified from the sources reachable here; the shooting percentages above are. He was Big Ten Freshman of the Year and Big Ten All-Freshman, first-team All-Big Ten, a consensus second-team All-American, and won the Jerry West Award as the nation's top shooting guard. His signature game was January 24, 2026 at Purdue: 46 points on 13-of-17 shooting and 9-of-11 from three with 11-of-13 at the line in an 88-82 upset of the No. 4 Boilermakers — an Illinois freshman record, a school single-game record for made threes, and the most points a Big Ten player has scored against an AP top-five opponent. He came out of Shawnee Mission Northwest (Kansas) as a four-star recruit, not a consensus top-tier prospect. Draft: 2026, first round, 5th overall, Los Angeles Clippers, June 23, 2026; no draft-night trade.

Wagler has no NBA regular-season record yet. He has not appeared in an NBA game, so no career table is given.

Summer League 2026 (Las Vegas, Clippers): through his first three games he averaged 15.3 points, 3.0 rebounds and 3.0 assists in 28.1 minutes on .342/.353/.800. The individual lines show the volatility: 7 points on 1-of-7 shooting (1-of-5 from three) with 2 rebounds and 1 assist in 27 minutes in the opener against Sacramento; 23 points on 4-of-7 from three with 4 assists and a steal against Utah; then 26 points (9-of-19 FG, 2-of-7 3PT, 4-of-5 FT) with 4 rebounds, 2 assists, a steal and a block in 28 minutes in the finale against Minnesota, in which the Clippers set a Summer League record with 128 points. The volume and the on-ball reps are the signal; the .342 field-goal percentage across the sample is too small and too noisy to read as a rate forecast.

Fantasy history: None. He has never played a fantasy-relevant NBA game, so there is no year-by-year nine-category record to report. Any 2026-27 rank is a projection, not history.

Durability: No NBA history. The available college data point is that he played and started all 37 games at Illinois as a freshman with no reported absence — a clean single-season record, though one year is weak evidence either way. The scouting flag carried through the draft cycle was a thin frame at roughly 6'5" and 188 pounds, which is a load-management question for an NBA rookie season rather than a documented injury history.

Trajectory: The relevant history is one college season, and what it shows is a genuine outlier freshman year rather than a system-inflated one: 17.9 points on .445/.397/.796 with 4.2 assists, at high minutes, in the Big Ten, at age 18. The three-point percentage on real volume plus a near-.800 free-throw mark is the most predictive pairing in a shooting prospect's profile, and it is the reason the fifth pick is defensible despite an ordinary recruiting ranking — he improved faster than his high school evaluation implied, which usually means the development curve is still steep. The counterweight is that his college efficiency came with the ball in his hands constantly, and his Summer League field-goal percentage of .342 against NBA-adjacent length is the first evidence of what happens when the shot quality drops. He turns 20 in February 2027, making him among the youngest players in the league next season, so the honest expectation for 2026-27 is a high-volume, low-efficiency rookie year whose threes and assists are usable and whose field-goal percentage is a category liability. The dynasty case rests on the shooting indicators; the redraft case rests entirely on Los Angeles handing him minutes he has not yet earned.
