1f:T3141d,<p>One end of the string lives on the big brane (D<span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span>) and slides freely; the other is pinned to the small brane (D<span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>q</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">q</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span></span></span></span>). A direction that runs <em>along</em> the big brane but <em>across</em> the small one leaves that string free at one end and frozen at the other. A string clamped on one side and loose on the other rings at half-integer frequencies — an organ pipe open at one end, whose lowest note is a quarter wavelength rather than a half. Half-integer instead of integer modes is the entire content of this note; the shifted vacuum energy and the missing photon both follow from it.</p>
<p>Builds on <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-iv-branes-and-duality/18-d-branes-as-boundary-geometry/boundary-conditions-as-geometry/" class="wiki-link">Boundary conditions as geometry</a> and <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-iv-branes-and-duality/18-d-branes-as-boundary-geometry/strings-between-parallel-branes/" class="wiki-link">Strings between parallel branes</a>.</p>
<h2 id="three-kinds-of-direction-not-two">Three kinds of direction, not two</h2>
<p>For one brane (or two parallel ones of the <em>same</em> dimension) every direction is either tangent (Neumann, free endpoint) or normal (Dirichlet, pinned endpoint). Put down a D<span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span>- and a D<span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>q</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">q</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span></span></span></span>-brane with <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>q</mi><mo>&#x3C;</mo><mi>p</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">q&#x3C;p</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7335em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">q</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">&#x3C;</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">p</span></span></span></span>, parallel and nested, and a third possibility opens up: a direction can be tangent to the big brane but normal to the small one. So directions split into <strong>three</strong> classes.</p>
