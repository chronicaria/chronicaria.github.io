    Free boson OPEs

    Every short-distance product in the free-boson worldsheet CFT is built by Wick-contracting fields against a single number: the propagator \partial X(z)\,\partial X(w)\sim-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}(z-w)^{-2} . This note runs the four contractions that matter — the propagator itself, T\,\partial X ( \partial X is a weight-1 primary), T\,e^{ikX} (a vertex operator of weight \alpha'k^2/4 ), and T\,T (which exposes the central charge c=1 ).

    What an OPE is

    Think of a local operator as a disturbance stamped onto the worldsheet at a point. When you bring two disturbances close together, from far away they look like one combined disturbance — some superposition of the operators the theory has to offer. The operator product expansion (OPE) is the precise Taylor-like series for that merger: \mathcal O_i(z)\,\mathcal O_j(w)=\sum_k C^k_{ij}(z-w)\,\mathcal O_k(w).\tag{1} It is not an approximation. Inside a correlation function (with all other operators kept far away) it is an exact identity, convergent up to the next nearest insertion. The singular terms — the ones that blow up as z→wz\to wz→w — carry all the physics; they play the role that commutators play in canonical quantization, telling you how operators transform and what algebra they close into. Everything finite we bundle into "+…+\ldots+…".

    For a free boson the entire theory is one contraction. Learn the double pole below, and every OPE is bookkeeping — count the ways two ∂X\partial X∂X's can touch, and Taylor-expand.

    The working recipe:

    1. Write composites with normal-ordering colons : ::\ :: : (the quantum "don't self-contract" instruction).
    2. Contract every pair of ∂X\partial X∂X's across the two operators, each contraction →\to→ one propagator.
    3. Keep only singular powers of z−wz-wz−w.
    4. Taylor-expand any surviving field from zzz to www.
    5. Read off weights by matching to the stress-tensor template.

    This is just Wick's theorem, reread as a short-distance calculus.

    The one number: the propagator

    The Euclidean free-scalar action and its equation of motion are S=14πα′∫d2σ ∂αX ∂αX,∂∂ˉX=0.S=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\partial_\alpha X\,\partial^\alpha X,\qquad \partial\bar\partial X=0.S=4πα′1​∫d2σ∂α​X∂αX,∂∂ˉX=0. To get the propagator, use that the path integral of a total derivative vanishes; inserting X(σ′)X(\sigma')X(σ′) before integrating by parts gives ⟨∂2X(σ) X(σ′)⟩=−2πα′ δ(σ−σ′)\langle\partial^2 X(\sigma)\,X(\sigma')\rangle=-2\pi\alpha'\,\delta(\sigma-\sigma')⟨∂2X(σ)X(σ′)⟩=−2πα′δ(σ−σ′). Solving this with the 2D Green's-function identity ∂2ln⁡∣σ−σ′∣2=4πδ(σ−σ′)\partial^2\ln|\sigma-\sigma'|^2=4\pi\delta(\sigma-\sigma')∂2ln∣σ−σ′∣2=4πδ(σ−σ′) yields X(σ)X(σ′)=−α′2ln⁡(σ−σ′)2+…X(\sigma)X(\sigma')=-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\ln(\sigma-\sigma')^2+\ldotsX(σ)X(σ′)=−2α′​ln(σ−σ′)2+… The logarithm is a warning: XXX itself has no clean transformation law under conformal maps, so the fundamental field XXX is not a good CFT operator. Its derivative is. Splitting X(z,zˉ)=X(z)+Xˉ(zˉ)X(z,\bar z)=X(z)+\bar X(\bar z)X(z,zˉ)=X(z)+Xˉ(zˉ), the holomorphic half obeys X(z)X(w)=−α′2ln⁡(z−w)+…X(z)X(w)=-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\ln(z-w)+\ldotsX(z)X(w)=−2α′​ln(z−w)+…, and differentiating once in each argument turns the log into a clean pole: \boxed{\,\partial X(z)\,\partial X(w)=-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\frac{1}{(z-w)^2}+\text{non-singular}\,}\tag{2} This single double pole is the whole dynamical content of the free boson. (Check: ∂z ⁣[−α′2ln⁡(z−w)]=−α′2(z−w)−1\partial_z\!\left[-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\ln(z-w)\right]=-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}(z-w)^{-1}∂z​[−2α′​ln(z−w)]=−2α′​(z−w)−1, then ∂w(z−w)−1=+(z−w)−2\partial_w(z-w)^{-1}=+(z-w)^{-2}∂w​(z−w)−1=+(z−w)−2, giving −α′2(z−w)−2-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}(z-w)^{-2}−2α′​(z−w)−2.) The α′\alpha'α′ out front will reappear as ℏ\hbarℏ would — it marks these as quantum effects.

    For the string's DDD embedding coordinates, attach a spacetime index and the flat metric: ∂Xμ(z) ∂Xν(w)∼−α′2ημν(z−w)2.\partial X^\mu(z)\,\partial X^\nu(w)\sim-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\frac{\eta^{\mu\nu}}{(z-w)^2}.∂Xμ(z)∂Xν(w)∼−2α′​(z−w)2ημν​. The ημν\eta^{\mu\nu}ημν (mostly-plus signature) means contractions can carry a timelike minus sign — yet each of the DDD directions contributes an identical, positive amount to the central charge below, because there the two indices meet as ημνημν=D\eta^{\mu\nu}\eta_{\mu\nu}=Dημνημν​=D. There is no sign subtlety in the OPE route to ccc; the well-known "timelike sign squares away" caveat belongs to the mode-commutator route in Virasoro algebra and central charge, not here.

    The weight dictionary

    An operator O\mathcal OO has weight (h,hˉ)(h,\bar h)(h,hˉ) if under z→z′(z)z\to z'(z)z→z′(z) it transforms as O→(∂z′/∂z)−h(∂zˉ′/∂zˉ)−hˉ O\mathcal O\to(\partial z'/\partial z)^{-h}(\partial\bar z'/\partial\bar z)^{-\bar h}\,\mathcal OO→(∂z′/∂z)−h(∂zˉ′/∂zˉ)−hˉO. Equivalently — and this is the version we use — its singular OPE with the stress tensor reads T(z)\,\mathcal O(w)=\frac{h\,\mathcal O(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\frac{\partial\mathcal O(w)}{z-w}+\ldots\tag{3} The double pole hands you hhh; the single pole is always ∂O\partial\mathcal O∂O (the operator's own derivative). An operator is primary when its TOT\mathcal OTO OPE has nothing worse than that double pole — no higher poles. The combination Δ=h+hˉ\Delta=h+\bar hΔ=h+hˉ is the scaling dimension, s=h−hˉs=h-\bar hs=h−hˉ the spin. All that follows is: build TTT, contract, and match to (3).

    Throughout, T(z)=−1α′: ⁣∂X ∂X ⁣:T(z)=-\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}:\!\partial X\,\partial X\!:T(z)=−α′1​:∂X∂X: from Worldsheet stress tensor.

    Example 1: T ∂XT\,\partial XT∂X — ∂X\partial X∂X is a weight-1 primary

    TTT holds two ∂X\partial X∂X's; either can contract with the lone ∂X(w)\partial X(w)∂X(w), so a combinatorial factor 2 appears: T(z) ∂X(w)=−1α′: ⁣∂X(z)∂X(z) ⁣: ∂X(w)=−2α′ ∂X(z)(−α′21(z−w)2)⏟propagator+…=∂X(z)(z−w)2+…T(z)\,\partial X(w)=-\frac{1}{\alpha'}:\!\partial X(z)\partial X(z)\!:\,\partial X(w)=-\frac{2}{\alpha'}\,\partial X(z)\underbrace{\left(-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\frac{1}{(z-w)^2}\right)}_{\text{propagator}}+\ldots=\frac{\partial X(z)}{(z-w)^2}+\ldotsT(z)∂X(w)=−α′1​:∂X(z)∂X(z):∂X(w)=−α′2​∂X(z)propagator(−2α′​(z−w)21​)​​+…=(z−w)2∂X(z)​+… The uncontracted ∂X(z)\partial X(z)∂X(z) still sits at zzz; Taylor-expand it to www via ∂X(z)=∂X(w)+(z−w)∂2X(w)+…\partial X(z)=\partial X(w)+(z-w)\partial^2X(w)+\ldots∂X(z)=∂X(w)+(z−w)∂2X(w)+…:  T(z) ∂X(w)=∂X(w)(z−w)2+∂2X(w)z−w+… \boxed{\,T(z)\,\partial X(w)=\frac{\partial X(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\frac{\partial^2 X(w)}{z-w}+\ldots\,}T(z)∂X(w)=(z−w)2∂X(w)​+z−w∂2X(w)​+…​ Matching to (3): the double-pole coefficient is 1⋅∂X1\cdot\partial X1⋅∂X, so h=1h=1h=1; the single pole is exactly ∂(∂X)=∂2X\partial(\partial X)=\partial^2X∂(∂X)=∂2X as required — nothing worse. So ∂X\partial X∂X is a primary of weight (1,0)(1,0)(1,0), the prototype holomorphic current. (Factor check: 2×(−1α′)×(−α′2)=+12\times(-\tfrac1{\alpha'})\times(-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2})=+12×(−α′1​)×(−2α′​)=+1.)

    Example 2: T eikXT\,e^{ikX}TeikX — a vertex operator of weight α′k2/4\alpha'k^2/4α′k2/4

    The normal-ordered exponential : ⁣eikX ⁣::\!e^{ikX}\!::eikX: is the tachyon vertex operator — the local operator that creates the string's ground state. Warm up with its ∂X\partial X∂X OPE: expand eikX=∑n(ik)nn!Xne^{ikX}=\sum_n\tfrac{(ik)^n}{n!}X^neikX=∑n​n!(ik)n​Xn; a single ∂X(z)\partial X(z)∂X(z) can contract with any one of the nnn factors of X(w)X(w)X(w), and the surviving Xn−1X^{n-1}Xn−1 resums back into the exponential: ∂X(z) : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:  =(ik) ⁣(−α′21z−w): ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:+…=−iα′k2 : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:z−w+…\partial X(z)\,:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:\;=(ik)\!\left(-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\frac{1}{z-w}\right):\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:+\ldots=-\frac{i\alpha' k}{2}\,\frac{:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:}{z-w}+\ldots∂X(z):eikX(w):=(ik)(−2α′​z−w1​):eikX(w):+…=−2iα′k​z−w:eikX(w):​+… (Here ∂X\partial X∂X contracts with XXX, not ∂X\partial X∂X, so only a single power of z−wz-wz−w appears — a simple pole, not a double.) Now feed this into T=−1α′: ⁣∂X∂X ⁣:T=-\tfrac1{\alpha'}:\!\partial X\partial X\!:T=−α′1​:∂X∂X:. Contracting both ∂X\partial X∂X's in TTT with the exponential gives the double pole; contracting one gives the single pole:  T(z) : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:  =α′k2/4(z−w)2 : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:+∂ : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:z−w+… \boxed{\,T(z)\,:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:\;=\frac{\alpha'k^2/4}{(z-w)^2}\,:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:+\frac{\partial\,:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:}{z-w}+\ldots\,}T(z):eikX(w):=(z−w)2α′k2/4​:eikX(w):+z−w∂:eikX(w):​+…​ The double-pole coefficient is h=α′k2/4h=\alpha'k^2/4h=α′k2/4, and the single pole is the full derivative of the operator, so : ⁣eikX ⁣::\!e^{ikX}\!::eikX: is primary with weight h=hˉ=α′k2/4h=\bar h=\alpha'k^2/4h=hˉ=α′k2/4. The weight is proportional to α′\alpha'α′ — a purely quantum result, invisible classically, born entirely from the log in the XXXXXX propagator (classically eikXe^{ikX}eikX would look dimensionless). This is why the marginality condition h+hˉ=2h+\bar h=2h+hˉ=2 reproduces the closed-string tachyon mass shell α′k2=4\alpha'k^2=4α′k2=4, i.e. M2=−4/α′M^2=-4/\alpha'M2=−4/α′; see Mass-shell relation.

    Example 3: T TT\,TTT — the central charge c=1c=1c=1

    Now the stress tensor with itself. Each TTT carries two ∂X\partial X∂X's, so there are two contraction patterns. Contracting both pairs (2 ways) makes the leading quartic pole; contracting one pair (4 ways) makes the term proportional to TTT: T(z)T(w)=1α′2[2(−α′21(z−w)2)2+4(−α′21(z−w)2): ⁣∂X(z)∂X(w) ⁣:]+…T(z)T(w)=\frac{1}{\alpha'^2}\left[2\left(-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\frac{1}{(z-w)^2}\right)^2+4\left(-\frac{\alpha'}{2}\frac{1}{(z-w)^2}\right):\!\partial X(z)\partial X(w)\!:\right]+\ldotsT(z)T(w)=α′21​[2(−2α′​(z−w)21​)2+4(−2α′​(z−w)21​):∂X(z)∂X(w):]+… The first bracket gives 1α′2⋅2⋅α′24(z−w)−4=12(z−w)−4\tfrac{1}{\alpha'^2}\cdot2\cdot\tfrac{\alpha'^2}{4}(z-w)^{-4}=\tfrac12(z-w)^{-4}α′21​⋅2⋅4α′2​(z−w)−4=21​(z−w)−4. In the second, Taylor-expand : ⁣∂X(z)∂X(w) ⁣::\!\partial X(z)\partial X(w)\!::∂X(z)∂X(w): to www and use −1α′: ⁣∂X∂X ⁣:=T-\tfrac1{\alpha'}:\!\partial X\partial X\!:=T−α′1​:∂X∂X:=T; the constant piece rebuilds 2T(w)(z−w)−22T(w)(z-w)^{-2}2T(w)(z−w)−2 and the linear piece ∂T(w)(z−w)−1\partial T(w)(z-w)^{-1}∂T(w)(z−w)−1:  T(z) T(w)=1/2(z−w)4+2T(w)(z−w)2+∂T(w)z−w+… \boxed{\,T(z)\,T(w)=\frac{1/2}{(z-w)^4}+\frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\frac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}+\ldots\,}T(z)T(w)=(z−w)41/2​+(z−w)22T(w)​+z−w∂T(w)​+…​ The (z−w)−2(z-w)^{-2}(z−w)−2 and (z−w)−1(z-w)^{-1}(z−w)−1 terms have coefficients 222 and ∂\partial∂: read off h=2h=2h=2, as TTT must have (dimension 2, spin 2). But the (z−w)−4(z-w)^{-4}(z−w)−4 term is new — TTT is not primary. Comparing with the universal form T(z)T(w)=c/2(z−w)4+…T(z)T(w)=\tfrac{c/2}{(z-w)^4}+\ldotsT(z)T(w)=(z−w)4c/2​+…, c2=12  ⟹  c=1(one free boson).\frac{c}{2}=\frac12\;\Longrightarrow\;\boxed{c=1}\quad\text{(one free boson).}2c​=21​⟹c=1​(one free boson). That quartic pole is the central charge. Physically it is the failure of TTT to be a genuine tensor — the quantum anomaly in how the stress tensor responds to conformal maps, and equivalently the theory's count of degrees of freedom. One free boson carries c=1c=1c=1; DDD decoupled bosons give c=Dc=Dc=D (the quartic poles simply add, all with the positive sign noted above). This is the OPE face of the object analyzed in Virasoro algebra and central charge, and the reason the DDD embedding coordinates push ccc toward the ctotal=0c_{\text{total}}=0ctotal​=0 condition that fixes D=26D=26D=26.

    Weight ≠\ne= primary (a caution)

    ∂2X\partial^2X∂2X has weight (2,0)(2,0)(2,0) but is not primary: acting with TTT and differentiating the Example-1 result once more produces a cubic pole, T(z) ∂2X(w)=2 ∂X(w)(z−w)3+2 ∂2X(w)(z−w)2+…T(z)\,\partial^2X(w)=\frac{2\,\partial X(w)}{(z-w)^3}+\frac{2\,\partial^2X(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\ldotsT(z)∂2X(w)=(z−w)32∂X(w)​+(z−w)22∂2X(w)​+… So "has a definite weight hhh" and "is primary" are different tests: primary additionally demands no pole worse than (z−w)−2(z-w)^{-2}(z−w)−2.

    Open questions

    • State-operator map. The line "h+hˉ=2h+\bar h=2h+hˉ=2 marginal ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ tachyon mass shell" is orientation only; the careful dictionary between vertex-operator weights and the string spectrum lives in State-operator correspondence and Vertex operators and physical-state conditions.
    • Normal ordering. Colons are kept here wherever a coincident product could confuse; every same-point product of fields inside TTT is normal-ordered by definition.

    Exercise

    The vertex-operator weight h=α′k2/4h=\alpha'k^2/4h=α′k2/4 came out proportional to α′\alpha'α′, which we called "purely quantum." Pin that down. Show, directly from the ∂X ⁣⋅ ⁣eikX\partial X\!\cdot\!e^{ikX}∂X⋅eikX contraction, that the double-contraction term in T(z) : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:T(z)\,:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:T(z):eikX(w): has coefficient α′k2/4\alpha'k^2/4α′k2/4 — and identify exactly where the classical answer (h=0h=0h=0) is overturned.

    Solution

    Write T=−1α′: ⁣∂X(z)∂X(z) ⁣:T=-\tfrac1{\alpha'}:\!\partial X(z)\partial X(z)\!:T=−α′1​:∂X(z)∂X(z): and contract both of its ∂X\partial X∂X's with the exponential. Doing this to both ∂X(z)\partial X(z)∂X(z)'s gives two such factors, and the exponential comes back out once: −1α′(−iα′k21z−w) ⁣2: ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:  =−1α′⋅(i)2α′2k241(z−w)2 : ⁣eikX(w) ⁣:.-\frac{1}{\alpha'}\left(-\frac{i\alpha' k}{2}\frac{1}{z-w}\right)^{\!2}:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:\;=-\frac{1}{\alpha'}\cdot\frac{(i)^2\alpha'^2k^2}{4}\frac{1}{(z-w)^2}\,:\!e^{ikX(w)}\!:.−α′1​(−2iα′k​z−w1​)2:eikX(w):=−α′1​⋅4(i)2α′2k2​(z−w)21​:eikX(w):. Here is where the classical answer dies: each contraction is one power of the propagator, and the propagator is ∝α′\propto\alpha'∝α′ (it came from the log in ⟨XX⟩=−α′2ln⁡∣⋯∣2\langle XX\rangle=-\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\ln|\cdots|^2⟨XX⟩=−2α′​ln∣⋯∣2).
