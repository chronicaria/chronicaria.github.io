<h2 id="foundations-for-reading-the-evidence">Foundations for reading the evidence</h2>
<ul>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/atmospheric-retrieval-degeneracies/" class="wiki-link">Atmospheric retrieval degeneracies</a> follows the full chain from calibrated photons through a forward model, likelihood, priors, and a conditional posterior rather than a unique literal atmosphere.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/radiative-and-advective-timescales-in-exoplanet-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Radiative and advective timescales in exoplanet atmospheres</a> compares radiation, transport, waves, rotation, drag, and chemistry without treating order-of-magnitude clocks as solved circulation.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/what-counts-as-weather-on-an-exoplanet/" class="wiki-link">What counts as weather on an exoplanet</a> defines an evidence threshold for atmospheric evolution and separates changing weather from rotation, static spatial structure, stellar variability, and instrumental drift.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="circulation-transport-and-chemistry">Circulation, transport, and chemistry</h2>
<ul>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/primitive-equations-of-planetary-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Primitive equations of planetary atmospheres</a> derives the hydrostatic, shallow-atmosphere, and traditional approximation bundle and identifies the exoplanet regimes that stress it.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/general-circulation-models-for-exoplanets/" class="wiki-link">General circulation models for exoplanets</a> explains how dynamical cores, radiation, closures, boundaries, and numerics turn governing equations into conditional atmospheric predictions.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/exoplanet-atmospheric-model-hierarchies/" class="wiki-link">Exoplanet atmospheric model hierarchies</a> organizes budgets, reduced models, columns, primitive-equation GCMs, full dynamics, and coupled models as controlled contrasts rather than a simple complexity ranking.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/drag-parameterizations-in-exoplanet-gcms/" class="wiki-link">Drag parameterizations in exoplanet GCMs</a> makes the torque, kinetic-energy sink, spatial geometry, numerical role, and physical interpretation of Rayleigh drag explicit.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/benchmarking-exoplanet-general-circulation-models/" class="wiki-link">Benchmarking exoplanet general circulation models</a> builds a verification ladder from analytic tests and conservation budgets through convergence, intercomparison, and observable-space evaluation.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/shallow-water-models-of-tidally-locked-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Shallow-water models of tidally locked atmospheres</a> isolates the forced Kelvin–Rossby response, wave adjustment, and eddy momentum transport while keeping the one-layer model distinct from a literal temperature or wind map.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/radiative-transfer-in-planetary-climate-models/" class="wiki-link">Radiative transfer in planetary climate models</a> compares Newtonian, grey, picket-fence, correlated-<span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>k</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">k</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0315em;">k</span></span></span></span>, and line-by-line schemes and shows how opacity, angular closure, and coupling choices shape simulated climate.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/atmospheric-circulation-on-tidally-locked-planets/" class="wiki-link">Atmospheric circulation on tidally locked planets</a> explains how stationary day–night forcing excites waves, overturning, and jets while leaving their observable signatures model-dependent.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/equatorial-superrotation/" class="wiki-link">Equatorial superrotation</a> derives the angular-momentum definition and wave-driven jet mechanism while explaining why an eastward hotspot is not itself a wind measurement.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/day-night-heat-redistribution/" class="wiki-link">Day-night heat redistribution</a> separates measured brightness contrasts from bolometric energy transport and develops the cloud, photosphere, opacity, and dynamical degeneracies behind redistribution estimates.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/cloud-formation-and-transport-beyond-earth/" class="wiki-link">Cloud formation and transport beyond Earth</a> connects saturation, nucleation, growth, settling, advection, and radiative feedback without treating aerosol opacity as a unique condensate identification.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/condensation-curves-and-cloud-base-prediction/" class="wiki-link">Condensation curves and cloud-base prediction</a> shows that a temperature-profile intersection is thermodynamic permission, not a unique prediction of particle formation, transport, opacity, or observability.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/cloud-radiative-feedbacks-in-exoplanet-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Cloud radiative feedbacks in exoplanet atmospheres</a> separates cloud radiative effect from feedback and shows when shortwave reflection, thermal blanketing, and patchy heating can reorganize circulation.</li>

<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/angular-momentum-budgets-of-superrotating-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Angular momentum budgets of superrotating atmospheres</a> closes the path from wave and mean-flow transports through physical torques and numerical residuals instead of treating a fast jet as its own explanation.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/magnetic-drag-in-ultra-hot-jupiter-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Magnetic drag in ultra-hot Jupiter atmospheres</a> distinguishes state-dependent Lorentz forces and induction from a universal linear braking time or a magnetic-field measurement.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/atmospheric-tides-on-exoplanets/" class="wiki-link">Atmospheric tides on exoplanets</a> separates phase-coherent thermal and gravitational forcing from arbitrary variability, then connects wave momentum transport, spin torque, and observability.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/wave-adjustment-in-tidally-locked-atmospheres/" class="wiki-link">Wave adjustment in tidally locked atmospheres</a> replaces a guessed advective clock with the gravity-wave, rotation, radiation, drag, and vertical-motion competition that controls thermal contrasts.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/photochemistry-circulation-coupling/" class="wiki-link">Photochemistry circulation coupling</a> follows the two-way loop from photolysis and kinetics through three-dimensional transport to opacity and radiative feedback.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="observation-and-inference">Observation and inference</h2>
<ul>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/transmission-spectroscopy/" class="wiki-link">Transmission spectroscopy</a> explains the slant geometry of a planetary limb, its sensitivity to scale height and opacity, and the coupled reference-radius, abundance, cloud, stellar, and instrumental degeneracies.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/stellar-contamination-in-exoplanet-spectra/" class="wiki-link">Stellar contamination in exoplanet spectra</a> shows how the transit chord and disk-average stellar spectrum can differ, producing slopes, bands, and epoch changes that overlap planetary signals.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/emission-spectroscopy-and-secondary-eclipses/" class="wiki-link">Emission spectroscopy and secondary eclipses</a> follows missing dayside photons from fitted eclipse depths through radiative transfer, temperature retrievals, and spatial degeneracies.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/exoplanet-phase-curves/" class="wiki-link">Exoplanet phase curves</a> separates the measured unresolved orbital light curve from brightness-temperature retrievals, maps, and circulation interpretations.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/high-resolution-doppler-spectroscopy-of-exoplanet-winds/" class="wiki-link">High-resolution Doppler spectroscopy of exoplanet winds</a> explains what planet-following line profiles measure and why orbital motion, rotation, winds, chemistry, and limb weighting must be separated.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/from-light-curves-to-atmospheric-maps/" class="wiki-link">From light curves to atmospheric maps</a> makes the mapping inverse problem explicit: disk integration has a null space, regularization supplies assumptions, and a plotting grid is not the same thing as spatial resolution.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/time-series-mapping-of-brown-dwarf-weather/" class="wiki-link">Time-series mapping of brown-dwarf weather</a> applies that inverse-problem discipline to rotational and spectral variability, including wavelength-dependent pressure sensitivity and the evolution–rotation degeneracy.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/contribution-functions-in-exoplanet-spectra/" class="wiki-link">Contribution functions in exoplanet spectra</a> explains why a spectral channel has a broad, state- and geometry-dependent pressure weighting rather than a uniquely observed altitude.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/radiative-timescale-diagnostics/" class="wiki-link">Radiative timescale diagnostics</a> defines perturbation protocols and prevents phase offsets or brightness contrasts from being mislabeled as directly observed relaxation times.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/opacity-uncertainty-and-circulation-inference/" class="wiki-link">Opacity uncertainty and circulation inference</a> traces opacity errors through both the climate model and the observation operator used to infer winds and temperatures.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/eclipse-mapping-and-atmospheric-structure/" class="wiki-link">Eclipse mapping and atmospheric structure</a> explains the occultation kernel, null space, and conditional path from ingress–egress photometry to low-order dayside brightness structure.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="current-frontier">Current frontier</h2>
<p>Rotational-transit spectra of WASP-121 b report different behavior at the planet's limbs, including increasing CO and slightly decreasing water signatures across the transit. This is evidence about spatial asymmetry, not a direct movie of weather.</p>
<p>Atmospheric work on WD 1856 b reports aerosol and hydrocarbon signatures and infers a migration and reheating history. Simulations of K2-18 b suggest conditions that may permit lightning, but do not establish observed lightning.</p>
<p><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/wasp-94a-b-morning-evening-cloud-asymmetry/" class="wiki-link">WASP-94A b morning-evening cloud asymmetry</a> separates the measured limb-spectrum difference from the retrieved cloudy-morning, clear-evening picture and the interpreted condensation–evaporation cycle.</p>
<p>Andrew's own numerical research enters this frontier through <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/mathematics/surface-pdes/bealesurfacesolver-research-moc/" class="wiki-link">BealeSurfaceSolver research (MOC)</a>. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/weather/exometeorology/one-gradient-two-physics-in-oblate-brown-dwarf-weather/" class="wiki-link">One gradient two physics in oblate brown-dwarf weather</a> uses an effective-potential level set to separate gravity-darkened thermal forcing from the circulation changes caused by oblate geometry; its reported signals are model results, not measured brown-dwarf figures.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why it matters</h2>
<p>The field is moving from one-dimensional retrieval summaries toward questions about limbs, circulation, clouds, chemistry, and time variability. The foundations above provide a common vocabulary for distinguishing measured, retrieved, simulated, interpreted, and forecast claims as that atlas grows.</p>
