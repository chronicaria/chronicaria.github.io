Role, floor and ceiling

Jordan is behind Derik Queen, Yves Missi and Karlo Matković. Floor: mentor with almost no rotation minutes. Ceiling: short bursts of 15 minutes, six rebounds and one block when two centers are unavailable. He won the 2025-26 Twyman-Stokes Teammate of the Year award, reinforcing the non-statistical reason for the signing. NBA, 2026

Injury and transaction monitor

The July re-signing secures his roster place more than his playing role. At his age, scheduled maintenance and matchup DNPs should be expected even without a specific carryover injury.

Career record

Pre-NBA: One season at Texas A&M (2007-08): 7.9 points, 6.0 rebounds and 1.3 blocks, starting 21 of 35 games, shooting .617 from the field and .437 from the line — the free-throw problem was visible from the beginning. Born July 21, 1988. Draft: 2008, second round, 35th overall, Los Angeles Clippers.

Honors: All-NBA First Team (2016), All-NBA Third Team (2015, 2017), All-Defensive First Team (2015, 2016), All-Star (2017), NBA rebounding leader (2014, 2015), field-goal-percentage leader five straight years (2013-2017), NBA champion with Denver (2023), Olympic gold (2016), and Twyman-Stokes Teammate of the Year (2026). He holds the NBA career regular-season field-goal-percentage record.

Durability: Exceptionally available in his prime — 360 consecutive games before pneumonia ended the streak in January 2016, and four seasons of 80-plus appearances. The decline since 2021-22 is role-driven rather than injury-driven: 48, 39, 36 and 56 games as a third center in Los Angeles, Philadelphia and Denver. The 12 games in 2025-26 are the fewest of his career, and the public record does not attribute them to a specific injury; they are consistent with sitting behind three younger centers.

Trajectory: Eighteen seasons and 1,123 games describe one of the most efficient finishers in league history — a career .673 from the field, two rebounding titles, and a five-year peak as an elite rim-protecting defensive anchor — attached to a free-throw stroke that made him unplayable in close games. That player has been gone for five years. What remains at 38 is a fourth center behind Derik Queen, Yves Missi and Karlo Matković, re-signed in July 2026 for the locker room as much as the roster spot. He becomes fantasy-relevant only if two of the three centers ahead of him are unavailable simultaneously, and even then the ceiling is a 15-minute rebounding burst, not a sustained role.
