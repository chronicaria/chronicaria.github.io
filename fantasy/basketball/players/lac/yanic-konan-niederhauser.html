Career record

Pre-NBA: Swiss-Ivorian center who played 2021-22 for Ehingen Urspring in Germany, splitting time between the youth side and the club's Pro A (second-division) team, before moving to US college basketball. Two seasons at Northern Illinois: 20 games and 2.2 points as a freshman in 2022-23, then 27 games with 26 starts and 7.3 points, 4.4 rebounds in 2023-24. Transferred to Penn State for 2024-25 and produced his one standout college year — 29 games, all starts, 25.1 MPG, 12.9 points, 6.3 rebounds and 2.3 blocks on 61% from the field in the Big Ten. That single season of shot-blocking and rim finishing is essentially the entire scouting case. Draft: 2025, round 1, pick 30, Los Angeles Clippers.

Totals reconcile: 64 field goals including one three, plus 47 free throws, gives 176 points over 41 games and 412 minutes, with 117 rebounds and 30 blocks.

Durability: 41 of 82 games, and the injury is the whole file. The Clippers announced a torn Lisfranc ligament in his right foot on March 6, 2026, requiring season-ending surgery. He then underwent a follow-up procedure in July 2026 to remove the hardware — described as a routine step — and was still in a walking boot in mid-July with no official return date. A five-to-eight-month estimate relayed in April pointed at November or December; the July procedure pushes that later, and local reporting suggests he may not play until after Christmas.

Trajectory: The rookie year did what a 30th pick's rookie year should: 64% from the field, 0.7 blocks in 10 minutes, no offense created outside the rim, no three-point game at all. That is a backup-center profile whose fantasy relevance is entirely blocks-plus-percentage, and it needs roughly 18 minutes a night to matter in nine-category leagues. He will not have those minutes at the start of 2026-27 because he will not be playing. The realistic path is a post-Christmas return, a G League ramp, and a spring push for the backup center job; he becomes worth adding only if he is cleared, shows the pre-injury vertical explosiveness, and an injury or trade moves the veteran center ahead of him out of the rotation.
