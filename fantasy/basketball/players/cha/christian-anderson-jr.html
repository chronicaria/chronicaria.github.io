Christian Anderson Jr

Christian Anderson Jr. is Charlotte's most natural developmental point guard and a strong dynasty shooting/passing bet, but size and rim-pressure questions make immediate redraft value unlikely.

Scouting-to-fantasy translation

Anderson may be the best shooter/passer combination in the class. He is a true pick-and-roll guard and can produce assists without dominating the ball. At roughly six feet, however, he struggles to pressure the rim and can lose passing windows against NBA length. That lowers his finishing, free-throw volume, and defensive floor.

  • Ceiling: wins 18 backup minutes; seven-to-eight points, four assists, 1.5 threes and one steal—deep-league value.
  • Median: 10-13 minutes with Greensboro assignments.
  • Floor: Charlotte signs a veteran PG and treats 2026-27 as a development year.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Two seasons at Texas Tech. As a freshman in 2024-25 he averaged 10.6 points, 3.0 rebounds and 2.2 assists on .429 from the floor and .380 from three (5.3 attempts), earning Big 12 All-Freshman honors. As a sophomore in 2025-26 he played all 33 games at 38.4 minutes and averaged 18.5 points, 3.6 rebounds, 7.4 assists and 1.5 steals on .472 FG and .415 from three — third-team All-American (AP, TSN, USBWA), first-team All-Big 12 and Big 12 Most Improved Player. German youth international: gold at the 2024 FIBA U18 EuroBasket (31 points and 5 assists in the final) and silver at the 2025 FIBA U19 World Cup at 17.3 points, 4.9 rebounds and 6.6 assists; called into the senior Germany squad for EuroBasket 2025. Draft: 2026, first round, 18th overall, Charlotte Hornets.

Anderson has not appeared in an NBA regular-season game. His most recent competitive season is the 2025-26 Texas Tech line above.

Durability: No missed time to report — 33 of 33 games as a sophomore at a 38.4-minute load.

Trajectory: The profile is unusually clean for a mid-first-rounder: two years of improvement, elite assist rate and 40-plus percent three-point shooting on real volume. The limits are physical — 6-1, 180, without the burst to collapse an NBA defense — so the questions are finishing and defending, not skill. He becomes fantasy-relevant if Charlotte declines to add a veteran point guard and hands him the backup job outright, or if the starter misses time; at 10-13 minutes with Greensboro assignments he is a dynasty hold only.
