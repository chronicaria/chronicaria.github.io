<h2 id="what-works">What Works</h2>
<p>Pendle has a real product: tokenized principal/yield markets, PT/YT trading, a custom AMM around implied APY, and Boros as a funding-rate/rates expansion. The product is one of the few DeFi designs that looks like a native capital-market primitive rather than another generic exchange.</p>
<p>The token mechanism is better than most crypto. Pendle routes a large share of yield and swap fees into PENDLE buybacks and sPENDLE rewards, and team/investor vesting cliffs appear largely past.</p>
<h2 id="what-the-deep-pass-changed">What The Deep Pass Changed</h2>
<p>The cash-flow asset is active sPENDLE, not passive liquid PENDLE. sPENDLE has a 14-day unstaking path, an instant-exit penalty, active eligibility rules, and legacy vePENDLE virtual balances that can dilute new stakers until they expire.</p>
<p>The fee base is not clean enough yet. Agents found a large share of active markets and active market TVL tied to points-colored assets, stable wrappers, RWA wrappers, or third-party redemption promises. Points-linked fees can be real, but they are less durable than cash-like trading fees.</p>
<p>Boros is strategically important but not thesis-carrying yet. It showed meaningful open interest and volume, but revenue was still small relative to the core Pendle thesis.</p>
<h2 id="main-risks">Main Risks</h2>
<ul>
<li>Points and yield-campaign cyclicality: recent revenue is far below peak 2025 levels.</li>
<li>Underlying-asset cascade risk: Pendle inherits depeg, default, bridge, oracle, freeze, and governance risk from the assets it tokenizes.</li>
<li>Admin/legal chokepoints: prior risk reviews flagged guardian/admin controls, fast pause/upgrade power, and incomplete trust minimization.</li>
<li>Maturity/liquidity risk: each PT maturity has isolated liquidity and can become hard to exit or liquidate.</li>
<li>Regulatory risk: Boros is not available to U.S. persons, and yield-bearing stablecoin wrappers face live regulatory scrutiny.</li>
</ul>
