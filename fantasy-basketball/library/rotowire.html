Audit method and evidence rules

This was a public/logged-out audit on 2026-07-18. I checked canonical pages, their public JSON endpoints where available, the official RotoWire Fantasy Basketball Podcast RSS feed, the official RotoWire Fantasy Basketball YouTube channel feed, and canonical X post URLs. A third-party Nitter RSS mirror was used only to discover the chronological order of recent @RotoWireNBA posts and retweets; every social item below links back to X.

The working reliability hierarchy is:

  1. Season-labeled table or dated article with an explicit format and horizon.
  2. Dated analyst podcast/video/post with the analyst and scoring context preserved.
  3. Current depth chart or news item as role evidence, not as a statistical projection.
  4. Offseason Summer League result as a watch signal, never as a direct NBA-minute forecast.
  5. Page shell update stamps, audience polls, and marketing methodology as metadata only.

What the custom tools are designed to do

The custom rankings, cheat-sheet and auction-value interfaces support H2H, rotisserie and points formats; ESPN, Yahoo, CBS and “Other” presets; custom team/roster counts; and a broad category menu including PTS, AST, REB, STL, BLK, 3PM, TO, FG%, FT%, 3P%, made/attempted shots, offensive/defensive rebounds, double-doubles and triple-doubles. RotoWire's front-end code also exposes Excel/CSV/PDF or image export actions on several tables, but complete data are generally a subscriber feature.

Early 2026–27 first-round conversation

The most explicit redraft discussion is the April 9 podcast episode “Everything You MUST Know For The NBA Playoff Run”, also on YouTube. The ranking segment begins at 19:00 and specifies nine-category rotisserie. Kirien Sprecher and Adam King were talking through personal boards, not publishing a final consensus list.

  • Clear Tier 1: Jokic and Wembanyama. King called Jokic the likely consensus No. 1 and safer choice; Sprecher personally preferred Wembanyama's ascending ceiling. Both treated them as the locked top two.
  • Clear Tier 2: SGA and Luka, with order depending on safety versus ceiling. SGA was framed as the steadier health/value bet; Luka as the plausible higher-upside choice.
  • Their final spoken recap of the first eight was Jokic, Wembanyama, SGA, Luka, Cade Cunningham, Tyrese Maxey, Giannis Antetokounmpo and Jayson Tatum. It was a pool more than a strict 1–8 order.
  • The next four discussed as a first-round tier were Cooper Flagg, Jalen Johnson, Tyrese Haliburton and Anthony Edwards. The conversation was iterative: King also argued Donovan Mitchell belonged in his top 12, so this should not be forced into a mechanically exact consensus top 12.
  • Scottie Barnes was just outside King's main group but could go top 10 for a compatible build. Kevin Durant and James Harden were placed just outside the top 12. Kawhi's per-game case was acknowledged, with availability risk making a pick around 14 hard to stomach.
  • Tatum's return was expected to push Jaylen Brown toward the third round. Derrick White was also discussed around that range, with a possible second-round outcome if his shooting normalizes because his value is supported by assists and defensive stats.

The April 6 episode “League-Winners, Early 2026-27 Draft Thoughts + Monday Mailbag” gives more market-shape evidence from Nick Whalen and Alex Barutha:

  • They talked themselves into Cooper Flagg as a first-rounder, with a defensible case as early as No. 7. Jokic, Wembanyama, Luka and SGA were treated as automatic players ahead of him; Cade and Anthony Edwards were likely ahead; the debate begins after that. Points leagues strengthen Flagg's case.
  • They preferred Flagg to Anthony Davis and Devin Booker in the thought exercise, but leaned toward the veteran safety of Tatum or Edwards.
  • Walker Kessler was projected as a likely sixth/seventh-round type because of rebounds and blocks, not as a clear per-minute breakout. Myles Turner was discussed around picks 90–110 and even later in points formats. Zach Edey was a possible injury discount with real risk. Amen Thompson was expected around the early second round, not as a consensus first-rounder.
  • In a contender-oriented keeper question, they preferred Trae Young over Keyonte George for the next one to two seasons; over a three-to-five-year horizon, George was the better asset argument.
  • The hosts repeatedly flagged the uncertainty at the back of Round 1: young players require star-level projection, while Curry, Durant, Harden, Davis and other older stars bring decline/availability risk.

These podcast takes are valuable because the scoring context, analyst and uncertainty are explicit. They are still April opinions and must be refreshed after free agency, Summer League, camp and a real ADP market.

Stated methodology

RotoWire says its season models use projected minutes, past performance, team context, pace and expected role. Daily projections additionally consider matchups/opponent defense and are analyst-reviewed for coaching decisions, injuries and lineup changes. Rankings convert multi-category projections into a single format-specific value. The content-syndication brief says projections exist for season-long, rest-of-season, daily and weekly horizons, while depth charts are updated through the draft, free agency and trades.

What RotoWire does not publish publicly is just as important: no feature weights, aging curves, playing-time algorithm, replacement-level calculation, confidence intervals or update log. Analyst review is explicitly described for daily projections; it should not be assumed for every full-season row without confirmation.

Auction values are described as proportional allocation of the league's total auction budget based on projected contribution. Position scarcity, role and category impact are mentioned, but no reproducible formula is disclosed.

Historical role tools that must not be mistaken for forecasts

  • NBA Minutes Report: public table contained 661 players and season MPG plus last 10/5/3 and game-by-game minutes. It is backward-looking 2025–26/postseason data. Large late-season values cannot be copied into 2026–27 minutes.
  • NBA Rotations: last-five rotation history by team, with old game dates. Useful to reconstruct how a coach finished the prior season, not to forecast a changed roster.
  • Playing Time Changes: compares recent 1/2/3/5/10/15/20-game windows. The logged-out page showed only a small gainers/losers sample. Treat it as retrospective signal.
  • Injury report: the public table returned 149 names with team, position, injury and status; expected return dates were subscriber-only. Summer League rest/minor statuses are mixed with serious injuries, so an offseason “Probable” label is not an opening-night forecast.
  • NBA news: current July 18 headlines and factual blurbs are public; the deeper fantasy analysis is generally locked. Summer League box-score notes are timely scouting inputs but weak evidence of regular-season minutes.

Access boundaries

  • Articles, podcast/video episodes, news headlines and a small set of article text are broadly public.
  • Full custom rankings, projections, cheat sheets, auction values, sleepers and draft software are subscription products.
  • The projection page exposes seven free rows; keeper rankings expose three; sleepers expose one; playing-time changes exposes a small sample.
  • The depth-chart index exposes five full teams, while an individual team page exposes one position.
  • Injury expected-return dates and detailed news analysis are usually locked.
  • The draft assistant is web-based and subscriber-only; it advertises continuously updated projections, custom scoring across 26 categories, custom rosters and generated ranks/dollar values.

This means “RotoWire has the data” and “the public encyclopedia can reproduce the data” are different claims. Preserve public evidence, never infer hidden table contents, and do not bypass the subscription boundary.

Learning note

RotoWire's most important lesson this early in the offseason is epistemic: freshness is field-specific. A player may have a current team code, a current news blurb and a current depth-chart position while the ranking, outlook and projection beside him still belong to 2025–26. The encyclopedia should therefore version season label, retrieval time, scoring format and evidence type separately instead of assigning one blanket “updated” status to a source.
