Role and range

Kalkbrenner's field-goal impact and blocks are real. In 31 starts he averaged roughly 8.5 points, 6.3 rebounds, and 1.7 blocks. Summer League also showed a possible passing wrinkle—10 points, nine rebounds, five assists, and three blocks on July 9—and Charlotte experimented with split-post actions and a Steinbach pairing.

Update 2026-07-19: end-of-Vegas coaching commentary confirmed both threads — he was used as an occasional post facilitator and directed pick-and-roll coverages vocally, while the staff asked for "more force" around the rim and better footwork. The same report shows Steinbach (22 points, 11 rebounds in Friday's game) playing "twin tower" lineups with him, which is the role-fragility risk this dossier already prices. HoopsRumors Hornets Notes, 2026-07-18

  • Ceiling: injury or trade produces 24-27 minutes; roughly 9/7 with 1.7-2.0 blocks and top-100-to-120 specialist value.
  • Median: 14-18 minutes and a blocks streamer.
  • Floor: 10-12 minutes as the fourth big, too little volume to roster outside very deep leagues.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Creighton, 2020-25, five seasons. Freshman 2020-21: 31 games, 5.9 points, 3.5 rebounds, 1.2 blocks on .645/.000/.489, leading all Big East freshmen in blocks. Sophomore 2021-22: 34 games and 34 starts, 29.4 minutes, 13.1 points, 7.7 rebounds, 2.6 blocks on .646/.250/.736, with 89 total blocks (10th nationally), Big East Defensive Player of the Year and honorable mention All-Big East; a knee injury in the 2022 NCAA tournament ended that postseason early. Junior 2022-23: 34 games, 15.9 points, 6.1 rebounds, 2.1 blocks on .695/.316/.795. Senior 2023-24: 35 games, 17.3 points, 7.6 rebounds, 3.1 blocks on .646/.296/.711. Fifth year 2024-25: 35 games, 19.2 points, 8.7 rebounds, 2.7 blocks on .653/.344/.681, opening the season with a career-high 49 points. He finished as a four-time Big East Defensive Player of the Year and took the Kareem Abdul-Jabbar Award, Naismith Defensive Player of the Year, and third-team All-American honors. Draft: 2025, second round, 34th overall, Charlotte. He posted 10 points and 11 rebounds in his NBA debut. In the 2026 Summer League opener against Orlando he had 10 points, nine rebounds, five assists, and three blocks in an 86-74 win.

The start count is 31, confirmed against ESPN, CBS, and Yahoo's season player grade; an earlier figure of 26 in this dossier was wrong and has been corrected. The per-start averages above come from CBS's starter split and should be treated as approximate.

Fantasy history: One NBA season, so there is no arc yet. The No. 143 per-game nine-category figure asserted earlier in this dossier could not be confirmed against a season-end source from Basketball Monster, Hashtag, or the ESPN Player Rater, so treat it as unverified rather than established. What is verifiable is the category shape, and it is extreme: a .753 field goal percentage that finished top-five in the NBA, 1.5 blocks in 21.4 minutes, and 5.5 rebounds, against literally zero made threes, 0.8 assists, and 7.6 points. That is a three-category specialist whose field goal percentage is the single most valuable thing he does, and it only carries weight in formats where FG% is weighted by volume. Points leagues rate him far worse, because 7.6 points and 0.9 turnovers on low usage produces almost no points-league total regardless of how efficient the shooting is.

Durability: 69 games in his only NBA season, 13 missed. The identified cause is a left elbow sprain suffered in late December 2025 — he was on the injury report as out for Charlotte's December 26 game at Orlando and December 31 against Golden State, by which point it was his fifth straight absence, and he returned January 10, 2026. He came back to a bench role rather than his previous starting job, with Moussa Diabaté having taken the spot. His only prior significant injury was the knee problem during the 2022 NCAA tournament; he then played 34, 35, and 35 games in his final three college seasons, which is a clean availability record.

Trajectory: There is one NBA season, so the college record does most of the work here, and it is unusually informative because five years at one school with monotonic scoring growth (5.9 to 13.1 to 15.9 to 17.3 to 19.2) on a field goal percentage that never dropped below .645 is a rare amount of evidence. The important thing that record shows is that the improvement was in volume and touches, not in shot difficulty — he shot .653 in his best college season and .753 as an NBA rookie, which means his diet narrowed to rolls and putbacks at the NBA level rather than expanding. That is the ceiling problem: an efficiency profile that high is a signature of a player who only takes shots he cannot miss, and it does not scale with minutes the way a scorer's would. He is also 24 and a fifth-year college player, so unlike most second-year players there is no developmental runway priced in — what he is now is close to what he will be. The genuine swing factor for 2026-27 is the three-point line, where he went from .000 as a freshman to .344 on his fifth-year attempts and 0.0 makes as a rookie; if Charlotte lets him shoot, his role stops being situational, and if they do not, the elbow-injury sequence that cost him the starting job is the template for how fragile a specialist's minutes are.
