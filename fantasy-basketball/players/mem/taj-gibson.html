Range and risks

  • Strengths: veteran rebounding, screening and defensive positioning.
  • Liabilities: age, tiny role, no category volume and a non-guaranteed salary that makes him the first body released.
  • Floor/base/ceiling: waived before opening night / rostered but out of the rotation / survives the cut and takes brief emergency minutes after multiple center injuries.
  • Branches: even a full season on the roster would not move him ahead of Edey, Stewart or Post in a healthy lineup.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Three seasons at USC (2006-07 through 2008-09), born June 24, 1985 and already 21 as a freshman. Averaged 12.2 points and 8.7 rebounds immediately, then 14.3 points, 9.0 rebounds and 2.9 blocks on .601 shooting as a junior, taking Pac-10 Defensive Player of the Year and second-team all-conference. The defense-and-rebounding profile he was drafted for is the one he still has. Draft: 2009, first round, 26th overall, Chicago.

NBA All-Rookie First Team, 2010.

Durability: 1,012 games over seventeen seasons, with four 80-plus-game years and only two seasons under 45 games before the roster-driven decline. The dip from 2022-23 onward is not health — it is 49, 20, 37 and 10 games as a veteran end-of-bench body signed for the locker room and emergency frontcourt cover. The last time he had a defined rotation role was 2021-22 in New York.

Trajectory: A seventeen-year career built on one repeatable thing — post defense, screening and offensive-glass work at above-.500 shooting — that peaked as a starter in Chicago and Minnesota and has been ceremonial since 2023. He is 41 and under a non-guaranteed second year, which makes him the arithmetic answer to a Memphis roster crunch rather than a rotation candidate. Fantasy value is zero in every format and stays there under every branch: even if he survives the cut and Edey, Stewart and Post all miss time simultaneously, the resulting six-minute assignments would not produce a startable line in any category.
