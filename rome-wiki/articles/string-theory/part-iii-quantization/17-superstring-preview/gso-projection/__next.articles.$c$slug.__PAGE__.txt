<p>The fix is startlingly cheap. Every state is built by hitting a vacuum with some number of fermionic oscillators. Count that number and ask whether it is even or odd; keep one parity, delete the other. That single yes/no cut deletes the tachyon and, once applied to both sectors, lines the surviving bosonic (NS) and fermionic (R) towers up on a common mass grid with equal counts at every level.</p>

<h2 id="why-the-counts-balance">Why the counts balance</h2>
<p>The balance is <em>between</em> the two sectors, not inside either one. All NS states are spacetime <strong>bosons</strong>; all R states are spacetime <strong>fermions</strong>. Check it by hand at the massless level.</p>

<h2 id="what-we-are-not-proving">What we are not proving</h2>
<p>The rule above is exactly right, but <em>why this particular parity cut</em> — and not some other — is the consistent, supersymmetric one is a deeper story. It rests on modular invariance of the one-loop amplitude and mutual locality of the sectors, which need the full RNS/superconformal machinery this preview does not build. Other parity truncations are also self-consistent, but they are <strong>not</strong> supersymmetric; GSO is the one that is.</p>
