    27 — AdS/CFT Orientation (Overview)

    AdS/CFT says a theory of gravity living in a curved 5D "bulk" is the same theory as an ordinary quantum field theory (no gravity) living on that bulk's 4D "boundary" — a hologram, where the extra dimension is stored as information on the edge.

    The hologram picture

    Picture a solid glass paperweight with a 3D image floating inside it. All the information for that 3D image is actually etched on the 2D surface — that is a hologram: a lower-dimensional surface encoding one dimension more. AdS/CFT is that statement made exact for a piece of physics. The floating 3D image is gravity (curved spacetime, strings, black holes) in a 5D world called the bulk. The etched surface is a quantum field theory with no gravity at all living on the 4D boundary of that world. Every question you can ask on one side has an exact translation to the other.

    Why anyone would believe two such different-looking theories are identical: they turn out to be two descriptions of the same physical object — a stack of NNN D3-branes. Look at the branes one way and you see a gauge theory; look at the same branes another way and you see gravity in a curved throat. Same object, two languages. The rest of this module makes each phrase precise.

    The slogan is "gravity in the bulk is dual to a field theory on the boundary." By the end of the module you should be able to say exactly what sits on each side, what "dual" means, and — just as important — what this does not claim.

    • Bulk. Closed superstrings — which contain gravitons, hence gravity — moving on the ten-dimensional geometry AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5. Here AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​ is a five-dimensional space of constant negative curvature (27.1 Anti-de Sitter geometry) and S5S^5S5 is a five-sphere of the same radius RRR.
    • Boundary. A four-dimensional gauge theory — N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 super-Yang–Mills with gauge group SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) — living on the 4D flat conformal boundary of AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​ (27.2 Conformal boundary). It is a conformal field theory: it has no preferred length scale, because its coupling does not run. Critically, it contains no gravity.

    The dimension count is the holography. Set the compact S5S^5S5 aside — it supplies the internal R-symmetry, not the holographic direction — and compare AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​ with its boundary: five bulk dimensions against four. The one extra is the radial direction of AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​, and on the boundary it reappears not as space but as energy scale (27.1 Anti-de Sitter geometry).

    Why believe it — the two pillars

    This is a conjecture, not a theorem. It has never been proven; it rests on two independent pieces of evidence.

    1. Symmetry match. The conformal symmetry of the 4D boundary theory has 151515 generators — 666 Lorentz transformations (333 rotations + 3+\,3+3 boosts) + 4+\,4+4 translations + 1+\,1+1 scale (dilatation) + 4+\,4+4 special conformal — closing into the algebra so(4,2)\mathfrak{so}(4,2)so(4,2). The isometries (distance-preserving maps) of the 5D bulk AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​ are generated by 151515 operators satisfying the same algebra. Independently, the isometries of the S5S^5S5 form SO(6)SO(6)SO(6), which shows up on the boundary as the R-symmetry rotating N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM's scalars and fermions. The bulk geometry already encodes the boundary theory's symmetry — developed in 27.2 Conformal boundary.

    2. D3-brane decoupling — one object, two descriptions. Take NNN coincident D3-branes and a low-energy limit E≪1/α′E \ll 1/\sqrt{\alpha'}E≪1/α′​ two ways:

    • As a gauge theory: open strings ending on the branes give SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) Yang–Mills on the 4D worldvolume, and gravity switches off because the 10D Newton constant G(10)∼gs2(α′)4→0G^{(10)}\sim g_s^2(\alpha')^4 \to 0G(10)∼gs2​(α′)4→0.
    • As curved spacetime: the branes carry energy and charge, so they bend spacetime into an infinite throat; low-energy physics survives only deep in the throat (the near-horizon region), whose geometry is exactly AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5.

    Both descriptions leave behind the same free closed strings in flat space. Equating what remains gives SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) Yang–Mills === IIB strings on AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5. The full argument is 27.3 Gauge-gravity duality.

    The dictionary and why the duality is useful but hard to test

    Each side has two dimensionless parameters, related by

    where gsg_sgs​ is the closed-string coupling, gYMg_{YM}gYM​ the gauge coupling, and λ=gYM2N\lambda = g_{YM}^2 Nλ=gYM2​N is the 't Hooft coupling. From (1), R/α′=λ1/4R/\sqrt{\alpha'} = \lambda^{1/4}R/α′​=λ1/4: large λ\lambdaλ means a large, gently curved AdS.

    This dictionary is exactly what makes the duality powerful and hard to check at once:

    • Gravity is easy when λ\lambdaλ is large — the geometry is weakly curved, so classical supergravity approximates the strings well.
    • Gauge theory is easy when λ\lambdaλ is small — you can expand in perturbation theory.

    The two easy regimes never overlap. So the duality is a machine for computing strongly-coupled gauge theory (hopeless directly) by doing classical gravity — but for the same reason a head-to-head check is rarely possible. Supersymmetry rescues testability by supplying protected observables, quantities independent of λ\lambdaλ that can be computed at weak coupling and matched against the gravity prediction. Several such matches have succeeded. The 't Hooft limit and its genus expansion are in 27.4 Large-N gauge theory; the observable-by-observable map is in 27.5 Bulk versus boundary observables.

    What you can and cannot claim

    Do claim, cleanly:

    • Both sides by name, and that the boundary has no gravity.
    • "Dual" === the same physics in two languages, computable in opposite regimes.
    • The dictionary (1), and that large λ⇔\lambda \Leftrightarrowλ⇔ weakly curved geometry ⇔\Leftrightarrow⇔ supergravity reliable.
    • It is holographic: 5D bulk gravity is stored on a 4D non-gravitational theory, echoing black-hole entropy scaling with area, not volume (Entropy-area relation). The extra radial dimension reads on the boundary as an energy scale (RG flow).

    Do not overclaim:

    • It is not proven — it is a well-tested conjecture.
    • The sharp case has maximal supersymmetry and is conformal, so it is not QCD: no string description of the real strong force follows from it yet.
    • Anything quantitative (actual correlators) needs the GKP–Witten dictionary and QFT/GR machinery this module does not build.

    Exercise

    Using only the dictionary (1), verify the module's central tractability claim: the regime where classical gravity is a good description is the regime where the gauge theory is strongly coupled. Concretely, (a) express R/α′R/\sqrt{\alpha'}R/α′​ in terms of λ\lambdaλ, (b) argue which range of λ\lambdaλ makes the AdS geometry weakly curved, and (c) conclude that "gravity easy" and "gauge theory easy" cannot hold at once. Then (d) show that at fixed λ\lambdaλ, taking N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞ sends gs→0g_s\to 0gs​→0, so the bulk becomes classical in the string-loop sense.

    Solution

    (a) The second relation in (1) is R4/α′2=λR^4/\alpha'^2 = \lambdaR4/α′2=λ. Take the fourth root: (R2α′)2=λ  ⇒  R2α′=λ  ⇒  Rα′=λ1/4.\left(\frac{R^2}{\alpha'}\right)^{2}=\lambda \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{R^2}{\alpha'}=\sqrt{\lambda}\;\Rightarrow\; \frac{R}{\sqrt{\alpha'}}=\lambda^{1/4}.(α′R2​)2=λ⇒α′R2​=λ​⇒α′​R​=λ1/4. (b) Curvature scales are set by RRR measured in string units α′\sqrt{\alpha'}α′​. The geometry is weakly curved (radius of curvature ≫\gg≫ string length) when R/α′≫1R/\sqrt{\alpha'}\gg 1R/α′​≫1, i.e. λ1/4≫1\lambda^{1/4}\gg 1λ1/4≫1, i.e. λ≫1\lambda \gg 1λ≫1. In that regime the massive string states are heavy compared to the curvature scale and classical supergravity approximates the full string theory — gravity is the easy description. (Equivalently, the IIB string expansion parameter is α′2/R4=1/λ→0\alpha'^2/R^4 = 1/\lambda \to 0α′2/R4=1/λ→0.) (c) The gauge theory is tractable by perturbation theory when its effective coupling is small, i.e. λ=gYM2N≪1\lambda = g_{YM}^2 N \ll 1λ=gYM2​N≪1. But (b) needs λ≫1\lambda \gg 1λ≫1. A single value of λ\lambdaλ cannot be both ≫1\gg 1≫1 and ≪1\ll 1≪1, so the two "easy" regimes are disjoint — you can almost never compute the same quantity easily on both sides. This is exactly why the duality is a tool (do hard gauge theory via easy gravity) rather than something checkable head-to-head, and why supersymmetry-protected, λ\lambdaλ-independent observables are needed to test it. (d) Hold λ\lambdaλ fixed and use the first relation gs=gYM2=λ/Ng_s = g_{YM}^2 = \lambda/Ngs​=gYM2​=λ/N. As N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞ with λ\lambdaλ fixed, gs=λ/N→0g_s = \lambda/N \to 0gs​=λ/N→0. Since gsg_sgs​ is the string-loop (genus) expansion parameter, the bulk becomes classical: at leading order it is classical supergravity, and 1/N1/N1/N corrections are bulk quantum/loop corrections. So the cleanest AdS/CFT statement — classical gravity ↔\leftrightarrow↔ planar, strongly-coupled N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM — lives at N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞, λ≫1\lambda\gg 1λ≫1, both simplifications flowing from the same limit.
