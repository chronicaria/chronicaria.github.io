    Ladder operators

    Repackaging \hat x,\hat p into the complex combinations a and a^\dagger turns the harmonic-oscillator Hamiltonian into H=\hbar\omega(a^\dagger a+\tfrac12) , governed by the single relation [a,a^\dagger]=1 — and that one relation is the entire oscillator, solved without a single derivative.

    A circle in phase space

    Rescale position and momentum by the oscillator's natural units and its energy becomes H∝P2+X2H\propto P^2+X^2H∝P2+X2: a circle in phase space. The energy is the squared radius, and classical motion runs around that circle at frequency ω\omegaω. For ordinary numbers you factor a sum of squares as A2+B2=(A−iB)(A+iB)A^2+B^2=(A-iB)(A+iB)A2+B2=(A−iB)(A+iB) — you split the radius into a complex amplitude and its conjugate. The whole trick is to do the same thing to the operator P2+X2P^2+X^2P2+X2. The amplitude becomes aaa, its conjugate becomes a†a^\daggera†, and because XXX and PPP don't commute the factorization leaves behind one extra term. That leftover is the zero-point energy, and generalized to infinitely many oscillators it is what fixes the critical dimension of the string. This note is the atom of everything downstream: factor a quadratic, keep track of the ordering term.

    Once we have that factorization, a†a^\daggera† climbs the energy ladder one rung at a time, aaa climbs down, and HHH reports which rung you are on. No differential equation, no special functions — the spectrum falls out of one commutator. This method, not the Hermite-polynomial slog, is the one that survives into string theory verbatim, with an index attached.

    The problem

    The harmonic oscillator is a particle of mass mmm in the potential V(x)=12mω2x2V(x)=\tfrac12 m\omega^2 x^2V(x)=21​mω2x2. Its Hamiltonian (the energy observable) is

    H=p^22m+12mω2x^2.H = \frac{\hat p^2}{2m} + \frac12 m\omega^2 \hat x^2 .H=2mp^​2​+21​mω2x^2.

    We want its energy spectrum. The brute-force route solves a Hermite differential equation. The slick route — the one the string inherits — never touches a differential equation. We just factor HHH.

    Defining aaa and a†a^\daggera†

    The constants in the definition look mysterious only until we nondimensionalize phase space. Set

    X=mωℏ x^,P=p^mωℏ.X=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}\,\hat x,\qquad P=\frac{\hat p}{\sqrt{m\omega\hbar}}.X=ℏmω​​x^,P=mωℏ​p^​​.

    These compare position to the natural oscillator length ℏ/(mω)\sqrt{\hbar/(m\omega)}ℏ/(mω)​ and momentum to mωℏ\sqrt{m\omega\hbar}mωℏ​, so XXX and PPP are pure numbers. Their commutator loses its units too:

    [X,P]=mωℏ 1mωℏ [x^,p^]=1ℏ(iℏ)=i.[X,P]=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{m\omega\hbar}}\,[\hat x,\hat p]=\frac{1}{\hbar}(i\hbar)=i.[X,P]=ℏmω​​mωℏ​1​[x^,p^​]=ℏ1​(iℏ)=i.

    In these units the Hamiltonian is the promised circle,

    H=ℏω2(P2+X2),H=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\left(P^2+X^2\right),H=2ℏω​(P2+X2),

    with ℏω\hbar\omegaℏω carrying all the dimensions out front. Factoring A2+B2=(A−iB)(A+iB)A^2+B^2=(A-iB)(A+iB)A2+B2=(A−iB)(A+iB) suggests the combinations

    a=X+iP2,a†=X−iP2.a=\frac{X+iP}{\sqrt2},\qquad a^\dagger=\frac{X-iP}{\sqrt2}.a=2​X+iP​,a†=2​X−iP​.

    Written back in x^,p^\hat x,\hat px^,p^​ this is

    a=mω2ℏ(x^+imωp^),a†=mω2ℏ(x^−imωp^).a = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x + \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right), \qquad a^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat x - \frac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right).a=2ℏmω​​(x^+mωi​p^​),a†=2ℏmω​​(x^−mωi​p^​).

    Since x^,p^\hat x,\hat px^,p^​ are Hermitian, a†a^\daggera† really is the Hermitian conjugate of aaa — the iii flips sign under daggering. Hence the dagger notation, and hence that aaa itself, not being Hermitian, is not an observable. The prefactor mω/(2ℏ)\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}mω/(2ℏ)​ (with the 1/21/\sqrt21/2​ absorbed into it) is tuned to make the commutator come out to exactly 111; we verify that next.

    Inverting the definitions — add them for x^\hat xx^, subtract for p^\hat pp^​ — gives the bridge back to physical variables:

    x^=ℏ2mω (a+a†),p^=−imωℏ2 (a−a†).\hat x = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\,(a+a^\dagger), \qquad \hat p = -i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}\,(a-a^\dagger).x^=2mωℏ​​(a+a†),p^​=−i2mωℏ​​(a−a†).

    We need these to read physical quantities off the algebra, and their string analogues will express each transverse string coordinate XIX^IXI as a sum over oscillator modes. One warning that saves confusion later: a†a^\daggera† is the adjoint of aaa, not its inverse. It raises a state up the tower, aaa lowers it, and their product a†aa^\dagger aa†a counts rungs — it is not the identity.

    Deriving [a,a†]=1[a,a^\dagger]=1[a,a†]=1

    Compute directly, using bilinearity and [x^,p^]=iℏ[\hat x,\hat p]=i\hbar[x^,p^​]=iℏ:

    [a,a†]=mω2ℏ[x^+imωp^,  x^−imωp^].[a,a^\dagger] = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[\hat x + \tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p,\;\hat x - \tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right].[a,a†]=2ℏmω​[x^+mωi​p^​,x^−mωi​p^​].

    The [x^,x^][\hat x,\hat x][x^,x^] and [p^,p^][\hat p,\hat p][p^​,p^​] terms vanish. The two cross terms are

    [x^, −imωp^]+[imωp^, x^]=−imω[x^,p^]+imω[p^,x^]=−imω(iℏ)+imω(−iℏ)=2ℏmω.\left[\hat x,\,-\tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right] + \left[\tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p,\,\hat x\right] = -\frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat x,\hat p] + \frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat p,\hat x] = -\frac{i}{m\omega}(i\hbar) + \frac{i}{m\omega}(-i\hbar) = \frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}.[x^,−mωi​p^​]+[mωi​p^​,x^]=−mωi​[x^,p^​]+mωi​[p^​,x^]=−mωi​(iℏ)+mωi​(−iℏ)=mω2ℏ​.

    Multiplying by the prefactor mω2ℏ\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}2ℏmω​ collapses everything to

    \boxed{\;[a,a^\dagger] = 1\;} \tag{1}

    This is the only input the rest of the theory needs. The two cross terms contributed ℏ/(mω)\hbar/(m\omega)ℏ/(mω) each; the prefactor mω/(2ℏ)m\omega/(2\hbar)mω/(2ℏ) was chosen precisely to cancel their sum and leave a bare 111. That is what "fixing the normalization of aaa" means.

    Factoring the Hamiltonian into a†aa^\dagger aa†a

    Now multiply a†a^\daggera† and aaa, keeping the order straight because x^,p^\hat x,\hat px^,p^​ do not commute:

    a†a=mω2ℏ(x^−imωp^)(x^+imωp^)=mω2ℏ(x^2+p^2m2ω2+imω[x^,p^]).a^\dagger a = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat x - \tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right)\left(\hat x + \tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right) = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat x^2 + \frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2} + \frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat x,\hat p]\right).a†a=2ℏmω​(x^−mωi​p^​)(x^+mωi​p^​)=2ℏmω​(x^2+m2ω2p^​2​+mωi​[x^,p^​]).

    The two mixed products combined into the commutator (that is where the ordering shows up), and imω[x^,p^]=imω(iℏ)=−ℏmω\frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat x,\hat p]=\frac{i}{m\omega}(i\hbar)=-\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}mωi​[x^,p^​]=mωi​(iℏ)=−mωℏ​. Hence

    a†a=mω2ℏx^2+12mωℏp^2−12=1ℏω(12mω2x^2+p^22m)−12=Hℏω−12.a^\dagger a = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\hat x^2 + \frac{1}{2m\omega\hbar}\hat p^2 - \frac12 = \frac{1}{\hbar\omega}\left(\tfrac12 m\omega^2\hat x^2 + \frac{\hat p^2}{2m}\right) - \frac12 = \frac{H}{\hbar\omega} - \frac12 .a†a=2ℏmω​x^2+2mωℏ1​p^​2−21​=ℏω1​(21​mω2x^2+2mp^​2​)−21​=ℏωH​−21​.

    The middle step just regroups the x^2\hat x^2x^2 and p^2\hat p^2p^​2 coefficients into exactly the potential and kinetic energy. Solving for HHH:

    \boxed{\;H = \hbar\omega\left(a^\dagger a + \tfrac12\right)\;} \tag{2}

    The +12+\tfrac12+21​ is not decoration — it is the ordering term [x^,p^][\hat x,\hat p][x^,p^​], the canonical commutation relation itself, made visible. Physically it is the zero-point energy: the algebraic echo of the uncertainty principle from Canonical commutation relations, the reason the oscillator can't sit still at x=p=0x=p=0x=p=0.

    Ordering is the whole story of that 12\tfrac1221​. Had we factored the other way, aa†=a†a+[a,a†]=a†a+1aa^\dagger = a^\dagger a + [a,a^\dagger] = a^\dagger a + 1aa†=a†a+[a,a†]=a†a+1, we would have found H=ℏω ⁣(aa†−12)H=\hbar\omega\!\left(aa^\dagger-\tfrac12\right)H=ℏω(aa†−21​) — same HHH, opposite sign on the constant. The gap between the two orderings is exactly [a,a†]=1[a,a^\dagger]=1[a,a†]=1:

    aa†−a†a=[a,a†]=1.aa^\dagger-a^\dagger a=[a,a^\dagger]=1.aa†−a†a=[a,a†]=1.

    For one oscillator that leftover 111 is harmless. For a string's infinitely many oscillators the analogous leftover is a divergent sum; normal-ordering sweeps it to one side and a finite constant is fixed by consistency. That is the same ∓12\mp\tfrac12∓21​ ambiguity, grown up — the shift that ultimately pins D=26D=26D=26.

    What we have achieved

    Equations (1) and (2) reduce the oscillator to pure algebra: one commutator plus one number-counting Hamiltonian, no calculus anywhere. Define N=a†aN=a^\dagger aN=a†a (the number operator) and the entire spectrum En=ℏω(n+12)E_n=\hbar\omega(n+\tfrac12)En​=ℏω(n+21​) follows from [a,a†]=1[a,a^\dagger]=1[a,a†]=1 alone — that derivation, the module's exit check, is in The number operator.

    Exercise

    The prefactor mω/(2ℏ)\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}mω/(2ℏ)​ was chosen to make [a,a†]=1[a,a^\dagger]=1[a,a†]=1. Suppose you instead defined an unnormalized pair b=λ ⁣(x^+imωp^)b=\lambda\!\left(\hat x+\tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right)b=λ(x^+mωi​p^​) and b†=λ ⁣(x^−imωp^)b^\dagger=\lambda\!\left(\hat x-\tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right)b†=λ(x^−mωi​p^​) with an arbitrary real constant λ>0\lambda>0λ>0. (a) Find [b,b†][b,b^\dagger][b,b†] in terms of λ\lambdaλ. (b) What value of λ\lambdaλ recovers the standard normalization, and does it match the note? (c) With a general λ\lambdaλ, is the Hamiltonian still H=ℏω(b†b+12)H=\hbar\omega(b^\dagger b+\tfrac12)H=ℏω(b†b+21​)? Explain what goes wrong.

    Solution

    (a) Only the prefactor changes relative to the boxed derivation, and [b,b†][b,b^\dagger][b,b†] is quadratic in it. The cross terms gave 2ℏmω\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}mω2ℏ​, so [b,b†]=λ2[x^+imωp^, x^−imωp^]=λ2⋅2ℏmω=2ℏλ2mω.[b,b^\dagger]=\lambda^2\left[\hat x+\tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p,\ \hat x-\tfrac{i}{m\omega}\hat p\right]=\lambda^2\cdot\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}=\frac{2\hbar\lambda^2}{m\omega}.[b,b†]=λ2[x^+mωi​p^​, x^−mωi​p^​]=λ2⋅mω2ℏ​=mω2ℏλ2​. (b) Setting this to 111 gives λ2=mω2ℏ\lambda^2=\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}λ2=2ℏmω​, i.e. λ=mω/(2ℏ)\lambda=\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}λ=mω/(2ℏ)​ — exactly the prefactor in the note. (c) No. Repeating the a†aa^\dagger aa†a computation with λ\lambdaλ gives b†b=λ2 ⁣(x^2+p^2m2ω2)−λ2ℏmωb^\dagger b=\lambda^2\!\left(\hat x^2+\frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right)-\lambda^2\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}b†b=λ2(x^2+m2ω2p^​2​)−λ2mωℏ​. Since x^2+p^2m2ω2=2Hmω2\hat x^2+\frac{\hat p^2}{m^2\omega^2}=\frac{2H}{m\omega^2}x^2+m2ω2p^​2​=mω22H​, and writing c≡[b,b†]=2ℏλ2mωc\equiv[b,b^\dagger]=\frac{2\hbar\lambda^2}{m\omega}c≡[b,b†]=mω2ℏλ2​ from part (a), this is b†b=cℏωH−c2b^\dagger b=\frac{c}{\hbar\omega}H-\frac{c}{2}b†b=ℏωc​H−2c​, i.e. H=ℏωc(b†b+c2).H=\frac{\hbar\omega}{c}\left(b^\dagger b+\tfrac{c}{2}\right).H=cℏω​(b†b+2c​). Only when c=1c=1c=1 does this collapse to H=ℏω(b†b+12)H=\hbar\omega(b^\dagger b+\tfrac12)H=ℏω(b†b+21​). Off normalization the counting operator b†bb^\dagger bb†b no longer steps by one: [b†b,b†]=c b†[b^\dagger b,b^\dagger]=c\,b^\dagger[b†b,b†]=cb†, so its eigenvalues are spaced by ccc and the clean "HHH counts quanta of ℏω\hbar\omegaℏω" picture breaks. The normalization λ=mω/(2ℏ)\lambda=\sqrt{m\omega/(2\hbar)}λ=mω/(2ℏ)​ is exactly what makes one rung equal one quantum.
