    Gauge-gravity duality

    Look at one stack of N D3-branes two ways — as gauge fields living on the branes, or as a curved throat in the surrounding spacetime — take the low-energy limit of each, and the leftover pieces must match. That match is the origin of AdS/CFT.

    Two descriptions of one object

    The same object has two honest low-energy descriptions; they share one identical piece, so the remaining pieces have to be the same theory.

    The object is a stack of NNN coincident D3-branes (Definition of a Dp-brane) — a 3+1 dimensional sheet sitting inside 9+1 dimensional spacetime. There are two legitimate ways to describe its low-energy physics:

    • As a place where open strings end. Open strings attached to the branes give a gauge theory on the branes.
    • As a heavy charged object. The branes carry mass and charge, so like a black hole they bend spacetime around themselves, digging an infinite "throat."

    Neither description is wrong. They are the same physics. Take the low-energy limit of each: both split into a decoupled free-gravity piece plus one interesting piece. The free-gravity pieces are identical. So the two interesting pieces must be the same theory — and that is the duality.

    The result we reach below: the interesting piece on side A is SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) Yang–Mills, and the interesting piece on side B is type IIB strings living in the throat, whose geometry turns out to be AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5.

    This is a conjecture, not a theorem. The argument below is evidence — a plausibility argument built from decoupling — not a proof.

    The low-energy limit

    Both descriptions are taken in the same limit: keep only physics below the string scale (ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1 throughout, so 1/α′1/\sqrt{\alpha'}1/α′​ is an energy), E \ll \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha'}} \equiv \frac{1}{\ell_s}. \tag{1} Equivalently, hold energies fixed and send α′→0\alpha'\to 0α′→0 with the string coupling gsg_sgs​ fixed. This throws away every massive string state (mass M∼1/α′→∞M\sim 1/\sqrt{\alpha'}\to\inftyM∼1/α′​→∞) and keeps only massless modes — plus whatever gets red-shifted down to low energy, which becomes the crux of side B.

    Description A — open strings on the branes

    Open strings ending on NNN coincident D3-branes have massless modes forming a U(N)U(N)U(N) gauge field, together with six adjoint scalars (from the six transverse directions) and their superpartner fermions (Open strings on one brane, Maxwell fields on branes). That field content is precisely N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 super-Yang–Mills in four dimensions, living on the brane worldvolume. In the limit (1) the massive open-string states drop out.

    Two decouplings happen as α′→0\alpha'\to 0α′→0:

    • The open strings decouple from the bulk closed strings. Gravity is controlled by the ten-dimensional Newton constant, which vanishes in the limit: G^{(10)} \sim g_s^2(\alpha')^4 \;\xrightarrow{\;\alpha'\to 0\;}\; 0. \tag{2} With G(10)→0G^{(10)}\to 0G(10)→0 the closed strings in the surrounding flat space become free (non-interacting), and their coupling to the brane fields switches off. Gravity turns itself off.
    • One U(N)U(N)U(N) photon decouples. The overall U(1)⊂U(N)U(1)\subset U(N)U(1)⊂U(N) describes the center-of-mass motion of the whole stack and separates out, leaving a fully interacting SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) gauge theory.

    Result A: interacting SU(N)SU(N)SU(N) Yang–Mills   ⊕  \;\oplus\;⊕ free closed strings in flat 10D.

    Description B — the branes as a curved throat

    The same NNN branes carry energy and Ramond–Ramond charge, so in the closed-string theory they source a nontrivial solution of the gravitational field equations, much like a charged black hole. The solution has an infinite throat: transverse spheres around the branes, whose circumference would shrink to zero near a point mass in flat space, instead approach a constant size as you descend, and the branes sit an infinite proper distance down the tube (schematically below).

       flat asymptotic region
   ────────────────────────
            │  throat
            │  (spheres → constant radius R)
            │
            ▼  D3-branes (infinitely far down)

    In the limit (1), low-energy excitations as seen from infinity split into two kinds:

    • Far from the branes: ordinary low-energy excitations in the asymptotically flat region — again free closed strings in flat space.
    • Deep in the throat (near-horizon): an excitation of any energy sitting deep down is gravitationally red-shifted to arbitrarily low energy as measured from infinity. So even non-negligible near-horizon physics survives the low-energy limit — this is why the interesting piece here is a full string theory, not just massless modes.

    These two kinds decouple: far excitations are almost never captured by the throat, and throat excitations cannot climb out to infinity. (This decoupling is supported by evidence — vanishing absorption in the limit — not proven here.)

    Result B: type IIB strings in the near-horizon throat   ⊕  \;\oplus\;⊕ free closed strings in flat 10D.

    The match

    Both results contain the same decoupled free closed strings in flat space. Cancel that common piece; the remainders must agree: \boxed{\;SU(N)\ \text{Yang–Mills} \;=\; \text{type IIB strings in the near-horizon geometry}\;} \tag{3}

    Reading the D3-brane supergravity solution, the near-horizon geometry turns out to be exactly AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5 (see Anti-de Sitter geometry, Conformal boundary). The four flat brane directions plus the radial throat coordinate assemble into AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​; the transverse spheres, whose radius levels off at the horizon value RRR, are the S5S^5S5, with the same radius RRR. So (3) reads:

    N=4 SU(N) SYM in 4D    ⟷    type IIB closed strings on AdS5×S5.\mathcal{N}=4\ SU(N)\ \text{SYM in 4D} \;\;\longleftrightarrow\;\; \text{type IIB closed strings on } \mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5.N=4 SU(N) SYM in 4D⟷type IIB closed strings on AdS5​×S5.

    That "the throat is exactly AdS5×S5\mathrm{AdS}_5\times S^5AdS5​×S5" is quoted from the explicit type IIB supergravity ppp-brane metric, which is beyond this module — the derivation of the metric itself is listed as a prerequisite in 00 — Overview.

    The parameter dictionary

    Each side carries two dimensionless parameters: Yang–Mills: gYM, NIIB strings: gs, Rα′.\text{Yang–Mills: } g_{YM},\ N \qquad\qquad \text{IIB strings: } g_s,\ \frac{R}{\sqrt{\alpha'}}.Yang–Mills: gYM​, NIIB strings: gs​, α′​R​. The correspondence relates them by \boxed{\; g_s = g_{YM}^2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{R^4}{\alpha'^2} = g_{YM}^2\,N \;}\tag{4}

    This dictionary is quoted: its numerical constants come from the D3-brane tension/charge normalization and the supergravity solution, which we do not build here. Reading it:

    • gs=gYM2g_s = g_{YM}^2gs​=gYM2​: the closed-string coupling is the square of the gauge coupling. Weak gauge coupling means weak string coupling. (This is Zwiebach's normalization, with no extra 4π4\pi4π; a common field-theory convention instead writes gs=gYM2/(4π)g_s = g_{YM}^2/(4\pi)gs​=gYM2​/(4π). The physics is convention-independent; we keep the no-4π4\pi4π form throughout this module.)
    • R4/α′2=gYM2NR^4/\alpha'^2 = g_{YM}^2 NR4/α′2=gYM2​N: the right side is the 't Hooft coupling λ≡gYM2N\lambda \equiv g_{YM}^2 Nλ≡gYM2​N (Large-N gauge theory). Taking the fourth root, \frac{R}{\sqrt{\alpha'}} = \lambda^{1/4}. \tag{5} The geometry measured in string units is set entirely by λ\lambdaλ: big λ\lambdaλ means a big, gently curved AdS.

    Why the two sides are never both easy

    Equation (5) is exactly what makes the duality powerful and hard to test at once:

    • Gravity is tractable when the geometry is weakly curved, i.e. R/α′≫1R/\sqrt{\alpha'}\gg 1R/α′​≫1, i.e. large λ\lambdaλ. Then classical supergravity (GR plus matter) is a good approximation to the full string theory, because α′\alpha'α′-corrections scale as α′2/R4=1/λ→0\alpha'^2/R^4 = 1/\lambda \to 0α′2/R4=1/λ→0.
    • Gauge theory is tractable when λ\lambdaλ is small — that is when perturbation theory in λ\lambdaλ converges.

    Large λ\lambdaλ: easy gravity, hard gauge theory. Small λ\lambdaλ: easy gauge theory, hard gravity. There is no overlap. What rescues testability is supersymmetry, which protects certain observables so they are λ\lambdaλ-independent: compute them cheaply at small λ\lambdaλ, then compare against the strong-coupling gravity prediction. Several such quantities have been matched — the strongest concrete evidence for the conjecture.

    What this buys, and the honest limits

    • It is a concrete realization of holography: the gravitational AdS5\mathrm{AdS}_5AdS5​ directions are fully encoded by a non-gravitational 4D boundary theory; the S5S^5S5 contributes internal SO(6)SO(6)SO(6) R-symmetry rather than extra boundary dimensions. See Bulk versus boundary observables.
    • The sharp case has maximal supersymmetry and is conformal — N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM has a vanishing beta function, so gYMg_{YM}gYM​ does not run. That vanishing is why the boundary is a CFT and the bulk is maximally symmetric AdS. It is therefore not QCD, which has no supersymmetry and does run. A string description of real QCD remains open.
    • The whole D3-brane argument motivates the correspondence in the low-energy limit. The actual conjecture is stronger: full type IIB strings ↔\leftrightarrow↔ full N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM, at all energies. That extension is assumed, not derived.

    Open questions

    • The decoupling on side B rests on far and near excitations "genuinely" not talking to each other. The supporting evidence is that absorption cross-sections into the throat vanish in the limit — a computation beyond this module. How airtight is it?
    • The dictionary constant in gs=gYM2g_s = g_{YM}^2gs​=gYM2​ hides in the D-brane action normalization (DBI action as a preview). Deriving it, not just the scaling, needs the DBI tension and RR charge quantization.
    • Does the equivalence hold only near the low-energy limit or for the full theories? The strong form (all energies) is the real Maldacena conjecture — assumed here, unproven.

    Exercise

    Assume the parameter dictionary (4), gs=gYM2g_s = g_{YM}^2gs​=gYM2​ and R4/α′2=gYM2NR^4/\alpha'^2 = g_{YM}^2 NR4/α′2=gYM2​N. Work entirely in Zwiebach's no-4π4\pi4π convention.

    (a) Express the string coupling gsg_sgs​ purely in terms of the 't Hooft coupling λ=gYM2N\lambda = g_{YM}^2 Nλ=gYM2​N and NNN, and use it to state what happens to gsg_sgs​ in the 't Hooft limit (N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞ at fixed λ\lambdaλ).

    (b) The IIB string's α′\alpha'α′-expansion parameter (the size of stringy corrections to classical geometry) is λ′≡α′2/R4\lambda' \equiv \alpha'^2/R^4λ′≡α′2/R4. Show λ′=1/λ\lambda' = 1/\lambdaλ′=1/λ, and state which side of the duality — gauge or gravity — is the tractable one when λ≫1\lambda \gg 1λ≫1.

    (c) Deduce the "clean" corner of the duality: which single limit makes both the classical-geometry approximation (λ′→0\lambda'\to 0λ′→0) and the tree-level-string approximation (gs→0g_s\to 0gs​→0) hold simultaneously? What is the boundary theory doing in that corner?

    Solution

    (a) From (4), gs=gYM2g_s = g_{YM}^2gs​=gYM2​. Since λ=gYM2N\lambda = g_{YM}^2 Nλ=gYM2​N, we have gYM2=λ/Ng_{YM}^2 = \lambda/NgYM2​=λ/N, so gs=λN.g_s = \frac{\lambda}{N}.gs​=Nλ​. In the 't Hooft limit λ\lambdaλ is held fixed while N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞, so gs=λ/N→0g_s = \lambda/N \to 0gs​=λ/N→0. The string coupling vanishes: at N=∞N=\inftyN=∞ the bulk is classical in the string-loop sense, and 1/N1/N1/N counts string loops (Large-N gauge theory).

    (b) Directly invert the second relation in (4): R4/α′2=gYM2N=λR^4/\alpha'^2 = g_{YM}^2 N = \lambdaR4/α′2=gYM2​N=λ, so λ′≡α′2R4=1λ.\lambda' \equiv \frac{\alpha'^2}{R^4} = \frac{1}{\lambda}.λ′≡R4α′2​=λ1​. Classical geometry is reliable when α′\alpha'α′-corrections are small, i.e. λ′≪1\lambda' \ll 1λ′≪1, i.e. λ≫1\lambda \gg 1λ≫1. So λ≫1\lambda\gg 1λ≫1 is the regime where the gravity side is tractable — and simultaneously the regime where gauge perturbation theory in λ\lambdaλ fails. (Consistent with (5): R/α′=λ1/4≫1R/\sqrt{\alpha'} = \lambda^{1/4}\gg 1R/α′​=λ1/4≫1 means a large, weakly curved AdS.)

    (c) Two smallness conditions must hold at once: λ′=1/λ→0\lambda' = 1/\lambda \to 0λ′=1/λ→0 needs λ→∞\lambda\to\inftyλ→∞, and gs=λ/N→0g_s = \lambda/N \to 0gs​=λ/N→0 needs N→∞N\to\inftyN→∞ faster than λ\lambdaλ grows. The clean corner is therefore N→∞first (fixing λ),then λ→∞.N \to \infty \quad\text{first (fixing } \lambda\text{),}\qquad \text{then } \lambda \to \infty.N→∞first (fixing λ),then λ→∞. There the bulk is classical supergravity (tree-level, weakly curved). On the boundary the theory is planar (1/N1/N1/N loops suppressed) and strongly coupled (λ≫1\lambda\gg 1λ≫1) N=4\mathcal{N}=4N=4 SYM. Both simplifications — classical bulk, planar boundary — come from the same large-NNN, large-λ\lambdaλ limit, which is exactly why this is the corner where AdS/CFT is used.
