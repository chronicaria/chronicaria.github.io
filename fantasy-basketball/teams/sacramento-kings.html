Sacramento Kings

Sacramento is a 22-win rebuild split between rookie lead-guard development and three expensive veterans, making health, trades, and role direction unusually important to every fantasy projection.

Fantasy hierarchy

  • Categories: Sabonis remains the only clear early-round target. Knee recovery and a March shutdown still move him below his old first-round floor, but with Perry having ruled out an offseason move the trade component of that discount now applies only to the deadline. Murray is the best balanced mid-round bet because threes, rebounds, stocks, and low turnovers can coexist. Acuff's assists and points are attractive, while rookie FG%, turnovers, and low blocks cap nine-cat value. LaVine, Monk, and Hunter are late-round specialists; Hunter is the one with a cleared medical and a raised minute base.
  • Points: Sabonis is the anchor; Acuff gains relative value because volume and assists matter more than efficiency. Murray and LaVine follow. Raynaud is a rosterable backup big in his own right at 16 minutes rather than a pure handcuff, and Cardwell is the next injury-away name.
  • Dynasty: Acuff is the franchise-value leader. Hashtag Basketball ranked him 55th in its July 2 dynasty list and the July 18 crowd board placed him 61st; Sabonis was 61st/63rd, Murray 68th/126th, and Raynaud 130th/120th. The disagreement around Murray and Raynaud is actionable evidence of role uncertainty, not noise to erase.

Role battles and transactions

  • Sacramento drafted Acuff seventh; FantasyPros ranked him fifth among 2026 fantasy rookies and argued that a starting role and high involvement were likely. ESPN now lists him first at point guard.
  • DeMar DeRozan was waived in July, opening wing usage, and Devin Carter was traded to Atlanta on June 30, clearing the lead-guard path.
  • Precious Achiuwa re-signed for two years; Daeqwon Plowden signed a reported two-year standard deal after producing in the tanking stretch.
  • Nique Clifford versus Hunter is the main wing-minute battle. Raynaud versus Achiuwa is the backup-center battle, while Cardwell is the superior blocks specialist but the least polished offensive fit.
  • LaVine exercised his 2026-27 player option. June reporting had Sacramento listening on both LaVine and Sabonis, but GM Scott Perry closed that door on the record in July: LaVine "is coming back here this year. I'm very clear with him on that," and Perry said he anticipates Sabonis remaining as well (NBC Sports Bay Area). Any veteran exit would still move 25-30 minutes and substantial usage to the young core, but that is now a deadline tail rather than a preseason base case.

Correction 2026-07-18: this desk previously priced an offseason trade of LaVine or Sabonis as a primary discount, citing a June RotoWire note. Perry's July on-record statements supersede it. Trade risk is demoted to a February tail, which raises the redraft floors of both veterans and lowers the probability weight on the Acuff/Raynaud/Clifford usage-spike branches.

Health and availability

Sabonis underwent left-knee meniscus surgery on February 18 and LaVine right-pinky tendon repair; both were ruled out for the rest of 2025-26 (NBA/AP, February 19). Sabonis has since said publicly that he feels "ahead of schedule" and expects to be ready for the start of next season (Sactown Sports); standard meniscus-repair return-to-cutting windows of roughly six months put him in mid-August, before camp. Hunter had surgery for a detached retina in February (NBA.com, February 21) and said in April that he had made a "complete and successful recovery" and was cleared for basketball activities, leaving him a normal offseason and a full camp (Hoops Rumors, April 2026). Murray missed most of 2025-26 with a moderate left-ankle sprain but returned late.

Correction 2026-07-18: this desk previously recorded that no post-surgical clearance for Hunter could be found and carried a slow-eye-recovery downside branch. That clearance was reported in April, three months before this file's as-of date; the branch is removed and Hunter's GP rises from 64 to 68 with active-night minutes from 25 to 28, in line with his 26-30 MPG career norm and his $23.3M starting-wing salary. Sabonis's 30.0 MPG is retained, but it is a tanking and load-management assumption for a 30-year-old nine months off meniscus repair on a 22-win roster — not a medical deduction. No report supports a medical cut from his 34.7 MPG 2024-25 baseline.

Summer League shutdowns on July 17 for Acuff, Clifford, Cardwell, and Raynaud were reported as rest/late-event absences rather than new long-term diagnoses. Karaban had already returned from a right-ankle sprain. Do not carry the ESPN day-to-day flags into October without fresh camp evidence.

Team outcome range

  • Median: 22-60, bottom three in the West, offense routed through Acuff development with veterans managed rather than maximized.
  • Ceiling: 30-34 wins if Sabonis, LaVine, Murray, and Hunter are healthy, Acuff is immediately efficient, and the team keeps its veterans.
  • Floor: 15-18 wins if one or two large contracts move, Sabonis has a slow knee recovery, or the club aggressively prioritizes lottery position.

The market confirms the low baseline: observed July win totals ranged from 20.5 at BetMGM to 22.5 at another major book. That range is context, not a roster projection, and can move sharply after trades.
