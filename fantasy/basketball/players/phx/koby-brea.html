Koby Brea

Koby Brea is a two-way shooting specialist with a plausible long-term NBA niche, but Luke Kennard and Phoenix's perimeter depth leave him a conditional six-minute appearance card for 2026-27.

Role and evidence

Phoenix acquired Brea's draft rights in the 2025 seven-team deal and signed him to a two-way contract on July 6, 2026. The current roster lists him among three two-way players. Sources: Suns 2025 transaction release, RealGM transaction history, accessed 2026-07-18, RealGM current roster, accessed 2026-07-18. Confidence: high.

Brea made six threes and scored 20 points in Phoenix's April 12 short-handed finale. That is the exact fantasy appeal—efficient catch-and-shoot volume—but Luke Kennard now occupies the veteran version of the same role. Source: official NBA gamebook, 2026-04-12. Confidence: high on the game, low on role translation.

Category shape

  • Wins: Threes, likely FT%, low turnovers.
  • Neutral: FG% can be positive if shots remain clean.
  • Costs: Rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, and opportunity.
  • Build fit: Very deep roto or dynasty leagues that can stash a specialist.

Points and dynasty outlook

The low peripheral rates make Brea less attractive in points scoring than in category formats. A standard contract would be a more meaningful dynasty catalyst than any single scoring game.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: G League development and rare NBA activations.
  • Base: 15 games at six minutes, used when Phoenix needs shooting.
  • Ceiling: Kennard or another wing misses time; Brea reaches 18 minutes and two threes per game, creating a specialist stream.

Draft verdict

Brea should be monitored for contract conversion and three-point volume, not drafted in standard leagues. His pathway is unusually direct but narrow: make enough threes to justify minutes despite limited peripheral production.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Five college seasons. Dayton (2020-24): 2.9 PPG as a freshman, 8.1 PPG in 2021-22 (A-10 Sixth Man of the Year), 6.8 PPG in an injury-marred 2022-23, then 11.1 PPG in 2023-24 while leading the nation from three at 49.8% (second A-10 Sixth Man award, 3.0 threes per game). Kentucky (2024-25): 11.6 PPG, led the SEC at 43.5% from three. Career: 149 games, 8.8 PPG, 43.4% from deep. Draft: 2025, round 2, pick 41, Golden State; rights traded to Phoenix July 6, 2025.

Durability: Leg stress fractures requiring surgery cost him most of his 2022-23 explosiveness at Dayton; no NBA injury flags since. The 12-game rookie total was two-way roster mechanics, not health.

Trajectory: The record is a single note played extremely well: two conference-leading three-point seasons in college (49.8%, then 43.5% in the SEC), 12-of-30 from deep in his 12 NBA appearances, and near-zero peripheral production everywhere. Re-signed to a two-way July 6, 2026, he sits behind Kennard in the same specialist role for 2026-27, with the Valley Suns as his default. He becomes fantasy-relevant only if a Phoenix wing injury hands him 18+ minutes of real shooting volume — at which point he is a threes-only specialist stream — or if a contract conversion signals a permanent rotation plan.
