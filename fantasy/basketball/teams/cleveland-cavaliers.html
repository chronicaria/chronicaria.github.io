Cleveland Cavaliers

Cleveland remains a contender built around Donovan Mitchell, Evan Mobley, and Jarrett Allen, but its 2026-27 fantasy map cannot be treated as settled until James Harden signs and the LeBron James pursuit ends.

Executive read

  • Base case: Harden re-signs, LeBron does not join, and the healthy starting five is Harden, Mitchell, Max Strus, Mobley, and Allen. That case projects 49-33, with a plausible 44-55-win band and a median second-round outcome.
  • 2025-26 baseline: Cleveland finished 52-30, fourth in the East, at 119.5 points (fourth), 44.4 rebounds (11th), and 28.3 assists (eighth) per game while allowing 115.4 points (15th). It then reached the Eastern Conference finals. Official NBA team page, accessed July 18, 2026
  • Coach/system: Kenny Atkinson will return for a third season despite the conference-finals sweep. Continuity favors the same high-assist, spacing-heavy regular-season offense; the postseason question is whether a small guard/wing group can defend and create efficiently when switching tightens. NBA.com offseason interview, May 27, 2026
  • Fantasy concentration: Mitchell, Mobley, and Harden are the only reliable standard-league draft targets in the base case. Allen is a build-sensitive center; Jaylon Tyson, Sam Merrill, and Strus form the next speculative tier.
  • Primary uncertainty: Harden declined his $42.3 million option on June 29 and was reported to be working on a multi-year Cleveland deal. A deal had not been announced by the cutoff. RotoWire team news, June 29, 2026 · Sportsnet, June 29, 2026

The second apron is the governing mechanism

Every transaction uncertainty on this page runs through one constraint. Cleveland entered the 2026 offseason with 12 players under contract at roughly $224.7M, about $3.4M above the $221.7M second-apron line and roughly $8.3M above it once the two open standard spots are filled at the $2.5M minimum. It was the only team above the second apron in 2025-26 and projects above it again. Third Apron offseason preview · SI Cavaliers cap tracker

Above that line a team cannot aggregate two or more salaries in a trade, cannot take back more salary than it sends, cannot send cash, cannot use prior-year trade exceptions, has no taxpayer mid-level exception, and has its 2033 first-round pick frozen. Three fantasy-relevant consequences follow:

  • Salaries move one at a time, not in packages. "Strus and/or Schröder could be moved" is legal as two separate one-for-one deals, not as a combined package, until Cleveland ducks the line. That is why Schröder is reported as being shopped individually.
  • Harden's restructure is the primary tool. He declined a $42.3M option and is reported to be negotiating a longer deal in the $32-38M range. That saving is what gets Cleveland under the second apron and unlocks the taxpayer mid-level.
  • Deep-bench spots get filled at the minimum. Any minimum signing competes directly with the Thomas Bryant (8) and Nae'Qwan Tomlin (15) allocations in the 240-minute table below.

There is also a pick-freeze incentive rather than a tanking incentive: staying above the second apron freezes the 2033 first-rounder, which is standing front-office pressure to shed salary in-season. That is a live mid-season risk for Strus, Schröder and Allen holders.

Category map

  • Points/3PM/FT volume: Mitchell leads all three; Harden is the second engine if signed. Merrill and Strus are specialist 3PM streams.
  • Assists: Harden is the category anchor. Mitchell supplies above-position average volume; Schröder and Porter become streamable only when an initiator sits.
  • FG%/rebounds/blocks: Mobley and Allen define the big-man build. Mobley's multi-position blocks are the scarcer contribution; Allen has the larger positive FG% impact.
  • Steals: Mitchell's 1.5 per game in 2025-26 was a major separator. No reserve projects as a high-volume replacement.
  • FT% tension: Mitchell and Harden support FT%; Mobley and Allen pull the other direction. Mobley shot .606 in 2025-26, so his projected .680 is an improvement assumption, not a safe baseline.
  • Turnovers: Harden and Mitchell can combine for roughly six. Allen, Merrill, Strus, and Tyson keep the rest of the rotation relatively clean.

Role, health, and transaction watch

  1. Harden's contract is the first gate. His return preserves an elite assist source but caps Mitchell's playmaking ceiling and keeps Schröder/Porter in low-minute roles. A departure adds 31 baseline minutes and roughly 7-8 assists to redistribute.
  2. Allen is the most-reported salary to leave, and that is the largest single value swing here. Through early-to-mid July, reporting identified Allen as both the likeliest route to salary relief under the apron and the centrepiece "bait" in a LeBron sign-and-trade; Cleveland has explored his market since February, and teams calling about the roster ask on Allen, Mobley, Tyson and Strus. Because the second apron forbids aggregating salaries, Allen's contract is one of the few single pieces large enough to matter. Traded branch: as a standalone starting centre elsewhere he leaves the 29-minute Mobley timeshare for 30-32 MPG with a 10-plus rebound floor and more interior scoring — plausibly a top-50 9-cat outcome rather than the pick 70-110 range modelled here. That branch is a real fraction of his outcome distribution, not a tail. Cavaliers Nation, July 5, 2026 · Fear the Sword — Cavs exploring Allen trades

Correction 2026-07-18: Allen previously carried no transaction-risk flag anywhere in this file and was modelled purely as an untouchable core starter. July reporting makes him the most likely large salary to move, so the risk flag and the traded branch are added. His base-case projection and pick range are unchanged; what changes is that the base case is no longer treated as the only case.

  1. LeBron is a scenario, not a projection. Reporting said Strus and/or Schröder could be moved to create room — individually, not as a package, while Cleveland is above the second apron. A LeBron arrival would sharply compress Harden/Mitchell/Mobley usage and move Tyson/Merrill minutes, even if team wins rise. The Athletic reporting discussed July 16 (community repost; verify the underlying report before treating a transaction as likely)
  2. Mobley's calf: recurring left-calf trouble cost him time in December and January; he played 65 games. The official January update gave a one-to-three-week window. NBA.com, January 27, 2026
  3. Allen's knee/finger: severe right-knee tendinitis cost 10 games in March; he had also fractured his left ring finger in October. His 67-game projection includes elevated maintenance risk. NBA.com return report · NBA.com finger report
  4. Strus's foot: he had surgery for a left-foot Jones fracture in August 2025 and did not debut until March 15, 2026. There is no current restriction, but the delayed return justifies a games discount. Cavaliers medical update, March 3, 2026
  5. Harden/Merrill recent injuries: Harden sustained a non-displaced right-thumb fracture in February and missed only two games. Merrill lost 14 games to a right-hand sprain and had late-season hamstring management. NBA.com Harden report · Cavaliers Merrill feature
  6. Rookie reality: Thomas averaged 15.6 points, 3.8 rebounds, 2.5 assists, and 1.5 steals in 30.5 college minutes while shooting 43/42/84. The official roster feed nevertheless said he is likely to spend most of the year in the G League unless he dominates camp. NBA draft profile · Cavaliers roster fantasy news, July 18, 2026
  7. Two-way churn: Minix averaged 19.3 points, 6.7 rebounds, 2.2 assists, and 1.3 steals in 31.6 G League minutes on .556/.457 shooting before reported talks with ASVEL. Enaruna produced 19.4/5.1/1.4/1.4 for the Charge before his two-way deal. G League Minix release · Cavaliers Enaruna release · Hoops Rumors Minix report

Team outcome range

  • Floor — 41-44 wins: Harden leaves or declines sharply, the veteran guard/wing depth is moved without an equal replacement, and Mobley/Allen again miss time. This version can fall into the play-in and offers better Mitchell/Tyson counting stats than real-life efficiency.
  • Base — 47-51 wins: Harden returns, the three core stars play 64-plus games, and the offense remains top eight. This model centers at 49, with no live market to check it against: no 2026-27 Cleveland win total had been posted publicly as of July 18. The only current market signal is title futures (+2500 to +2700 across books).
