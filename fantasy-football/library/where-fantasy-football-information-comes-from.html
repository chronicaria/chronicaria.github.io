Where fantasy football information comes from

Read this before the table

Three terms recur. ADP is average draft position — where a player actually goes in real drafts, averaged over many of them. A depth chart is a team's internal ordering at each position: who starts, who is second. A snap is one play; a player not on the field for a play cannot score on it. These and every other jargon term here live in Fantasy football glossary.

The landscape is cheap. Nothing load-bearing in a 12-team home league sits behind a paywall. What costs money — charting grades, advanced efficiency metrics — answers questions that matter less than the free sources answer. Do not buy anything before the draft.

What a working set looks like

Twenty-one sources is a map, not a routine. The routine is four things:

  1. One official channel for facts — the injury report in season, the transactions wire the rest of the year. Identity, status and dates come from here and nowhere else.
  2. One projection source that publishes stat lines, so points can be recomputed under this league's rules instead of imported with someone else's.
  3. One ADP, explicitly labelled with the platform it came from.
  4. Beat writers only for the teams you actually own players on — after a draft, about eight teams, not thirty-two.

Everything else is optional. The failure mode is not missing a source; it is reading six, finding disagreement, and mistaking the disagreement for information.

This build used two files, both dated in What I checked and when: FFToday projected stat lines, and Fantasy Football Calculator PPR ADP — the latter a labelled proxy for ESPN's, which could not be extracted programmatically. See ADP, rankings, and projections are three different things.

Open questions

  • Is there a free, extractable route to ESPN's own ADP? Every attempt in July 2026 failed, and running mock drafts on ESPN is currently the substitute measurement rather than a real fix.
  • Which single beat-writer feed per team is worth following once the roster is set? Untested — this is a post-draft question, not a pre-draft one.
  • The advanced-metrics tier is asserted here to be low-value for a 12-team home league. That is a judgement about effort-to-payoff, not something this build measured.
