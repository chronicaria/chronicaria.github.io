Role and value

Fox averaged 18.6 points, 6.2 assists, 3.8 rebounds and 1.2 steals in 31.0 minutes across 72 games in 2025-26, shooting 48.6% from the field. Those are the right baselines: the fall from his Sacramento-level usage was real, not merely a slow integration sample. ESPN stats, accessed 2026-07-18

He remains the nominal starter, but Stephon Castle led San Antonio at 7.4 assists per game and Dylan Harper earned a larger postseason role. Fox's ceiling therefore comes from efficiency, steals and transition scoring rather than a return to 24-26 points. A trade would materially reset the projection; absent one, 30 minutes is the disciplined base case.

Ceiling, floor and risk

  • Ceiling: 21/4/7 with 1.5 steals and top-40 value if he controls closing possessions.
  • Floor: 28 minutes, 17 points and fewer than six assists if Harper becomes the preferred closer.
  • Category fit: helpful FG% for a point guard and useful steals; middling FT% on nontrivial volume limits roto upside.
  • Health: the leading risk on this file, not a footnote. Fox suffered a right high ankle sprain in Game 4 of the second round against Minnesota when Ayo Dosunmu landed on the ankle, played through it in Games 5 and 6, missed the first two Western Conference Finals games, and reaggravated it in Game 3 on a Lu Dort loose-ball dive. San Antonio attributes his Finals collapse to it: 18.8 points on 46.2% over his first 11 playoff games, then 12.0 on 35.3% after the injury, and 12.8 in the Finals. Across the full run that is 15.6 points on 41.4% in 21 playoff games against 18.6 on 48.6% in the regular season. The 70-game, 30-minute projection above assumes the ankle is sound by camp; if it is not, both the games and the efficiency are exposed. NBA, 2026-05-19 · ESPN, 2026-05-20 · Hoops Rumors, 2026-07

Correction 2026-07-18: this file previously described the injury as right-ankle soreness confined to two Western Conference Finals games and called it "a draft-season check, not an active absence." That understated it. The correct diagnosis is a right high ankle sprain sustained in the second round, and the team's own account has it degrading his production through the Finals. Career record

Pre-NBA: One season at Kentucky (2016-17, SEC): 36 games, 16.7 points, 4.0 rebounds, 4.6 assists on .479 FG, .246 3P, .736 FT. SEC All-Freshman team and SEC Tournament MVP; scored 39 against UCLA in the Sweet 16, then an NCAA Tournament record for a freshman. Left after one year. Draft: 2017, round 1, pick 5, Sacramento Kings — no draft-night trade. Traded to San Antonio at the February 2025 deadline; signed a reported four-year, $229M extension with the Spurs.

Fantasy history: 2023-24 was his best category season and the first time he finished inside the top 20 in 8-cat, driven by a career-high 2.0 steals and 2.9 threes on top of 26.6 points. His 2020-21 through 2022-23 stretch was consistently early-to-mid-round on the strength of scoring plus a strong FG% for a lead guard. I could not find a citable season-end 9-cat rank for 2024-25 or 2025-26, so I will not put a number on those — the 2025-26 raw line (18.6 / 3.8 / 6.2 / 1.2 / 0.3, .486 / .760, 1.8 threes, 2.3 turnovers in 31 minutes) is a mid-round shape rather than an early-round one. Points leagues have always rated him higher than categories, because his value concentrates in points and assists and he has never provided blocks or elite percentages.

Durability: GP by season — 73, 81, 51, 58, 59, 73, 74, 62, 72. Career average of about 67 in nine seasons, with four seasons under 63. The 2019-20 (51) and 2020-21 (58) seasons came in shortened 72-game schedules, so those are less alarming than they look; 2021-22 (59 of 82) is the genuinely poor one. 2024-25 ended early: he tore the extensor tendon in his left pinkie during Kings training camp, played through it all year, and finally had surgery in March 2025, missing the last 18 games. 2025-26: missed the first seven games with a hamstring strain suffered while working out in Las Vegas during July 2025 Summer League, debuting 2025-11-08 against New Orleans, then played 72 of the remaining 75. In the 2026 playoffs he sprained the right ankle high in Game 4 of the second round against Minnesota, played the rest of that series, sat the first two Western Conference Finals games against Oklahoma City, returned for Game 3 and reaggravated it in that game. He was available for the Finals but visibly diminished.

Trajectory: Fox's arc peaked in a three-year window from 2020-21 to 2023-24, cresting at 26.6 points and 2.0 steals in 2023-24 as the sole engine of a Kings offense built around him. Everything since has been a downshift, and 2025-26 shows it is not purely a minutes story: per-36 he scored roughly 21.6 in 2025-26 against roughly 26.7 in 2023-24, and his steal rate fell from 2.0 per game in 35.9 minutes to 1.2 in 31.0. That is a real efficiency-for-volume trade — his .486 FG in 2025-26 was the second-best mark of his career, but he got there by taking fewer hard shots inside a Wembanyama-anchored offense where Stephon Castle led the team in assists. He turns 29 in December 2026, which is exactly where speed-first guards start losing the first step that produced his rim pressure and his steals. The history therefore argues against betting on a bounce-back: the 2025-26 line is the new baseline, and the realistic 2026-27 variance is a couple of minutes and a trade, not a return to 24-plus points.
