<p>The key naming caution is that <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>o</mi><mi>S</mi><mi>C</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">oSC</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">o</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0715em;">C</span></span></span></span> and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi><mi>S</mi><mi>C</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">dSC</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0715em;">C</span></span></span></span> are <strong>second-chance value</strong>, not raw offensive- and defensive-rebound rates. A player can affect the value of the entire sequence after a miss: securing the rebound, denying it, generating a putback, forcing a kickout, or changing the quality of the next shot.</p>
<h2 id="from-eight-solve-targets-to-six-factors">From eight solve targets to six factors</h2>
<p>The current engine solves eight underlying possession-value components separately on offense and defense:</p>
<ul>
<li>rim finishing value;</li>
<li>midrange finishing value;</li>
<li>three-point finishing value;</li>
<li>rim-attempt frequency value;</li>
<li>three-point-attempt frequency value;</li>
<li>free-throw value;</li>
<li>turnover value;</li>
<li>clean second-chance value.</li>
</ul>
<p>The first six scoring components sum into <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">TS</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.1389em;">T</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span>, turnover value becomes <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>T</mi><mi>O</mi><mi>V</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">TOV</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.1389em;">T</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0278em;">O</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.2222em;">V</span></span></span></span>, and the final component becomes <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi><mi>C</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">SC</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0715em;">C</span></span></span></span>. This preserves a fine-grained research substrate while keeping the public explanation readable.</p>

<h2 id="why-decomposition-is-useful">Why decomposition is useful</h2>
<p>A single net number hides very different player profiles. Two +3 players may reach that total through unrelated mechanisms: one through shooting, another through possession creation and defense. The factors make the model useful for:</p>
<ul>
<li>explaining why a player ranks highly;</li>
<li>testing which skills persist from season to season;</li>
<li>diagnosing which components box scores can and cannot predict;</li>
<li>translating lower-league performance factor by factor in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/cross-league-rapm-translation/" class="wiki-link">Cross-league RAPM translation</a>;</li>
<li>projecting different aging paths in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/nba-player-forward-projection-model/" class="wiki-link">NBA player forward projection model</a>;</li>
<li>attributing team strengths and weaknesses rather than producing an opaque win total.</li>
</ul>
<p>The decomposition also exposes weak spots. Traditional box features carry much less information about <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi><mi>T</mi><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">dTS</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.1389em;">T</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span> and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>d</mi><mi>S</mi><mi>C</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">dSC</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0715em;">C</span></span></span></span> than about offense or forced turnovers because they cannot observe contest quality, rim deterrence, rotations, deflections, or box-outs. A detailed factor is not automatically a well-identified factor.</p>
<h2 id="pure-prior-informed-and-box-views">Pure, prior-informed, and box views</h2>
<p>Each factor can be considered through three measured views:</p>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>pure</strong> coefficient comes only from the regularized lineup design and shrinks toward zero.</li>
<li>The <strong>prior-informed</strong> coefficient is still estimated from possession lineups but can shrink toward a causal box/EPM-feature prior on enabled components.</li>
<li>The <strong>box prior</strong> is the prior's standalone estimate, useful as a second opinion and diagnostic.</li>
</ul>
<p>The prior is most influential at low effective exposure. It should not be described as extra possessions or as a second independent observation. It is a structured shrinkage target whose predictive value must be established separately. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/rapm-evaluation-and-uncertainty/" class="wiki-link">RAPM evaluation and uncertainty</a> records that its net improvement has been small and regime-dependent even when individual factors improve.</p>
<h2 id="exact-closure-does-not-imply-exact-attribution">Exact closure does not imply exact attribution</h2>
<p><code>net = sum6</code> guarantees arithmetic coherence, not causal completeness. The six targets partition the model's observed possession value under a chosen taxonomy. Interactions, scheme, screen setting, communication, off-ball gravity, and role effects can enter indirectly or be distributed among correlated player columns. Exact addition tells us the ledger balances; it does not prove that every basketball mechanism received its philosophically perfect label.</p>
