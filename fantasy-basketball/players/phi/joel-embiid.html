Embiid's 2025-26 baseline was 38 games and 31.6 minutes: .489/.854, 1.4 threes, 26.9 points, 7.7 rebounds, 3.9 assists, 0.6 steals, 1.2 blocks, and 2.9 turnovers. The projection holds the category shape, trims usage for Jaylen Brown, and adds two games. Hashtag dynasty/stat page, updated July 2, 2026

Role and category profile

  • 9-cat: rare PTS/FT%/FG%/BLK combination at center with useful assists and threes. Turnovers are high; steals are modest. Per-game value can fit the second/third round, but total value can miss the top 100.
  • Points: ESPN's early board ranked him 71st, an explicit availability discount. A pick 35-75 range is rational because scoring systems, injury slots, weekly lineups, and games caps materially change his value. ESPN, April 14, 2026
  • Dynasty: Hashtag listed him 97th and its keeper crowd 88th. Contenders can pay above that for category impact; rebuilders should not. He is a top-70-to-110 asset with a much higher single-season ceiling. Hashtag dynasty · Hashtag keeper
  • Handcuff effect: Adem Bona is the first block/verticality substitute, Dominick Barlow the switchable 4/5, and Ariel Hukporti/Johni Broome deeper layers. There is no single automatic 30-minute replacement.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: fewer than 30 games or repeated shutdowns; unusable total value even if active rates remain elite.
  • Base: 40 games at 31 minutes, with top-25-to-40 per-game category value and a draft window around picks 25-45 for risk-tolerant 9-cat managers.
  • Ceiling: 55-60 games, roughly 27/9/4 with 1.5 combined steals/blocks; a league-winning top-15 total season at a third-round cost.

Health timeline

Embiid played only 38 regular-season games. He underwent an appendectomy April 9, returned 17 days later for Game 4 against Boston, scored 34 points with 12 rebounds and six assists in the Game 7 win, then missed Game 2 against New York with ankle and hip injuries. The appendectomy itself is not assumed to limit October; the cumulative right-knee/soft-tissue history and three straight seasons at 39 or fewer games drive the forecast. NBA return report, April 26, 2026 · AP Game 7 report, May 2, 2026 · NBA Game 2 absence, May 6, 2026

There is an affirmative offseason signal, and it is positive. At the June 8, 2026 introduction of new president of basketball operations Mike Gansey, HBSE president of sports Bob Myers said Embiid has no scheduled surgeries this summer and no injury going into the offseason, describing it as an opportunity to get better rather than to play catch-up. Embiid separately said his knees feel good and credited his most recent procedure and his work with Dr. Jonathan Glashow. This is his first offseason in several years without a rehab timeline or a scheduled knee procedure, and it recasts 2025-26 as a season ended by an appendectomy and its complications rather than by a new structural knee injury. Myers on Embiid, June 8, 2026

That signal moves the shape of the distribution, not its center. Three consecutive seasons under 40 games, a formalized no-back-to-backs policy, and a list of separate structural events rather than one healing injury are what set the 40-game base rate; a clean summer is necessary for a better season but has not historically been sufficient. The practical read is that the upside tail is now considerably fatter—55-plus games is a live outcome rather than a courtesy ceiling—while the downside is unchanged. Drafting him still requires an explicit replacement-level plan for 35-45 missed games, and the operative weekly constraint is the back-to-back policy rather than a games cap.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Kansas, one season (2013-14). 28 games, 23.1 minutes, 11.2 points, 8.1 rebounds, 1.4 assists, 2.6 blocks on .626 shooting; set Kansas freshman records for blocks (72), field goal percentage and rebounding. Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year, All-Big 12 second team, Big 12 All-Defensive and All-Newcomer teams. A back stress fracture ended his freshman year early and he missed the final six games plus the NCAA tournament. Embiid did not start playing basketball until age 15 in Cameroon, so the college sample is a single partial season of a converted volleyball/soccer athlete. Draft: 2014, round 1, pick 3, Philadelphia. He then missed the entire 2014-15 and 2015-16 seasons after two surgeries on a navicular stress fracture in his right foot, debuting only in year three.

Fantasy history: The category shape has been elite on a per-game basis for most of his career and mediocre on a total-value basis for all of it. His 2021-22 and 2022-23 seasons — 68 and 66 games with scoring titles in both — are the only two where per-game and total value converged, and those are the seasons he was drafted in the first round. 2023-24 was his best per-game category season (34.7 points, 5.6 assists, .883 from the line) and simultaneously a total-value disaster at 39 games. 2024-25 at 19 games was worthless in any format. Exact season-end 9-cat ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag are not verifiable from public pages, so specific per-season rankings are not asserted here. The points-league shape is materially worse than the category shape for one structural reason: points formats penalize his 3.4 career turnovers without rewarding the elite FT% and FG% that carry two full categories in roto, which is why ESPN's early points board put him 71st while category boards run him considerably higher.

Durability: 2014-15: 0. 2015-16: 0. 2016-17: 31. 2017-18: 63. 2018-19: 64. 2019-20: 51. 2020-21: 51. 2021-22: 68. 2022-23: 66. 2023-24: 39. 2024-25: 19. 2025-26: 38. Causes, in order: navicular stress fracture in the right foot (two surgeries, cost both of his first two seasons); a rookie-year meniscus tear; orbital fracture and concussion in 2018; recurring left knee soreness and a torn meniscus after a Jonathan Kuminga collision on January 30, 2024, which required surgery and cost most of the second half of 2023-24; a left knee that never resolved through 2024-25, ending that season on February 28, 2025 when Philadelphia shut him down for treatment and rehabilitation. In 2025-26 he opened late off knee surgery, played under a declared no-back-to-backs policy all year, missed time with right shin soreness that surfaced inside a right-knee management program, and had his regular season ended April 9, 2026 by an appendectomy. The mechanisms are not a single recurring injury; they are a long list of separate structural events on a 7-foot, 280-pound frame, which is why the base rate does not improve with any one surgery healing.

Trajectory: The leap seasons are legible and were all about role and shot diet rather than health. 2018-19 to 2021-22 was the rebound-and-defense phase; 2021-22 to 2023-24 was the shift into a full offensive hub, with assists climbing from 2.8 to 5.6 and free throw rate rising to the point where the FT% category alone justified a top-15 pick. That version peaked at age 29. What 2025-26 shows is the post-peak shape: scoring efficiency recovered to .489 and turnovers actually fell to a career low 2.9, but rebounds collapsed to 7.7 — the lowest of his career by a full 0.5 from his rookie year — and blocks (1.2) and steals (0.6) sit at or near career lows. That combination is the signature of a big man who has been told to conserve, not one who lost skill: the scoring and shooting held while every category that requires repeated explosive movement decayed. At 32 in 2026-27 he is on the wrong side of the center aging curve with a load-management regime now formalized into the schedule. The honest read is that the per-game category profile is still second-round quality and probably will be for two more years, but the games-played distribution has been below 40 for three consecutive seasons and there is no mechanism in the history that suggests reversion. Draft him as a per-game specialist with a roster spot budgeted for his absences, not as a foundation.
