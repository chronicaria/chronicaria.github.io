    00 — Overview — Compactification and Effective Actions

    Two ideas run through this module: a string wrapped on a small circle sees more than a point particle can (winding, T-duality), and a string in a curved background forces spacetime to obey Einstein's equations. Both come down to one fact — the two-dimensional worldsheet is in charge.

    Loops do two things points cannot

    A string is a loop, not a point, and a loop can do two things a point cannot.

    It can wrap. Put a point particle on a circle and the only new thing that happens is that its momentum around the loop becomes quantized, p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R — a ladder of states (the Kaluza–Klein tower) whose spacing grows as the circle shrinks. A closed string does all that and can wind around the circle like a rubber band around a pole, mmm times, and it costs energy to stretch it. Winding is topological: you cannot unwrap without cutting the string. This one extra integer mmm is the source of everything stringy in the compact directions — including the fact that a string literally cannot tell a circle of radius RRR from one of radius α′/R\alpha'/Rα′/R (T-duality). Momentum and winding trade places; the string sees a symmetry a point particle has no access to.

    It sweeps a surface. As it moves, a point particle traces a line; a string traces a 2d sheet, the worldsheet. That sheet carries its own quantum field theory, and the geometry of spacetime enters as the coupling constants of that theory. Demanding that this 2d theory stay quantum-mechanically consistent — specifically, that it keep its conformal (Weyl) symmetry, which we cannot give up — forces the spacetime fields to obey equations of motion. Those equations turn out to be Einstein's equations. Gravity is not put in by hand; it is the price of keeping the worldsheet a CFT.

    So the module has two threads, and they are the same thread seen from two sides:

    1. Compactification — what new structure the extended string detects in spacetime when a dimension is curled up: winding, T-duality, enhanced gauge symmetry, orbifold fixed points.
    2. Effective actions — what constraints that same worldsheet imposes back on spacetime: the metric GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ (graviton), the antisymmetric BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ (Kalb–Ramond field), and the scalar Φ\PhiΦ (dilaton) get dynamics from the vanishing of worldsheet beta functions.

    This is the bridge from "string on flat R1,25\mathbb{R}^{1,25}R1,25" (Modules 22–24) to "string as a theory of gravity in a real, possibly compact universe."

    The two results to hold in your head

    Compactified mass formula. Curl the last spatial coordinate into a circle, X∼X+2πRX\sim X+2\pi RX∼X+2πR. The closed string then carries quantized momentum p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R and winding w=mR/α′w=mR/\alpha'w=mR/α′, and a 25-dimensional observer measures

    M2=n2R2+m2R2α′2+2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2),N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm.(1)M^2 = \frac{n^2}{R^2} + \frac{m^2 R^2}{\alpha'^2} + \frac{2}{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp + \bar N^\perp - 2\big), \qquad N^\perp - \bar N^\perp = nm. \tag{1}M2=R2n2​+α′2m2R2​+α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2),N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm.(1)

    The −2-2−2 is the closed-string zero-point energy a+aˉ=−1−1a+\bar a=-1-1a+aˉ=−1−1 (the intercept −a=1-a=1−a=1 per sector) that also forced D=26D=26D=26; the level-matching is no longer "equal left and right" but is offset by exactly nmnmnm. Momentum enters as 1/R1/R1/R, winding as R/α′R/\alpha'R/α′ — symmetric under swapping them. Full derivation in 25.1 Toroidal compactification.

    Einstein from conformal invariance. A string in a metric Gμν(X)G_{\mu\nu}(X)Gμν​(X) is a 2d nonlinear sigma model. Its one-loop metric beta function is the target-space Ricci tensor,

    βμν(G)=α′ Rμν,(2)\beta_{\mu\nu}(G) = \alpha'\,\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}, \tag{2}βμν​(G)=α′Rμν​,(2)

    and Weyl invariance demands βμν(G)=0\beta_{\mu\nu}(G)=0βμν​(G)=0, i.e. Rμν=0\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}=0Rμν​=0 — the vacuum Einstein equation. Turning on BBB and Φ\PhiΦ sources it, and all three field equations come from one 26d effective action. Derivations in 25.2 Beta functions and conformal invariance and 25.3 Einstein equations from conformal invariance.

    Exercise

    Close the book and answer both parts using only the two boxed results above.

    1. What can a string detect about a compact dimension that a point particle cannot, and why does it make big and small circles indistinguishable? State the mass formula (1) and show it is invariant under R→α′/RR\to\alpha'/RR→α′/R with n↔mn\leftrightarrow mn↔m.
    2. How does worldsheet conformal invariance constrain spacetime fields? In one clean chain of reasoning, get from "string in a curved background" to "spacetime obeys Einstein's equations."
    Solution

    Part 1. A point particle on a circle sees only quantized momentum p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R: a KK tower with M2=n2/R2M^2=n^2/R^2M2=n2/R2. A closed string additionally winds, mmm times around the circle, contributing M2⊃(mR/α′)2M^2\supset (mR/\alpha')^2M2⊃(mR/α′)2. The winding mass has a one-line physical origin: a string wound ∣m∣|m|∣m∣ times has length ∣m∣ 2πR|m|\,2\pi R∣m∣2πR, and with tension T0=1/(2πα′)T_0=1/(2\pi\alpha')T0​=1/(2πα′) its stretched rest energy is T0⋅∣m∣2πR=∣m∣R/α′=∣w∣T_0\cdot|m|2\pi R = |m|R/\alpha' = |w|T0​⋅∣m∣2πR=∣m∣R/α′=∣w∣ — literally the energy stored in the stretched loop. A point particle has no winding integer mmm.

    Invariance: under R→R~=α′/RR\to\tilde R=\alpha'/RR→R~=α′/R and n↔mn\leftrightarrow mn↔m, the first term of (1) becomes n2R2  ⟶  m2R~2=m2R2α′2,\frac{n^2}{R^2}\;\longrightarrow\;\frac{m^2}{\tilde R^2}=\frac{m^2 R^2}{\alpha'^2},R2n2​⟶R~2m2​=α′2m2R2​, which is exactly the second term of the original; likewise the second term maps to the first. The oscillator term 2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)\frac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2)α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2) is untouched, and the constraint N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nmN^\perp-\bar N^\perp=nmN⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm is symmetric in n,mn,mn,m. So as (n,m)(n,m)(n,m) range over all integers the two radii produce the identical list of masses: M2(R;n,m)=M2(α′/R;m,n)M^2(R;n,m)=M^2(\alpha'/R;m,n)M2(R;n,m)=M2(α′/R;m,n). The string cannot tell the two circles apart — this is T-duality, and it is intrinsically stringy because it requires winding.

    (Consistency check on the offset: the winding energy also explains why level-matching shifts. The zero-mode part of L0−Lˉ0L_0-\bar L_0L0​−Lˉ0​ is 12(α02−αˉ02)\tfrac12(\alpha_0^2-\bar\alpha_0^2)21​(α02​−αˉ02​) with α0,αˉ0=α′/2 (p∓w)\alpha_0,\bar\alpha_0=\sqrt{\alpha'/2}\,(p\mp w)α0​,αˉ0​=α′/2​(p∓w), giving −α′pw=−nm-\alpha' pw = -nm−α′pw=−nm; setting L0=Lˉ0L_0=\bar L_0L0​=Lˉ0​ then forces N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nmN^\perp-\bar N^\perp=nmN⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm.)

    Part 2. The chain: a string propagating in a background metric Gμν(X)G_{\mu\nu}(X)Gμν​(X) is a 2d nonlinear sigma model, and the spacetime fields are its coupling constants (the Taylor coefficients of GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​ about each point). Weyl (conformal) invariance is a gauge symmetry of the Polyakov action — the thing that removed the unphysical modes and forced D=26D=26D=26 — so it cannot be lost when we quantize. But quantizing needs a UV cutoff, which introduces a scale and makes the couplings run; the running is measured by the beta functional βμν(G)\beta_{\mu\nu}(G)βμν​(G). Conformal invariance survives only if βμν(G)=0\beta_{\mu\nu}(G)=0βμν​(G)=0. At one loop βμν(G)=α′Rμν\beta_{\mu\nu}(G)=\alpha'\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}βμν​(G)=α′Rμν​, so the condition is Rμν=0\mathcal R_{\mu\nu}=0Rμν​=0 — the vacuum Einstein equation. Turning on BBB and Φ\PhiΦ adds source terms, and all three β=0\beta=0β=0 conditions follow from extremizing one 26d effective action whose leading term is Einstein–Hilbert. Spacetime gravity is, in the end, the statement that the string's worldsheet is a consistent CFT.
