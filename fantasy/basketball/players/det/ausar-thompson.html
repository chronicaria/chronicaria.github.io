Ausar Thompson

Ausar Thompson is a rare wing who can approach three combined steals and blocks, making him a category-league difference maker even while threes and free throws remain actively harmful.

Nine-cat: top-50 ceiling, top-70 median; build-dependent because FT% and zero threes are severe. Points: B-, usually outside the top 80. Dynasty: 8.5/10 on age, defensive floor, and shooting-upside asymmetry.

Role and evidence

Thompson's 2025-26 line was 9.9 points, 5.7 rebounds, 3.1 assists, 2.0 steals, and 0.9 blocks in 26.0 minutes across 73 games. He made first-team All-Defense, while NBA Communications also recognized him as an Eastern Conference defensive player of the month.

Detroit's extra shooting is a functional vote of confidence: lineups with Joe, Robinson, or Huerter can absorb Thompson's non-shooting and keep his defense on the court. The downside is that those same specialists can replace him late when Detroit needs half-court spacing. A March fantasy-basketball community discussion centered on exactly that tradeoff; it is retained as sentiment, not authority.

Extension reporting was positive but not official at the cutoff. His role is secure; minutes confidence is medium-high because offensive matchup decisions still matter.

Range

  • Ceiling: 32 MPG, 13/7/4 with 2.2 steals and 1.2 blocks; top-35 in punt-FT/threes builds.
  • Floor: 25 MPG, poor free throws, negligible threes, and specialist-only value despite elite defense.

Career record

Pre-NBA: No college. Thompson played two seasons in Overtime Elite, the Atlanta-based professional development league, alongside twin brother Amen. In 2021-22 he averaged 14.7 points, 8.2 rebounds, 3.0 assists and 2.2 blocks and won the championship and Finals MVP. In 2022-23, as co-captain of the City Reapers, he averaged 16.3 points, roughly 7 rebounds, 6.1 assists and roughly 2.5 steals in 27.6 minutes on shooting splits near 48/30/67, taking league MVP, All-OTE First Team, a second championship and a second Finals MVP. Sources differ slightly on his rebound and steal figures for that year; the scoring, assist and shooting numbers are consistent across them. The 30 percent three-point mark in OTE is the single most predictive pre-NBA number in his file, and nothing since has contradicted it. Draft: 2023, first round, 5th overall by Detroit — no draft-night trade. Amen went 4th to Houston one pick earlier.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not verifiable from public sources here, so none are asserted. The structural read is stable across all three seasons and does not require a ranking to state: in standard nine-cat he is a two-category monster (steals, and field goal percentage on volume near the rim) who actively loses two others (free throw percentage, threes), which caps him well outside where his defensive reputation suggests. He is materially better in punt-FT and punt-threes builds than in any balanced roster, and that gap is the whole story. In points leagues he is worse still: 9.9 points with 1.5 turnovers and no threes gives him almost no scoring path, and steals and blocks are worth a fraction of what they carry in categories.

Durability: 63, 59, 73 games. The rookie season ended on March 20, 2024, when he was shut down for blood clot treatment. That condition also cost him the start of 2024-25 — he was not cleared by the NBA's fitness-to-play panel until November 11, 2024, missing at least the first 11 games, with the balance of his 23 absences that year coming from the ramp-up and subsequent maintenance. In 2025-26 he played 73 games and started 72, his first fully normal season, with no reported injury of consequence. The blood clot history is the kind of thing that never fully leaves a file, but two years of clean availability since clearance is meaningful evidence, and the 73-game season is the best predictor available.

Trajectory: The arc is not the usual young-player expansion — it is a narrowing that happens to be working. Steals went 1.1 to 1.7 to 2.0 and assists 1.9 to 2.3 to 3.1, and both hold up per-36 (steals roughly 1.6 to 2.8, assists roughly 2.7 to 4.3), so the defensive and playmaking growth is real improvement rather than minutes. The offense moved the other way: three-point attempts have fallen every single year, to the point that 0.1 made threes per game means he has effectively stopped shooting them, and the high field goal percentage is a consequence of a shot diet confined to the rim rather than evidence of skill growth. Note also that per-36 scoring actually declined in 2025-26 versus 2024-25 (roughly 16.2 to 13.7) even as raw points held — the counting line was carried by minutes and the extra passing, not by scoring gains. At 23 he is young enough that a jumper could still arrive, but three professional seasons plus an OTE year all pointing the same direction is a lot of consistent evidence, and the free throw percentage regressing to .571 argues against imminent touch improvement. For 2026-27 the base case is more of 2025-26 with slightly more minutes: elite stocks, real passing, and a shooting profile that keeps him a build-dependent specialist rather than a general-purpose asset.
