    Bulk equations versus boundary terms

    Integrating the Nambu–Goto variation by parts splits it into a bulk momentum-conservation law that governs the string's interior and an endpoint term that must be separately switched off at each open end.

    One variation, two questions

    Extremizing an action means the variation δS\delta SδS must vanish for every allowed wiggle δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ. The variation from Variation of the worldsheet area contains derivatives of the wiggle, ∂τ(δXμ)\partial_\tau(\delta X^\mu)∂τ​(δXμ) and ∂σ(δXμ)\partial_\sigma(\delta X^\mu)∂σ​(δXμ). Integration by parts moves those derivatives off the wiggle and onto the momentum densities, at the price of a total-derivative term that lives on the edge of the parameter rectangle. This is the same move as ordinary Lagrangian mechanics — ∫q˙ δq˙ dt→−∫q¨ δq dt+[q˙ δq]\int \dot q\,\delta\dot q\,dt \to -\int \ddot q\,\delta q\,dt + [\dot q\,\delta q]∫q˙​δq˙​dt→−∫q¨​δqdt+[q˙​δq] — but now in two variables, so the leftover "edge" is a whole boundary curve rather than two endpoints.

    The result cleanly separates two questions:

    • Interior (bulk): what equation must the string satisfy at every point? Answer: a local conservation law for spacetime momentum.
    • Edges (boundary): what happens where the sheet stops — the initial/final times, and the two ends of an open string? Answer: the time edges are fixed by the rules of the variational game, but each spatial end needs a real physical boundary condition, because momentum could otherwise leak out there.

    One identification drives everything: Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ is the flow of spacetime momentum out the end of the string. A free end says nothing leaks out (Pμσ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0Pμσ​=0); a pinned (Dirichlet) end says the endpoint cannot move, so what would leak is irrelevant (δXμ=0\delta X^\mu=0δXμ=0). Everything below works out those two options.

    Integration by parts

    From Variation of the worldsheet area the variation is

    δS=∫τiτfdτ∫0σ1dσ [Pμτ ∂τ(δXμ)+Pμσ ∂σ(δXμ)],\delta S=\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}d\tau\int_0^{\sigma_1}d\sigma\, \left[ \mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,\partial_\tau(\delta X^\mu) +\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,\partial_\sigma(\delta X^\mu) \right],δS=∫τi​τf​​dτ∫0σ1​​dσ[Pμτ​∂τ​(δXμ)+Pμσ​∂σ​(δXμ)],

    where Pμτ=∂L/∂X˙μ\mathcal P^\tau_\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot X^\muPμτ​=∂L/∂X˙μ is the spacetime-momentum density carried along worldsheet time, and Pμσ=∂L/∂X′μ\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=\partial\mathcal L/\partial X'^\muPμσ​=∂L/∂X′μ is the momentum flux carried along the string. (The endpoint label σ1\sigma_1σ1​ stays arbitrary until a parameterization is chosen; the gauge-fixed notes later take σ∈[0,π]\sigma\in[0,\pi]σ∈[0,π].) Use the product rule in each variable, P ∂(δX)=∂(δX P)−δX ∂P\mathcal P\,\partial(\delta X)=\partial(\delta X\,\mathcal P)-\delta X\,\partial\mathcal PP∂(δX)=∂(δXP)−δX∂P:

    δS=∫dτ dσ[∂τ(δXμPμτ)+∂σ(δXμPμσ)⏟total derivatives  →  edge terms−  δXμ(∂τPμτ+∂σPμσ)⏟bulk Euler–Lagrange].(1)\delta S =\int d\tau\,d\sigma\left[ \underbrace{\partial_\tau(\delta X^\mu\mathcal P^\tau_\mu) +\partial_\sigma(\delta X^\mu\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu)}_{\text{total derivatives}\;\to\;\text{edge terms}} -\;\delta X^\mu\underbrace{\left(\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu+\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\right)}_{\text{bulk Euler–Lagrange}} \right]. \tag{1}δS=∫dτdσ​total derivatives→edge terms∂τ​(δXμPμτ​)+∂σ​(δXμPμσ​)​​−δXμbulk Euler–Lagrange(∂τ​Pμτ​+∂σ​Pμσ​)​​​.(1)

    Integrating each total derivative collapses it onto the corresponding edge of the rectangle [τi,τf]×[0,σ1][\tau_i,\tau_f]\times[0,\sigma_1][τi​,τf​]×[0,σ1​]: the ∂τ\partial_\tau∂τ​ term lands on the time edges τi,τf\tau_i,\tau_fτi​,τf​, the ∂σ\partial_\sigma∂σ​ term on the space edges 0,σ10,\sigma_10,σ1​.

    Time edges: fixed by the rules of the game

    The τ\tauτ-total-derivative gives

    [∫0σ1dσ δXμPμτ]τiτf.(2)\left[\int_0^{\sigma_1}d\sigma\,\delta X^\mu\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\right]_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}. \tag{2}[∫0σ1​​dσδXμPμτ​]τi​τf​​.(2)

    Hamilton's principle only compares histories that share the same initial and final string configurations, so by construction

    δXμ(τi,σ)=δXμ(τf,σ)=0.\delta X^\mu(\tau_i,\sigma)=\delta X^\mu(\tau_f,\sigma)=0.δXμ(τi​,σ)=δXμ(τf​,σ)=0.

    The term (2) vanishes automatically. This is not a physical condition on the string — it is the definition of the variational problem (we hold the past and future fixed and ask what happens in between).

    Space edges: real physical ends

    The σ\sigmaσ-total-derivative gives, for an open string,

    ∫τiτfdτ [δXμPμσ]0σ1=∫τiτfdτ (δXμ Pμσ∣σ1−δXμ Pμσ∣0).(3)\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}d\tau\, \Big[\delta X^\mu\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\Big]_0^{\sigma_1} =\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}d\tau\, \Big( \delta X^\mu\,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\big|_{\sigma_1} -\delta X^\mu\,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\big|_{0} \Big). \tag{3}∫τi​τf​​dτ[δXμPμσ​]0σ1​​=∫τi​τf​​dτ(δXμPμσ​​σ1​​−δXμPμσ​​0​).(3)

    The lower end carries a minus sign because [ ⋅ ]0σ1[\,\cdot\,]_0^{\sigma_1}[⋅]0σ1​​ means top minus bottom. That sign is harmless here: we will force each endpoint term to vanish separately, so how they combine never matters.

    For a closed string there are no ends — σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 and σ=σ1\sigma=\sigma_1σ=σ1​ are the same point. Periodicity makes δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ and Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ match at the identified ends, the two contributions in (3) cancel, and no endpoint condition is needed. Boundary conditions are an open-string phenomenon.

    The bulk equation of motion

    In the interior δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ is completely arbitrary, so its coefficient in (1) must vanish pointwise:

      ∂τPμτ+∂σPμσ=0  \boxed{\;\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu+\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0\;}∂τ​Pμτ​+∂σ​Pμσ​=0​

    Read it as a continuity equation for spacetime momentum on the sheet: the momentum stored in a strip of string (Pμτ\mathcal P^\tau_\muPμτ​) changes in time only through the flux Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ crossing the strip's two edges in σ\sigmaσ. It is one conservation law per spacetime component μ\muμ, and it holds for open and closed strings alike. Compact as it looks, it is badly nonlinear — each P\mathcal PP is a quotient of quadratics in X˙\dot XX˙ and X′X'X′ over a square root of the same — and becomes tractable only after a gauge choice like Static gauge collapses it.

    The two ways to kill an endpoint term

    Focus on one spacetime component μ\muμ at one end σ∗∈{0,σ1}\sigma_*\in\{0,\sigma_1\}σ∗​∈{0,σ1​}. The term to kill is

    δXμ(τ,σ∗) Pμσ(τ,σ∗)(no sum on μ).\delta X^\mu(\tau,\sigma_*)\,\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu(\tau,\sigma_*) \qquad(\text{no sum on }\mu).δXμ(τ,σ∗​)Pμσ​(τ,σ∗​)(no sum on μ).

    A product vanishes if either factor does, giving exactly two choices per component per end.

    Dirichlet — pin the end. Restrict the allowed variations:

    δXμ(τ,σ∗)=0,\delta X^\mu(\tau,\sigma_*)=0,δXμ(τ,σ∗​)=0,

    so every competing history shares the same prescribed endpoint position. When the end is pinned to a wall that does not move — the case in this module — that position is constant in time, and on the solution

    ∂τXμ(τ,σ∗)=0.\partial_\tau X^\mu(\tau,\sigma_*)=0.∂τ​Xμ(τ,σ∗​)=0.

    The endpoint coordinate is frozen. Freezing is only available to a spatial component: a frozen X0X^0X0 would stop time from advancing at the end, and time must advance as τ\tauτ advances. So μ=0\mu=0μ=0 can never be Dirichlet.

    Free (Neumann) — let the end swing, but leak nothing. Allow arbitrary endpoint variation and instead kill the flux:

      Pμσ(τ,σ∗)=0  \boxed{\;\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu(\tau,\sigma_*)=0\;}Pμσ​(τ,σ∗​)=0​

    No μ\muμ-momentum flows out the end. Because the time component cannot be Dirichlet, it must be free at both ends:

    P0σ(τ,0)=P0σ(τ,σ1)=0.\mathcal P^\sigma_0(\tau,0)=\mathcal P^\sigma_0(\tau,\sigma_1)=0.P0σ​(τ,0)=P0σ​(τ,σ1​)=0.

    Counting. With D=d+1D=d+1D=d+1 spacetime dimensions there are two ends, giving 2D2D2D endpoint terms, hence 2D2D2D conditions to fix. Each spatial direction at each end is an independent choice — Dirichlet if the end is pinned in that direction, free if it may slide.

    Why "free" becomes "Neumann"

    At the raw Nambu–Goto level the invariant free condition is Pμσ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0Pμσ​=0, not yet the tidy Xμ′=0X'_\mu=0Xμ′​=0 — in a generic parameterization Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ is a messy combination of X˙\dot XX˙ and X′X'X′. It simplifies once we pick an orthonormal (conformal-type) gauge, whose constraints are

    X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.\dot X\cdot X'=0,\qquad \dot X^2+X'^2=0.X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.

    Substitute the first constraint into the explicit flux Pμσ=−T0c(X˙⋅X′)X˙μ−X˙2Xμ′(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=-\frac{T_0}{c}\dfrac{(\dot X\cdot X')\dot X_\mu-\dot X^2X'_\mu}{\sqrt{(\dot X\cdot X')^2-\dot X^2X'^2}}Pμσ​=−cT0​​(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2​(X˙⋅X′)X˙μ​−X˙2Xμ′​​ from Variation of the worldsheet area (this module keeps ccc explicit): the X˙⋅X′\dot X\cdot X'X˙⋅X′ pieces drop, leaving

    Pμσ=−T0c −X˙2 Xμ′−X˙2X′2.\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu =-\frac{T_0}{c}\,\frac{-\dot X^2\,X'_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot X^2X'^2}}.Pμσ​=−cT0​​−X˙2X′2​−X˙2Xμ′​​.

    Now use the second constraint, X′2=−X˙2>0X'^2=-\dot X^2>0X′2=−X˙2>0 — the tangent along the string is spacelike and the velocity timelike (mostly-plus signature) — so the denominator is (−X˙2)(−X˙2)=−X˙2\sqrt{(-\dot X^2)(-\dot X^2)}=-\dot X^2(−X˙2)(−X˙2)​=−X˙2, and

    Pμσ=−T0c Xμ′.\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=-\frac{T_0}{c}\,X'_\mu.Pμσ​=−cT0​​Xμ′​.

    Hence Pμσ=0  ⟺  Xμ′=0\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0 \iff X'_\mu=0Pμσ​=0⟺Xμ′​=0: the familiar Neumann condition. At a fully free (covariant Neumann) end, X′μ=0X'^\mu=0X′μ=0 turns the constraint X˙2+X′2=0\dot X^2+X'^2=0X˙2+X′2=0 into X˙2=0\dot X^2=0X˙2=0: the endpoint's velocity is null, so a free string end moves at the speed of light. The physics was always "no momentum leaks out"; the gauge just makes it look like a slope condition.

    D-brane preview

    Dirichlet directions pin open-string endpoints to a surface. An end with ppp spatial directions free and the rest Dirichlet is confined to a ppp-dimensional spatial hypersurface — a Dppp-brane. Module 10 turns this endpoint bookkeeping into geometry in Neumann versus Dirichlet conditions and D-branes (first appearance).

    Open questions

    • Background gauge fields on a D-brane replace pure Neumann by mixed boundary conditions; see Maxwell fields coupled to open strings.
    • Curved or moving D-branes require time-dependent or curved Dirichlet data, beyond this static local analysis.

    Exercise

    An open string has D=4D=4D=4 spacetime dimensions (X0,X1,X2,X3X^0,X^1,X^2,X^3X0,X1,X2,X3). Its two endpoints are attached to a flat D2-brane spanning the (X1,X2)(X^1,X^2)(X1,X2) plane at X3=0X^3=0X3=0.

    (a) For each of the four spacetime components μ=0,1,2,3\mu=0,1,2,3μ=0,1,2,3, state whether each endpoint obeys a Dirichlet or a free/Neumann condition, and write the condition. (b) Confirm the total count of boundary conditions matches the 2D2D2D expected from the variation, and explain in one sentence why μ=0\mu=0μ=0 could not have been chosen Dirichlet.

    Solution

    (a) The brane fills X1,X2X^1,X^2X1,X2 and sits at fixed X3=0X^3=0X3=0; time X0X^0X0 is never Dirichlet.

    • μ=0\mu=0μ=0 (time): free at both ends, P0σ(τ,0)=P0σ(τ,σ1)=0\mathcal P^\sigma_0(\tau,0)=\mathcal P^\sigma_0(\tau,\sigma_1)=0P0σ​(τ,0)=P0σ​(τ,σ1​)=0.
    • μ=1,2\mu=1,2μ=1,2 (along the brane): free/Neumann at both ends; in conformal gauge X′1=X′2=0X'^1=X'^2=0X′1=X′2=0, so the endpoints slide freely on the plane.
    • μ=3\mu=3μ=3 (transverse to the brane): Dirichlet at both ends, X3(τ,0)=X3(τ,σ1)=0X^3(\tau,0)=X^3(\tau,\sigma_1)=0X3(τ,0)=X3(τ,σ1​)=0 (equivalently ∂τX3=0\partial_\tau X^3=0∂τ​X3=0), pinning the endpoints to X3=0X^3=0X3=0.

    (b) Two ends ×\times× D=4D=4D=4 components =8=2D=8=2D=8=2D conditions, one per endpoint term in the variation — matching. And μ=0\mu=0μ=0 cannot be Dirichlet because freezing X0(τ,σ∗)X^0(\tau,\sigma_*)X0(τ,σ∗​) in time is impossible: time must advance as the worldsheet time τ\tauτ advances, so the time component of each endpoint is forced to be free.
