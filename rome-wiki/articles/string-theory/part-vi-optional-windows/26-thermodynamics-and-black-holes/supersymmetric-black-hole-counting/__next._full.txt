<p>A black hole has entropy <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi><mo>=</mo><mi>k</mi><mi>ln</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mtext>number of microstates</mtext><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S = k\ln(\text{number of microstates})</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mrel">=</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0315em;">k</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mop">ln</span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord text"><span class="mord">number of microstates</span></span><span class="mclose">)</span></span></span></span>, but classically it has no visible microstates — just a mass and a few charges. <p>The catch — and the reason this works where the Schwarzschild case only worked parametrically (<a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-vi-optional-windows/26-thermodynamics-and-black-holes/entropy-area-relation/" class="wiki-link">Entropy-area relation</a>) — is that we count the strings at <strong>zero coupling</strong> (no gravity, so it is just combinatorics) and then claim the count is unchanged at the coupling where the black hole actually exists. That leap is legal only because <strong>supersymmetry</strong> protects the count. This is the Strominger–Vafa calculation (1996), string theory's one quantitative, coefficient-level statement about black holes.</p>
