Every number is a Rome desk projection—not an NBA fact, vendor consensus, ADP, or coach announcement.

How to use the board

  • Format: standard nine-category redraft—FG%, FT%, 3PM, PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, and fewest TO—as defined by Yahoo's default categories. Custom leagues must translate their own settings.
  • Unit: counting tables rank projected production per active game. Parentheses show projected GP so a manager can see availability risk without conflating per-game and totals value. Games played versus per-game value in fantasy basketball explains the distinction.
  • Source table: player-projections-2026-07-18.csv, a reproducible 532-row normalization of team and player cards. Later conditional/deep-roster dossier additions can sit outside that frozen export, so it defines the counting-table population; the latest direct-dossier screen supplies the percentage tables. Every displayed player resolves to the linked dossier, whose role, injury, and transaction branches outrank this compact table.
  • Counting pool: 248 rows with at least 40 projected GP, 16 MPG, and complete 3PM/PTS/REB/AST/STL/BLK/TO fields. The tables show the first 40. Ties at the cutoff are effectively unordered.
  • Percentages: ratio value is attempts multiplied by the gap from a declared reference—not raw rate. The partial impact tables use only supplied attempts; they do not infer attempts from position or percentage.
  • Confidence: steals, blocks, rookies, injury returners, and unsettled rotations carry wider uncertainty than their one-decimal display suggests. See 2026-27 fantasy basketball evidence and confidence policy.

Percentage-impact rankings

The impact score is surplus makes per active game relative to a fixed comparison reference:

\[ I_{FG}=FGA(FG\%-.475), \qquad I_{FT}=FTA(FT\%-.780). \]

The references are transparent neutral yardsticks, not claims about the final 2026-27 NBA or fantasy-pool averages. A positive score means projected surplus makes; a negative score means a drag. The relevant roster-level benchmark changes as the roster changes, so these are discovery tables—not permanent player values. See Percentage categories and volume and LineupExperts' makes/attempt explanation.

Missing stars are unranked, not neutral. The desk deliberately does not infer attempts from percentage alone; this also avoids the percentage proxy used by the older mechanical overall-rank export.

Punt-build gainers and losers

“Gainer” means the player's relative value rises after a category receives little weight. “Loser” means part of his normal premium is being discarded—not that he becomes unusable. These are desk inferences from the primitive lines; Robust punt builds in fantasy basketball requires roster-level thresholds, replacement supply, and stress tests before any hard punt.

Draft workflow

  1. Start with the primitive line, not the overall rank.
  2. After each pick, sum counting categories and percentage makes/attempts; never average player percentages.
  3. Compare the roster with attainable category thresholds and replacement supply, not with abstract league averages alone.
  4. Treat a weak category as repairable until the market cost proves otherwise; soft punts preserve more options than early hard punts.
  5. Stress-test the roster without each scarce-category anchor and with lower GP/minutes scenarios.
  6. Re-run this board after schedule release, camp roles, and full attempt projections. July category leaders are a baseline, not draft-day truth.
