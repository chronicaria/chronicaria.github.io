Aday Mara

Aday Mara is a high-feel, 7-foot-3 dynasty center with passing and rim-protection upside, but Hartenstein's new three-year deal and Mara's mobility/FT limitations erase his 2026-27 redraft case.

That figure was a residual used to force the 240 sum rather than an achievable rotation number — no NBA player logs one or two minutes across 28 appearances — so he moves to the same appearance-card treatment already used for the two-ways, and the freed minutes go to Thomas Sorber, whose July knee procedure leaves him on track for training camp.

Nine-category / points: no redraft draft value. Dynasty: Hashtag No. 173/keeper No. 122; a taxi-squad center whose passing/blocks ceiling is real but whose first useful season may come after 2026-27.

Why the line looks like this

Mara averaged 12.1 points, 6.8 rebounds, 2.4 assists and 2.6 blocks in 23.4 minutes for Michigan's national champion, then went 12th overall. Oklahoma City also re-signed Isaiah Hartenstein through 2028-29 and already has Chet Holmgren, Jaylin Williams and recovering Thomas Sorber.

Summer League showed the same split. Mara opened with 10 points, four assists, two blocks and a steal; later produced 10-9-4 and 14-7-6 lines. Observers also noted slow perimeter recovery, conditioning and poor free throws, including 1-for-5 against Denver. Those games support passing/rim skill and development needs, not rotation certainty.

Sources: official draft transaction, NBA player/college profile, NBA Warriors Summer League recap, NBA Nuggets recap, Hashtag dynasty, community mechanism discussion.

Category shape

  • Projectable strengths: blocks, FG%, rebounds and unusually good passing for a massive center.
  • Costs: FT%, no threes, turnovers and mobility-based minutes risk.
  • Dynasty fit: punt-FT taxi stash; passing can separate him from ordinary rim-running prospects.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: Blue development year with almost no competitive NBA minutes.
  • Base: 28 appearances at one simultaneous minute; fantasy value lives beyond this season.
  • Ceiling: Hartenstein and another big miss time, yielding 12 minutes and about 5 points/4 rebounds/1 assist/1 block.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Professional debut with Basket Zaragoza (Liga ACB, Spain) as a 17-year-old in 2022-23, then three college seasons: UCLA 2023-24 (3.5 PPG, 1.9 RPG, 0.7 BPG, 9.5 MPG) and 2024-25 (6.4 PPG, 4.0 RPG, 1.6 BPG, 13.1 MPG), then Michigan 2025-26 — 12.1 PPG, 6.8 RPG, 2.4 APG, 2.6 BPG on .668 FG / .564 FT in 23.4 MPG across 40 games (39 starts), leading the Big Ten in blocks (103), winning Big Ten Defensive Player of the Year and an NCAA national championship. Draft: 2026, Round 1, Pick 12, Oklahoma City Thunder.

No NBA record — 2026 draftee; most recent season is the 2025-26 national-title year at Michigan above.

Durability: No significant injury history; his college minutes were capped by conditioning and matchup mobility, not health. Trajectory: The record shows a three-year climb from ACB teenager to the most productive per-minute rim protector in the Big Ten, with genuine passing (2.4 APG at 7-foot-3) that ordinary rim-runners lack — and a .564 FT% plus perimeter-mobility questions that followed him into Summer League. In 2026-27 he competes only for the developmental fifth-big slot behind Hartenstein, Holmgren, Jaylin Williams, and Sorber, with heavy Blue assignments likely. He becomes fantasy-relevant only if two OKC bigs miss time simultaneously, which would make him a deep-league punt-FT blocks/FG% speculation at around 12 minutes. Career sources: Wikipedia, Sports-Reference college stats, Michigan athletics bio, official OKC draft release.

Open questions

  • Can he defend in space well enough for Oklahoma City's switching identity?
  • Does the free throw reach even 65-70%?
  • Will the Blue use him as a post hub to accelerate the passing advantage?
