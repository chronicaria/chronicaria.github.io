<h2 id="current-edition">Current edition</h2>
<p>The front-end lives in the separate <code>rome-wiki</code> project and uses a restrained old-newspaper design: warm paper, editorial serif type, fine rules, dense navigation, and no faux aging. Its article template now borrows Wikipedia's information hierarchy without imitating Wikipedia's visual branding.</p>
<ul>
<li>The validated snapshot publishes the human-facing corpus across its reading rooms; article, link, word, and research-seed counts are recalculated on every sync as the continuous committee loop expands it.</li>
<li>The universe map arranges articles into separated, proportionally sized, labeled topic galaxies. Their boundaries remain invisible: local topic gravity creates readable neighborhoods, nearby documents repel, links and shared tags shape each galaxy, and cross-topic links remain visible as bridges. Map labels now use the same editorial serif as the site, with a larger vertical topic-and-color legend on the left.</li>
<li>Article pages use a wide 100-character reading measure, a compact contents rail, smaller display type, flatter heading hierarchy, subtle title rules, and lightweight source/status metadata.</li>
<li>The String Theory reading room is a six-part, 27-chapter course table of contents built from the module folders and <code>00 — Overview</code> notes.</li>
<li>The homepage research ledger records additions, revisions, retirements, relocations, topics, and contributing agents.</li>
<li>Search covers titles, tags, headings, summaries, and body text; full vault paths keep repeated titles distinct.</li>
<li>The renderer supports backlinks, related notes, Obsidian wikilinks, unwritten research seeds, callouts, math, code, tables, tasks, and local media.</li>
<li>Rome is explicitly identified as Andrew Park's brain; article-level provenance remains visible separately.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="publishing-loop">Publishing loop</h2>
<ol>
<li>All nineteen standalone subject committees launch together at the top of every hour and write only their exclusive routes, state, and unique receipts.</li>
<li><code>npm run sync</code> copies only the publishable vault into a frozen repository snapshot and rebuilds the graph.</li>
<li>The public audit, route/graph tests, lint, type checking, and GitHub Pages build must all pass.</li>
<li>The publisher stages only the approved content paths, pushes <code>main</code> non-destructively, dispatches the Pages workflow, and verifies the deployment.</li>
</ol>
<p>The continuous committees can expand Rome without requiring the front-end to know which agent produced a note: the vault and ledger remain the contract.</p>
<p><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/rome/autonomous-research-committees/" class="wiki-link">Autonomous research committees</a> describes the parallel hourly wave, collision-free receipts, dynamic worker-refill model, half-hour publisher, and unlimited expansion mandate.</p>
<h2 id="publication-boundary">Publication boundary</h2>
<p>The wiki publishes the durable human knowledge layer, not every operational file. A shared publication policy excludes agent instructions, prompts, runbooks, Claude/Codex memory and skill files, selected AI status reports, internal String Theory plans and review reports, and any note marked <code>publish: false</code> before the snapshot is copied.</p>
<p>Private wikilink targets render as plain text rather than public pages or false “unwritten article” assignments. Andrew approved public publication after a path-level review on July 9, 2026; individualized health and training notes, personal balances and wealth plans, broker-workflow notes, and capital-action memos remain explicitly excluded. The dispatch-only Pages workflow runs only after each half-hour edition passes every gate.</p>
<h2 id="editorial-organization">Editorial organization</h2>
<ul>
<li>The 30 NBA team research briefings now live together under <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-team-briefings-2026-27/00-index/" class="wiki-link">2026-27 NBA team briefing index</a>; the obsolete <code>Code/</code> branch is gone.</li>
<li>Individual team roster audits were retired, while the two aggregate audit records remain.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/crypto/crypto/" class="wiki-link">Crypto</a> now researches the dynamic top 100 by market capitalization; JUP, PENDLE, SYRUP, AERO, and PUMP remain a conviction subset rather than a universe ceiling.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/mathematics/surface-pdes/bealesurfacesolver-research-moc/" class="wiki-link">BealeSurfaceSolver research (MOC)</a> brings Andrew's surface-PDE solver, level-set geometry, equilibration, shallow-water verification, and exometeorology research into the public knowledge graph.</li>
<li><a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/rapm/" class="wiki-link">RAPM</a> connects Andrew's six-factor RAPM decomposition, regularization, cross-league translation, player/team forward models, evaluation, implementation, and public model site to the basketball corpus.</li>
<li>Operational AI, agent, skill, memory, and internal course-report material stays in Rome for tools to use but outside the public edition.</li>
</ul>

<li>Which additional reading rooms deserve purpose-built course or atlas pages?</li>
<li>Should agent-authored notes eventually declare <code>audience: human</code> or <code>audience: agent</code> explicitly at creation time?</li>
<li>When the corpus reaches tens of thousands of pages, should galaxy coordinates be precomputed during the publication build rather than settled in the browser?</li>
