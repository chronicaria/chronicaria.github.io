    The four-open-string modulus

    Two open strings that join and then re-split trace a worldsheet with exactly one adjustable shape — the time gap T between joining and splitting — and the conformal map to the upper half-plane \overline{\mathbb{H}} turns T into the modulus \lambda ; as T runs over (-\infty,\infty) , \lambda sweeps all of (0,1) , so the physical diagrams generate the entire moduli space \mathcal{N}_4 .

    The physical picture

    Two open strings come in, fuse into one intermediate string, and that string later splits into two outgoing strings. In light-cone gauge this is a strip of constant total width with two horizontal slits: the first slit closes (the join) at worldsheet time T2T_2T2​, the second opens (the split) at worldsheet time T1T_1T1​. (Throughout, TTT and T1,T2T_1,T_2T1​,T2​ denote worldsheet interaction time — values of τ=ℜw\tau=\Re wτ=ℜw, not temperature.) The only knob you can turn — once the momenta are fixed — is how long the intermediate string lives, i.e. the gap T=T1−T2T=T_1-T_2T=T1​−T2​.

    That single physical knob TTT is a disguised coordinate on the space of abstract worldsheet shapes. Conformal invariance says the surface is a disk (equivalently H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H) with four marked boundary points, and its one shape parameter is where the fourth point sits, λ∈(0,1)\lambda\in(0,1)λ∈(0,1) (see Moduli spaces). We will show T↦λT\mapsto\lambdaT↦λ is onto: stretching the intermediate string from nothing to forever draws out every inequivalent four-punctured disk exactly once. This is why the scattering amplitude is an integral over λ\lambdaλ rather than a coupling at a vertex — there is no vertex, only a one-parameter family of shapes to sum over.

    For intuition, think of the intermediate string as a rubber tube joining two Y-junctions. You cannot change the pipe widths (those are the fixed momenta), but you can slide the junctions apart or together. The single number "junction separation" is the whole moduli space, and λ\lambdaλ is a relabeling of it.

    The diagram and its one parameter

    Strings 3 and 4 join at the interaction point Q2Q_2Q2​ (at worldsheet time τ=T2\tau=T_2τ=T2​); the intermediate string splits at Q1Q_1Q1​ (at τ=T1\tau=T_1τ=T1​) into outgoing strings 1 and 2. Each external string iii occupies a horizontal sub-strip of width 2πα′pi+2\pi\alpha' p_i^+2πα′pi+​, fixed by its light-cone momentum, and total width is conserved. With generic momenta the only free shape parameter is the time between interactions, T = T_1 - T_2, \qquad -\infty < T < \infty. \tag{1} T<0T<0T<0 simply means the second slit has slid to before the first — the surfaces are still legitimate, so the natural range is the whole real line. (The lone exception is equal momenta p2+=p3+p_2^+=p_3^+p2+​=p3+​: then the two slits would have to collide at T=0T=0T=0, and TTT is restricted to [0,∞)[0,\infty)[0,∞); that special case reaches the same λ\lambdaλ-interval and we set it aside.)

    Claim to prove: this physical TTT is a coordinate on the moduli space N4\mathcal{N}_4N4​ of H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H with four ordered boundary punctures, and it covers all of it. The plan has three moves:

    1. Spend the SL(2,R)SL(2,\mathbb{R})SL(2,R) freedom of H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H to pin three punctures at 0,1,∞0,1,\infty0,1,∞.
    2. The fourth puncture's position λ∈(0,1)\lambda\in(0,1)λ∈(0,1) is the one surviving invariant — the modulus.
    3. Show the light-cone gap TTT drives λ\lambdaλ from one end of (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) to the other.

    Mapping the strip to the upper half-plane

    A light-cone diagram is a degenerate polygon: the boundary turns by +π+\pi+π (a 180∘180^\circ180∘ hairpin) at each external string-end PiP_iPi​, and by −π-\pi−π at each interaction corner QjQ_jQj​. The Schwarz–Christoffel map sends H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H to such a polygon via \frac{dw}{dz} = A\,(z-x_1)^{-\alpha_1/\pi}(z-x_2)^{-\alpha_2/\pi}\cdots, \tag{2} where xix_ixi​ are the real-axis preimages of the corners and AAA is a constant. Each factor injects one turning angle: taking arg⁡\argarg of both sides, arg⁡(dw)\arg(dw)arg(dw) jumps by αi\alpha_iαi​ as zzz crosses xix_ixi​ along the real axis, because arg⁡(z−xi)\arg(z-x_i)arg(z−xi​) swings by −π-\pi−π there. The corner sent to z=∞z=\inftyz=∞ needs no factor.

    Read off the boundary order of the four-string diagram: (P1 Q1 P2 P3 Q2 P4)(P_1\,Q_1\,P_2\,P_3\,Q_2\,P_4)(P1​Q1​P2​P3​Q2​P4​). Use SL(2,R)SL(2,\mathbb{R})SL(2,R) to send P1→0P_1\to0P1​→0, P3→1P_3\to1P3​→1, P4→∞P_4\to\inftyP4​→∞. Then P2→λP_2\to\lambdaP2​→λ is the unprescribable modulus, and the interaction images Q1→x1Q_1\to x_1Q1​→x1​, Q2→x2Q_2\to x_2Q2​→x2​ are squeezed between their neighbors: 0 < x_1 < \lambda, \qquad 1 < x_2 < \infty. \tag{3} With exponent −1-1−1 at the PPP's (angle +π+\pi+π) and +1+1+1 at the QQQ's (angle −π-\pi−π), and P4P_4P4​ at ∞\infty∞ carrying no factor, the map equation is \frac{dw}{dz} = A\,\frac{1}{z}\,(z-x_1)\,\frac{1}{z-\lambda}\,\frac{1}{z-1}\,(z-x_2). \tag{4} The three reciprocal factors are the string-ends P1,P2,P3P_1,P_2,P_3P1​,P2​,P3​; the two numerator factors are the interaction corners Q1,Q2Q_1,Q_2Q1​,Q2​.

    Writing the map directly — and why the coefficients are the momenta

    The same map has a second, more physical form, and deriving it (rather than asserting it) fixes every coefficient. Near an external string-end the worldsheet is a semi-infinite strip of width 2πα′pi+2\pi\alpha' p_i^+2πα′pi+​. The single-string map (a free open string is the strip 0≤ℑw≤2πα′p+0\le\Im w\le 2\pi\alpha' p^+0≤ℑw≤2πα′p+ sent to H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H by z=exp⁡(w/(2α′p+))z=\exp(w/(2\alpha' p^+))z=exp(w/(2α′p+)); see Riemann surfaces replace Feynman graphs) inverts to w=2α′p+ln⁡zw = 2\alpha' p^+\ln zw=2α′p+lnz near the puncture. So each finite external puncture contributes one logarithm, w(z) = \sum_i (\pm)\,2\alpha' p_i^+\,\ln(z-x_i) + \text{const}, \tag{5} with coefficient fixed by the strip width and sign fixed by whether the string is incoming or outgoing. Concretely, with P1,P2,P3P_1,P_2,P_3P1​,P2​,P3​ at 0,λ,10,\lambda,10,λ,1: w(z) = -2\alpha' p_1^+ \ln z \;-\; 2\alpha' p_2^+ \ln(z-\lambda) \;+\; 2\alpha' p_3^+\ln(z-1). \tag{6}

    Two checks pin down (6) completely.

    The width check. ℑw=σ\Im w=\sigmaℑw=σ is the string coordinate. As zzz crosses a real point xix_ixi​, arg⁡(z−xi)\arg(z-x_i)arg(z−xi​) swings by π\piπ in magnitude (with the orientation that opens the strip upward), so ℑw\Im wℑw jumps by 2πα′pi+2\pi\alpha' p_i^+2πα′pi+​ — exactly the strip width of string iii. The logarithm's coefficient is the momentum.

    The sign / conservation check. ℜw=τ\Re w=\tauℜw=τ is worldsheet time. Outgoing strings live at τ→+∞\tau\to+\inftyτ→+∞, incoming at τ→−∞\tau\to-\inftyτ→−∞. Near P1P_1P1​ (z→0z\to0z→0): ℜw=−2α′p1+ln⁡∣z∣→+∞\Re w=-2\alpha' p_1^+\ln|z|\to+\inftyℜw=−2α′p1+​ln∣z∣→+∞, so P1P_1P1​ is outgoing — string 1, correct. Near P3P_3P3​ (z→1z\to1z→1): ℜw=+2α′p3+ln⁡∣z−1∣→−∞\Re w=+2\alpha' p_3^+\ln|z-1|\to-\inftyℜw=+2α′p3+​ln∣z−1∣→−∞, so P3P_3P3​ is incoming — string 3, correct. Now the puncture with no explicit log: as z→∞z\to\inftyz→∞, w≈−2α′(p1++p2+−p3+)ln⁡zw\approx -2\alpha'(p_1^++p_2^+-p_3^+)\ln zw≈−2α′(p1+​+p2+​−p3+​)lnz. Light-cone conservation p1++p2+=p3++p4+p_1^++p_2^+=p_3^++p_4^+p1+​+p2+​=p3+​+p4+​ makes the coefficient exactly −2α′p4+-2\alpha' p_4^+−2α′p4+​, so P4P_4P4​ is incoming with the right width. The single omitted logarithm is not a gap — it is forced by momentum conservation.

    Differentiating (6) gives poles with residues −2α′p1+, −2α′p2+, +2α′p3+-2\alpha' p_1^+,\,-2\alpha' p_2^+,\,+2\alpha' p_3^+−2α′p1+​,−2α′p2+​,+2α′p3+​; matching them to the partial-fraction expansion of (4) yields three relations among A,λ,x1,x2A,\lambda,x_1,x_2A,λ,x1​,x2​ and the momenta:

    -2\alpha' p_2^+ = A\,\frac{(\lambda-x_1)(x_2-\lambda)}{\lambda(1-\lambda)},\quad -2\alpha' p_3^+ = A\,\frac{(1-x_1)(x_2-1)}{1-\lambda}. \tag{7}$$ ## Extracting $T$ and proving $\lambda$ sweeps $(0,1)$ The interaction *times* are the real parts of $w$ at the corner images: $$T_1 = \Re\,w(x_1), \qquad T_2 = \Re\,w(x_2). \tag{8}$$ Since $\Re\ln z=\ln|z|$, eq. (6) gives the gap $T=T_1-T_2$ explicitly: $$\frac{T}{2\alpha'} = p_1^+\ln\frac{x_2}{x_1} + p_2^+\ln\!\frac{x_2-\lambda}{\lambda-x_1} + p_3^+\ln\!\frac{1-x_1}{x_2-1}. \tag{9}$$ Eliminating the overall constant $A$ from (7) gives the momentum-ratio constraints $$\frac{p_2^+}{p_1^+} = \frac{(\lambda-x_1)(x_2-\lambda)}{(1-\lambda)\,x_1 x_2}, \qquad \frac{p_2^+}{p_3^+} = \frac{(\lambda-x_1)(x_2-\lambda)}{(1-x_1)(x_2-1)\,\lambda}. \tag{10}$$ Equations (9)–(10) at fixed momenta implicitly fix $\lambda=\lambda(T)$. The system is ugly to solve in closed form, but we do not need to: the two endpoints settle everything. **End 1 — $\lambda\to0$ gives $T\to+\infty$.** By (3), $x_1\to0$ too. In (9) the singular pieces are $-p_1^+\ln x_1 - p_2^+\ln(\lambda-x_1)$ (the ratios (10) keep $x_2$ and $x_1/\lambda$ finite). Both $x_1\to0^+$ and $\lambda-x_1\to0^+$, so $$\frac{T}{2\alpha'} \simeq -p_1^+\ln x_1 - p_2^+\ln(\lambda-x_1) \;\longrightarrow\; +\infty. \tag{11}$$ Geometrically: $P_1$ and $P_2$ (strings 1 and 2) collide on the boundary, the sub-strip carrying strings 1&2 grows infinitely long, and the two slits separate infinitely far in time. **End 2 — $\lambda\to1$ gives $T\to-\infty$.** Now the ratios (10) force the regime $x_2\to1$ with $x_1$ finite (and, with the conventional labeling $p_2^+>p_3^+$, this is the consistent branch). In (9) the singular piece is $$\frac{T}{2\alpha'} \simeq (p_2^+ - p_3^+)\ln(x_2-\lambda) + \text{finite}. \tag{12}$$ With $p_2^+-p_3^+>0$ and $x_2-\lambda\to0^+$, this drives $T\to-\infty$. Geometrically: $P_2$ and $P_3$ (strings 2 and 3) collide instead, and the *other* sub-strip grows long — the slits have slid past each other. Since $\lambda(T)$ is continuous and reaches both endpoints, it hits **every** value in between: $$\boxed{\;T\in(-\infty,\infty)\ \longrightarrow\ \lambda\in(0,1)\ \text{(all of it)}\;} \tag{13}$$ That surjectivity is the essential content: **the light-cone string diagrams generate the entire moduli space $\mathcal{N}_4$.** (One can further check that $\lambda(T)$ is monotone, so each surface is hit exactly once and no shape is double-counted; the endpoint analysis already gives full coverage.) The two ends are the two **factorization channels**. When $\lambda\to0$ the disk pinches so strings 1&2 flow through a long thin neck — an intermediate string in the "$s$-channel". When $\lambda\to1$ it pinches the other way — the "$t$-channel". The single interval $\lambda\in(0,1)$ remembers *both* ways the process can nearly become two free strings joined by a long propagator. This is the seed of the duality that makes the [[The Veneziano amplitude|Veneziano amplitude]] famous. ## From one shape parameter to the amplitude integral Four-string tree scattering is **not** a coupling times a vertex, because the worldsheet has no vertex — locally every patch looks like a free string (see [[Riemann surfaces replace Feynman graphs]]). What replaces the vertex is a one-parameter family of shapes: 1. The physical interaction has a continuous shape knob $T$ (the intermediate string's lifetime). 2. Conformally inequivalent worldsheets are labeled by $\lambda\in(0,1)$, and $T$ covers the whole interval. 3. Each inequivalent surface is one "path" contributing to the amplitude, so *summing all contributions = integrating $\lambda$ over $(0,1)$*. That integral is the [[The Veneziano amplitude|Veneziano integral]] $\int_0^1 d\lambda\,\lambda^{2\alpha' p_1\cdot p_2}(1-\lambda)^{2\alpha' p_2\cdot p_3}$. A $\phi^3$ vertex in field theory is one number at one point. A string "vertex" is smeared across a whole one-dimensional family of worldsheets, and conformal invariance *forces* you to integrate the modulus over that family. The general lesson, of which this is the cleanest example: fix the topology, vary the diagram's natural parameters, and you sweep out exactly the inequivalent Riemann surfaces of that type. **String interactions generate moduli spaces.** ## Open questions - Monotonicity of $\lambda(T)$ (so that the map is one-to-one, not just onto) is stated but not proved here; the endpoint argument establishes surjectivity. A clean proof belongs in a later light-cone string-field-theory / moduli-cell note. - Closed-form $\lambda(T)$ is not extracted — the system (9)–(10) is analyzed only at its two ends, which is all the coverage claim needs. An explicit $\lambda(T)$ (or its equal-momentum special case, where $T\in[0,\infty)$ already fills $(0,1)$) would be a nice numerical exercise. ## Exercise Take the omitted fourth puncture seriously.
