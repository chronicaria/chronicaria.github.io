    The picture

    The string is a wire carrying a current, not a rod smeared with static charge. The current is charge under the closed-string 19.1 Kalb-Ramond field BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​, and it flows along the string. A steady current in a wire has no loose ends: it loops back on itself or runs to infinity. Cut the wire in mid-air and the current stops, which is impossible.

    So what happens when an open string ends on a D-brane, its endpoint stopping in the middle of the brane? The current must go somewhere. The module builds to the answer: the endpoint becomes an electric point charge, and the missing current continues as electric field lines on the brane. The string is the wire; the brane completes the circuit. A current can't stop, so the string's endpoint becomes a charge and hands its current off to the brane.

    The spine: conservation ⇔\Leftrightarrow⇔ gauge invariance

    A conserved current and a gauge-invariant coupling are the same statement. Couple a current jjj to a gauge field, integrate by parts, and the variation collapses to δS  =  − ⁣∫(gauge parameter) (∂⋅j)  +  (boundary term).\delta S \;=\; -\!\int (\text{gauge parameter})\,(\partial\cdot j) \;+\; (\text{boundary term}).δS=−∫(gauge parameter)(∂⋅j)+(boundary term). For an arbitrary gauge parameter this vanishes iff ∂⋅j=0\partial\cdot j = 0∂⋅j=0 in the interior and the boundary term vanishes. Gauge invariance is not a force; it is the compatibility condition that forbids an unbalanced source.

    Every result in this module comes from watching that boundary term. For a closed string there is no boundary and everything is conserved. For an open string the boundary is in σ\sigmaσ — the two endpoints — and the boundary term survives. That surviving term is the entire physics: it is non-conservation made visible, and cancelling it is what forces the endpoint charge. This is the same "boundary terms carry the physics" theme as Boundary terms and allowed variations, now with the boundary at the string's ends.

    Why this module is the hinge of Part IV

    Earlier modules quantized strings and found the spectrum. Here we learn what strings and branes carry. Three payoffs:

    1. Strings are the natural charged 1-dimensional objects. A point particle (0-dimensional) couples to the 1-index field AμA_\muAμ​; a string (1-dimensional) couples to the 2-index BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​; a Dppp-brane (ppp-dimensional) to a (p+1)(p{+}1)(p+1)-index tensor. The rule "a ppp-dimensional object couples to a (p+1)(p{+}1)(p+1)-index gauge field" organizes the whole subject — an nnn-dimensional worldvolume sweeps an oriented nnn-form, and the natural thing to integrate it against is an nnn-index antisymmetric field.
    2. D-branes carry worldvolume fields, not just boundary conditions. The Maxwell field living on a brane and the charge on its open-string endpoints are forced by gauge invariance, not postulated.
    3. It seeds all of D-brane physics. Branes carrying a conserved charge are stable (nothing lighter can carry their charge away), and stacks of coincident branes give non-abelian gauge groups — the door to particle physics. The endpoint-as-charge story is where that begins; the full superstring and Ramond–Ramond machinery is previewed and deferred.

    The module in seven notes

    Read them roughly in this order; each link says what it delivers.

    1. 19.1 Kalb-Ramond field — the two-form gauge field BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​, its field strength H=dBH=dBH=dB, and why a string (not a particle) is its natural source.
    2. 19.2 Fundamental string charge — derive the conserved current jμνj^{\mu\nu}jμν from the BBB-coupling; show its charge density is a vector j⃗ 0\vec j^{\,0}j​0 pointing along the string, with ∇⋅j⃗ 0=0\nabla\cdot\vec j^{\,0}=0∇⋅j​0=0.
    3. 19.3 Visualizing string charge — that current cannot stop, so strings close or run to infinity; measure it by linking (an S1S^1S1 around the string) rather than enclosing, and count strings with the string number N\mathcal NN.
    4. 19.4 Endpoint charge — the core. Vary SBS_BSB​ for an open string; the leftover σ\sigmaσ-boundary term shows gauge invariance fails at the endpoints; the cure makes each endpoint a point charge q=±1q=\pm1q=±1.
    5. 19.5 Maxwell fields on branes — the payoff. The joint transformation δAm=−Λm\delta A_m=-\Lambda_mδAm​=−Λm​ forces FFF and BBB into the invariant F=F+B\mathcal F = F+BF=F+B; the brane's electric field lines carry the string charge.
    6. 19.6 D-brane charge as a preview — the pattern one rung up: Dppp-branes couple to (p+1)(p{+}1)(p+1)-index (Ramond–Ramond) tensors; charged branes are stable; a wrapped brane has charge Q=Vp/ℓs pQ=V_p/\ell_s^{\,p}Q=Vp​/ℓsp​.
    7. 19.7 D-branes and the Standard Model — optional orientation: stacks of NNN branes give U(N)U(N)U(N) gauge fields, and strings between stacks give bifundamental matter.

    Work products distilling the core: 19.8 How an endpoint becomes a source (the one-page essay) and 19.9 Charge flow — open string ending on a brane (the diagram).

    Recurring concepts linked outward: Boundary terms and allowed variations, Neumann versus Dirichlet conditions, Chan-Paton factors, Born-Infeld action, Yang-Mills from D-branes.

    Open questions

    • 19.7 D-branes and the Standard Model is only orientation; a real model needs compactification, chirality, anomaly cancellation, orientifolds, and supersymmetry-breaking input.
    • The Ramond–Ramond / Wess–Zumino coupling is previewed only at leading order in 19.6 D-brane charge as a preview; a later superstring module should derive the R–R spectrum, charge quantization, and the full Wess–Zumino action.
    • Absolute normalizations — the physical strength of the endpoint charge and the brane gauge dynamics — are deferred to the Born-Infeld action and the string-coupling discussion.

    Exercise

    Close the notes. On a blank page, explain — in words and equations — how an open-string endpoint can behave like an electric charge. A complete answer must hit all four links of the chain:

    1. Why a string carries a conserved vector charge density that flows like a current and cannot just stop (state ∇⋅j⃗ 0=0\nabla\cdot\vec j^{\,0}=0∇⋅j​0=0 and say why it holds).
    2. Vary the BBB-coupling SBS_BSB​ for an open string and exhibit the leftover boundary term δSB=∫dτ Λm ∂τXm∣σ=0σ=π≠0\delta S_B=\int d\tau\,\Lambda_m\,\partial_\tau X^m\big|_{\sigma=0}^{\sigma=\pi}\neq 0δSB​=∫dτΛm​∂τ​Xm​σ=0σ=π​=0 — gauge invariance fails at the endpoints.
    3. State the cure: add ± ⁣∫Am dXm\pm\!\int A_m\,dX^m±∫Am​dXm at each endpoint (q=±1q=\pm1q=±1), with the joint rule δAm=−Λm\delta A_m=-\Lambda_mδAm​=−Λm​, so δS=0\delta S=0δS=0. The endpoint is now a point charge.
    4. Say where the charge goes: on the brane F0k=EkF^{0k}=E_kF0k=Ek​ couples to B0kB_{0k}B0k​, so electric field lines carry the string charge through the worldvolume.

    If you can produce that chain cold — current can't stop →\to→ gauge term survives at the endpoint →\to→ add an endpoint charge →\to→ brane flux continues the current — you have the module.

    Solution

    1 — The current can't stop. The string couples to BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​ through SB=−12∫dτ dσ ϵαβ∂αXμ∂βXνBμνS_B=-\tfrac12\int d\tau\,d\sigma\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha X^\mu\partial_\beta X^\nu B_{\mu\nu}SB​=−21​∫dτdσϵαβ∂α​Xμ∂β​XνBμν​. Reading this as a spacetime coupling −∫dDx Bμνjμν-\int d^Dx\,B_{\mu\nu}j^{\mu\nu}−∫dDxBμν​jμν defines the string current jμνj^{\mu\nu}jμν, which is antisymmetric (j00=0j^{00}=0j00=0). Varying the BBB kinetic term ∝H2\propto H^2∝H2 gives the field equation 1κ2∂ρHμνρ=jμν\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2}\partial_\rho H^{\mu\nu\rho}=j^{\mu\nu}κ21​∂ρ​Hμνρ=jμν; because HHH is totally antisymmetric and partials commute, ∂μjμν=0\partial_\mu j^{\mu\nu}=0∂μ​jμν=0. Taking ν=0\nu=0ν=0 and using j00=0j^{00}=0j00=0 leaves ∂kj0k=0\partial_k j^{0k}=0∂k​j0k=0, i.e. ∇⋅j⃗ 0=0\nabla\cdot\vec j^{\,0}=0∇⋅j​0=0. The charge density is the vector j⃗ 0\vec j^{\,0}j​0, and (in static gauge) it points along X⃗′\vec X'X′, the tangent to the string. A divergenceless current has no loose ends — like current in a wire, it must loop or run to infinity.

    2 — Gauge invariance fails at the ends. Under δBμν=∂μΛν−∂νΛμ\delta B_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\Lambda_\nu-\partial_\nu\Lambda_\muδBμν​=∂μ​Λν​−∂ν​Λμ​, the two antisymmetric terms double up and δSB\delta S_BδSB​ reduces to two total worldsheet derivatives, − ⁣∫dτ dσ[∂τ(Λν∂σXν)−∂σ(Λν∂τXν)]-\!\int d\tau\,d\sigma\big[\partial_\tau(\Lambda_\nu\partial_\sigma X^\nu)-\partial_\sigma(\Lambda_\nu\partial_\tau X^\nu)\big]−∫dτdσ[∂τ​(Λν​∂σ​Xν)−∂σ​(Λν​∂τ​Xν)]. The ∂τ\partial_\tau∂τ​ piece dies (Λ→0\Lambda\to0Λ→0 at the ends of time). The ∂σ\partial_\sigma∂σ​ piece is a boundary term at σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π. Splitting Xμ=(Xm,Xa)X^\mu=(X^m,X^a)Xμ=(Xm,Xa) into Neumann (along-brane) and Dirichlet (normal), the Dirichlet condition ∂τXa=0\partial_\tau X^a=0∂τ​Xa=0 at the endpoints kills the normal part, leaving δSB=∫dτ Λm ∂τXm∣σ=π−∫dτ Λm ∂τXm∣σ=0≠0.\delta S_B=\int d\tau\,\Lambda_m\,\partial_\tau X^m\Big|_{\sigma=\pi}-\int d\tau\,\Lambda_m\,\partial_\tau X^m\Big|_{\sigma=0}\neq0.δSB​=∫dτΛm​∂τ​Xm​σ=π​−∫dτΛm​∂τ​Xm​σ=0​=0. By conservation ⇔\Leftrightarrow⇔ gauge invariance, this surviving term is the statement that the standalone string current is not conserved at the endpoints.

    3 — The cure forces a point charge. Add an endpoint coupling to the brane Maxwell field, S=SB+∫dτ Am∂τXm∣σ=π−∫dτ Am∂τXm∣σ=0S=S_B+\int d\tau\,A_m\partial_\tau X^m\big|_{\sigma=\pi}-\int d\tau\,A_m\partial_\tau X^m\big|_{\sigma=0}S=SB​+∫dτAm​∂τ​Xm​σ=π​−∫dτAm​∂τ​Xm​σ=0​. Each term is the point-particle coupling q ⁣∫Aμdxμq\!\int A_\mu dx^\muq∫Aμ​dxμ with q=±1q=\pm1q=±1 (−1-1−1 at the tail σ=0\sigma=0σ=0, +1+1+1 at the head σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π). Now let BBB and AAA transform together: δAm=−Λm\delta A_m=-\Lambda_mδAm​=−Λm​. Then the added terms vary by − ⁣∫Λm∂τXm-\!\int\Lambda_m\partial_\tau X^m−∫Λm​∂τ​Xm at each end, with matching signs, cancelling δSB\delta S_BδSB​ term by term. So δS=0\delta S=0δS=0: consistency forced the endpoint to be an electric charge.

    4 — The charge continues on the brane. The same joint rule makes δFmn=−δBmn\delta F_{mn}=-\delta B_{mn}δFmn​=−δBmn​, so only Fmn=Fmn+Bmn\mathcal F_{mn}=F_{mn}+B_{mn}Fmn​=Fmn​+Bmn​ is gauge invariant on the brane. Expanding the brane Lagrangian −14FmnFmn-\tfrac14\mathcal F^{mn}\mathcal F_{mn}−41​FmnFmn​ produces the cross term −12FmnBmn=−F0kB0k+⋯-\tfrac12 F^{mn}B_{mn}=-F^{0k}B_{0k}+\cdots−21​FmnBmn​=−F0kB0k​+⋯. Since j0kj^{0k}j0k is by definition whatever couples to B0kB_{0k}B0k​, and F0k=EkF^{0k}=E_kF0k=Ek​ is the electric field, the brane's electric field lines carry the string charge: flux spreads out from the +++ endpoint and converges into the −-− endpoint, confined to the worldvolume. The current never stopped — it changed channels.

    The chain: current can't stop →\to→ gauge term survives at the endpoint →\to→ endpoint becomes a charge q=±1q=\pm1q=±1 →\to→ brane electric flux carries the current onward. Endpoint === source.
