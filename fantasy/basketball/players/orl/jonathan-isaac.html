2025-26 baseline

Isaac appeared in 52 games at 10.0 minutes, averaging 2.6 points, 2.5 rebounds, 0.4 assists, 0.4 steals, 0.6 blocks, and 0.3 turnovers. He shot .422 from the field and .603 from the line. A left-knee sprain contributed to late-season and playoff absences.

Orlando waived Isaac on June 27, then re-signed him July 1 on a smaller free-agent deal. The sequence preserved the relationship while resetting expectations; it does not support carrying forward an old contract-driven role.

Role and pathway

Isaac is a defensive specialist at either frontcourt spot. His healthy base is only six minutes because Banchero, Wagner, da Silva, Carter, Vucevic, and Bitadze all have rotation claims. He can be a matchup tool, a planned low-minute participant, or inactive.

The upside remains familiar: in any stretch near 18 minutes, steals and blocks can become category-relevant immediately. The problem is that neither health nor role supports drafting as if those minutes are assured.

Category shape

  • Elite per minute: blocks and steals.
  • Helpful: rebounds and low turnovers.
  • Weak: FT percentage, scoring, assists, threes.
  • Volume issue: six minutes is not enough for even elite event rates to matter.

Injury and role risk

Isaac's history makes a single GP estimate less informative than the range. The base treats him as both a low-minute player and an availability risk. Managers should require evidence of consecutive unrestricted appearances and a stable rotation assignment before adding.

Durability: 328 games across what should have been nine seasons. A right ankle sprain cut the rookie year to 27 games. He was playing the best basketball of his career in 2019-20 — 11.9 and 6.8 with 2.3 blocks — when a left knee posterior lateral corner injury in January and then a torn left ACL in the Bubble on August 2, 2020 cost him the next two full seasons. He returned in 2022-23 for 11 games before a torn left adductor ended that season in surgery, with a hamstring problem during rehab before it. The healthiest stretch came in 2023-24 and 2024-25, 58 and 71 games, but at 15-16 minutes. A left knee sprain contributed to late-season and playoff absences in 2025-26.

Trajectory: The per-minute defensive profile is still real — 1.2 blocks and 0.9 steals per game for a career at 18.8 minutes is elite event production for a forward — but 2025-26 was the year the role finally collapsed rather than the health. Ten minutes a night, 2.6 points, a three-point shot that has gone .258 and .184 in the last two seasons, and 60.3% from the line. Orlando waived him on June 27, 2026, off a five-year, $84 million extension and brought him back on July 1 at roughly $3.5 million for one year, which prices him as a matchup piece rather than a rotation player. Ahead of him are Paolo Banchero, Franz Wagner, Tristan da Silva, Wendell Carter Jr, Nikola Vucevic and Goga Bitadze. He is a watch-list stocks specialist: he becomes ownable only if two Orlando frontcourt players are out simultaneously and he gets a 18-to-20-minute defensive assignment for a multi-week stretch. Do not draft on the per-minute rates alone.
