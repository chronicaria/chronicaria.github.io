The role

He is an alpha X: his team's clear first option at receiver, lined up wide, thrown to regardless of who is covering him. That matters because a receiver only scores when the ball comes his way, and targets — throws in his direction, counted whether or not he catches them — are the most stable input in fantasy football (Fantasy football glossary). Kansas City also carries a three-down bell cow at running back, so Rice is not the only mouth in the offense, but he is the only alpha receiver in it.

The number

96 catches, from the note column of Data/players.csv (the projected stat line behind his 249.7 proj_points). This is a projection dated 2026-07-23, not an observation — nothing has been played. In full PPR every reception pays a full point before a yard is gained, so 96 catches is 96 points banked, a little under six a game across a 17-game season. That is the whole case: the floor is made of volume, not of touchdowns.

The risk

The CSV flags him low risk, which is honest — the archetype is durable and the projection is not leaning on scoring luck. The real exposure is timing: his ADP is 26.9, a Fantasy Football Calculator PPR proxy rather than ESPN's own number, which puts him a hair past your pick at 24. He may simply not be there. That flag prices the archetype, not the man: he has played 28 of a possible 51 games since 2023, and the board's availability model — avail 0.624, a model and not an observation, the lowest of anyone at this turn — drops him roughly seventy places once the projection is scaled for it (Availability and injury risk).

The context

He is 26 on the September 9 opener, and Kansas City spent the 55th pick on him in 2023 — second-round capital, not the top-ten kind that hands a man the job (Draft capital dominance). What he has not done is play: 16 games, then 4, then 8 — 28 of a possible 51. The official injury report listed him Out for two of those weeks, a hamstring and a concussion; the other 21 missed games never appeared on it at all, so those absences were not medical. In 2025 he took 74.9 percent of Kansas City's plays (his snap share, Fantasy football glossary) and 78 targets in eight games, close to ten a game. The rate supports 96 catches; the games do not. He is on a rookie deal, about $1.6 million a year, and a 2027 free agent.
