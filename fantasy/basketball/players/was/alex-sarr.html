Role and baseline

  • 2025-26: 48 games, 27.2 MPG for Washington.
  • Health: broke his right foot during an offseason workout and underwent surgery June 15. The official report expected a full recovery before the season, but an early limitation branch remains necessary.
  • 2026-27 base role: starting center; 28 base minutes split 20 at centre and 8 at power forward, the four minutes coming when Davis slides to the five. Davis and Ayton cap pure workload but can also keep Sarr away from bruising full-time center assignments. His rebounding falls in the minutes he shares with Davis, which is the main cost of the split. An Ayton trade is the cleanest single path to 32-plus minutes.

Fantasy dossier

  • 9-cat: top 50-70 base. Two-plus blocks with positive rebounds and some threes are scarce; FT% and post-foot-surgery volume are the primary drags.
  • Points: top 60-80 if the scoring growth holds, with less format leverage from blocks than in category leagues.
  • Dynasty: top 25-35 center asset. His youth, defensive ceiling and skill growth matter more than the immediate veteran logjam.
  • Category fit: strongest in punt-FT% or balanced builds that can absorb moderate free-throw damage.

Floor, median and ceiling

  • Floor: recovery lingers, Ayton takes more center minutes and Sarr finishes outside the top 100.
  • Median: 57 games, 28 minutes and top-60-ish value.
  • Ceiling: full health, 30-plus minutes, 2.5 blocks and a top-35 finish.

Career record

Pre-NBA: no college. Born in Bordeaux on April 26, 2005 and raised in Toulouse, Sarr joined Real Madrid's youth program in 2019 for two seasons, then moved to Atlanta in 2021 for two seasons in Overtime Elite — first with Team Overtime, then YNG Dreamerz — earning second-team All-OTE in 2022-23. He signed with the Perth Wildcats under the NBL's Next Stars program on May 9, 2023, and in 2023-24 averaged 9.4 points, 4.3 rebounds, 0.9 assists and 1.5 blocks in about 17 minutes across 27 games, missing four weeks with a hip strain in December while Perth reached the semifinals; some sources list the sample as 30 games at 9.6/4.5/1.0/1.5, so treat the game count as approximate. Internationally he took silver at the 2023 U19 World Cup and bronze at the 2022 U17 World Cup, and debuted for senior France on August 5, 2025 with 20 points against Montenegro before an ankle injury ended his EuroBasket 2025. Draft: 2024, first round, 2nd overall, Washington — part of a historic French top ten alongside Zaccharie Risacher (1st) and Tidjane Salaün (6th).

All 67 rookie appearances and all 48 sophomore appearances were starts. He won an Eastern Conference Rookie of the Month award and made the NBA All-Rookie First Team in 2025.

Fantasy history: neither season has a verified season-end category rank and I will not estimate one. The shape is clear enough without it. 2024-25 was close to unrosterable in nine-category formats despite 1.5 blocks and 1.6 threes, because .394 from the field on that volume is among the most damaging percentage lines a starter can post, and .679 free throws compounded it. 2025-26 flipped the FG% from a severe negative to a mild positive and pushed blocks to 2.0, which is the profile of a genuinely useful category center — but 48 games caps the totals value regardless of the per-game quality. Points leagues rate him more kindly in 2024-25 than category leagues did (the threes and blocks scored without the percentage penalty) and slightly less kindly in 2025-26, since the shot-diet change traded three-point volume for twos. Free throw percentage remains the standing drag and makes him a natural fit in punt-FT builds.

Durability: 67, 48. The rookie year was fine. 2025-26 was not: he dealt with a right adductor strain earlier in the year, then left big toe capsulitis and inflammation that cost him 23 of Washington's final 30 games — he last played March 27 and was formally shut down. He then broke his right foot in an offseason workout and had surgery on June 15, 2026, with the official report expecting full recovery before the season. That is three separate lower-body problems in twelve months on a 7-foot, 205-pound frame, plus the ankle injury that ended his EuroBasket in August 2025 and the hip strain that cost him four NBL weeks in December 2023. The pattern is not one recurring injury but a thin frame accumulating unrelated soft-tissue and bone events, which is the harder version to project away.

Trajectory: The second-year jump is the most encouraging kind because minutes did not move at all — 27.1 to 27.2 — so every gain is per-minute. Scoring rose 25 percent, rebounds and assists both climbed, and blocks went from 1.5 to 2.0 at identical playing time. The mechanism is visible in the shot diet: three-point makes fell from 1.6 to 1.0 while field goal percentage jumped 88 points from .394 to .482, meaning he stopped hunting the low-value perimeter looks that defined his rookie year and started finishing inside. That is a coaching-and-maturity outcome, not a fluke, and it is the single most important fact in his profile. What has not improved is the free throw line, .679 to .692, which caps how much of the scoring growth converts to category value. He turns 21 in April and is nowhere near his physical ceiling; a 205-pound seven-footer who adds strength typically gains both durability and finishing. For 2026-27 the production case is strong and the availability case is weak: the foot surgery timeline, the veteran logjam with Anthony Davis and Deandre Ayton, and a two-year games-played trend heading the wrong direction all argue for pricing him at the per-game value minus a real games discount rather than at the ceiling.
