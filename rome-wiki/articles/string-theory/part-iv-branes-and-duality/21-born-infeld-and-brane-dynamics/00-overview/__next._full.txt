<h2 id="why-this-is-the-payoff-of-the-t-duality-chapter">Why this is the payoff of the T-duality chapter</h2>
<p>In <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-iv-branes-and-duality/19-string-charge-and-gauge-fields/endpoint-charge/" class="wiki-link">module 19</a> we quantized open strings on a D-brane and found a massless <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>U</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mn>1</mn><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">U(1)</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.109em;">U</span><span class="mopen">(</span><span class="mord">1</span><span class="mclose">)</span></span></span></span> vector — a photon on the world-volume. But that was a <em>low-energy</em> accident. Open strings are extended objects whose charged endpoints carry tension, and at strong field the stringy nature of the charge asserts itself.</p>
