    Virasoro algebra and central charge

    The modes L_n of the worldsheet stress tensor obey the Virasoro algebra [L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\tfrac{c}{12}m(m^2-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0} : the classical symmetry algebra of conformal transformations, plus one purely quantum correction whose size c — the central charge — counts the theory's degrees of freedom and must total zero for the string to be consistent.

    The anomaly in one number

    The conformal transformations of the worldsheet form a symmetry, and every symmetry has generators with an algebra. Classically that algebra is rigid and well known (the Witt algebra). Quantizing adds exactly one new term — a c-number that no rearrangement of operators can remove. That term is an anomaly: a classical symmetry that the quantum theory cannot quite keep. Its strength is a single number ccc, and the whole drama of the bosonic string — why 26 dimensions? — is the demand that this anomaly cancel.

    One way to picture ccc: it is the "zero-point noise" the vacuum makes when you try to run a conformal transformation on it. Empty space has more structure quantum-mechanically than classically, and ccc measures how much. You cannot make it go away in any single sector; you can only arrange for the sectors to cancel.

    From constraints to operators

    The classical Virasoro constraints say the Worldsheet stress tensor vanishes, T(z)=0T(z)=0T(z)=0. Since T(z)T(z)T(z) is holomorphic it has a Laurent expansion, and its coefficients are the operators we care about: T(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\frac{L_n}{z^{n+2}},\qquad L_n=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dz\,z^{n+1}\,T(z).\tag{1} The contour integral just extracts a Laurent coefficient. Physically, LnL_nLn​ is the conserved charge generating the conformal transformation δz=zn+1\delta z=z^{n+1}δz=zn+1 — its Noether current is zn+1T(z)z^{n+1}T(z)zn+1T(z), and the contour is a spatial slice. So L−1L_{-1}L−1​ generates translations, L0L_0L0​ generates scaling (it is the energy/dilatation operator), and L1L_1L1​ generates special conformal maps.

    For DDD free bosons — the string's embedding coordinates — the same LnL_nLn​ can be written directly in oscillators: L_n=\frac12\sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_{n-p}\cdot\alpha_p,\qquad [\alpha^\mu_m,\alpha^\nu_n]=m\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\delta_{m+n,0}.\tag{2} The dot sums over all spacetime directions μ=0,…,D−1\mu=0,\dots,D-1μ=0,…,D−1, timelike included — these are the covariant Virasoro operators, unlike the transverse Ln⊥L_n^\perpLn⊥​ of light-cone gauge (Transverse Virasoro operators). Quantum-mechanically L0L_0L0​ needs normal ordering because α−p\alpha_{-p}α−p​ and αp\alpha_pαp​ do not commute; the other LnL_nLn​ do not.

    The classical statement "T=0T=0T=0" cannot survive as "Ln=0L_n=0Ln​=0 for all nnn" in the quantum theory — the algebra below shows why — so it weakens to Ln>0∣ψ⟩=0L_{n>0}\lvert\psi\rangle=0Ln>0​∣ψ⟩=0 on physical states (see Virasoro constraints).

    The algebra

    \boxed{\,[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)\,L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}\,m(m^2-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}\,}\tag{3}

    Piece by piece:

    • (m−n)Lm+n(m-n)L_{m+n}(m−n)Lm+n​ — the Witt algebra. This is the classical algebra of conformal transformations, and it is purely structural (no ℏ\hbarℏ, no ccc). You can check it directly from the vector fields ℓn=−zn+1∂z\ell_n=-z^{n+1}\partial_zℓn​=−zn+1∂z​ that generate δz=zn+1\delta z=z^{n+1}δz=zn+1: the Leibniz rule gives [ℓm,ℓn]=(m−n)ℓm+n[\ell_m,\ell_n]=(m-n)\ell_{m+n}[ℓm​,ℓn​]=(m−n)ℓm+n​. This term alone is the entire classical content.
    • c12m(m2−1) δm+n,0\frac{c}{12}m(m^2-1)\,\delta_{m+n,0}12c​m(m2−1)δm+n,0​ — the central extension. A c-number (proportional to the identity), nonzero only when m+n=0m+n=0m+n=0, and purely quantum: it arises from normal-ordering the operator product inside TTT. The constant ccc is the central charge. "Central" means it commutes with everything — it sits in the center of the algebra.

    Two consistency checks are built into the structure of (3):

    • The global subgroup is anomaly-free. The central term carries m(m2−1)m(m^2-1)m(m2−1), which vanishes at m=0,±1m=0,\pm1m=0,±1. So {L−1,L0,L1}\{L_{-1},L_0,L_1\}{L−1​,L0​,L1​} close without any anomaly. These generate the global conformal group of the sphere, SL(2,C)SL(2,\mathbb C)SL(2,C) (translation, dilation/rotation, special conformal) — the maps that are globally well-defined, one-to-one, and invertible. The anomaly is an obstruction only for the local transformations that are singular somewhere.
    • Antisymmetry. Under m↔nm\leftrightarrow nm↔n with m+n=0m+n=0m+n=0, m(m2−1)→−m(m2−1)m(m^2-1)\to -m(m^2-1)m(m2−1)→−m(m2−1), matching [Lm,Ln]=−[Ln,Lm][L_m,L_n]=-[L_n,L_m][Lm​,Ln​]=−[Ln​,Lm​].

    Where the central charge comes from

    Two routes give the same ccc. Both make the same point: the central term is the quartic short-distance singularity of TTT with itself.

    Route 1 — the oscillator commutator

    Compute [Lm,Ln][L_m,L_n][Lm​,Ln​] from (2) using [αm,αn]=m η δm+n,0[\alpha_m,\alpha_n]=m\,\eta\,\delta_{m+n,0}[αm​,αn​]=mηδm+n,0​. The double sum produces the Witt term (m−n)Lm+n(m-n)L_{m+n}(m−n)Lm+n​ plus a leftover c-number from reordering oscillators past each other. That leftover is c12m(m2−1),c=ημνημν=D.\frac{c}{12}m(m^2-1),\qquad c=\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}=D.12c​m(m2−1),c=ημν​ημν=D. The coefficient is the trace of the spacetime metric, ημνημν=D\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\mu\nu}=Dημν​ημν=D — one unit per dimension. The timelike direction contributes +1+1+1 just like the spacelike ones: it enters the central term through a pair of contractions, and a pair of η00=−1\eta^{00}=-1η00=−1 factors multiply to +1+1+1. So c=Dc=Dc=D for DDD free bosons.

    Route 2 — the TTTTTT OPE (the CFT-native computation)

    The central charge is defined as the coefficient of the quartic pole in the stress-tensor self-OPE: T(z)\,T(w)=\frac{c/2}{(z-w)^4}+\frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^2}+\frac{\partial T(w)}{z-w}+\ldots\tag{4} The (z−w)−2(z-w)^{-2}(z−w)−2 and (z−w)−1(z-w)^{-1}(z−w)−1 terms just say TTT has weight 222 (it is a dimension-2, spin-2 object). The (z−w)−4(z-w)^{-4}(z−w)−4 term is new: it is the reason TTT is not primary. For one free boson the Wick contractions give c/2=1/2c/2=1/2c/2=1/2, i.e. c=1c=1c=1 per boson (worked in Free boson OPEs), so again c=Dc=Dc=D.

    Turning (4) into (3). The two are the same fact. Write Lm=∮dz2πizm+1T(z)L_m=\oint\frac{dz}{2\pi i}z^{m+1}T(z)Lm​=∮2πidz​zm+1T(z) and Ln=∮dw2πiwn+1T(w)L_n=\oint\frac{dw}{2\pi i}w^{n+1}T(w)Ln​=∮2πidw​wn+1T(w). The commutator is a local collision: only the singular OPE terms contribute to the residue. Taylor-expand the smooth factor zm+1z^{m+1}zm+1 about www, zm+1=wm+1+(m+1)wm(z−w)+m(m+1)2wm−1(z−w)2+m(m2−1)6wm−2(z−w)3+…,z^{m+1}=w^{m+1}+(m+1)w^m(z-w)+\tfrac{m(m+1)}{2}w^{m-1}(z-w)^2+\tfrac{m(m^2-1)}{6}w^{m-2}(z-w)^3+\ldots,zm+1=wm+1+(m+1)wm(z−w)+2m(m+1)​wm−1(z−w)2+6m(m2−1)​wm−2(z−w)3+…, and pair each power of (z−w)(z-w)(z−w) against the matching pole in (4). Now do the www-integral. Integrate the first term by parts, ∮dw2πiwm+n+2∂T=−(m+n+2)∮dw2πiwm+n+1T\oint\frac{dw}{2\pi i}w^{m+n+2}\partial T=-(m+n+2)\oint\frac{dw}{2\pi i}w^{m+n+1}T∮2πidw​wm+n+2∂T=−(m+n+2)∮2πidw​wm+n+1T, and combine with the second: [−(m+n+2)+2(m+1)]∮dw2πi wm+n+1T(w)=(m−n) Lm+n.\big[-(m+n+2)+2(m+1)\big]\oint\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\,w^{m+n+1}T(w)=(m-n)\,L_{m+n}.[−(m+n+2)+2(m+1)]∮2πidw​wm+n+1T(w)=(m−n)Lm+n​. The factor m(m2−1)m(m^2-1)m(m2−1) traces straight back to the third Taylor coefficient — which is why the anomaly needs a quartic pole and vanishes for the low modes m=0,±1m=0,\pm1m=0,±1, whose cubic term is absent.

    What the central charge means

    Four faces of the same number; any two of them make a working intuition.

    1. It counts degrees of freedom. One free boson has c=1c=1c=1; DDD non-interacting bosons give c=Dc=Dc=D (the quartic poles just add). So ccc is a measure of "how much stuff" the CFT contains — though it need not be an integer. This is made sharp by Zamolodchikov's ccc-theorem: a quantity that equals ccc at conformal points decreases monotonically along renormalization-group flow, cUV≥cIRc_{\text{UV}}\ge c_{\text{IR}}cUV​≥cIR​. Integrating out high-energy modes can only lose degrees of freedom.

    2. It is the anomaly in how TTT transforms. Because of the quartic pole, TTT is not a tensor. Under a finite conformal map z→z~(z)z\to\tilde z(z)z→z~(z) it picks up an inhomogeneous Schwarzian term: \tilde T(\tilde z)=\Big(\frac{\partial\tilde z}{\partial z}\Big)^{-2}\Big[T(z)-\frac{c}{12}\,S(\tilde z,z)\Big],\qquad S=\frac{\tilde z'''}{\tilde z'}-\frac32\Big(\frac{\tilde z''}{\tilde z'}\Big)^2.\tag{5} The Schwarzian vanishes for global SL(2,C)SL(2,\mathbb C)SL(2,C) maps — matching the anomaly-free subalgebra above — but not for general conformal maps. So ccc is literally the obstruction to TTT being a good tensor: no anomaly means c=0c=0c=0 means TTT transforms cleanly.

    3. It is a physical energy — the cylinder Casimir. The Schwarzian term does not depend on TTT, so it shifts every state's energy by the same constant: a vacuum energy. Mapping the cylinder to the plane (z=e−iwz=e^{-iw}z=e−iw) has Schwarzian S=1/2S=1/2S=1/2, and the ground-state energy on the cylinder comes out E=-\frac{2\pi(c+\bar c)}{24}.\tag{6} For one boson (c=cˉ=1c=\bar c=1c=cˉ=1) this is E=−π/6E=-\pi/6E=−π/6, i.e. energy density E/(2π)=−1/12E/(2\pi)=-1/12E/(2π)=−1/12 — the familiar −1/12-1/12−1/12 of the zero-point sum, but here it appears with no divergent sum to regulate. The central charge is the finite Casimir energy of conformal symmetry.

    4. It is the Weyl (trace) anomaly. On a curved worldsheet the stress tensor is no longer traceless; instead \langle T^\alpha{}_\alpha\rangle=-\frac{c}{12}\,R,\tag{7} with RRR the worldsheet Ricci scalar (cf. Weyl invariance). Classically Tαα=0T^\alpha{}_\alpha=0Tαα​=0 is exactly the conformal symmetry; a nonzero ccc is the statement that regulating short-distance divergences leaves a curvature-dependent footprint the symmetry cannot erase.

    (A fifth face, Cardy's formula S(E)∼2πcE/6S(E)\sim 2\pi\sqrt{cE/6}S(E)∼2πcE/6​, makes ccc control the high-energy density of states — the entropy of the CFT.)

    Why c=0c=0c=0 forces D=26D=26D=26

    Faces 3 and 4 are why the string cares. On the string worldsheet, conformal (Weyl) invariance is not a bonus symmetry — it is a gauge symmetry, the leftover freedom after fixing the auxiliary metric to conformal gauge. A gauge symmetry with an anomaly is fatal: the theory would depend on the unphysical Weyl factor. So consistency demands the total central charge vanish.

    Add up the sectors of the gauge-fixed Polyakov string: ctotal=cmatter⏟= D, embedding Xμ+cgh⏟= −26, bc ghosts=D−26=0  ⟹  D=26.c_{\text{total}}=\underbrace{c_{\text{matter}}}_{=\,D,\ \text{embedding }X^\mu}+\underbrace{c_{\text{gh}}}_{=\,-26,\ bc\text{ ghosts}}=D-26=0\;\Longrightarrow\;D=26.ctotal​==D, embedding Xμcmatter​​​+=−26, bc ghostscgh​​​=D−26=0⟹D=26. The critical dimension is nothing more mystical than the no-Weyl-anomaly condition. The ghost value c=−26c=-26c=−26 comes from Faddeev–Popov quantization of the diffeomorphism gauge-fixing and is not derived here — see The bc ghost CFT and Total central charge and D = 26.

    States: primaries, descendants, the spectrum

    Hermiticity of the Hamiltonian forces Ln†=L−nL_n^\dagger=L_{-n}Ln†​=L−n​. A primary state is annihilated by every positive mode, Ln∣ψ⟩=Lˉn∣ψ⟩=0L_n\lvert\psi\rangle=\bar L_n\lvert\psi\rangle=0Ln​∣ψ⟩=Lˉn​∣ψ⟩=0 for n>0n>0n>0, and is an L0,Lˉ0L_0,\bar L_0L0​,Lˉ0​ eigenstate with weights (h,hˉ)(h,\bar h)(h,hˉ) — the lowest-energy state of its tower. Acting with negative modes L−nL_{-n}L−n​ builds descendants; the primary and all its descendants form a Verma module. If null states (zero-norm descendants) appear, the physical representation is the quotient by them. Knowing the primaries, their weights, and which nulls are quotiented determines the spectrum — this is the representation-theoretic version of "build the string spectrum."

    Two positivity facts, both from (3), constrain a unitary CFT. Take ∣ψ⟩\lvert\psi\rangle∣ψ⟩ a primary of weight hhh, so L1∣ψ⟩=0L_1\lvert\psi\rangle=0L1​∣ψ⟩=0 and ∥L−1∣ψ⟩∥2=⟨ψ∣L1L−1∣ψ⟩=⟨ψ∣[L1,L−1]∣ψ⟩\lVert L_{-1}\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert^2=\langle\psi\rvert L_1L_{-1}\lvert\psi\rangle=\langle\psi\rvert[L_1,L_{-1}]\lvert\psi\rangle∥L−1​∣ψ⟩∥2=⟨ψ∣L1​L−1​∣ψ⟩=⟨ψ∣[L1​,L−1​]∣ψ⟩: ∥L−1∣ψ⟩∥2=⟨ψ∣[L1,L−1]∣ψ⟩=2h ⟨ψ∣ψ⟩≥0  ⇒  h≥0,\lVert L_{-1}\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert^2=\langle\psi\rvert[L_1,L_{-1}]\lvert\psi\rangle=2h\,\langle\psi|\psi\rangle\ge0\;\Rightarrow\;h\ge0,∥L−1​∣ψ⟩∥2=⟨ψ∣[L1​,L−1​]∣ψ⟩=2h⟨ψ∣ψ⟩≥0⇒h≥0, ∥L−n∣0⟩∥2=⟨0∣[Ln,L−n]∣0⟩=c12 n(n2−1)≥0  ⇒  c≥0.\lVert L_{-n}\lvert 0\rangle\rVert^2=\langle 0\rvert[L_n,L_{-n}]\lvert 0\rangle=\frac{c}{12}\,n(n^2-1)\ge0\;\Rightarrow\;c\ge0.∥L−n​∣0⟩∥2=⟨0∣[Ln​,L−n​]∣0⟩=12c​n(n2−1)≥0⇒c≥0. The second uses the central term directly (the Witt part annihilates the vacuum). So any unitary CFT has c≥0c\ge0c≥0 — which is exactly why the ghost sector, with its c=−26c=-26c=−26, cannot be unitary on its own, and why the full string needs the BRST machinery to remove negative-norm states.

    Exercise

    The central term in (3) is fixed by three numbers. Suppose someone hands you a would-be anomaly of the form [Lm,Ln]⊃A(m) δm+n,0[L_m,L_n]\supset A(m)\,\delta_{m+n,0}[Lm​,Ln​]⊃A(m)δm+n,0​ with an unknown odd polynomial A(m)=αm3+βmA(m)=\alpha m^3+\beta mA(m)=αm3+βm. Two physical inputs pin it down completely up to overall normalization. Find A(m)A(m)A(m) (up to a constant) using:

    (a) Antisymmetry / Jacobi. Why must A(m)A(m)A(m) be an odd function of mmm?

    (b) The anomaly-free global subalgebra. Which values of mmm must give A(m)=0A(m)=0A(m)=0, and why? Use this to fix the ratio α/β\alpha/\betaα/β.

    Solution

    (a) The bracket is antisymmetric: [Lm,Ln]=−[Ln,Lm][L_m,L_n]=-[L_n,L_m][Lm​,Ln​]=−[Ln​,Lm​]. On the central term, swapping m↔nm\leftrightarrow nm↔n under the constraint m+n=0m+n=0m+n=0 sends n=−m↦mn=-m\mapsto mn=−m↦m, i.e. m↦−mm\mapsto -mm↦−m. Antisymmetry then demands A(−m)=−A(m)A(-m)=-A(m)A(−m)=−A(m), so AAA is odd. (An even piece like a constant or m2m^2m2 term is forbidden — this is why m2m^2m2 never appears alone, only inside m(m2−1)=m3−mm(m^2-1)=m^3-mm(m2−1)=m3−m.) Hence A(m)=αm3+βmA(m)=\alpha m^3+\beta mA(m)=αm3+βm is the most general odd cubic; higher odd powers are excluded because the TTTTTT OPE can be no more singular than a quartic pole (a (z−w)−4(z-w)^{-4}(z−w)−4 term), which supplies at most the cubic Taylor coefficient ∝m(m2−1)\propto m(m^2-1)∝m(m2−1).

    (b) The generators L−1,L0,L1L_{-1},L_0,L_1L−1​,L0​,L1​ generate the global conformal group SL(2,C)SL(2,\mathbb C)SL(2,C), which is genuinely a symmetry — those maps are globally well-defined, so they cannot be anomalous. Therefore A(m)=0A(m)=0A(m)=0 for m=0,±1m=0,\pm1m=0,±1. The value m=0m=0m=0 is automatic for any odd AAA. Requiring A(1)=0A(1)=0A(1)=0 gives α+β=0  ⟹  β=−α,\alpha+\beta=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\beta=-\alpha,α+β=0⟹β=−α, so A(m)=α(m3−m)=α m(m2−1)A(m)=\alpha(m^3-m)=\alpha\,m(m^2-1)A(m)=α(m3−m)=αm(m2−1). Writing the normalization as α=c/12\alpha=c/12α=c/12 recovers exactly the central term of (3). The two inputs — oddness and an anomaly-free SL(2,C)SL(2,\mathbb C)SL(2,C) — determine the entire structure m(m2−1)m(m^2-1)m(m2−1); only the single number ccc is left for the dynamics to fix.
