Fantasy thesis

Utah selected Peterson second and signed him to a rookie-scale contract July 1. He scored 28 on 11-of-21 shooting in his Salt Lake City summer debut. NBA draft report, 2026-06-24 · NBA summer-league recap, 2026-07-05 · NBA movement data, accessed 2026-07-18

Hashtag ranks him 18th in analyst dynasty and 44th in crowd keeper; RotoWire ranks him 70th. RotoWire's draft analysis calls him arguably the class's best pure scorer but warns that George, Bailey, and Markkanen already demand shots. Hashtag dynasty, accessed 2026-07-18 · RotoWire rookie guide, 2026-06-25

Ceiling: an early promotion to starter, 31 minutes and five assists yield top-60 to top-75. Floor: a 24-minute bench scoring role with ordinary rookie efficiency lands outside the top 130.

Career record

Pre-NBA: High school across three programs — Cuyahoga Valley Christian Academy in Ohio, Huntington Prep in West Virginia, and Prolific Prep in California, where he averaged 30.4 points, 7.2 rebounds, 7.4 assists and 2.2 steals. He was a consensus five-star, ranked first overall in the 2025 class by 247Sports and second by both Rivals and ESPN, and took Naismith Prep Player of the Year and McDonald's All-American Co-MVP. He committed to Kansas on November 1, 2024. Kansas, one season (2025-26): 24 games with 23 starts at 29.0 minutes, averaging 20.2 points, 4.2 rebounds, 1.6 assists, 1.4 steals and 0.6 blocks on .438 from the field, .382 from three and .826 at the line, earning second-team All-Big 12, Big 12 All-Freshman, and honorable mention All-American from both the Associated Press and the USBWA. Made threes and turnovers per game at Kansas are not verified here and are not stated. The number that matters most in the file is the 1.6 assists — a lead-guard-sized scoring load paired with a shooting-guard's passing rate, which is the central question about his NBA position. Draft: 2026, first round, 2nd overall by Utah on June 23, 2026 — no draft-night trade. AJ Dybantsa went first to Washington.

2026 Summer League: Four games across Salt Lake City and Las Vegas, then shut down for load management. Salt Lake City, July 4 against Atlanta: 28 points on 11-of-21 from the field and 4-of-7 from three, five rebounds, two assists, two blocks and eight turnovers in 27 minutes. Salt Lake City, July 6 against Memphis: 25 points on 8-of-15 and 3-of-9, 12 assists, two steals and two turnovers in 28 minutes. Las Vegas produced 23 points against the Clippers and 24 against Washington. Across the four games he averaged roughly 25.0 points, 5.5 assists, 5.0 turnovers and 2.25 combined steals and blocks on about .430 from the field and .380 from three, scoring 20 or more every time out and leading the Las Vegas field in free throw attempts at eight per game. The 12-assist game against Memphis is the only evidence in his entire record of the playmaking rate his NBA role would require; the eight-turnover opener is the counterweight.

Peterson has no NBA regular-season record. He has not played an NBA game, so there is no career table and no NBA rate data to project from — the 2026-27 projection in this dossier is built entirely on the Kansas season and four Summer League games.

Fantasy history: None. He has never been rostered in an NBA season, so no nine-category or points-league finish exists and none is asserted. The only forward-looking structural note the record supports: the .826 college free throw percentage on heavy attempts and the .382 from three are both genuine category assets from day one, while 1.6 college assists and 0.6 blocks mean assists and blocks are the two places a rookie-year line is most likely to disappoint relative to his draft slot.

Durability: The one available season is a warning that has since been partly explained. He played 24 of Kansas's games, sat out 11, and was held under 25 minutes in seven of the games he did play, with hamstring and ankle injuries compounded by persistent severe cramping that went undiagnosed for much of the season and drew public speculation. Peterson told ESPN that doctors determined after the season the cramping was caused by high doses of creatine; Utah team sources told ESPN their own medical review produced no red flags, which is why he still went second. That explanation, if correct, converts the single worst item in his file from a structural durability concern into a correctable supplementation error. It is worth flagging that the explanation is self-reported and post-hoc, and that the hamstring and ankle issues are separate from the cramping and remain unexplained by it. He was also managed out of the back half of Summer League, which is standard for a No. 2 pick and should not be read as a flag.

Trajectory: There is no arc yet, only a shape, and the shape is unusually clear for a one-and-done. He arrived as the most decorated scorer in his class and produced exactly that at Kansas — 20.2 points on 29 minutes with a .382 three-point mark and a free throw stroke that projects — while producing almost nothing as a passer, and the 1.4 steals suggest the defensive activity is real. The gap between his high school assist rate at Prolific Prep, 7.4 per game, and his college rate of 1.6 is the most interesting line in the file: it argues the passing is a latent skill suppressed by Kansas asking him to score rather than an absent one, and the 12-assist Summer League game is the first evidence in eighteen months on that side of the argument. For 2026-27 the realistic read is that the scoring translates in some diminished form and the efficiency does not, which is the standard rookie pattern, while the assist number is the single variable that decides whether he is a top-75 category asset or a points-league-only scorer. Utah's roster works against him here: George, Bailey and Markkanen all need possessions, so the touches that would produce assists are contested, and the most likely rookie outcome is a high-volume, mid-efficiency scoring line with turnovers that hurt in nine-cat. The dynasty case rests on the pedigree and the age, not on anything the 24-game college sample has actually demonstrated.
