How to read this

  • Andrew mode is on by default. It sorts by fit against one specific résumé — Duke mathematics and computer science, May 2028, a daily-RAPM basketball valuation model, a Julia PDE library, a real-money Polymarket and Kalshi book, a county-level degree-day pricing engine — orders roles QR before QT before QD, hides grade F, and prints the reason each seat scored what it did. Turn it off (or press a) and the board reverts to a neutral, grade-ordered list. The weights live in one table at the top of assets/app.js.
  • Grade is a read on selectivity, compensation and exit value — not a judgement of the firm as a business. S is the handful of seats where an offer resets your career. A is elite and pays at the top of the market. B is a real quant seat at a real quant firm. C is smaller, regional, or a genuine quant desk inside a firm that is not primarily a quant shop. Every firm on this board has been vetted against community sources — r/quant, QuantNet, Blind, levels.fyi — for whether the work is actually quantitative. Firms whose "quant" seat turned out to be operations, risk reporting, regulatory engineering or team-side sports analytics have been removed rather than parked in a low tier.
  • Interview process sits in a collapsed panel on each card. It is assembled from what candidates have publicly reported — round counts, assessment formats and time limits, the question types that actually come up, and the stage people say they were unprepared for. Each panel carries a confidence mark and links its sources. It is a prior, not a promise: formats change between cycles, and community reports are anecdote.
  • Type. QT quantitative trader, QR quantitative researcher, QD quantitative developer. Pure software, FPGA and hardware roles are left out even at quant firms.
  • The policy line under each firm name is the application limit. Where it is red the choice is usually irreversible for the season, and Andrew mode prints a spend it on recommendation naming the single role to use that application on.
  • Pay is what the firm posts where a pay-transparency law forces it, and what candidates report otherwise. The source is printed under every figure. Treat reported figures as approximate.
  • Search reaches inside the interview panels. That is the point of having them: cooldown returns Optiver alone, market making game finds the firms that run one, and take-home, superday, case study and poker each pick out a different shape of process. Search a format, not just a firm.
  • Mark stores an "applied" flag in this browser only. Nothing leaves the page.

The shape of the decision

Undergraduate QR and undergraduate QT are not the same contest, and the difference is not the one most people assume.

  • You are not competing with PhDs for QR. At Citadel Securities, IMC, Jump, Two Sigma, Virtu, HRT, SIG, Optiver, Five Rings and Tower the PhD candidates apply to a different requisition. Those reqs are excluded from this board by name, in the header of assets/data.js. The undergraduate-eligible QR seat pools you with master's students, not with fifth-year PhDs.
  • QT is the only pool that is one hundred per cent your peers — and that cuts against it, not for it. There is no degree gate, so the funnel is the largest in the market and the résumé stops mattering. The filter moves to the assessment.
  • So the résumé does its work in QR and almost none of it in QT. That is the argument for leading QR wherever an undergraduate-eligible QR seat exists, and taking QT where it does not.
  • Where a firm forces one application, that is the only place the question actually bites. Andrew mode makes the call on the card.

Applying

  • Almost every prop shop here reviews on a rolling basis. Applying in early August is materially better than applying in late September with the same résumé. Banks are the exception — they have hard deadlines. Filter to Has a deadline and sort by Deadline before doing anything else.
  • The online assessments are the real filter, not the résumé. Most are timed mental arithmetic and probability. Practise before you spend an application on a firm you care about — and sequence the firms with a re-application cooldown last.
  • Watch the graduation windows. Several postings name a range rather than a class year; the range is quoted verbatim under each role title.
  • Apply on the firm's own board. Scraper mirrors — Lensa, Jobright, ZipRecruiter reposts, BeBee — re-host real requisitions with stale windows, wrong pay and dead links, and they outrank the firms themselves. Three impostor applicant-tracking board tokens turned up while building this page: Greenhouse bridgeway, Ashby dimensional and Ashby numeric all resolve to unrelated companies. And nothing that sells you a "funded account" evaluation — FTMO, Apex Trader Funding, TopStep — is an employer; the CFTC sued MyForexFunds over that model in 2023.
