Baseline and 2026-27 projection

Across Indiana and LA, Jackson played 55 games at 16.5 MPG and averaged 6.7 points, 5.3 rebounds, 0.9 assists, 0.7 steals, 0.9 blocks and 1.1 turnovers on .637/.648 shooting. His 17-game LAC field-goal rate was 76.4%, an obvious small-sample high rather than a new median. ESPN statistics

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: rank 130-185; better in punt-FT% and during starts.
  • Points: viable injury streamer because rebounds, blocks and efficient finishes scale with minutes.
  • Dynasty: deep hold at center; age helps, but he still lacks a stable 24-minute role.

Role and scenarios

  • Low: Lopez holds 24 minutes, Niederhauser returns early and Jackson remains near 14 minutes.
  • Base: 18 minutes as the first reserve center and occasional small-ball starter.
  • High: a Lopez or Niederhauser absence creates 26 minutes, roughly 10 points, eight rebounds and 1.5 blocks.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Kentucky, one season (2020-21) as a one-and-done: 8.4 PTS / 6.6 REB / 2.6 BLK on .540 FG, earning SEC All-Defensive and All-Freshman honors. He blocked eight shots against Kansas on December 1, 2020, the most in a game by a Kentucky player since Willie Cauley-Stein in 2013. The profile was already what it is now — shot-blocking and efficient interior finishing, with no perimeter game and modest scoring volume. Draft: 2021, first round, 22nd overall, Los Angeles Lakers, traded to Indiana on draft night.

The 2025-26 split: 38 games with Indiana (14 starts) at 16.8 MPG, 6.4 PTS, 5.6 REB, 0.8 BLK on .582 FG and .689 FT, then 17 games with the Clippers at 15.9 MPG, 7.5 PTS, 4.6 REB, 1.2 BLK on .764 FG and .548 FT. He moved February 5, 2026, alongside Bennedict Mathurin, two first-round picks and a second, for Ivica Zubac and Kobe Brown. As this dossier already notes, the .764 Clippers field-goal rate is a 17-game artifact, not a level; the .548 free-throw mark over the same stretch is equally small-sample and should not be extrapolated either.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not verifiable from public sources for Jackson and none is asserted. The shape has been fixed across five seasons: blocks and field-goal percentage are the two real assets, rebounds are useful but not elite in his minutes, steals are quietly respectable for a center at 0.6-0.7, and free-throw percentage is a persistent drag — .666 career, never above .716 in a full season. He has never been a standard-league draft asset in a full year, because he has never held a rotation role larger than 16.5 minutes. The build implication is sharper than the rank: in punt-FT% teams he is a genuinely efficient source of the two scarcest big-man categories, while in standard builds his FT line cancels much of what his FG% earns. Points leagues underuse him badly, since blocks and percentage carry little weight and his scoring has never reached 8.4.

Durability: GP by season — 36, 63, 60, 5, 55. The 2024-25 season is the whole story: he tore his right Achilles tendon on November 1, 2024 against New Orleans, underwent surgery days later, and missed the remainder of the year. That injury also cost him the start of 2025-26 — he described himself in August 2025 as roughly 80 to 90 percent and targeting training camp — which is the primary reason his 2025-26 total is 55 rather than a full season, not in-season fragility. This dossier states he ended 2025-26 with a right-ankle sprain; that specific claim could not be confirmed in the sources checked here, so it should be treated as unverified pending a camp report rather than as established. The encouraging read is that his post-Achilles rate stats held: blocks, rebounds per minute and finishing efficiency all returned near his pre-injury norms within one season.

Trajectory: Jackson's five-year record is not a development curve, it is a series of interruptions around a stable and narrow skill set. His per-36 block rate has been strong throughout — roughly 3.4 as a rookie, 3.3 in 2022-23, 3.4 in the 2024-25 fragment — and the 2025-26 figure near 2.0 is the one genuine decline, most plausibly a first-year-back-from-Achilles effect on explosiveness rather than a permanent loss. Rebounding per minute has moved the other way, improving to about 11.6 per 36 in 2025-26 from 11.2 as a rookie, so the body of work post-injury is not obviously diminished overall. The important context is that he is 25 in 2026-27 and has never received a real audition: no season above 16.5 minutes, no stretch as an unambiguous starter. That makes him one of the few players in this range whose upside is genuinely untested rather than known and capped — but it also means five years of evidence cannot tell us whether the block rate survives a 26-minute role. The Achilles is now nearly two years back, which is the point at which recovery effects typically resolve, so 2026-27 is the first clean read on him since 2023-24.
