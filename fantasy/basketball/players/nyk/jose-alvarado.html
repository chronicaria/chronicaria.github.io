Jose Alvarado

Jose Alvarado is a steals-and-assists specialist whose new multi-year deal secures his roster place more clearly than it secures enough minutes for standard-league drafting.

New York's bench leans small after Mitchell Robinson's departure, and the shooting need that keeps Shamet on the floor competes directly with Alvarado's minutes rather than with McBride's defensive role. Alvarado's three-year deal secures the roster spot, not the allocation; steals per minute remain the reason to roster him.

Nine-cat: top 250-350, better as a steals/assists streamer. Points: D+. Dynasty: 4.2/10.

Role and evidence

Across New Orleans and New York, Alvarado's 2025-26 line was 7.4 points, 2.5 rebounds, 3.4 assists, 1.0 steal, 1.5 threes, and 1.1 turnovers in 19.9 minutes over 69 games (Hashtag Basketball). In 28 Knicks games specifically, NBA.com reported 6.6 points, 3.8 assists, 2.0 rebounds, and 1.0 steal in 16.9 minutes (team player feed).

New York retained him on a reported three-year deal worth more than $14 million (NBA.com, July 6). He is the most natural bench organizer, but McBride, Shamet, Clarkson, and Kolek prevent a clean 20-minute claim.

Role branches

  • Ceiling: Brunson misses time, Alvarado starts or reaches 24 minutes, and he supplies five assists plus 1.4 steals.
  • Median: 14 energetic minutes with category value concentrated in steals and assists.
  • Floor: healthy-night DNPs against large backcourts; percentage drag without enough volume elsewhere.

Market and confidence

Hashtag ranked Alvarado 335th in its July analyst dynasty board. Contract confidence is high, exact rotation minutes medium-low, assists/steals per minute high, and shooting efficiency medium-low.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Georgia Tech, four seasons (2017-21), rising from 12.1 PPG freshman to 15.2 PPG, 4.1 APG, 2.8 SPG senior; 2021 ACC Defensive Player of the Year and second-team All-ACC. Draft: undrafted (2021); signed with New Orleans on a two-way and converted.

Durability: Has never reached 70 games (54/61/56/56/69). The 2022-23 season ended with a right-tibia stress reaction in late February; the other shortfalls were a mix of minor soft-tissue absences and rotation decisions.

Trajectory: Five seasons of a remarkably stable identity: elite steal rate (1.0-1.3 per game at 15-24 minutes every year), real assists, sub-.42 field-goal shooting. The 2024-25 New Orleans season (10.3 points, 4.6 assists, 1.3 steals in 24.4 minutes) is his proof of concept as a full bench guard; the Knicks stint shows the same per-minute player in a smaller box. In 2026-27 he competes with McBride for first reserve point guard behind Brunson on the three-year deal. He becomes standard-league relevant only if Brunson misses extended time and Alvarado holds 24+ minutes — steals and assists arrive immediately at that workload, with FG% drag as the permanent tax.
