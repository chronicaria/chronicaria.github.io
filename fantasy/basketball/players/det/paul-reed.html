Nine-cat: top-150 per-minute profile, shallow-league injury stash. Points: C. Dynasty: 5.0/10.

Role and evidence

Reed averaged 7.8 points, 4.5 rebounds, 1.2 assists, 0.9 steals, and 0.9 blocks in only 13.9 minutes, shooting .617 from the field over 65 games (Basketball-Reference). Those rates are fantasy-friendly even when the rotation is not.

Stewart's trade clears the backup-center job, but Collins can take small-ball five minutes and Duren is projected to return. The NBA's June movement analysis quantified Stewart's elite rim deterrence; Reed can approximate event production, not necessarily the same team defense. Foul rate and offensive spacing remain the reasons his minutes have historically trailed his box-score output.

Range

  • Ceiling: 24 MPG after a Duren absence/departure, with 10/8 and roughly 2.5 stocks—clear top-100 value.
  • Floor: 11-13 MPG behind Duren plus Collins, leaving him undraftable outside deep leagues.

Career record

Pre-NBA: DePaul, 2017-20. He went 3.6 points and 3.1 rebounds in 28 games as a freshman, 12.3 and 8.5 in 36 games as a sophomore (Big East Most Improved Player), then 15.1 points, 10.7 rebounds, 1.9 steals, and 2.6 blocks in 31.7 minutes across 29 starts as a junior on .516/.308/.738 shooting, taking second-team All-Big East and 18 double-doubles. Freshman and sophomore shooting splits were not verified and are omitted. Draft: 2020, second round, 58th overall, Philadelphia. He signed a two-way deal on December 3, 2020 and was converted to a standard contract on March 26, 2021. In the 15-game Orlando G League bubble season he averaged 22.3 points, 11.9 rebounds, 2.3 assists, 2.0 steals, and 1.8 blocks on .588/.444/.789 for the Delaware Blue Coats, winning G League MVP and Rookie of the Year — the only player in the league that year above 20 and 11, and the only one with at least 25 steals and 25 blocks. Philadelphia waived him July 6, 2024; Detroit claimed him July 9, 2024 and re-signed him June 30, 2025 on a two-year, $11 million deal.

The 2025-26 line in this dossier reconciles exactly with ESPN.

Fantasy history: No season-end 9-cat rank from Basketball Monster, Hashtag, or the ESPN Player Rater was retrievable for any season, so none is stated. The only year in which he held standard-league value on total production was 2023-24, when 82 games at 19.4 minutes produced 6.0 rebounds, 0.8 steals, and 1.0 blocks. Every other season the value has been per-minute rather than per-game. The category shape is unusually clean for a late-round big: he has posted at least 0.7 steals and 0.6 blocks in four straight seasons, his career field goal percentage is .565, and his turnover rate is genuinely low at 0.7 per game for his career and 0.9 even in his highest-usage year. The offsetting problems are that he takes essentially no threes (0.1 per game for his career) and his free throw percentage is both bad and erratic — .664 in 2025-26 against .762 the year before — which caps how much a low-volume line can help. Points leagues rate him worse than categories do, because stocks and efficiency are worth far more in roto than in a points format where his 5.5 career points per game is the ceiling of what he contributes.

Durability: 26, 38, 69, 82, 45, 65. The missed games are overwhelmingly rotational rather than medical, which is the key distinction in his profile. He played all 82 games in 2023-24. In 2024-25 he recorded 14 DNPs in Detroit's first 26 games as a healthy scratch behind Jalen Duren and Isaiah Stewart, and his minutes only opened up after Stewart's injury, after which he averaged 16.0 minutes. In 2025-26 he was the third-string center behind Duren and Stewart, started 11 games, and the only absence identified in reporting was a single game for personal reasons. No significant injury was found in his six NBA seasons.

Trajectory: The per-minute production has been stable and good since 2022-23, which means the entire career variance has been coaching decisions rather than skill change. That is the useful signal: 2023-24 in Philadelphia established what he does at real minutes — 6.0 rebounds and 1.8 stocks in 19.4 minutes over a full 82 — and nothing since has contradicted it, because nothing since has given him those minutes. The 2025-26 field goal percentage of .617 on 13.9 minutes is the best mark of his career and reflects a shot diet narrowed almost entirely to rim finishes as his role shrank, so it should be read as role-induced rather than as a skill leap. At 27 he is at the flat part of the aging curve, where a big man with no shooting range and no offensive creation gains nothing further from development and is priced purely on opportunity. For 2026-27 the honest framing is that he is a bet on Duren or Collins missing time, not a bet on Reed improving; his history says he will convert an opening into top-100 category production within a week of getting it, and will otherwise sit at 13-15 minutes with no path to more.
