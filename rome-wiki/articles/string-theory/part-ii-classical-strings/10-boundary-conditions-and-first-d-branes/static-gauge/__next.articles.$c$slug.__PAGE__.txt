<h2 id="why-this-matters">Why this matters</h2>
<p>The static gauge is the hinge of the whole module. It converts the abstract worldsheet area into a plain mechanics problem with an ordinary time integral, which is what lets us read off a potential energy in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-ii-classical-strings/10-boundary-conditions-and-first-d-branes/string-tension-and-the-energy-of-a-stretched-string/" class="wiki-link">String tension and the energy of a stretched string</a> and a Lagrangian in <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-ii-classical-strings/10-boundary-conditions-and-first-d-branes/endpoint-velocity-and-endpoint-force/" class="wiki-link">Endpoint velocity and endpoint force</a>. Every concrete number in Module 10 rides on this one gauge choice: we glued the string's clock to ours, so "the string at time <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>t</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">t</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6151em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">t</span></span></span></span>" is now literally a grid line, and Nambu–Goto becomes ordinary dynamics in ordinary time.</p>
