Team context: Chicago Bulls · Research cutoff: July 18, 2026.

Role and baseline

Chicago drafted Swain fifteenth. He led Texas in scoring, rebounding, assists, steals, and minutes, averaging 17.3 points, 7.5 rebounds, 3.6 assists, and 1.6 steals in 32.8 minutes across 36 games on .542/.344/.815 shooting. Bulls release, June 23, 2026 · College Basketball Reference

RotoWire's draft-night capsule praised the size, glass work, and frontcourt playmaking. Chicago then force-fed him point-guard work in Summer League; he failed to make a field goal in consecutive games before right-wrist soreness ended the event. Those July results lower the immediate floor without erasing the college sample. RotoWire/CBS, July 17, 2026 · Bulls recap, July 15, 2026

Update 2026-07-19: the final Vegas ledger is as bad as the game-by-game read suggested — 4.3 points, 5.0 rebounds, and 2.3 assists in 28.0 minutes across four games on 4-of-31 shooting (12.9%), 0-of-7 from three, 4-of-12 from the line, with 5.2 turnovers per game. Swain told reporters he had "definitely been overthinking." The rebounds and assists at that minute load are the only salvage; the projection here already priced in the wide floor and does not move. Fantasy fit

  • 9-cat/points redraft: watch-list prospect in standard leagues; draft only in deep formats until the role stabilizes.
  • Dynasty: first-round draft capital and all-around college production make him a patient top-150 type hold.
  • Strengths: positional rebounds, steals, secondary assists, FT%; potential multi-position eligibility.
  • Weaknesses: NBA three, half-court separation, turnovers when overtasked, and crowded wings.

Range, health, and triggers

  • Floor: begins in the G League or plays under 10 NBA minutes.
  • Ceiling: 24 minutes with roughly 10/5/3 and one steal, becoming a top-160 multi-category player.
  • Health: held out July 17 with right-wrist soreness; reporting said there was no reason to expect a camp limitation.
  • Watch: whether Chicago uses him as a wing instead of a point guard, catch-and-shoot percentage, and rotation position relative to Isaac Okoro.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Three college seasons. Xavier 2023-24: 4.6 PTS / 3.0 REB / 1.2 AST / 1.0 STL in 19.0 MPG over 29 games (.459 FG, .154 3P, .813 FT), Big East All-Freshman despite a season interrupted by an appendectomy. Xavier 2024-25: 11.0 / 5.5 / 2.6 with 1.6 STL in 28.4 MPG over 34 games (.532/.250/.817). Transferred to Texas 2025-26: 17.3 PTS / 7.5 REB / 3.6 AST / 1.6 STL / 2.6 TO in 32.8 MPG over 36 games (all starts) on .542/.344/.815 — SEC Newcomer of the Year, Second-team All-SEC. Draft: 2026, Round 1, Pick 15, Chicago Bulls.

No NBA record; most recent season is 2025-26 at Texas. His only NBA-adjacent tape is the 4-game 2026 Vegas Summer League (4-of-31 FG) covered above.

Durability: 29, 34, and 36 games across three college seasons — the only real absence was the freshman-year appendectomy; right-wrist soreness ended his July 2026 Summer League early with no reported camp concern.

Trajectory: The college arc is a clean year-over-year climb in usage, scoring, and efficiency without ever losing the steals-and-boards base, and the three-point percentage improved each season (.154 → .250 → .344) even if the release remains a question. In 2026-27 he competes for backup wing minutes behind Chicago's veteran logjam, not the point-guard role Summer League miscast him in. He becomes fantasy-relevant only if Chicago commits to him as a wing for 20+ minutes — at that point the positional rebounds, steals, and secondary assists give him deep-league 9-cat utility even with a modest three-point volume.
