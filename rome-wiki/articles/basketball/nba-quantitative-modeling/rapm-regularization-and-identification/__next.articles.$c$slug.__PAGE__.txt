3:T7760,<h2 id="why-the-unregularized-problem-is-unstable">Why the unregularized problem is unstable</h2>
<p>In <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/rapm-lineup-model/" class="wiki-link">RAPM lineup model</a>, player columns are generated by actual coaching choices rather than an experiment. Starters share many possessions. Bench units appear in particular contexts. A traded player may be observed under only two schemes. Some low-minute teammates never separate.</p>
<p>These patterns make columns of the lineup matrix nearly dependent. Ordinary least squares can fit the possession outcomes with implausibly large positive and negative coefficients whose sum is stable but whose individual values are not. Small changes to the sample can flip credit between correlated players.</p>
<p>Ridge addresses this by paying a quadratic cost for coefficient magnitude. <h2 id="component-specific-shrinkage">Component-specific shrinkage</h2>
<p>The production configuration does not pretend every target has the same signal-to-noise ratio. It uses separate offense and defense penalties for the eight underlying components. Current penalties span roughly <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>750</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">750</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">750</span></span></span></span> to <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>6,000</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">6{,}000</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8389em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord">6</span><span class="mord"><span class="mpunct">,</span></span><span class="mord">000</span></span></span></span> on offense and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>750</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">750</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">750</span></span></span></span> to <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>24,000</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">24{,}000</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.8389em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;"></span><span class="mord">24</span><span class="mord"><span class="mpunct">,</span></span><span class="mord">000</span></span></span></span> on defense. Noisier defensive three-point attribution receives much stronger shrinkage than better-identified components.</p>
<p>A larger <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>λ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\lambda</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6944em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">λ</span></span></span></span> has a concrete meaning: the data need more effective possession evidence to move a player far from the prior center. This improves stability but compresses the tails. A ranking can change because the evidence changed, the penalty changed, or the scale calibration changed; model comparisons must hold those layers straight.</p>

<h2 id="centering-and-scale">Centering and scale</h2>
<p>Each solve removes a decay-weighted target mean before estimating player effects. The ridge penalty then selects a particular offensive/defensive allocation from a design with weak location identification. After solving, component offsets calibrate the NBA output onto the project's DataBallR reference scale. Under the exact <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/six-factor-rapm-decomposition/" class="wiki-link">six-factor closure</a>, <code>net_offset</code> is zero and the six component offsets carry the scale alignment.</p>
<p>This calibration is a coordinate choice, not new basketball evidence. It lets the model reproduce a familiar public range and compare versions, while the lineup contrasts determine relative player values.</p>

The prior is helpful only to the degree that it is causal, well calibrated, and predictive for the factor in question. The project therefore keeps a pure series as an essential control.</p>
<h2 id="identification-limits-that-remain">Identification limits that remain</h2>
<p>Regularization answers “which stable coefficient vector should this design prefer?” It does not turn observational lineup data into randomized causal effects.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Persistent pairs:</strong> teammates who rarely separate still share credit according to the penalty and surrounding lineups.</li>
<li><strong>Role selection:</strong> a player's coefficient includes the environments coaches choose for him.</li>
<li><strong>Scheme and interaction:</strong> an additive model can distribute synergy or incompatibility across individual terms.</li>
<li><strong>Opponent targeting:</strong> the response to a player may alter which actions opponents attempt, complicating the factor label.</li>
<li><strong>Data coverage:</strong> structural zeros and missing play-by-play weaken particular eras and targets.</li>
</ul>
<p>The safe interpretation is a regularized adjusted association, conditional on the design and scale.</p>

Two teammates with the same exposure can receive the same reported standard error even when one is much more collinear and therefore less separately identified.</p>
<p>The net standard error also requires a factor-covariance adjustment; simply summing six variances assumes independent component errors. The project uses empirical calibration and inflation artifacts, but <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/nba-quantitative-modeling/rapm-evaluation-and-uncertainty/" class="wiki-link">RAPM evaluation and uncertainty</a> treats the full ridge diagonal and factor covariance as correctness work still worth doing.</p>
<h2 id="open-questions">Open questions</h2>

<li>How much would full ridge-covariance standard errors widen bands for persistent teammate pairs?</li>
<li>Should scale calibration be refit on a larger reference panel rather than a small set of public targets?</li>
<li>Can partial pooling across positions or roles improve identification without smuggling box-score bias into the pure model?</li>
<li>Which interactions are stable enough out of sample to justify moving beyond additivity?</li>
