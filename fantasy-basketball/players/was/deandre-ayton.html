Deandre Ayton

Deandre Ayton is an efficient rebounds-and-FG% specialist whose value is capped by a projected 16-minute reserve role behind the Davis-Sarr frontcourt, on an expiring contract that makes him the roster's most nameable midseason trade candidate.

Role and baseline

  • 2025-26: 72 games, 27.2 MPG; 12.5 points, 8.0 rebounds, 0.6 steals and 1.0 block on .671 FG and .645 FT.
  • Transaction: acquired July 7 for Jaden Hardy and two second-round picks.
  • Contract: final year at $8.1M, acquired from the Lakers on July 7 2026. An expiring mid-size salary on a non-contender is the single most tradeable asset shape in the league, and his departure is the cleanest path to Sarr reaching 32-plus minutes.
  • 2026-27 base role: backup center and injury hedge; 16 base minutes. His path to 28-plus requires a Davis/Sarr absence, trade or two-big lineup commitment.

Fantasy dossier

  • 9-cat: top 140-180 base, useful mainly for FG% and rebounds with low turnovers. FT%, threes, assists and steals limit the ceiling.
  • Points: shallow-league fringe at 16 minutes; a clear streamer when starting.
  • Dynasty: outside the top 100 because his best fantasy seasons require workload, not a new skill leap.
  • Build note: category managers can use the efficient center line in punt-FT% builds, but the low scoring volume may not justify a roster spot.

Floor, median and ceiling

  • Floor: minutes fall into the teens and he is unrosterable outside deep leagues.
  • Median: 16-minute FG%/rebound specialist around the top 160.
  • Ceiling: Sarr or Davis misses extended time, Ayton reaches 28-30 minutes and returns top-100 value.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Born in Nassau, Bahamas; moved to the United States for high school, attending Balboa School in San Diego before transferring to Hillcrest Prep in Phoenix. One season at Arizona (2017-18): 20.1 PTS / 11.6 REB / 1.9 BLK on .612 FG, sweeping Pac-12 Player of the Year and Pac-12 Freshman of the Year, winning the Karl Malone Award as the nation's top power forward, and earning consensus first-team All-American. That is close to the strongest possible one-and-done statistical profile. Draft: 2018, first round, 1st overall, Phoenix Suns. NBA All-Rookie First Team in 2019.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not verifiable from public sources here and none is asserted. The category identity has been consistent and unusually lopsided for eight years: elite FG% on high volume, top-tier rebounding, roughly a block, and essentially zero threes, with FT% oscillating from a genuine asset (.823 in 2023-24) to a liability (.667, then .645 in the last two years). Through 2023-24 the rebounding volume alone made him a mid-round category asset in most builds. The 2025-26 season is the break: 8.0 rebounds is far below every prior year, and once the rebounding premium goes, what remains is a FG% specialist with a bad free-throw line and no peripheral counting stats. Points leagues have always rated him below his category value because the scoring is efficient but low-usage, and at a projected 16 minutes that gap widens further.

Durability: GP by season — 71, 38, 69, 58, 67, 55, 40, 72. Two of the three worst years have identifiable causes and they are different in kind. The 38 games in 2019-20 were driven by a 25-game suspension for testing positive for a diuretic in violation of the league's anti-drug policy, plus a later right ankle sprain — a compliance event, not a fragility signal. The 40 games in 2024-25 were a genuine injury: a left calf strain suffered against Denver on February 10, 2025, initially given a four-week timeline, which ultimately kept him out roughly two months and ended his Portland season. In 2025-26 he was comparatively available at 72 games, missing about ten with a knee contusion and recurring back soreness, none of it structural. The composite read is a player who has never had a career-threatening injury but has also cleared 70 games only three times in eight years.

Trajectory: Ayton's arc is the rare one where the counting stats decline while the efficiency reaches a career peak, and the two are the same story. His .671 FG in 2025-26 led to a near-league-best mark, but it came from a shrunken, purely-at-the-rim shot diet in a Lakers offense that used him as a finisher rather than an option — scoring fell to 12.5, assists collapsed to 0.8 from a career 1.5, and turnovers dropped to 1.2 because he simply handled the ball less. The alarming number is rebounding: 8.0 per game and roughly 10.6 per 36, against a career rate near 12.0 and 12.3 per 36 as recently as 2023-24. That is a real decline in his signature skill, not a minutes artifact, and it happened at 27, which is early for a center to lose the boards. The 2019-20 season remains his statistical peak at 18.2 and 11.5 per game, meaning his best basketball is now six years behind him despite his still being in nominal prime. For 2026-27, projecting him behind Washington's younger frontcourt at 16 minutes is the right default, and the history argues the upside case is workload-dependent rather than skill-dependent — a bigger role would restore volume but there is no evidence in eight seasons of a new skill arriving.
