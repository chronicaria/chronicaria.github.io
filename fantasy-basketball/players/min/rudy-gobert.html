Snapshot

  • Role: starting center, defensive anchor, and only proven NBA rim protector on the roster.
  • Build fit: dramatically more valuable in punt-FT% than in balanced nine-category builds.
  • Current market: Hashtag's July crowdsourced keeper rank placed him 127th, reflecting age and format sensitivity rather than a loss of specialist utility.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: 90-130 in a balanced build; materially higher in punt-FT%.
  • Points: 90-130, with stronger value in systems that reward rebounds, blocks, and double-doubles.
  • Dynasty: 110-160. A win-now specialist whose value can decline quickly at this age.

Role, floor and ceiling

Gobert should play approximately 30 minutes because Joan Beringer and Rocco Zikarsky are developmental. Floor: age-related mobility decline, 26 minutes, and fewer than 65 games. Ceiling: another 70-game double-double season with two combined steals and blocks, returning top-60 value in a punt build.

The rebounding case is the strongest single reason to hold him. Randle and Reid took roughly a third of Minnesota's frontcourt rebounding chances with them, the replacements are Lyles at 17 minutes and a second-year Beringer, and Gobert's rebound rate actually ticked up in 2025-26 rather than down. His board count is the one counting category on this file with a clear tailwind.

Risk monitor

Gobert turns 35 during 2027. Minnesota's lack of proven center depth may force heavier minutes, not lighter ones. Any ankle, knee, or back absence would immediately elevate Beringer and push Trey Lyles into small-ball center duty.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Cholet Basket, French LNB Pro A, 2010-11 through 2012-13. One senior appearance as an 18-year-old, then 4.2 points and 3.6 rebounds across 29 games in 2011-12, then 8.4 points and 5.4 rebounds across 27 games in 2012-13. He was never a scoring prospect; the draft case was entirely measurement and rim deterrence, and the 2013 combine wingspan and standing reach records are what moved him up boards. Per-game shooting splits for the Cholet seasons are not verified here. Draft: 2013, first round, 27th overall by Denver, traded to Utah on draft night.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag are not retrievable from open sources here, so no year-by-year finish is asserted. What the box scores do support: the peak value window was 2016-17 through 2021-22, when he paired 12.8-14.7 rebounds and 2.0-2.7 blocks with a league-leading field goal percentage, and the Minnesota years have been a steady decline in the counting categories that carried him. The shape has always been format-dependent — zero three-pointers and a career .633 from the line mean a balanced nine-category build pays a real price for him, while punt-FT% and punt-3PM builds get a top-tier anchor. Points leagues treat him better than category leagues at this stage because rebounds and blocks accrue without the FT% penalty attaching.

Durability: 45, 82, 61, 81, 56, 81, 68, 71, 66, 70, 76, 72, 76. The two worst seasons were injury-driven: 2015-16 (knee sprain and bone bruise) and 2017-18 (knee sprain that cost roughly a month early, then a second knee issue). In 2024-25 he missed roughly ten straight games with a lower back injury, returning against San Antonio in late February 2025. In 2025-26 he played 76 games and sat the regular-season finale for rest; the specific causes of the handful of other absences are not verified. The Minnesota-era pattern is the useful one — four straight seasons of 70 to 76 games with no structural injury. He is a durable player, not a fragile one, and the availability risk for 2026-27 is age-driven rather than history-driven.

Trajectory: The leap seasons are legible. 2014-15 was pure minutes (9.6 to 26.3 MPG on the same skill set), and 2016-17 was the real one — a full-time 34-minute role plus a shot diet reduced almost entirely to rim finishes, which is what took his field goal percentage from .559 to .661 and has kept it above .650 in every season since. Since arriving in Minnesota the trend has been minutes holding and per-minute production falling: 14.0 points in 2023-24 down to 12.0 and then 10.9, with the block rate cut nearly in half from the Utah peak as Minnesota moved him to more drop-and-contain coverage alongside a switch-heavy wing group. The one genuinely new datum in 2025-26 is the free throw collapse to .526, a fifteen-point drop from the prior season and the worst mark of his career outside his rookie year, which changes his category profile more than the scoring dip does. At 34 with a rebound rate that actually ticked back up in 2025-26, the reasonable 2026-27 read is another 70-plus games of 10-12 points and 11 rebounds on elite field goal percentage, with the blocks and the free throw line as the two variables that decide whether he is a punt-build cornerstone or a roster-clogging specialist.
