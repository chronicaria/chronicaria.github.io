Scope and cutoff

Research cutoff: 2026-07-18, America/New_York. This is an offseason decision ledger, not a live in-season injury report. It covers all 30 teams and emphasizes injuries, rehabilitation, medical maintenance, suspensions, investigations, and rest risks capable of changing a fantasy rank or a rotation. Fringe-player injuries are included only when they create a meaningful dynasty stash, roster, or minute consequence.

“No later public clearance found” means exactly that. It does not prove that a player remains injured. Conversely, a signing, trade, workout clip, or player statement is not automatically formal medical clearance. Official team and league releases are preferred; credible reporting is labeled when it supplies the latest useful status.

Risk key

Tier Meaning for this ledger Draft treatment before camp
Red Confirmed current rehabilitation, no safe opening-night assumption, or a public timeline that reaches into the season Do not pay a healthy-season price; build an absence branch
Orange Expected to be ready or has encouraging activity, but recent major surgery, recurrence, or missing final clearance still materially affects games/minutes Apply a games-played or ramp discount and identify the handcuff
Yellow Returned or effectively cleared, yet history, age, or repeated maintenance makes rest/recurrence materially more likely than average Preserve per-game value; lower totals confidence
Green No material unresolved restriction located at the cutoff Do not carry a stale injury tag; use ordinary durability variance

The tier is a fantasy exposure grade, not a medical diagnosis or prediction that a setback will occur.

Camp recheck protocol

  1. Medical milestone: Look for running, change of direction, controlled contact, full five-on-five, unrestricted practice, preseason game participation, and removal from the official report—in that order. “Working out” alone is weak evidence.
  2. Workload milestone: Record announced minutes caps, practice-day rest, back-to-back rules, and whether the player starts both halves of preseason games.
  3. Role transfer: When an absence remains, move minutes and usage to named beneficiaries in the team page; do not merely lower the injured player's projection and leave team totals short.
  4. Return does not mean full baseline: Post-Achilles, ACL, patellar-tendon, Lisfranc, and recurrent hamstring cases can be active while still below normal minutes, burst, or schedule density.
  5. Stale-label purge: A player who returned to playoffs, FIBA, or Summer League has direct game-action evidence. Remove the old “out” label unless a new setback is reported, while retaining an appropriate recurrence discount.
  6. Opening-week reset: Replace this offseason ledger with the first official 2026-27 injury reports. Team game notes, league injury PDFs, and official medical releases supersede summer fantasy blurbs.

Source and modeling limits

  • This pass used publicly reachable English-language material. Private medical information, subscriber-only beat reporting, unauthenticated social posts, and undisclosed team workload plans are necessarily absent.
  • Official releases often provide recovery expectations rather than clearance. The ledger preserves that difference.
  • Surgery dates and return windows do not establish individual outcomes. No generalized medical timetable is used to overrule a player's or team's stated status.
  • Trades and unsigned contracts can change which beneficiary matters. The team named beside each player reflects the roster snapshot at the cutoff.
  • “No active suspension found” is a documented search result, not a guarantee that later league discipline will not occur.
  • The ledger supports projections; it is not medical advice.
