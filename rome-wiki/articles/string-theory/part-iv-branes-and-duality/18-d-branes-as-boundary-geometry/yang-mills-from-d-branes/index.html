    Yang-Mills from D-branes

    N coincident D-branes carry U(N) Yang–Mills: the massless open-string vector picks up two endpoint labels, becomes an N\times N matrix-valued gauge field, and string joining supplies exactly the matrix multiplication that makes the field strength non-abelian.

    Two facts and their product

    Two results from earlier notes combine here. One brane carries one photon — a U(1)U(1)U(1) Maxwell field Am(x)A_m(x)Am​(x) on its world-volume (Open strings on one brane). And endpoints carry labels — on a stack of NNN branes each string end can land on any brane, so a state acquires a pair of Chan–Paton indices [ij][ij][ij] (Chan-Paton factors).

    Multiply them and the photon becomes a matrix of photons AmijA_m{}^i{}_jAm​ij​. The genuinely new ingredient is interaction: when two open strings join end-to-end, the touching endpoint labels must match and are summed over, and that sum is matrix multiplication. It is what turns N2N^2N2 free photons into a single interacting non-abelian gauge theory. Yang–Mills is what open strings look like at low energy, and its non-abelian structure is nothing but endpoints tracking which brane they sit on.

    The rest of the note makes "matrix of photons plus string joining equals Yang–Mills" precise.

    From one photon to a matrix of photons

    A single Dppp-brane has one massless open-string vector, which at low energy is a U(1)U(1)U(1) gauge potential Am(x)A_m(x)Am​(x), m=0,…,pm=0,\dots,pm=0,…,p, with abelian field strength

    Fmn=∂mAn−∂nAm.(1)F_{mn}=\partial_m A_n-\partial_n A_m. \tag{1}Fmn​=∂m​An​−∂n​Am​.(1)

    For NNN coincident branes, the vector comes in N2N^2N2 copies — one per label pair [ij][ij][ij] (this is exactly the N2N^2N2-fold spectrum from Chan-Paton factors). Collect them into a single matrix-valued potential

    Amij(x),i,j=1,…,N,m=0,…,p,(2)A_m{}^i{}_j(x),\qquad i,j=1,\dots,N,\quad m=0,\dots,p, \tag{2}Am​ij​(x),i,j=1,…,N,m=0,…,p,(2)

    where mmm is the world-volume vector index and (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) is the matrix index. Swapping the two endpoints conjugates the state labels, so the physical fields sit in Hermitian N×NN\times NN×N matrices — the Lie algebra u(N)\mathfrak{u}(N)u(N).

    Where the commutator comes from

    The abelian curvature (1) is not gauge-covariant once AmA_mAm​ is a matrix. The correct field strength is

     Fmn=∂mAn−∂nAm+i[Am,An] .(3)\boxed{\,F_{mn}=\partial_m A_n-\partial_n A_m+i[A_m,A_n]\,}. \tag{3}Fmn​=∂m​An​−∂n​Am​+i[Am​,An​]​.(3)

    The commutator term i[Am,An]i[A_m,A_n]i[Am​,An​] is the non-abelian piece. It vanishes for a single brane and whenever the matrices commute (all diagonal, say), and is nonzero exactly when the gauge fields fail to commute — the hallmark of Yang–Mills.

    The commutator is string joining made local. A three-string interaction glues the σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π end of one string to the σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 end of the next; the shared endpoint must sit on the same brane, so the labels contract like matrix units,

    EijEkl=δjk Eil,(4)E_{ij}E_{kl}=\delta_{jk}\,E_{il}, \tag{4}Eij​Ekl​=δjk​Eil​,(4)

    and the Kronecker δjk\delta_{jk}δjk​ is a matrix product (Chan-Paton factors). Two orderings of the same joining differ by which string is glued first; their difference is the antisymmetric combination AmAn−AnAm=[Am,An]A_mA_n-A_nA_m=[A_m,A_n]Am​An​−An​Am​=[Am​,An​]. So the commutator in (3) is the low-energy shadow of "endpoints must match, and order matters" — the reason branes give non-abelian gauge theory rather than N2N^2N2 unrelated Maxwell fields.

    Gauge invariance (get the sign right)

    A gauge transformation is a spacetime-dependent unitary rotation U(x)∈U(N)U(x)\in U(N)U(x)∈U(N) of the endpoint labels. It acts on the fundamental representation by ψ↦Uψ\psi\mapsto U\psiψ↦Uψ, which fixes the covariant derivative to be Dm=∂m+iAmD_m=\partial_m+iA_mDm​=∂m​+iAm​. Demanding Dmψ↦U(Dmψ)D_m\psi\mapsto U(D_m\psi)Dm​ψ↦U(Dm​ψ) forces

    Am↦UAmU−1+i(∂mU)U−1.(5)A_m\mapsto U A_m U^{-1}+i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}. \tag{5}Am​↦UAm​U−1+i(∂m​U)U−1.(5)

    The inhomogeneous term is +i(∂mU)U−1+i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}+i(∂m​U)U−1, not −i-i−i: its sign is locked to the convention F=∂A+i[A,A]F=\partial A+i[A,A]F=∂A+i[A,A] in (3). Flip the sign in (3) and the sign in (5) flips with it; they are not independent choices. With (5), the field strength rotates homogeneously,

    Fmn↦UFmnU−1,(6)F_{mn}\mapsto U F_{mn}U^{-1}, \tag{6}Fmn​↦UFmn​U−1,(6)

    because its two inhomogeneous pieces — one from ∂m(UAnU−1)\partial_m(UA_nU^{-1})∂m​(UAn​U−1), one from the commutator — cancel (checked in the exercise). Homogeneity is the payoff: any trace of a product of FFF's is then gauge-invariant, since Tr(UXU−1)=Tr X\mathrm{Tr}(UXU^{-1})=\mathrm{Tr}\,XTr(UXU−1)=TrX, which is what lets us build a gauge-invariant action.

    Two special cases sanity-check the picture: for N=1N=1N=1, U=eiλU=e^{i\lambda}U=eiλ is a phase, the commutator dies, and (5) collapses to ordinary Maxwell gauge invariance Am↦Am+∂mλA_m\mapsto A_m+\partial_m\lambdaAm​↦Am​+∂m​λ. For constant UUU, the inhomogeneous term drops and it is a plain global rotation of the branes among themselves.

    The low-energy action

    Keep only the lightest open-string modes and expand in powers of α′\alpha'α′. The leading local action for the matrix gauge field is Yang–Mills:

     SYM=−14gYM2∫dp+1x  Tr FmnFmn ,(7)\boxed{\,S_{\mathrm{YM}}=-\frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2}\int d^{p+1}x\;\mathrm{Tr}\,F_{mn}F^{mn}\,}, \tag{7}SYM​=−4gYM2​1​∫dp+1xTrFmn​Fmn​,(7)

    in mostly-plus signature, with FmnF_{mn}Fmn​ from (3) and the trace over the N×NN\times NN×N matrix indices. The trace makes it gauge-invariant by (6) and sews the N2N^2N2 components into one action with a single coupling.

    The coupling is fixed, not free. Expanding the abelian Born–Infeld action −Tp−det⁡(η+2πα′F)-T_p\sqrt{-\det(\eta+2\pi\alpha' F)}−Tp​−det(η+2πα′F)​ to quadratic order gives the Maxwell term with prefactor Tp(2πα′)2T_p(2\pi\alpha')^2Tp​(2πα′)2 (Born-Infeld action), so

    1gYM2=(2πα′)2 Tp⟹gYM2∼gs ℓs p−3,(8)\frac{1}{g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2}=(2\pi\alpha')^2\,T_p \quad\Longrightarrow\quad g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2\sim g_s\,\ell_s^{\,p-3}, \tag{8}gYM2​1​=(2πα′)2Tp​⟹gYM2​∼gs​ℓsp−3​,(8)

    using the Dppp-brane tension Tp∼1/(gs ℓs p+1)T_p\sim 1/(g_s\,\ell_s^{\,p+1})Tp​∼1/(gs​ℓsp+1​) with ℓs=α′\ell_s=\sqrt{\alpha'}ℓs​=α′​. The gauge coupling is set by the string coupling and the brane dimension. A D3-brane (p=3p=3p=3) is special: gYM2∼gsg_{\mathrm{YM}}^2\sim g_sgYM2​∼gs​ is dimensionless, the mark of a classically scale-invariant four-dimensional gauge theory.

    What lives in the "⋯\cdots⋯": the transverse scalars

    The full low-energy action is not just (7). The same stack carries (d−p)(d-p)(d−p) adjoint scalars ϕaij\phi^a{}_{ij}ϕaij​, a=p+1,…,da=p+1,\dots,da=p+1,…,d — the matrix versions of the transverse-position scalars from Open strings on one brane:

    S=−(2πα′)2Tp∫dp+1x  Tr ⁣(14FmnFmn+12DmϕaDmϕa−14∑a≠b[ϕa,ϕb]2)+⋯ ,(9)S=-(2\pi\alpha')^2 T_p\int d^{p+1}x\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\tfrac14 F_{mn}F^{mn}+\tfrac12 D_m\phi^a D^m\phi^a-\tfrac14\sum_{a\neq b}[\phi^a,\phi^b]^2\right)+\cdots, \tag{9}S=−(2πα′)2Tp​∫dp+1xTr​41​Fmn​Fmn+21​Dm​ϕaDmϕa−41​a=b∑​[ϕa,ϕb]2​+⋯,(9)

    with Dmϕa=∂mϕa+i[Am,ϕa]D_m\phi^a=\partial_m\phi^a+i[A_m,\phi^a]Dm​ϕa=∂m​ϕa+i[Am​,ϕa]. The remaining ⋯\cdots⋯ are fermions (in the superstring), higher-derivative terms, and stringy corrections.

    The scalar potential −14∑a≠bTr[ϕa,ϕb]2-\tfrac14\sum_{a\neq b}\mathrm{Tr}[\phi^a,\phi^b]^2−41​∑a=b​Tr[ϕa,ϕb]2 makes the geometry–gauge dictionary dynamical. It is positive (the commutator of two Hermitian matrices is anti-Hermitian, so Tr[ϕa,ϕb]2≤0\mathrm{Tr}[\phi^a,\phi^b]^2\le0Tr[ϕa,ϕb]2≤0) and vanishes exactly when all the ϕa\phi^aϕa commute — when they are simultaneously diagonalizable. The NNN common eigenvalues of ϕa\phi^aϕa are then the NNN brane positions in the aaa-th transverse direction, xia=2πα′ ϕiiax^a_i=2\pi\alpha'\,\phi^a_{ii}xia​=2πα′ϕiia​. So the flat directions of the potential are the moduli space of where the NNN branes sit. Turning on a diagonal ϕ\phiϕ separates the branes and Higgses U(N)→U(1)NU(N)\to U(1)^NU(N)→U(1)N: the off-diagonal [ij][ij][ij] fields eat the extra scalar and become massive with exactly the stretched-string mass (∣Δxij∣/(2πα′))2\big(|\Delta x_{ij}|/(2\pi\alpha')\big)^2(∣Δxij​∣/(2πα′))2 (Strings between parallel branes). Brane geometry and gauge-symmetry breaking are one fact seen from two sides.

    Relation to Born–Infeld

    For a single brane, the exact-in-FFF (constant, abelian) action is Born–Infeld, and (7) is its small-field limit (Born-Infeld action). For a stack there is a real subtlety: since FFF is now a matrix, the "det⁡\detdet" inside the square root needs a prescription for ordering non-commuting factors, and no fully consistent non-abelian Born–Infeld action is known — it may not exist in closed form. The symmetrized-trace proposal reproduces low orders but is not the last word. The reliable statement is the modest one: at low energy (small α′F\alpha' Fα′F) the matrix gauge field obeys U(N)U(N)U(N) Yang–Mills, equation (7). That is the load-bearing result of this note.

    Open questions

    • Non-abelian Born–Infeld beyond leading order. Simply symmetrizing the trace inside the determinant is not a unique or complete prescription; the correct all-orders matrix action, if it exists, is unsettled.
    • Precise numerical coupling. Equation (8) fixes the α′\alpha'α′, gsg_sgs​, and ppp scaling of gYM2g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2gYM2​; the exact numerical constant (conventions, π\piπ's) rides on the precise Dppp-brane tension normalization, tracked with the brane effective action.

    Exercise

    Verify the sign in the gauge-transformation law (5) is forced by the field-strength convention (3), and confirm one direction of the homogeneous transformation (6). Take the abelian-order part: expand Fmn′F'_{mn}Fmn′​ under Am↦UAmU−1+i(∂mU)U−1A_m\mapsto UA_mU^{-1}+i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}Am​↦UAm​U−1+i(∂m​U)U−1 and check that the ∂∂U\partial\partial U∂∂U terms cancel — then argue what would go wrong if the inhomogeneous term carried a −i-i−i instead.

    Solution

    Write Am′=UAmU−1+i(∂mU)U−1A'_m=UA_mU^{-1}+i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}Am′​=UAm​U−1+i(∂m​U)U−1 and compute the linear-derivative part of Fmn′=∂mAn′−∂nAm′+i[Am′,An′]F'_{mn}=\partial_m A'_n-\partial_n A'_m+i[A'_m,A'_n]Fmn′​=∂m​An′​−∂n​Am′​+i[Am′​,An′​].

    The clean route is Dm=∂m+iAmD_m=\partial_m+iA_mDm​=∂m​+iAm​ and Fmn=−i[Dm,Dn]F_{mn}=-i[D_m,D_n]Fmn​=−i[Dm​,Dn​]. Under (5), Dm↦UDmU−1D_m\mapsto UD_mU^{-1}Dm​↦UDm​U−1: acting on ψ↦Uψ\psi\mapsto U\psiψ↦Uψ, Dm′(Uψ)=(∂m+iAm′)(Uψ)=(∂mU)ψ+U∂mψ+iAm′Uψ,D'_m(U\psi)=(\partial_m+iA'_m)(U\psi)=(\partial_m U)\psi+U\partial_m\psi+iA'_m U\psi,Dm′​(Uψ)=(∂m​+iAm′​)(Uψ)=(∂m​U)ψ+U∂m​ψ+iAm′​Uψ, and iAm′U=iUAm−(∂mU)iA'_m U=iUA_m-(\partial_m U)iAm′​U=iUAm​−(∂m​U) from (5) cancels the (∂mU)ψ(\partial_m U)\psi(∂m​U)ψ, leaving Dm′(Uψ)=U(Dmψ)D'_m(U\psi)=U(D_m\psi)Dm′​(Uψ)=U(Dm​ψ). Hence Fmn′=−i[Dm′,Dn′]=U(−i[Dm,Dn])U−1=UFmnU−1F'_{mn}=-i[D'_m,D'_n]=U(-i[D_m,D_n])U^{-1}=UF_{mn}U^{-1}Fmn′​=−i[Dm′​,Dn′​]=U(−i[Dm​,Dn​])U−1=UFmn​U−1, which is (6). The ∂m∂nU\partial_m\partial_n U∂m​∂n​U pieces cancel because they enter only through the symmetric part of Dm′Dn′D'_mD'_nDm′​Dn′​, which the commutator drops.

    Direct check of the cancellation. If you insist on expanding F′F'F′ by hand, the two purely-inhomogeneous contributions are: ∂mAn′−∂nAm′ ⊃ i(∂mU)U−1(∂nU)U−1−i(∂nU)U−1(∂mU)U−1\partial_m A'_n-\partial_n A'_m\ \supset\ i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}(\partial_n U)U^{-1}-i(\partial_n U)U^{-1}(\partial_m U)U^{-1}∂m​An′​−∂n​Am′​ ⊃ i(∂m​U)U−1(∂n​U)U−1−i(∂n​U)U−1(∂m​U)U−1 (using ∂mU−1=−U−1(∂mU)U−1\partial_m U^{-1}=-U^{-1}(\partial_m U)U^{-1}∂m​U−1=−U−1(∂m​U)U−1; the symmetric ∂m∂nU\partial_m\partial_n U∂m​∂n​U drops on antisymmetrizing), and i[Am′,An′] ⊃ i[i(∂mU)U−1,i(∂nU)U−1]=−i(∂mU)U−1(∂nU)U−1+i(∂nU)U−1(∂mU)U−1.i[A'_m,A'_n]\ \supset\ i\big[i(\partial_m U)U^{-1},i(\partial_n U)U^{-1}\big]=-i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}(\partial_n U)U^{-1}+i(\partial_n U)U^{-1}(\partial_m U)U^{-1}.i[Am′​,An′​] ⊃ i[i(∂m​U)U−1,i(∂n​U)U−1]=−i(∂m​U)U−1(∂n​U)U−1+i(∂n​U)U−1(∂m​U)U−1. These are equal and opposite, so they cancel term by term. The cross terms reassemble into U(i[Am,An])U−1U(i[A_m,A_n])U^{-1}U(i[Am​,An​])U−1, and the abelian curvature rotates to U(∂mAn−∂nAm)U−1U(\partial_m A_n-\partial_n A_m)U^{-1}U(∂m​An​−∂n​Am​)U−1, giving (6).

    Why the sign is forced. The +i+i+i in (5) is not a choice: Dm=∂m+iAmD_m=\partial_m+iA_mDm​=∂m​+iAm​ transforms covariantly (Dm↦UDmU−1D_m\mapsto UD_mU^{-1}Dm​↦UDm​U−1) only with Am↦UAmU−1+i(∂mU)U−1A_m\mapsto UA_mU^{-1}+i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}Am​↦UAm​U−1+i(∂m​U)U−1. If instead one used Am↦UAmU−1−i(∂mU)U−1A_m\mapsto UA_mU^{-1}-i(\partial_m U)U^{-1}Am​↦UAm​U−1−i(∂m​U)U−1 while keeping F=∂A+i[A,A]F=\partial A+i[A,A]F=∂A+i[A,A], the two inhomogeneous contributions above would add rather than cancel, leaving a leftover ∼(∂U)U−1(∂U)U−1\sim(\partial U)U^{-1}(\partial U)U^{-1}∼(∂U)U−1(∂U)U−1 in F′F'F′ — so FFF would not transform homogeneously and Tr F2\mathrm{Tr}\,F^2TrF2 would not be gauge-invariant. The −i-i−i inhomogeneous term is self-consistent only if paired with the opposite bracket sign F=∂A−i[A,A]F=\partial A-i[A,A]F=∂A−i[A,A]. The pair used here — F=+i[A,A]F=+i[A,A]F=+i[A,A] with gauge term +i(∂U)U−1+i(\partial U)U^{-1}+i(∂U)U−1, matching Dmϕ=∂mϕ+i[Am,ϕ]D_m\phi=\partial_m\phi+i[A_m,\phi]Dm​ϕ=∂m​ϕ+i[Am​,ϕ] — is the standard one; mixing the two conventions (as an earlier draft did) is the error this exercise guards against.
