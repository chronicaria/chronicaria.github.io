Nine-cat: top-45 median, with elite FG% and rebounds but ordinary blocks for a center. Points: A-, roughly top 30-45. Dynasty: 8.5/10, top-30 caliber if his long-term team and role settle.

The 18.8-point median is a regular-season number and should be read as one. His scoring has already proven matchup-fragile against playoff-caliber defensive centers — see the postseason section — so the distribution around it is wider on the downside than a 70-game .650 shooting line suggests.

Role and evidence

Duren averaged 19.5 points, 10.5 rebounds, 2.0 assists, 0.8 steals, and 0.8 blocks on .650 shooting in 28.2 minutes over 70 games, per Basketball-Reference. Detroit's All-NBA announcement records his third-team selection after the breakout.

The projection assumes he re-signs and starts. The reporting on whether that happens is split. Locked On Pistons on July 17 summarized Athletic and Jake Fischer reporting that Detroit viewed Duren as its top priority and that talks had moved positively, while explicitly treating a short or descending structure as analysis rather than a reported agreement. Against that: Duren was reportedly underwhelmed by Detroit's opening offer, his representatives are targeting roughly $40-45M annually against a 25% max, and he took meetings with the Lakers and Kings about sign-and-trade constructions. ESPN reported Detroit will not entertain a sign-and-trade and will match any offer sheet; Sacramento's only route would have required Domantas Sabonis. Trajan Langdon said in mid-June that talks with both Duren and Ausar Thompson had not been progressing, and the Lakers subsequently pivoted to a sign-and-trade for Walker Kessler, which removed Duren's most credible outside bidder and cut his leverage.

That leaves a live second branch: the one-year $9.6M qualifying offer, which would make him an unrestricted free agent in 2027 (Forbes, July 18). The QO branch does not obviously cost him minutes — he would still open at center — but it changes the incentive structure around a player whose team has just seen him shrink in a playoff series, and it is the branch most likely to end with him somewhere other than Detroit in 2027. Until a transaction is official, roster confidence is medium and every redraft rank should carry a holdout/team-change branch.

2026 postseason

Duren's playoff collapse is the single most important thing about his 2026-27 price and the dossier previously did not mention it. He averaged 10.2 points and 8.5 rebounds on .514 shooting in 30.1 minutes over 14 games (StatMuse), against 19.5 and 10.5 on .650 in the regular season. The 9.3-point drop is the largest by a player in an All-Star season in NBA history other than Wilt Chamberlain in 1962. Against Cleveland in the conference semifinals — a series Detroit lost in seven — he averaged 9.4 points and 6.6 rebounds, and Detroit benched him in Game 5; Paul Reed outscored him on materially fewer minutes (SI).

Two things keep this from being a straightforward projection cut. His playoff minutes went up, not down (30.1 versus 28.2), so this was not primarily a rotation demotion; and his blocks rose to 1.2, so the defensive value did not vanish with the scoring. What collapsed was the finishing — .650 to .514 — which is consistent with a lob-and-putback diet being taken away by set defenses and size, and inconsistent with a general talent re-rating. The regular-season projection therefore holds, because 82 games are mostly not played against Cleveland's frontcourt. The correct adjustment is to the shape of the distribution, not its center.

Collins can space and play small-ball center, but his arrival should not displace a signed Duren. Stewart's departure removes a defensively dominant backup and makes Duren's foul discipline and rim protection more important to both team wins and minutes.

Range

  • Ceiling: 21/12 on 67% FG, a block per game, 72 appearances, and top-30 value.
  • Floor: negotiation delay, a qualifying-offer year, or sign-and-trade; on-court floor is 27 minutes if Collins/Reed take more center possessions.
  • Role-contraction floor: the playoff version — mid-teens scoring on low-.500s shooting with Reed absorbing fourth-quarter center minutes. Detroit has already run this lineup arrangement in a high-leverage series and chose it deliberately, so it is a demonstrated option rather than a hypothetical one.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Memphis, one season (2021-22). 29 games, 25.3 minutes, 12.0 points, 8.1 rebounds, 1.3 assists, 2.1 blocks, 0.8 steals on .597 from the field and .625 from the line. First Team All-AAC and AAC Freshman of the Year; Memphis reached the NCAA tournament for the first time since 2014. He reclassified from the 2022 high school class to 2021 on 2021-08-06, which is the single most important fact about his career shape — it made him one of the youngest players in the 2022 draft and means his fourth NBA season was played at an age most lottery picks spend as sophomores. Draft: 2022, first round, 13th overall, Charlotte Hornets. Rights traded on draft night to New York, then immediately on to Detroit.

Honors: All-Rookie second team (2022-23), All-Star reserve (named 2026-02-01), All-NBA third team (2025-26).

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-category ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag could not be retrieved for this dossier, and no verifiable per-season rank is asserted here. What the raw lines support is a profile whose category value has always been decided by two cells rather than by scoring. The positives are stable and large: .652 career from the field on high volume is a top-of-league percentage anchor, and double-digit rebounds have been there since year two. The drags are equally structural: zero threes in 276 career games, blocks that have never reached 1.2, and a free-throw percentage that has swung .611, .790, .669, .747 — on a center's attempt volume that swing is worth several rounds of category value by itself, and it is the main reason his 9-cat finishes have been noisier than his counting stats suggest. His points-league shape is materially better than his category shape and always has been, because points formats pay for the 19.5 points and 10.5 rebounds and charge nothing for the missing threes and blocks. The 2025-26 season is the first in which the scoring is large enough that both formats agree he is valuable.

Durability: 67, 61, 78, 70. The cause through his first two seasons was one joint: ankles. As a rookie he lost time to a left ankle sprain, a right ankle sprain, and a stretch of bilateral ankle soreness, plus a game to cervical whiplash. In 2023-24 he reaggravated the ankle early and missed about five games, returning 2023-11-23, then sprained the left ankle against Memphis on 2023-12-07 and missed roughly another eight, returning on Christmas. 2024-25 was his clean season at 78 games and no ankle event of consequence. In 2025-26 he sprained the right ankle against Miami in early January 2026 and was shut down about a week, then handled right knee injury management late, sitting the regular-season finale before being cleared for the playoffs. The trend is favorable — the ankle problem looks like a young-player issue he has grown out of rather than a chronic one — but the late-season knee management is new and worth watching rather than dismissing.

Trajectory: The 2025-26 leap is real and it is not a minutes illusion. His per-36 scoring went 13.2, 17.1, 16.3, 24.9 — an eight-and-a-half point per-36 jump in one year while his minutes rose only two. That is a change in what he does, not how long he does it, and the reporting supports the mechanism: he added self-creation off the dribble to a diet that had been lob finishes and putbacks, and finished second in the league in paint points per game at 14.6 while remaining the only player to average 19 and 10 on better than 60 percent shooting. Two costs came with it. Rebounds per-36 drifted down from 14.4 to 13.4 as he spent more possessions initiating, and assists per-36 fell from 3.7 to 2.6 — the short-roll passing hub role he grew into under the 2024-25 arrangement got partially traded away for scoring. He turns 23 in November 2026, which places him well before the normal aging curve peak with four NBA seasons already banked, so the base case is continued improvement rather than regression to the pre-leap line. For 2026-27 the two swing factors are the free-throw percentage, which is the difference between a top-40 and a top-70 category season, and whether Detroit's frontcourt arrangement lets him hold 28-plus minutes; the scoring skill itself now looks load-bearing enough to survive either.
