Nine-category: late-round rebounds/FG/assists center, best where FT% is already protected or punted. ESPN-default points: fringe standard-league draft target; ESPN's April list ranked him 135th. Dynasty: Hashtag No. 85, but mostly a contender buy because age and Mara/Sorber limit the long runway.

Why the line looks like this

Hartenstein averaged 9.2 points, 9.4 rebounds, 3.5 assists, 1.0 steal and 0.8 blocks in 24.2 minutes over 47 games in 2025-26, shooting 62.2% from the floor. Multiple soleus/calf management absences explain the conservative 58-game base. Oklahoma City nevertheless committed three years and a reported $75 million, strong evidence that he remains a core matchup piece rather than a bridge to Aday Mara.

Chet Holmgren can close at center, Jaylin Williams supplies spacing, and recovering Thomas Sorber adds another developmental body. Hartenstein's fantasy value therefore comes from per-minute category density, not 30-plus minutes.

Sources: NBA re-signing report, July 6, 2025-26 Thunder stats, ESPN points rankings, Hashtag dynasty.

Category shape

  • Wins: rebounds, FG%, assists and steals from center.
  • Useful: blocks and low scoring-volume efficiency.
  • Costs: no threes, weak FT% and moderate turnovers for limited points.
  • Build fit: punt-FT or guard-heavy builds needing rebounds without sacrificing assists.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: recurring soleus management limits him below 45 games and 19 minutes.
  • Base: 58 games at 23 minutes with 9-9-3 and 1.8 stocks.
  • Ceiling: 65 healthy games at 27 minutes approach a double-double with four assists and top-90 value.

Career record

Pre-NBA: No college. Born in Oregon and raised in Germany, Hartenstein came up through Artland Dragons, winning a U-16 German championship and MVP in 2013-14. His senior club record before the draft is short: 14 games for Artland in Germany's third-tier 2. Bundesliga ProB in 2015-16 at 11.6 points, 8.9 rebounds, 2.1 blocks, 1.6 steals, and 1.4 assists, then a season at Žalgiris Kaunas in 2016-17 where he averaged 4.7 points and 3.5 rebounds across 29 LKL games as a teenage reserve and won both the Lithuanian League title and the inaugural King Mindaugas Cup. He also appeared in EuroLeague for Žalgiris that year, but in minutes too limited to produce a meaningful per-game line, and the numbers are not verified here. Draft: 2017, second round, 43rd overall, Houston Rockets — not traded. He did not debut until 2018-19, spending the intervening year developing in the G League, where he won a championship and Finals MVP with Rio Grande Valley and recorded a triple-double (12 points, 16 rebounds, 11 assists) on February 7, 2019.

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-cat ranks are not verifiable from the sources reachable for this entry, and no specific finishes are asserted. What the table establishes is a category profile that arrived fully formed in 2023-24 and has not changed since: rebounds and field goal percentage as the anchors, a genuinely unusual assist rate for a center, and a full steal per game — four columns from one roster slot, which is why he drafts above where his 9.2 points suggest. The costs are equally fixed. He has made 24 three-pointers in eight NBA seasons, so that column is a hard zero rather than a weakness, and the free-throw percentage is a live tax that got worse in 2025-26 at .610. He is materially better in categories than in points formats — ESPN had him 135th on its April points board — because a low-volume, high-efficiency, no-three center is close to the worst possible shape for points scoring while being close to an ideal complementary piece in 9-cat. The build that maximizes him is punt-FT or punt-threes with guard-heavy scoring already secured.

Durability: GP by season: 28, 23, 46, 68, 82, 75, 57, 47. The early low totals are role, not health — he was a third-string big in Houston and a fringe roster player in Denver. The relevant history starts in 2021-22, and it splits cleanly: 68, 82, and 75 games across the Clippers and Knicks years, then 57 and 47 in Oklahoma City. The 2024-25 shortfall has a single discrete cause — a non-displaced fracture in his left hand suffered in a preseason game at Denver in October 2024, which cost him roughly the first two months. The 2025-26 shortfall is the concerning one because it is soft-tissue and recurrent: he started the Thunder's first 19 games averaging 12.2 points, 10.7 rebounds, 3.4 assists, and 1.3 steals on 67.1 percent shooting, strained the right soleus on November 26, missed six games through December 10, then reaggravated it and sat an eleven-game stretch running into January. Calf and soleus strains recur at high rates in heavy 7-footers, and a second episode within the same season is the specific pattern that justifies discounting his games rather than his rates.

Trajectory: The important thing the table shows is that Hartenstein's 2025-26 decline was not a decline. Per 36 minutes his rebounding actually rose across the last three seasons — 11.8, then 13.8, then 14.0 — and his assist rate rose in a straight line from 3.6 to 4.9 to 5.2, which is a passing volume almost no center in the league matches. Only the scoring rate dipped, from 14.5 to 13.7 per 36, and it did so while field goal percentage climbed from .581 to .622. Every counting-stat drop between 2024-25 and 2025-26 is explained by the 3.7-minute reduction and the 10 missing games; nothing in the rate profile suggests an aging player. That matters because the two forces on his 2026-27 value are entirely structural rather than physical: Oklahoma City has Holmgren able to close at center, Jaylin Williams for spacing lineups, and Mara and Sorber developing behind him, so the minutes ceiling is set by roster construction, and the games ceiling is set by a soleus that has already failed twice. He enters his age-28 season, which for a low-usage, high-efficiency big is still inside the plateau rather than the decline. Project him as a rate-stable player with a volume problem — the per-minute production is bankable, the exposure is not.
