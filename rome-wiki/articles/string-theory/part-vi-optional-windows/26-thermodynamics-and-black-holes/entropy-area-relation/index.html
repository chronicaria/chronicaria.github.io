    Entropy-area relation

    A black hole's entropy is one quarter of its horizon area, measured in Planck-area tiles : S_B/k = A/(4\ell_P^2) . Given only the Hawking temperature and one line of calculus, this drops out. The shock is that entropy scales with area , not volume — and that a string of the right mass carries the same entropy.

    What a black hole must be counting

    Entropy counts microstates. Throw a bucket of hot gas into a black hole and it vanishes behind the horizon, carrying its entropy with it. The second law forbids entropy from just disappearing, so the black hole must have swallowed that entropy — it has entropy of its own. But a classical black hole is bald: it is fully specified by mass, charge, and spin, a handful of numbers. So what is it counting? That is the mystery this module chases. Here we compute the number; the string picture at the end says what the microstates might be.

    Just-in-time GR: the Schwarzschild radius

    A black hole is matter collapsed inside an event horizon — a sphere of radius RRR from which nothing, not even light, escapes. Estimate RRR with pure Newtonian bookkeeping: a particle of mass mmm sitting at the horizon has rest energy mc2mc^2mc2 and gravitational potential energy −GMm/R-GMm/R−GMm/R. The horizon is the edge where a particle can just barely not escape, i.e. its total energy is zero:

    mc2−GMmR=0  ⟹  R≃GMc2.mc^2 - \frac{GMm}{R} = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; R \simeq \frac{GM}{c^2}.mc2−RGMm​=0⟹R≃c2GM​.

    Full general relativity fixes a missing factor of 2 the Newtonian argument can't see:

    R = \frac{2GM}{c^2}. \tag{1}

    R∝MR \propto MR∝M: double the mass, double the radius. (The Sun's would be ~3 km; Earth's ~1 cm.) We take the factor of 2 from GR — the Newtonian estimate only nails the scale, not the coefficient.

    Hawking temperature → Bekenstein entropy

    Black holes are not truly black: they glow with thermal radiation at the Hawking temperature THawT_{\rm Haw}THaw​ (the subscript keeps it distinct from the Hagedorn temperature THT_HTH​ met earlier in this module), set by the strength of gravity at the horizon (the "surface gravity"), which falls off as 1/M1/M1/M. So heavier black holes are colder. Restoring ℏ,c,G\hbar, c, Gℏ,c,G:

    kT_{\rm Haw} = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi GM}. \tag{2}

    We quote this — deriving it needs quantum field theory in curved spacetime, beyond this course. But once we have a temperature and an energy, thermodynamics does the rest. The energy is just the rest energy E=Mc2E = Mc^2E=Mc2, and the first law dE=THaw dSBdE = T_{\rm Haw}\,dS_BdE=THaw​dSB​ relates a change in energy to a change in entropy:

    c2 dM=THaw dSB=ℏc38πGM dSBk.c^2\,dM = T_{\rm Haw}\,dS_B = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi GM}\,\frac{dS_B}{k}.c2dM=THaw​dSB​=8πGMℏc3​kdSB​​.

    Solve for dSBdS_BdSB​ and use 2M dM=d(M2)2M\,dM = d(M^2)2MdM=d(M2):

    dSBk=8πGMℏc dM=4πGℏc d(M2).\frac{dS_B}{k} = \frac{8\pi GM}{\hbar c}\,dM = \frac{4\pi G}{\hbar c}\,d(M^2).kdSB​​=ℏc8πGM​dM=ℏc4πG​d(M2).

    The factor of 2 in d(M2)=2M dMd(M^2) = 2M\,dMd(M2)=2MdM is what turns 8π8\pi8π into 4π4\pi4π. Integrate, taking a massless black hole to have zero entropy:

    \boxed{\;\frac{S_B}{k} = \frac{4\pi G}{\hbar c}\,M^2\;} \tag{3}

    Entropy goes as mass-squared. Remember this — a free string will give S∝MS \propto MS∝M instead, and the mismatch is the whole point of the last section.

    Rewriting as area: the A/4A/4A/4 law

    Now trade M2M^2M2 for the horizon area. With A=4πR2A = 4\pi R^2A=4πR2 and R=2GM/c2R = 2GM/c^2R=2GM/c2,

    A=4π(2GMc2)2=16πG2M2c4  ⟹  M2=Ac416πG2.A = 4\pi\left(\frac{2GM}{c^2}\right)^2 = \frac{16\pi G^2 M^2}{c^4} \;\Longrightarrow\; M^2 = \frac{A c^4}{16\pi G^2}.A=4π(c22GM​)2=c416πG2M2​⟹M2=16πG2Ac4​.

    Substitute into (3):

    SBk=4πGℏc⋅Ac416πG2=c34ℏG A=A4ℓP2,\frac{S_B}{k} = \frac{4\pi G}{\hbar c}\cdot\frac{A c^4}{16\pi G^2} = \frac{c^3}{4\hbar G}\,A = \frac{A}{4\ell_P^2},kSB​​=ℏc4πG​⋅16πG2Ac4​=4ℏGc3​A=4ℓP2​A​,

    where the Planck length is ℓP2=ℏG/c3\ell_P^2 = \hbar G/c^3ℓP2​=ℏG/c3 (the only length you can build from ℏ,c,G\hbar, c, Gℏ,c,G). The 16π16\pi16π against the 4π4\pi4π leaves the clean 1/41/41/4:

    \boxed{\;\frac{S_B}{k} = \frac{A}{4\ell_P^2}\;} \tag{4}

    This is the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy. Given (1) and (2), the area law is one-variable calculus — nothing more. Two features make it profound:

    • It scales with area, not volume. Every ordinary system you know has extensive entropy: pack twice the volume with gas, get twice the entropy. A black hole breaks this — its information content lives on the surface. Picture the horizon tiled by squares of side ℓP\ell_PℓP​; the entropy is one bit's worth of freedom per tile, give or take. This is the first hint of holography (see Bulk versus boundary observables, AdS-CFT correspondence).
    • It is astronomically large. ℓP2≈10−70 m2\ell_P^2 \approx 10^{-70}\,\mathrm{m}^2ℓP2​≈10−70m2, so a horizon of any macroscopic size has an unthinkable number of tiles — hence microstates. A solar-mass black hole has SB/k∼1077S_B/k \sim 10^{77}SB​/k∼1077.

    The chain is short: entropy thrown in cannot vanish, so the black hole keeps it; the Hawking temperature turns that stored entropy into A/4A/4A/4 in Planck units; and — against every ordinary system — the total scales with the horizon's area, not its volume.

    The string–black-hole entropy comparison

    Now the string-theory payoff. A free string of mass MMM has entropy linear in MMM (from Hagedorn temperature, in ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1 units):

    \frac{S_{\rm str}}{k} = 4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}\,M, \qquad\text{versus}\qquad \frac{S_B}{k} = 4\pi GM^2. \tag{5}

    MMM versus M2M^2M2 — they look incompatible. But they should differ, because each is computed in a regime where the other is nonsense:

    • Sstr∝MS_{\rm str} \propto MSstr​∝M is a free-string (gs=0g_s=0gs​=0) result. A string's mass is proportional to its length (M∼L/α′M \sim L/\alpha'M∼L/α′, since the tension is ∼1/α′\sim 1/\alpha'∼1/α′), and entropy is extensive in length — count the ways to fold a random walk of L/ℓsL/\ell_sL/ℓs​ "string bits," each pointing any direction, and you get Sstr/k∼Mα′=MℓsS_{\rm str}/k \sim M\sqrt{\alpha'} = M\ell_sSstr​/k∼Mα′​=Mℓs​. No gravity anywhere.
    • SB∝M2S_B \propto M^2SB​∝M2 needs gravity (gs≠0g_s \neq 0gs​=0). Newton's constant itself is set by the coupling: G∼gs2α′=gs2ℓs2G \sim g_s^2\alpha' = g_s^2\ell_s^2G∼gs2​α′=gs2​ℓs2​. At gs=0g_s=0gs​=0 gravity switches off, R=2GM/c2→0R = 2GM/c^2 \to 0R=2GM/c2→0, and there is no black hole to speak of.

    So they live at opposite ends of a dial. The reconciliation is to slide the coupling gsg_sgs​ down while holding the entropy fixed, and watch the black hole shrink into a string.

    Fix a big black hole (S0S_0S0​, mass M0M_0M0​, radius R0≫ℓsR_0 \gg \ell_sR0​≫ℓs​) at some coupling gs,0g_{s,0}gs,0​, with

    S0k∼gs,02ℓs2M02,R0∼GM0∼gs,02ℓs2M0.\frac{S_0}{k} \sim g_{s,0}^2\ell_s^2 M_0^2, \qquad R_0 \sim GM_0 \sim g_{s,0}^2\ell_s^2 M_0.kS0​​∼gs,02​ℓs2​M02​,R0​∼GM0​∼gs,02​ℓs2​M0​.

    Turn gsg_sgs​ down slowly. Holding S/k∼gs2ℓs2M2S/k \sim g_s^2\ell_s^2 M^2S/k∼gs2​ℓs2​M2 fixed forces M∼1/gsM \sim 1/g_sM∼1/gs​ (the mass grows), while the radius R∼gs2ℓs2M∼gsR \sim g_s^2\ell_s^2 M \sim g_sR∼gs2​ℓs2​M∼gs​ shrinks.     At this correspondence point both entropies land on the same value. Black-hole side:

    SBk∼gs,∗2ℓs2M∗2∼gs,∗2ℓs2⋅1gs,∗4ℓs2=1gs,∗2.\frac{S_B}{k} \sim g_{s,*}^2\ell_s^2 M_*^2 \sim g_{s,*}^2\ell_s^2\cdot\frac{1}{g_{s,*}^4\ell_s^2} = \frac{1}{g_{s,*}^2}.kSB​​∼gs,∗2​ℓs2​M∗2​∼gs,∗2​ℓs2​⋅gs,∗4​ℓs2​1​=gs,∗2​1​.

    \frac{S_{\rm str}}{k} \sim M_*\ell_s \sim \frac{1}{g_{s,*}^2\ell_s}\cdot\ell_s = \frac{1}{g_{s,*}^2}. \tag{6}

    They agree — parametrically, SB∼Sstr∼1/gs,∗2S_B \sim S_{\rm str} \sim 1/g_{s,*}^2SB​∼Sstr​∼1/gs,∗2​. The MMM-vs-M2M^2M2 conflict dissolves because it compared the two objects at different couplings; matched at the crossover, they are the same entropy. This is evidence, not proof, that a Schwarzschild black hole is what a hugely excited string looks like once gravity is turned on — the black hole is the string, at strong coupling.

    Open questions

    • Only powers, not coefficients. The matching in (6) fixes how SSS scales with gs,∗g_{s,*}gs,∗​ but drops every numerical factor — it cannot reproduce the exact 1/41/41/4 in SB=A/(4ℓP2)S_B = A/(4\ell_P^2)SB​=A/(4ℓP2​). To match a coefficient you need a black hole protected by supersymmetry, where the zero-coupling count survives to nonzero gsg_sgs​ — see Supersymmetric black-hole counting.
    • Hawking's temperature is an input, not a result. Equation (2) is quoted; the entropy then follows by pure thermodynamics. Deriving THawT_{\rm Haw}THaw​ needs quantum fields in curved spacetime.
    • Why area, not volume? The string-bits picture explains S∝M∝LS \propto M \propto LS∝M∝L for a string, but says nothing about the horizon area law. Area-vs-volume is a separate, deeper fact whose explanation is holographic — see AdS-CFT correspondence.

    Exercise

    The horizon of a Schwarzschild black hole feels a gravitational field ∣g⃗∣=c4/(4GM)|\vec g| = c^4/(4GM)∣g​∣=c4/(4GM) at its surface (Newtonian, ∣g⃗∣=GM/R2|\vec g| = GM/R^2∣g​∣=GM/R2 with R=2GM/c2R = 2GM/c^2R=2GM/c2).
