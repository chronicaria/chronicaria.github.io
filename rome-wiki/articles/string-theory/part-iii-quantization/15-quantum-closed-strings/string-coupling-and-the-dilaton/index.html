    String coupling and the dilaton

    The dilaton \phi — the trace state at the massless closed-string level — sets the strength of every string interaction: the string coupling is g_s=e^{\langle\phi\rangle} . How strongly strings talk to each other is not a number dialed in by hand; it is the value of a dynamical field .

    Two consequences follow.

    • gsg_sgs​ is not a constant of the theory. It is set by a field with its own equations of motion — in principle computed, not measured. That is the property you want in a candidate theory of everything.
    • The perturbation series is a sum over topologies. Each handle added to a closed-string worldsheet costs a factor gs2g_s^2gs2​, so at weak coupling (gs≪1g_s\ll1gs​≪1) the simplest surfaces dominate, just as low-order loop diagrams dominate a weakly coupled field theory.

    What a coupling constant is: the electromagnetic analogy

    A coupling constant is a dimensionless number that controls how strong an interaction is. We set ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1 throughout, with Heaviside–Lorentz units for electromagnetism; the textbook case is then the fine-structure constant, α≡e24π≃1137.\alpha\equiv\frac{e^2}{4\pi}\simeq\frac{1}{137}.α≡4πe2​≃1371​. It fixes, for instance, the hydrogen ground-state binding energy: with Bohr radius a0=4π/(mee2)=1/(αme)a_0=4\pi/(m_e e^2)=1/(\alpha m_e)a0​=4π/(me​e2)=1/(αme​), E=e24π 12a0=12 α2me≃13.6 eV.E=\frac{e^2}{4\pi}\,\frac{1}{2a_0}=\frac12\,\alpha^2 m_e\simeq 13.6\ \text{eV}.E=4πe2​2a0​1​=21​α2me​≃13.6 eV. Send α→0\alpha\to0α→0 and electromagnetism switches off: a0→∞a_0\to\inftya0​→∞, E→0E\to0E→0, the atom unbinds. A dimensionless coupling is the on/off knob for an interaction.

    The string coupling gsg_sgs​

    String theory has one dimensionful parameter, α′\alpha'α′ (equivalently the string length ℓs=α′\ell_s=\sqrt{\alpha'}ℓs​=α′​), and one dimensionless coupling for closed strings, gsg_sgs​. If gs=0g_s=0gs​=0, strings do not interact — and since the graviton is a closed-string state (Graviton antisymmetric tensor and dilaton), switching off string interactions switches off gravity: Newton's constant must vanish with gsg_sgs​. More sharply, the leading gravitational interaction between two sources is a single closed string exchanged — emitted at one, absorbed at the other — and each emission costs a factor of gsg_sgs​ (the topology count below confirms this), so G∼gs2G\sim g_s^2G∼gs2​. Dimensional analysis supplies the powers of α′\alpha'α′.

    Why gs=eϕg_s=e^{\phi}gs​=eϕ: the dilaton–curvature coupling

    Turning on a background of the string's own massless fields adds terms to the worldsheet action, and the dilaton's term is unlike the others: it couples ϕ(X)\phi(X)ϕ(X) to the intrinsic curvature of the worldsheet Σ\SigmaΣ, Sϕ=14π∫Σd2σ h ϕ(X) R(2)S_\phi=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_\Sigma d^2\sigma\,\sqrt h\,\phi(X)\,R^{(2)}Sϕ​=4π1​∫Σ​d2σh​ϕ(X)R(2) (Euclidean worldsheet, so the path-integral weight is e−Se^{-S}e−S). Here R(2)R^{(2)}R(2) is the Ricci scalar of the surface the string sweeps out — not of spacetime. Of the three massless fields (GμνG_{\mu\nu}Gμν​, BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​, ϕ\phiϕ), only the dilaton couples to worldsheet geometry rather than to ∂X\partial X∂X.

    Take a constant background ϕ(X)=ϕ0\phi(X)=\phi_0ϕ(X)=ϕ0​. Then ϕ0\phi_0ϕ0​ pulls out of the integral, and the Gauss–Bonnet theorem says what remains is a pure topological number, the Euler characteristic: 14π∫Σh R(2)=χ(Σ),χ=2−2g  (closed, oriented, genus g).\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_\Sigma \sqrt h\,R^{(2)}=\chi(\Sigma),\qquad \chi=2-2g\ \ (\text{closed, oriented, genus }g).4π1​∫Σ​h​R(2)=χ(Σ),χ=2−2g  (closed, oriented, genus g). So Sϕ=ϕ0 χ(Σ)S_\phi=\phi_0\,\chi(\Sigma)Sϕ​=ϕ0​χ(Σ): a constant dilaton contributes nothing local — it counts topology.     Each added handle multiplies the amplitude by gs2g_s^2gs2​. This is the closed-string loop expansion: handles play the role of loops and gs2g_s^2gs2​ is the expansion parameter. A handle is an emitted and reabsorbed closed string, so its cost gs2g_s^2gs2​ splits as one gsg_sgs​ per emission — the gravitational-exchange count from above, now made precise. (Vertex operators for the external strings contribute a fixed power of gsg_sgs​ common to all topologies; the genus dependence is the robust part.)

    This is also why the coupling is eϕe^{\phi}eϕ rather than, say, ϕ\phiϕ itself: the dilaton enters amplitudes only through e−ϕ0χe^{-\phi_0\chi}e−ϕ0​χ, so each handle multiplies by (eϕ0)2(e^{\phi_0})^2(eϕ0​)2, never by a power of ϕ0\phi_0ϕ0​. Where the dilaton background is large and positive the coupling is strong (gs≫1g_s\gg1gs​≫1 and the genus expansion breaks down); where it is very negative the coupling is weak (gs≪1g_s\ll1gs​≪1 and perturbation theory is reliable). A non-constant background even lets the coupling vary from place to place in spacetime.

    The payoff: the coupling is a field

    Add to a free Hamiltonian H0H_0H0​ an interaction g Hintg\,H_{\text{int}}gHint​ with ggg dimensionless. If ggg is declared a constant, you must fix it by measurement. If instead ggg is a dynamical field with its own dynamics HgH_gHg​, you cannot fix it by hand even if you want to: the equations of motion of H0+g Hint+HgH_0+g\,H_{\text{int}}+H_gH0​+gHint​+Hg​ decide its value. String theory is exactly the second case — the closed-string coupling is the vacuum value of the dilaton: gs=e⟨ϕ⟩.g_s=e^{\langle\phi\rangle}.gs​=e⟨ϕ⟩. So gsg_sgs​ is not an adjustable parameter of the theory; it could in principle be calculated. The humble trace scalar is operationally the most consequential of the three massless states: the graviton supplies gravity, but the dilaton decides how strongly everything — gravity included — couples.

    The catch: at this order nothing generates a potential for ϕ\phiϕ, so ⟨ϕ⟩\langle\phi\rangle⟨ϕ⟩ is a flat direction — every constant value solves the equations of motion equally well. Pinning it down is the moduli-stabilization problem.

    Two relations worth carrying

    • Open vs closed coupling: go2∼gsg_o^2\sim g_sgo2​∼gs​. In a theory with both, the open coupling is not independent. With boundaries, Gauss–Bonnet gives χ=2−2g−b\chi=2-2g-bχ=2−2g−b, so each boundary costs one power of gsg_sgs​: the disc (χ=1\chi=1χ=1) carries gs−1g_s^{-1}gs−1​, the annulus (χ=0\chi=0χ=0) carries gs0g_s^{0}gs0​. Read the same step diagrammatically: an open string propagates on a strip — topologically a disc — and its one-loop self-energy, where the string splits into two open strings that rejoin, is an annulus. That is one extra boundary, hence one factor of gsg_sgs​; but as a diagram it contains two three-open-string vertices, hence go2g_o^2go2​. Matching the two counts gives go2∼gsg_o^2\sim g_sgo2​∼gs​. (The same annulus, read in the other channel, is a single closed string exchanged between the two boundaries — an open-string loop secretly contains gravity.)
    • Four-dimensional Newton constant. Curl up 6 of the 10 dimensions on a volume V(6)V^{(6)}V(6): G∼G(10)/V(6)G\sim G^{(10)}/V^{(6)}G∼G(10)/V(6). For a compactification of fixed volume in string units, G∼gs2 α′G\sim g_s^2\,\alpha'G∼gs2​α′. The observed 4D gravitational strength is tied to both gsg_sgs​ and the size of the hidden dimensions.

    Connection to the spectrum

    The dilaton entered this module as the trace part S′δIJS'\delta_{IJ}S′δIJ​ of the massless tensor decomposition (Graviton antisymmetric tensor and dilaton, Closed-string mass formula). A single scalar in the spectrum governing the global coupling has no analogue in point-particle field theory, where couplings are just numbers in the Lagrangian. The dilaton is the bridge from the kinematics of the spectrum (what states exist) to the dynamics of interactions (how strongly they talk).

    Open questions

    • The normalization of ϕ\phiϕ, the string-frame/Einstein-frame distinction, and the full spacetime effective action are later sigma-model topics; this note fixes only the scaling.
    • Why the dilaton potential is flat at leading order, and how a realistic vacuum stabilizes ⟨ϕ⟩\langle\phi\rangle⟨ϕ⟩, is the moduli-stabilization problem.

    Exercise

    Using only Gauss–Bonnet, show that adding one handle to an oriented worldsheet multiplies a string amplitude by gs2g_s^2gs2​, and that adding one boundary (an open-string effect) multiplies it by gsg_sgs​. Then explain in one line why this makes the sphere the leading closed-string diagram rather than a subleading one.

    Solution

    A constant dilaton contributes Sϕ=ϕ0 χS_\phi=\phi_0\,\chiSϕ​=ϕ0​χ, so the worldsheet weight is e−ϕ0χ=gs−χe^{-\phi_0\chi}=g_s^{-\chi}e−ϕ0​χ=gs−χ​ with gs=eϕ0g_s=e^{\phi_0}gs​=eϕ0​. For a surface with ggg handles and bbb boundaries, Gauss–Bonnet gives χ=2−2g−b.\chi=2-2g-b.χ=2−2g−b. (With boundaries, χ\chiχ acquires a geodesic-curvature term 12π∮ds k\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint ds\,k2π1​∮dsk alongside the bulk integral, and the dilaton coupling contains the matching boundary piece, so Sϕ=ϕ0χS_\phi=\phi_0\chiSϕ​=ϕ0​χ still holds.)

    Add one handle (g→g+1g\to g+1g→g+1): χ→χ−2\chi\to\chi-2χ→χ−2, so the weight changes by gs−(χ−2)/ gs−χ=gs2.g_s^{-(\chi-2)}/\,g_s^{-\chi}=g_s^{2}.gs−(χ−2)​/gs−χ​=gs2​. Add one boundary (b→b+1b\to b+1b→b+1): χ→χ−1\chi\to\chi-1χ→χ−1, so the weight changes by gs−(χ−1)/gs−χ=gs1g_s^{-(\chi-1)}/g_s^{-\chi}=g_s^{1}gs−(χ−1)​/gs−χ​=gs1​. (The disc, χ=1\chi=1χ=1, carries gs−1g_s^{-1}gs−1​; the annulus, χ=0\chi=0χ=0, carries gs0g_s^{0}gs0​ — one boundary more, one power of gsg_sgs​.)

    Why the sphere leads. The weight is gs−χg_s^{-\chi}gs−χ​, and χ\chiχ is largest (=2=2=2) for the sphere, so the sphere carries the most negative power of gsg_sgs​ — the largest weight when gs≪1g_s\ll1gs​≪1. Every handle or boundary lowers χ\chiχ, raises the power of gsg_sgs​, and suppresses the diagram: the genus-0 sphere is tree level, and the expansion in gs2g_s^2gs2​ is an expansion in handles.
