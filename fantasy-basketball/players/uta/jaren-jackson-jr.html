Health and fantasy thesis

Utah announced February surgery to remove a localized PVNS growth from Jackson's left knee; no public July restriction was found. The risk belongs in games played and draft cost, not an assumption that he cannot play. NBA/AP, 2026-02-17

Dynatyze ranks him 37th, ESPN's early points list 57th, and Hashtag's analyst dynasty list 36th. He can beat all three if blocks return toward his prior elite levels; he can miss badly if the knee is managed and center minutes raise foul exposure. Ceiling: 65-plus games and 1.9 blocks support top-25 to top-35. Floor: reduced blocks, 55 games, and low rebounds push him outside the top 80.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Michigan State, one season (2017-18). 35 games, 21.8 minutes, 10.9 points, 5.8 rebounds, 1.1 assists, 3.0 blocks, 0.6 steals on .513/.396/.797. Big Ten Defensive Player of the Year and Big Ten Freshman of the Year, All-Big Ten third team. The shape of the NBA player was already there at 18: rim protection at a conference-leading rate, a real three-point stroke for a 6-11 freshman, and foul trouble severe enough to cap him near 22 minutes a night on a 30-win team. Draft: 2018, first round, fourth overall, Memphis Grizzlies. Not traded on draft night.

The 2025-26 split is a trade season: Memphis dealt him to Utah on 2026-02-03 in an eight-player, three-first-round-pick deal, he played three games in a Jazz uniform, and his season ended nine days later. Combined, 48 games at 30.3 minutes, 19.4 points, 5.7 rebounds, 2.0 assists, 1.1 steals, 1.4 blocks, 1.8 threes. The three-game Utah sample is not evidence of anything; the 0.3 blocks in it should be read as noise, not decline.

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-category ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag could not be retrieved for this dossier, so the following is anchored only to what is verifiable. ESPN's in-season category rankings listed him 21st overall on 2025-03-20, late in the 2024-25 season. Beyond that single citation, the honest read is structural rather than ranked: 2022-23 is his best category season on paper (3.0 blocks with .506 from the field and 6.8 rebounds), 2023-24 is the outlier the other way despite a career-high 22.5 points, because .444 shooting on higher volume plus 2.4 turnovers and blocks halved to 1.6 gut a profile that earns its keep in percentages and blocks. 2024-25 recovered the field goal percentage without recovering the blocks. His points-league shape has run materially better than his category shape since 2023-24 — scoring and threes rose while the two categories that separated him from replacement level did not.

Durability: 58, 57, 11, 78, 63, 66, 74, 48. Three seasons above 70 games in eight tries, and never in back-to-back years. Causes: a right quad contusion ended 2018-19 early; a torn left meniscus in the Orlando bubble on 2020-08-03 wiped out most of 2020-21 (11 games, returning in late April 2021); offseason surgery on a right-foot stress fracture cost him the first 14 games of 2022-23; 2025-26 was bracketed by injury at both ends — turf toe surgery on his right foot on 2025-07-02 that he returned from ahead of schedule, then season-ending surgery on 2026-02-12 to remove a pigmented villonodular synovitis growth from his left knee. The 2021-22 and 2024-25 seasons show he can carry a full load when healthy; nothing in the record suggests a chronic soft-tissue pattern, but the specific joints involved (knee twice, foot twice) are the ones that punish a 6-11 player who defends in space.

Trajectory: The career has two distinct engines and they have never both run at once. The first is defensive: blocks climbed 1.4 to 1.6 to 2.3 to 3.0 through 2022-23, peaking with the Defensive Player of the Year award, and then fell to 1.6 and 1.5 and stayed there for three straight seasons. That is not a fluke — it coincides with Memphis moving him further from the rim and asking him to guard perimeter forwards, and per-36 it is a genuine drop, not a minutes artifact. The second engine is offensive: usage and scoring jumped in 2023-24 with the leap to 32.2 minutes and a heavier shot diet, and the per-36 scoring rate has held since, meaning the 22-point seasons were real skill growth rather than volume. He turns 27 in September 2026, which is the top of the curve, so the base case is that neither engine changes much on its own. What changes it is Utah: with Walker Kessler gone, more of Jackson's minutes come at center, and that is the one lever that historically restores his blocks and rebounds — but it also raises his foul rate, which has been the tax on every minute of his career since college. The knee is the swing factor on games played, not on production; the production has been remarkably stable for three years, and the draft-cost question is whether you are paying for the 2022-23 defensive profile that has not appeared since.
