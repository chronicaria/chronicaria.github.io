Baseline and projection

Poeltl played 46 games, starting 44, and averaged 25.0 minutes, 10.7 points, 7.0 rebounds, 2.0 assists, 0.9 steals, 0.7 blocks and 1.2 turnovers on .700/.602 shooting. A lower-back strain caused a 24-game absence and a minutes-restricted return; he said he felt good before the playoffs. NBA.com later noted his career-worst 63.9% opponent conversion at the rim and Murray-Boyles's 191-to-134 playoff-minute advantage. Raptors Republic, 2026-04-17 · NBA.com, 2026-07-01

He has played 50, 57, and 46 games over the last three seasons (StatMuse, mean 51), and 60 sat above that mean plus any reasonable health bounce while the same dossier flags a lingering back issue, a 24-game absence with a minutes-restricted return, and Murray-Boyles's closing-minutes advantage. A 30-year-old center with a recurrent back problem and a younger challenger does not get projected for more games than any of his last five seasons; 55 still credits his "felt good before the playoffs" self-report (Raptors Republic, 2026-04-17).

Fantasy value

  • Nine-category: rank 80-110 if punting FT; nearer 110-140 in balanced builds.
  • Points: 25.2 simple FP/G, roughly rank 110-150.
  • Dynasty: outside the top 170 because he is 30, has back risk and faces a younger closing center.
  • Ceiling: healthy 27 MPG restores 12/8.5 with one block and elite FG%.
  • Floor: recurrent back pain or a full CMB takeover reduces him to 18-20 MPG and a specialist-only fantasy role.

Toronto needs Poeltl against large centers but can close switch-heavy games without him. That matchup dependence makes weekly category formats more forgiving than shallow daily-lock leagues.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Seven years in the Vienna DC Timberwolves youth program, then Arkadia Traiskirchen Lions of the Austrian Bundesliga in 2013-14. Two seasons at Utah: 2014-15, 34 games, 23.3 MPG, 9.1 PTS, 6.8 REB, 0.7 AST, 1.9 BLK on .681 FG and .444 FT; 2015-16, 36 games, 30.4 MPG, 17.2 PTS, 9.1 REB, 1.9 AST, 1.6 BLK on .646 FG and .689 FT. Consensus second-team All-American, Pac-12 Player of the Year, Pete Newell Big Man Award and Kareem Abdul-Jabbar Award in 2016. Draft: 2016, first round, No. 9 overall, Toronto Raptors — the first Austrian ever drafted. Not traded on draft night.

The 2022-23 split: 46 games with San Antonio at 26.1 MPG for 12.1 PTS, 9.0 REB, 3.1 AST, 0.8 STL, 1.1 BLK, then 26 games with Toronto after the 2023-02-09 trade at 27.2 MPG for 13.1 PTS, 9.1 REB, 2.2 AST, 1.2 STL, 1.3 BLK. He first left Toronto on 2018-07-18, going to San Antonio with DeMar DeRozan and a protected 2019 first for Kawhi Leonard and Danny Green; he returned in 2023 for Khem Birch, a 2024 first and two seconds. He signed four years and $78 million on 2023-07-06 and extended four years and $104 million on 2025-07-07 through 2029-30.

Fantasy history: verifiable season-end nine-category finishes from Basketball Monster, Hashtag or the ESPN Player Rater were not located for any Poeltl season, so no rank is asserted. The shape is unambiguous from the table and has been the same for a decade: elite FG% on real volume, rebounds, blocks and — unusually for a center — genuine assists, all paid for with a free-throw percentage that has been below .600 in seven of ten seasons and below .510 in three. He is a punt-FT% build's ideal middle piece and close to unrosterable in a balanced nine-cat team, which is why the dossier's 80-110 versus 110-140 split above is the right way to state his value. Zero three-pointers in his career is the other structural limit. Points leagues treat him better than his category profile suggests, since FT% is invisible there and the FG% plus rebounds plus assists combination scores well, but 2025-26's 25 minutes caps the raw total.

Durability: 54, 82, 77, 66, 69, 68, 72, 50, 57, 46. The first seven years are a genuinely durable big man — one 82-game season and nothing below 66 outside his rookie year. The last three are the problem and the trend is monotonic. In 2023-24 he missed 32 games, with reporting citing a leg issue and then a finger injury that ended his season roughly a month early; exact dates were not verifiable. In 2024-25 he missed 25 games; causes were not verifiable from the sources checked. In 2025-26 he missed 36. That last one is well documented: a back problem surfaced in training camp and was manageable through November, then he aggravated it for good in December — he lasted seven minutes against Brooklyn before being shut down — and missed the rest of December and all of January, returning mid-February against Detroit and playing sporadically to the end. He said before the playoffs that he felt good, but the same back limited him against Cleveland's frontcourt. Three consecutive declining seasons with a recurring lumbar issue in the most recent one is the single most important fact in this dossier.

Trajectory: Poeltl's career is a slow, honest climb followed by a health-driven fall, and it is important to separate the two. From 2016-17 to 2021-22 he added minutes and skill in equal measure, and the San Antonio years converted him from a rim-runner into a genuine offensive hub — assists went 0.7, 1.2, 1.8, 1.9, 2.8, which for a non-shooting center is a real handoff-and-short-roll skill rather than a minutes artifact. His peak is clearly 2024-25 at 29.6 minutes: 14.5 points and 9.6 rebounds with 1.2 steals and a career-best .674 from the line, at age 29, and Toronto paid for it with the $104 million extension. The 2025-26 line is not aging, it is injury. The .700 field-goal percentage on reduced volume is what a hurt big man looks like when he stops taking anything but layups, and the collapse from 1.2 blocks to 0.7 alongside the career-worst 63.9% opponent rim conversion cited above is the tell that the back cost him his lower-body explosion, not his skill. At 30 turning 31 that is a bad combination: he is past the point where a lost athletic step reliably comes back, and Toronto has a younger, more switchable center in Collin Murray-Boyles who already out-played him in postseason minutes. For 2026-27 the honest read is that a healthy Poeltl is still a starting NBA center worth roughly the dossier's 24-minute projection, but the burden of proof has shifted onto him and the blocks category — the thing that made him a fantasy asset at all — is the least likely to return.
