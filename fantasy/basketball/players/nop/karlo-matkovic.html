Career record

Pre-NBA: No college — a European club product. He came up at KK Cedevita in Croatia, was promoted to the senior team during 2018-19, and signed his first professional contract in April 2019. He was loaned to OKK Beograd in the Serbian League for 2019-20, then to KK Mega Basket in August 2020 for two ABA League seasons. In 2021-22 with Mega he played 25 games with 13 starts, averaging 11.6 points, 6.1 rebounds, and 1.1 assists in 22.0 minutes, and was named ABA League Round 7 MVP. He joined Cedevita Olimpija on July 23, 2022 and won the Slovenian League championship and Slovenian Cup in 2023; his 2022-23 per-game line is reported inconsistently across sources and is therefore not stated here. His 2023-24 season before leaving was the breakout: 14.4 points, 6.3 rebounds, 1.9 blocks, and 1.1 assists in EuroCup, and 15.4 points, 6.9 rebounds, and 2.8 blocks in the regional league, and he led EuroCup in blocks. He left Cedevita Olimpija on February 4, 2024 and joined the Birmingham Squadron of the G League on February 22, 2024. Draft: 2022, second round, 52nd overall, New Orleans — a draft-and-stash who stayed in Europe two more years and did not sign with the Pelicans until July 14, 2024. He debuted October 23, 2024 against Chicago. In an early four-game G League assignment stretch during 2024-25 he averaged 17.3 points, 9.5 rebounds, and 2.3 blocks in 27.4 minutes, including a 16-point, 14-rebound, five-block, four-assist game that November.

Three-point percentage was .318 as a rookie and .422 in 2025-26, .378 for his career. The 2025-26 line in this dossier reconciles exactly with ESPN.

Fantasy history: Two NBA seasons, neither of which produced a verifiable season-end nine-category finish — no Basketball Monster, Hashtag, or ESPN Player Rater rank was retrievable, so none is stated. The category shape is the whole argument for him: a big who shot .604 from the field, hit 0.6 threes on .422 shooting, blocked 0.9 shots in 14.7 minutes, and turned it over 0.5 times per game. Field goal percentage, blocks, and threes in one player is a genuinely scarce combination at the end of a draft, and the low turnover count means he does not leak value back. What he does not offer is scoring volume, assists, or steals, and the rebounding is ordinary for a center. Points leagues rate him materially worse than categories do, because 5.7 points and 3.7 rebounds is close to nothing in a points format while the percentages that carry his roto case count for zero there.

Durability: 42, then 62. The 2024-25 total reflects rotation status and repeated Birmingham assignments as a first-year signee rather than injury. In 2025-26 he missed 20 games; the identified cause is low back spasms, which cost him the regular-season finale and the five games before it — effectively ending his season. The calf and hamstring trouble referenced earlier in this dossier could not be corroborated in reporting and should be treated as unverified. No public clearance on the back had been reported as of July 18, and a recurring lumbar issue in a 25-year-old big is the one item in his profile that carries real forward risk.

Trajectory: The two NBA seasons move in opposite directions on the surface and the same direction underneath, which is the thing to get right. Raw production fell — 18.8 minutes and 7.7 points down to 14.7 and 5.7 — but that is a minutes story driven by New Orleans adding Derik Queen alongside Yves Missi, not a decline. Per-minute he improved in every efficiency dimension: field goal percentage from .574 to .604, three-point percentage from .318 to .422, and turnovers halved from 1.0 to 0.5, while holding blocks nearly flat on 22 percent fewer minutes. The per-36 line of roughly 13.9 points, 9.0 rebounds, 2.1 blocks, and 1.5 threes is consistent with what he did in EuroCup at 22, where he led the competition in blocks while scoring 14.4 per game, so the European record and the NBA per-minute record corroborate each other rather than conflict. At 25 he is at the point where a backup big either converts per-minute production into a role or gets passed by younger lottery bigs, and New Orleans has two of those ahead of him on investment if not on production. For 2026-27 the honest read is that the skill case is stronger than the opportunity case, and the back is the variable that could settle it badly.
