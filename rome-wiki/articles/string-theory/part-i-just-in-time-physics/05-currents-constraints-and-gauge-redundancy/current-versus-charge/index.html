    Current versus charge

    A current is a local flux field — how much flows where; a charge is the running-total number you get by integrating its density part — how much is inside.

    Put a region VVV in your mind with a counter on its wall. The charge QQQ is the count inside. The current is the field that says, at every wall patch, how fast stuff is streaming across. The entire content of "conservation" is one accounting rule: the count inside changes only by what crosses the wall. Nothing appears or vanishes in the interior.

    Current and charge live at different levels: the current is a field on the whole domain; the charge is one number at one instant. The rest of the note makes this precise, then builds the machine that manufactures a current from any symmetry.

    The local rule and its global consequence

    The current is a vector field jα=(j0,ȷ⃗ )j^\alpha=(j^0,\vec\jmath\,)jα=(j0,​); the index α\alphaα runs over the directions of whatever world the fields live on — ordinary spacetime here, the string worldsheet later in the module. The time part j0j^0j0 is a density (charge per unit volume, up to a factor of ccc); the space part ȷ⃗\vec\jmath​ is a flux (charge per unit area per unit time). "Conserved current" means it is divergence-free:

    ∂αjα=0.(1)\partial_\alpha j^\alpha=0. \tag{1}∂α​jα=0.(1)

    With x0=ctx^0=ctx0=ct so that ∂0=(1/c)∂t\partial_0=(1/c)\partial_t∂0​=(1/c)∂t​, splitting time from space turns (1) into the continuity equation:

    ∂αjα=0⟺∂tρ+∇⋅ȷ⃗=0,ρ≡j0/c.(2)\partial_\alpha j^\alpha=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \partial_t\rho+\nabla\cdot\vec\jmath=0, \qquad \rho\equiv j^0/c. \tag{2}∂α​jα=0⟺∂t​ρ+∇⋅​=0,ρ≡j0/c.(2)

    Read it locally: the density at a point can drop only if flux carries charge away from that point. For electric charge, ρ\rhoρ and ȷ⃗\vec\jmath​ are exactly the charge density and current density of Maxwell's theory. The name "conserved current" is a mild abuse — the current is merely divergence-free; what is actually conserved is the integrated charge, and only when nothing escapes.

    Now integrate the density over a fixed region to get the charge, and watch local conservation force global conservation:

    Q(t)=∫Vρ d3x,dQdt=∫V∂tρ d3x  =(2)  −∫V∇⋅ȷ⃗ d3x  =  −∮∂Vȷ⃗⋅da⃗.(3)Q(t)=\int_V\rho\,d^3x, \qquad \frac{dQ}{dt} =\int_V\partial_t\rho\,d^3x \;\overset{(2)}{=}\;-\int_V\nabla\cdot\vec\jmath\,d^3x \;=\;-\oint_{\partial V}\vec\jmath\cdot d\vec a. \tag{3}Q(t)=∫V​ρd3x,dtdQ​=∫V​∂t​ρd3x=(2)−∫V​∇⋅​d3x=−∮∂V​​⋅da.(3)

    Three moves: differentiate the density inside a fixed region, substitute the continuity equation, then use the divergence theorem to turn a volume integral of a divergence into a boundary flux. The result is the accounting rule in equation form:

      dQdt=−∮∂Vȷ⃗⋅da⃗.  (4)\boxed{\;\frac{dQ}{dt}=-\oint_{\partial V}\vec\jmath\cdot d\vec a.\;} \tag{4}dtdQ​=−∮∂V​​⋅da.​(4)

    If no current crosses ∂V\partial V∂V, QQQ is constant. If current does cross, QQQ changes by exactly that boundary flux — no more, no less. The string reuses this template: its endpoints play the role of ∂V\partial V∂V, and whether the string alone conserves a component of momentum comes down to whether flux leaks out the ends.

    Noether's machine: symmetry manufactures a current

    Electric charge was just one example. The real engine is this: every continuous symmetry of the action hands you a conserved current for free. Start with the cleanest case, where the symmetry leaves the Lagrangian density itself untouched.

    Write the action as an integral of a Lagrangian density over whatever world the fields live on:

    S=∫dξ0⋯dξk  L(ϕa,∂αϕa),∂αϕa=∂ϕa∂ξα.S=\int d\xi^0\cdots d\xi^k\;\mathcal L(\phi^a,\partial_\alpha\phi^a), \qquad \partial_\alpha\phi^a=\frac{\partial\phi^a}{\partial\xi^\alpha}.S=∫dξ0⋯dξkL(ϕa,∂α​ϕa),∂α​ϕa=∂ξα∂ϕa​.

    Keep three index families straight — confusing them is the usual beginner's error:

    • α\alphaα labels directions of the world ξα\xi^\alphaξα (spacetime axes, or the string worldsheet's τ,σ\tau,\sigmaτ,σ),
    • aaa labels which field ϕa\phi^aϕa,
    • iii labels which symmetry parameter (a symmetry can have several independent knobs).

    An infinitesimal symmetry is a rule for nudging every field configuration:

    δϕa=ϵihia(ϕ).\delta\phi^a=\epsilon^i h_i^a(\phi).δϕa=ϵihia​(ϕ).

    The parameters ϵi\epsilon^iϵi are constants — the same nudge at every point of the world — which is what lets δ\deltaδ commute with ∂α\partial_\alpha∂α​ below.

    Strictly invariant case (δL=0\delta\mathcal L=0δL=0). Then the current is

    ϵijiα=∂L∂(∂αϕa) δϕa,∂αjiα=0.(5)\epsilon^i j_i^\alpha =\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\,\delta\phi^a, \qquad \partial_\alpha j_i^\alpha=0. \tag{5}ϵijiα​=∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​δϕa,∂α​jiα​=0.(5)

    The upper index α\alphaα is which direction the current flows; the label iii is which charge is being carried. The proof is two steps. The chain rule always gives

    δL=∂L∂ϕaδϕa+∂L∂(∂αϕa) ∂α(δϕa),\delta\mathcal L =\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^a}\delta\phi^a +\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\,\partial_\alpha(\delta\phi^a),δL=∂ϕa∂L​δϕa+∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​∂α​(δϕa),

    and substituting the Euler–Lagrange equation ∂L∂ϕa=∂α ⁣(∂L∂(∂αϕa))\dfrac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\phi^a}=\partial_\alpha\!\Big(\dfrac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\Big)∂ϕa∂L​=∂α​(∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​) packages the right-hand side into a single total derivative (the product rule read backward):

    δL=∂α ⁣(∂L∂(∂αϕa) δϕa)=ϵi ∂αjiα.(6)\delta\mathcal L =\partial_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\,\delta\phi^a\right) =\epsilon^i\,\partial_\alpha j_i^\alpha . \tag{6}δL=∂α​(∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​δϕa)=ϵi∂α​jiα​.(6)

    If δL=0\delta\mathcal L=0δL=0, the current is divergence-free on shell — on solutions of the equations of motion — since Euler–Lagrange was used. Its integrated charge is

    Qi=∫dξ1⋯dξk  ji0,Q_i=\int d\xi^1\cdots d\xi^k\;j_i^0,Qi​=∫dξ1⋯dξkji0​,

    and dQi/dξ0=0dQ_i/d\xi^0=0dQi​/dξ0=0 whenever the spatial flux through the boundary vanishes — the same ∫→∮\int\to\oint∫→∮ argument as (3)–(4), now in kkk spatial dimensions.

    Total-derivative symmetries

    Here is the case string theory actually needs. Some transformations do not leave L\mathcal LL strictly fixed; they change it by a total derivative,

    δL=∂α(ϵiKiα).(7)\delta\mathcal L=\partial_\alpha(\epsilon^i K_i^\alpha). \tag{7}δL=∂α​(ϵiKiα​).(7)

    This is still a symmetry, because the action changes only by a boundary term, which is discarded (or held fixed by boundary conditions). Equation (6) is unchanged — it is just the chain rule plus Euler–Lagrange, true regardless of what δL\delta\mathcal LδL equals. Setting (6) equal to (7),

    ∂α ⁣(∂L∂(∂αϕa) δϕa)=∂α(ϵiKiα)⟹∂α ⁣(∂L∂(∂αϕa) δϕa−ϵiKiα)=0.\partial_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\,\delta\phi^a\right) =\partial_\alpha(\epsilon^i K_i^\alpha) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \partial_\alpha\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\,\delta\phi^a-\epsilon^i K_i^\alpha\right)=0.∂α​(∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​δϕa)=∂α​(ϵiKiα​)⟹∂α​(∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​δϕa−ϵiKiα​)=0.

    So the divergence-free object is the naive current (5) minus the boundary piece:

      ϵijiα=∂L∂(∂αϕa) δϕa−ϵiKiα.  (8)\boxed{\; \epsilon^i j_i^\alpha= \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi^a)}\,\delta\phi^a-\epsilon^i K_i^\alpha . \;} \tag{8}ϵijiα​=∂(∂α​ϕa)∂L​δϕa−ϵiKiα​.​(8)

    The −Kα-K^\alpha−Kα is not optional. Without it the naive current has on-shell divergence ∂α(ϵiKiα)≠0\partial_\alpha(\epsilon^i K_i^\alpha)\neq0∂α​(ϵiKiα​)=0; subtracting KαK^\alphaKα is exactly what cancels that leak.

    This is why spacetime translations produce a stress-energy tensor and not merely the naive term. A translation shifts the field's argument, δϕ=ϵβ∂βϕ\delta\phi=\epsilon^\beta\partial_\beta\phiδϕ=ϵβ∂β​ϕ, so for an L\mathcal LL with no explicit coordinate dependence

    δL=ϵβ∂βL=∂α(ϵβ δβα L),\delta\mathcal L=\epsilon^\beta\partial_\beta\mathcal L =\partial_\alpha(\epsilon^\beta\,\delta^\alpha_\beta\,\mathcal L),δL=ϵβ∂β​L=∂α​(ϵβδβα​L),

    which is form (7) with Kαβ=δβαLK^\alpha{}_\beta=\delta^\alpha_\beta\mathcal LKαβ​=δβα​L. Feeding this into (8) turns the −Kα-K^\alpha−Kα into the −δβαL-\delta^\alpha_\beta\mathcal L−δβα​L term of the canonical stress tensor; the full derivation lives in Stress-energy tensor as momentum flow. (By contrast, a string's target-space translation δXμ=ϵμ\delta X^\mu=\epsilon^\muδXμ=ϵμ leaves L\mathcal LL strictly fixed — no KαK^\alphaKα — because L\mathcal LL depends only on ∂αXμ\partial_\alpha X^\mu∂α​Xμ; that gives the worldsheet momentum current in World-sheet momentum current.)

    Point-mechanics sanity check

    Point mechanics is the same machinery with the spatial integrals dropped: the "density" collapses to the whole Lagrangian L(q,q˙)L(q,\dot q)L(q,q˙​), and the current collapses directly to a conserved number — no local field in between. For LLL invariant under δq=ϵ h(q)\delta q=\epsilon\,h(q)δq=ϵh(q),

    ϵQ=∂L∂q˙ δq,dQdt=0\epsilon Q=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\,\delta q, \qquad \frac{dQ}{dt}=0ϵQ=∂q˙​∂L​δq,dtdQ​=0

    on shell. The cleanest instance: if LLL depends only on q˙\dot qq˙​ (not on qqq), then the constant shift δq=ϵ\delta q=\epsilonδq=ϵ is a symmetry and

    Q=∂L∂q˙=p.Q=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}=p.Q=∂q˙​∂L​=p.

    Translation invariance of a free particle gives conserved momentum — the ancestor of every current above. Field theory simply inserts a local current between the symmetry and the conserved number; integrating the current's density recovers the number.

    Open questions

    • Boundary conditions are as decisive as the differential equation. D-branes revisit cases where string-endpoint flux makes the string-only charge nonconserved — momentum then leaks into the brane, exactly per (4).
    • The active/passive sign convention for translations flips the sign of the stress tensor; keep it explicit whenever comparing formulas across books.

    Exercise

    The −Kα-K^\alpha−Kα subtraction in (8) looks like it might be optional. Show it is forced, using the simplest total-derivative symmetry you can check by hand: a free particle under a Galilean boost.

    Take L=12q˙2L=\tfrac12\dot q^2L=21​q˙​2 (unit mass) and δq=ϵ t\delta q=\epsilon\,tδq=ϵt (drift the trajectory at constant velocity). (a) Show δL\delta LδL is a total time derivative and read off KKK (the mechanics analogue of KαK^\alphaKα). (b) Write the naive current (∂L/∂q˙) δq(\partial L/\partial\dot q)\,\delta q(∂L/∂q˙​)δq and check whether it is conserved on shell. (c) Apply (8) and check that the corrected charge is conserved. Explain in one sentence what went wrong in (b).

    Solution

    (a) The boost changes the velocity, δq˙=ddt(ϵt)=ϵ\delta\dot q=\dfrac{d}{dt}(\epsilon t)=\epsilonδq˙​=dtd​(ϵt)=ϵ, so δL=∂L∂q˙ δq˙=q˙ ϵ=ddt ⁣(ϵ q).\delta L=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\,\delta\dot q=\dot q\,\epsilon=\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\epsilon\,q\right).δL=∂q˙​∂L​δq˙​=q˙​ϵ=dtd​(ϵq). This is a genuine total derivative, form (7) with K=qK=qK=q — nonzero, so the subtraction has something to cancel. (Contrast the constant shift δq=ϵ\delta q=\epsilonδq=ϵ, which gives δL=0\delta L=0δL=0 and K=0K=0K=0: too degenerate to test anything.)

    (b) Naive current: ∂L∂q˙ δq=q˙ (ϵt)\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial\dot q}\,\delta q=\dot q\,(\epsilon t)∂q˙​∂L​δq=q˙​(ϵt), i.e. Qnaive=q˙ tQ_{\text{naive}}=\dot q\,tQnaive​=q˙​t.     (c) Corrected charge from (8): Q=Qnaive−K=q˙ t−qQ=Q_{\text{naive}}-K=\dot q\,t-qQ=Qnaive​−K=q˙​t−q. Then dQdt=(q¨ t+q˙)−q˙=q¨ t=0\dfrac{dQ}{dt}=(\ddot q\,t+\dot q)-\dot q=\ddot q\,t=0dtdQ​=(q¨​t+q˙​)−q˙​=q¨​t=0 on shell. Conserved.     One sentence: the naive current's on-shell divergence equals dK/dtdK/dtdK/dt, not zero, precisely because δL\delta LδL is a total derivative rather than strictly zero — so subtracting KKK is not a convention but the only thing that plugs that leak.
