    Proper length and proper time

    A moving clock's own ticking, the proper time , is the one duration every observer agrees on; ds is that same tick measured as a length (proper time times c ), and the minus sign in ds^2=-\eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu keeps it positive for a real particle.

    The riding stopwatch

    Strap a stopwatch to a particle and let it fly. The number the stopwatch reads between two events on its path is the proper time — "proper" in the old sense of belonging to it. Two events, one clock riding between them: that reading is a fact about the trip, not about who is watching.

    Different observers disagree about how much coordinate time ttt passed and how far x⃗\vec xx the particle went — moving clocks run slow, moving rulers shrink. But they disagree in a coordinated way, and the combination

    c2 dt2−dx⃗ 2c^2\,dt^2 - d\vec x^{\,2}c2dt2−dx2

    comes out the same for all of them: the invariant left over after the frame-dependent pieces cancel, and exactly what the riding stopwatch measures. What remains is to name this quantity and fix its units.

    This is why the invariant matters. The Proper-time action for a relativistic particle is built from exactly this quantity, because an action all observers compute identically gives physics all observers agree on. The four-momentum, the mass shell, the string generalization — everything downstream hangs on getting this one quantity and its units straight.

    The convention

    The metric is mostly-plus,

    ημν=diag(−1,+1,+1,+1).\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1).ημν​=diag(−1,+1,+1,+1).

    For a timelike displacement dxμ=(c dt, dx⃗)dx^\mu=(c\,dt,\,d\vec x)dxμ=(cdt,dx) the invariant combination is

    ημν dxμdxν=−c2dt2+dx⃗ 2<0,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu=-c^2dt^2+d\vec x^{\,2}<0,ημν​dxμdxν=−c2dt2+dx2<0,

    negative because a real (timelike) particle covers less distance than light would in the same dtdtdt: ∣dx⃗∣<c dt|d\vec x|<c\,dt∣dx∣<cdt. A negative square is inconvenient if we want a length, so we flip the sign once and for all:

    \boxed{\;ds^2=-\eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nu=c^2dt^2-d\vec x^{\,2}\;}\tag{1}

    dsdsds has units of length — the invariant above, repackaged as a positive number. The minus sign is not physics; it is the toll for describing a timelike path with a mostly-plus metric. The physical proper time is dsdsds divided by ccc:

    \boxed{\;d\tau_{\text{proper}}=\frac{ds}{c}=dt\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\;}\tag{2}

    This is the stopwatch reading, in units of time, and the factor 1−v2/c2<1\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}<11−v2/c2​<1 is time dilation made local: per tick dtdtdt of a lab clock, the moving clock advances by less. At v=0v=0v=0 it gives dτproper=dtd\tau_{\text{proper}}=dtdτproper​=dt — a clock at rest reads coordinate time, as it must. To get (2) from (1), factor c2dt2c^2dt^2c2dt2 out of the root: ds=c2dt2−dx⃗ 2=c dt1−(dx⃗/dt)2/c2=c dt1−v2/c2ds=\sqrt{c^2dt^2 - d\vec x^{\,2}} = c\,dt\sqrt{1-(d\vec x/dt)^2/c^2} = c\,dt\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}ds=c2dt2−dx2​=cdt1−(dx/dt)2/c2​=cdt1−v2/c2​, with v⃗=dx⃗/dt\vec v=d\vec x/dtv=dx/dt.

    Two names, one quantity: dsdsds is the tick as a length, ds/cds/cds/c is the tick as a time. The elapsed proper time along a path is ∫ds/c\int ds/c∫ds/c; the elapsed proper length is ∫ds=c∫dτproper\int ds = c\int d\tau_{\text{proper}}∫ds=c∫dτproper​. In this module the length variable serves as the worldline parameter, written sss, with physical proper time s/cs/cs/c.

    Unit tangent versus four-velocity

    Once we have a scalar to parameterize by, we can differentiate position along the path. Two natural choices of parameter give two objects that differ only by a factor of ccc.

    Using the length sss as parameter gives the dimensionless tangent vector

    u^\mu\equiv \frac{dx^\mu}{ds},\qquad \eta_{\mu\nu}\,u^\mu u^\nu=-1.\tag{3}

    The normalization u2=−1u^2=-1u2=−1 is not a law of motion — it is (1) divided by ds2ds^2ds2. Any curve parameterized by its own proper length has a unit-norm tangent, automatically; this one fact drives the Mass-shell relation.

    Using the physical proper time τproper=s/c\tau_{\text{proper}}=s/cτproper​=s/c as parameter gives the four-velocity a physicist usually means,

    U^\mu\equiv \frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau_{\text{proper}}}=c\,\frac{dx^\mu}{ds}=c\,u^\mu,\qquad U^\mu U_\mu=-c^2.\tag{4}

    UμU^\muUμ carries units of velocity, and its normalization is (3) times c2c^2c2. In the rest frame Uμ=(c,0⃗)U^\mu=(c,\vec 0)Uμ=(c,0): a particle at rest still "moves through time" at rate ccc, which is the geometric content of U2=−c2U^2=-c^2U2=−c2.

    The four-momentum can be written either way, and this is where the factors of ccc throughout the module come from:

      pμ=mUμ=mc dxμds  \boxed{\;p^\mu=mU^\mu=mc\,\frac{dx^\mu}{ds}\;}pμ=mUμ=mcdsdxμ​​

    Read it as rest mass times four-velocity. The mc dxμ/dsmc\,dx^\mu/dsmcdxμ/ds form is the one that appears when we vary the action in Free particle equation of motion; the mUμmU^\mumUμ form is the one that resembles Newtonian p⃗=mv⃗\vec p=m\vec vp​=mv. They are the same identity in the two unit conventions.

    Working rule for the rest of the module: when you see dsdsds, read "proper length"; when you see ds/cds/cds/c, read "proper time." Everything else is a factor of ccc.

    Open questions

    • Massless particles. A photon has ds=0ds=0ds=0 along its path (it moves at ccc, so c2dt2=dx⃗2c^2dt^2=d\vec x^2c2dt2=dx2): no proper time ticks, and neither sss nor τproper\tau_{\text{proper}}τproper​ can parameterize it. The invariant constraint still makes sense, but the square-root action −mc∫ds-mc\int ds−mc∫ds must be traded for an auxiliary-field (einbein) form — flagged for later.
    • Is straight-line motion really the maximum? Among nearby timelike worldlines between two fixed events, the straight one maximizes elapsed proper time (the twin who coasts ages most). Globally this can fail past conjugate points in curved spacetime — a caveat for the geodesic version, not needed here.
    • One tick vs. a whole sheet. A string sweeps a 2D worldsheet with an induced 2×22\times22×2 metric, not a single proper-time element. That is why Worldline versus worldsheet treats "proper time" as a particle-only convenience rather than a literal string gauge.

    Exercise

    A muon is created at altitude and flies straight down at v=0.99cv=0.99cv=0.99c toward a detector, taking t=34 μst=34\ \mu\text{s}t=34 μs of lab time to arrive. Its rest-frame lifetime is 2.2 μs2.2\ \mu\text{s}2.2 μs, so a lab observer, ignoring relativity, would expect it to have decayed roughly 151515 times over. Yet many muons arrive.
