    Polarizations of massless spin-1 and spin-2

    Light-cone gauge strips the photon A_\mu to D-2 transverse polarizations and the graviton h_{\mu\nu} to a symmetric traceless transverse tensor with \tfrac12 D(D-3) polarizations; each surviving transverse field quantizes into one oscillator species per polarization.

    Where the extra components go

    A scalar has one component, so it has one particle state per momentum: no bookkeeping needed. The photon field AμA_\muAμ​ has DDD components and the graviton hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ has more — yet a photon has only 222 polarizations and a gravitational wave only 222 (in our D=4D=4D=4 world). Where do the extra components go?

    Two places, and neither is a physical particle:

    • Gauge redundancy — some components are not new physics, just the same physical configuration described in more than one way. Shifting AμA_\muAμ​ by a gradient, or hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ by a coordinate wiggle, changes the components but not a single measurable thing. This is over-description, and we are free to delete it.
    • Constraints — once we delete the redundancy, the field equation solves some of the remaining components in terms of the others. Those are not independent data; they are determined.

    A rigid rod in 3D makes the constraint concrete: its two endpoints carry 666 coordinates, but the fixed length ties one combination down, leaving 555 genuine degrees of freedom. Light-cone gauge is the coordinate choice that exposes both mechanisms at once: the redundancy is spent deleting the +++ components, the constraints then determine the −-− components, and what survives is the transverse field. The physical content of a massless field lives entirely in its transverse directions, so counting polarizations is tensor combinatorics on D−2D-2D−2 slots. That same D−2D-2D−2 transverse count reappears line for line when we quantize the string.

    Light-cone coordinates. Split a vector index μ∈{+,−,I}\mu \in \{+, -, I\}μ∈{+,−,I}, where I=2,…,D−1I = 2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1 labels the D−2D-2D−2 transverse directions and x±=(x0±x1)/2x^\pm = (x^0 \pm x^1)/\sqrt2x±=(x0±x1)/2​. The metric is off-diagonal in the ±\pm± block: for any two vectors, a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbIa\cdot b = -a^+ b^- - a^- b^+ + a^I b^Ia⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI (mostly-plus signature). We always assume p+≠0p^+ \neq 0p+=0 — this is what makes the gauge-fixing and the constraint-solving possible.

    Photon: the Maxwell field AμA_\muAμ​

    Field equation and gauge symmetry. From the field strength Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμF_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\muFμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​, the source-free Maxwell equation ∂νFμν=0\partial_\nu F^{\mu\nu} = 0∂ν​Fμν=0 is, in momentum space (Fourier transforming Aμ(x)=∫dDp(2π)Deip⋅xAμ(p)A_\mu(x) = \int \frac{d^Dp}{(2\pi)^D}e^{ip\cdot x}A_\mu(p)Aμ​(x)=∫(2π)DdDp​eip⋅xAμ​(p)),

    p^2 A^\mu - p^\mu (p\cdot A) = 0. \tag{1}

    Every term carries two powers of momentum and there is no term proportional to AμA^\muAμ alone — no mass term. So any physical wave that survives will satisfy p2=0p^2 = 0p2=0: the Maxwell field is massless, not by assumption but because the equation has nowhere to put a mass. FμνF_{\mu\nu}Fμν​ is built to be invariant under

    \delta A_\mu(x) = \partial_\mu \epsilon(x) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \delta A_\mu(p) = i p_\mu\, \epsilon(p). \tag{2}

    This is the gauge redundancy: shifting AμA_\muAμ​ by a gradient leaves FμνF_{\mu\nu}Fμν​, hence all physics, untouched.

    Fix the gauge. In light-cone components the gauge shift (2) of A+A^+A+ is δA+=ip+ϵ\delta A^+ = i p^+ \epsilonδA+=ip+ϵ. Since p+≠0p^+ \neq 0p+=0, choose ϵ=iA+/p+\epsilon = i A^+/p^+ϵ=iA+/p+ to cancel it exactly:

    \boxed{\ A^+(p) = 0\ } \qquad \text{(light-cone gauge for Maxwell).} \tag{3}

    This spends all the gauge freedom — one function's worth of redundancy, one condition; with p+≠0p^+ \neq 0p+=0, no further transformation preserves (3).

    Solve the constraints. Take μ=+\mu = +μ=+ in (1). By the gauge condition (3) the p2A+p^2 A^+p2A+ term drops and we get p+(p⋅A)=0p^+(p\cdot A) = 0p+(p⋅A)=0, hence (using p+≠0p^+\neq0p+=0)

    p⋅A=0.p\cdot A = 0.p⋅A=0.

    Expand this with the light-cone metric, p⋅A=−p+A−−p−A++pIAIp\cdot A = -p^+ A^- - p^- A^+ + p^I A^Ip⋅A=−p+A−−p−A++pIAI, and set A+=0A^+ = 0A+=0:

    A^- = \frac{1}{p^+}\, p^I A^I. \tag{4}

    So A−A^-A− is not independent — the constraint hands it to us in terms of the transverse AIA^IAI. Now feed p⋅A=0p\cdot A = 0p⋅A=0 back into (1): the second term vanishes and only p2Aμ=0p^2 A^\mu = 0p2Aμ=0 survives. For μ=+\mu = +μ=+ this is trivial (A+=0A^+=0A+=0), and for μ=−\mu = -μ=− it follows once we know the transverse case, since (4) makes A−A^-A− a multiple of the AIA^IAI. The only genuine content is

    p^2 A^I(p) = 0. \tag{5}

    Each transverse AIA^IAI obeys the massless-scalar equation (5). For p2≠0p^2 \neq 0p2=0 it forces AI=0A^I = 0AI=0, and then (4) kills A−A^-A− too — the entire field vanishes, so an off-shell Maxwell field is pure gauge. On the shell p2=0p^2 = 0p2=0 the AIA^IAI are free. The physical content is the D−2D-2D−2 transverse fields AIA^IAI, each a massless scalar. Hence D−2D-2D−2 degrees of freedom per mass-shell point.

    Photon states. Quantize each AIA^IAI exactly as the scalar in One-particle states: it gives one oscillator species apI,apI†a^I_p, a^{I\dagger}_papI​,apI†​ per transverse direction. The one-photon states are

    \boxed{\ \text{One photon:}\quad \sum_{I=2}^{D-1}\xi_I\, a^{I\dagger}_{p^+,p_T}\,|\Omega\rangle\ } \tag{6}

    The polarization vector ξI\xi_IξI​ names which linear combination of the D−2D-2D−2 transverse oscillators is excited. In D=4D=4D=4 that is 222 states — the two familiar transverse polarizations of light. The would-be longitudinal and timelike photons were never lost particles: one was gauge (deleted), one was constraint (determined).

    Graviton: the linearized metric hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​

    Gravity's field is the spacetime metric gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​. In a weak field write

    gμν=ημν+hμν,hμν=hνμ,g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}, \qquad h_{\mu\nu} = h_{\nu\mu},gμν​=ημν​+hμν​,hμν​=hνμ​,

    with hμνh_{\mu\nu}hμν​ a small symmetric fluctuation — the spin-2 analog of the photon field. We use only its free, linearized equation, not full nonlinear general relativity.

    Field equation and gauge symmetry. The linearized, source-free Einstein equation in momentum space is

    S^{\mu\nu}(p) \equiv p^2 h^{\mu\nu} - p_\rho\big(p^\mu h^{\nu\rho} + p^\nu h^{\mu\rho}\big) + p^\mu p^\nu h = 0, \qquad h \equiv \eta_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu}. \tag{7}

    Again every term has two derivatives and none is a bare hμνh^{\mu\nu}hμν: no mass term, so a surviving excitation is massless. The gauge parameter is now a vector ϵμ\epsilon^\muϵμ — this is linearized coordinate reparameterization invariance, the statement that relabeling spacetime points cannot change physics:

    \delta h^{\mu\nu}(p) = i p^\mu \epsilon^\nu + i p^\nu \epsilon^\mu. \tag{8}

    That (7) is invariant under (8) is the spin-2 version of Maxwell gauge invariance. (Direct check: δh=2i p⋅ϵ\delta h = 2i\,p\cdot\epsilonδh=2ip⋅ϵ, and substituting (8) into (7) every term cancels, so δSμν=0\delta S^{\mu\nu}=0δSμν=0.) One vector's worth of redundancy — DDD functions — is exactly what we will spend gauging away the +++ components.

    Fix the gauge. The symmetric hμνh^{\mu\nu}hμν has components (hIJ,h−I,h−−,h+I,h+−,h++)(h^{IJ}, h^{-I}, h^{--}, h^{+I}, h^{+-}, h^{++})(hIJ,h−I,h−−,h+I,h+−,h++). Under (8), each component carrying a +++ index shifts by a term proportional to p+≠0p^+ \neq 0p+=0, so the DDD gauge functions are spent in sequence — ϵ+\epsilon^+ϵ+ cancels h++h^{++}h++, then ϵ−\epsilon^-ϵ− cancels h+−h^{+-}h+−, then the ϵI\epsilon^IϵI cancel h+Ih^{+I}h+I:

    \boxed{\ h^{++} = h^{+-} = h^{+I} = 0\ } \qquad \text{(light-cone gauge for gravity).} \tag{9}

    This is the direct analog of A+=0A^+ = 0A+=0: one gauge condition per gauge function.

    Solve the constraints. Take μ=ν=+\mu = \nu = +μ=ν=+ in (7). With the +++ components gone by (9), only the last term survives: (p+)2h=0(p^+)^2 h = 0(p+)2h=0, so the trace vanishes, h=0h = 0h=0. Now h=ημνhμν=−2h+−+hIIh = \eta_{\mu\nu}h^{\mu\nu} = -2h^{+-} + h^{II}h=ημν​hμν=−2h+−+hII, and h+−=0h^{+-} = 0h+−=0 in our gauge, so

    h^{II} = 0 \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \text{the transverse block } h^{IJ} \text{ is traceless.} \tag{10}

    With h=0h = 0h=0, equation (7) reduces to p2hμν−pμ(pρhνρ)−pν(pρhμρ)=0p^2 h^{\mu\nu} - p^\mu(p_\rho h^{\nu\rho}) - p^\nu(p_\rho h^{\mu\rho}) = 0p2hμν−pμ(pρ​hνρ)−pν(pρ​hμρ)=0. Setting μ=+\mu = +μ=+ gives p+(pρhνρ)=0p^+(p_\rho h^{\nu\rho}) = 0p+(pρ​hνρ)=0, hence the constraint

    p_\rho h^{\nu\rho} = 0. \tag{11}

    For ν=I\nu = Iν=I, expand (11): −p+h−I+pJhIJ=0-p^+ h^{-I} + p_J h^{IJ} = 0−p+h−I+pJ​hIJ=0, so h−I=1p+pJhIJh^{-I} = \frac{1}{p^+}p_J h^{IJ}h−I=p+1​pJ​hIJ. For ν=−\nu = -ν=− likewise h−−=1p+pIh−Ih^{--} = \frac{1}{p^+}p_I h^{-I}h−−=p+1​pI​h−I. Both minus-blocks are determined by the transverse hIJh^{IJ}hIJ — constraint data, not independent. What is left of the field equation, for transverse indices, is again the massless-scalar equation

    p^2 h^{IJ}(p) = 0. \tag{12}

    For p2≠0p^2 \neq 0p2=0, (12) forces hIJ=0h^{IJ} = 0hIJ=0 and the constraints kill everything else — pure gauge again. On the shell the transverse block is free. Physical content: a symmetric, traceless, transverse tensor hIJh^{IJ}hIJ.

    Counting. hIJh^{IJ}hIJ is a symmetric traceless (D−2)×(D−2)(D-2)\times(D-2)(D−2)×(D−2) matrix. Symmetric gives 12(D−2)(D−1)\tfrac12(D-2)(D-1)21​(D−2)(D−1) entries; tracelessness removes one:

    \boxed{\ n(D) = \tfrac12(D-2)(D-1) - 1 = \tfrac12 D(D-3)\ } \tag{13}

    Values: n(4)=2n(4) = 2n(4)=2, n(5)=5n(5) = 5n(5)=5, n(10)=35n(10) = 35n(10)=35, n(26)=299n(26) = 299n(26)=299. In D=4D=4D=4 the graviton has exactly 222 polarizations — the two helicities of a gravitational wave. That this matches the photon's D−2=2D-2 = 2D−2=2 is a four-dimensional coincidence; (13) separates the two counts in every other dimension.

    Graviton states. Quantize each independent component of hIJh^{IJ}hIJ into oscillators apIJ,apIJ†a^{IJ}_p, a^{IJ\dagger}_papIJ​,apIJ†​. One-graviton states are

    \boxed{\ \text{One graviton:}\quad \sum_{I,J=2}^{D-1}\xi_{IJ}\, a^{IJ\dagger}_{p^+,p_T}\,|\Omega\rangle,\qquad \xi_{II} = 0\ } \tag{14}

    The polarization tensor ξIJ\xi_{IJ}ξIJ​ is symmetric and traceless with exactly n(D)n(D)n(D) independent components — the spin-2 analog of ξI\xi_IξI​ in (6), carrying two transverse indices because the graviton is spin-2. The classical tracelessness (10) has become the tracelessness condition ξII=0\xi_{II}=0ξII​=0 on the polarization tensor.

    What "spin-2 is gravity" does and does not mean here

    This note proves a free-field dictionary entry: a massless symmetric tensor with gauge symmetry (8) carries exactly the polarization content of a graviton. That is enough to identify the string's massless closed-string tensor state as "the graviton" at the free level. It is not a derivation of Einstein's equations.

    The interacting statement "massless spin-2 forces general relativity" needs more input: a massless spin-2 field couples consistently only to a conserved symmetric source (the Stress-energy tensor as momentum flow); it carries energy-momentum so it must couple to its own source; iterating that self-coupling turns the linear gauge symmetry (8) into full nonlinear coordinate invariance, whose unique two-derivative completion is Einstein gravity (up to field redefinitions and a cosmological term). That argument uses locality, Lorentz invariance, and a low-energy two-derivative truncation — it is a signpost, not a theorem provable by polarization counting. We return to it via the closed-string massless spectrum (Module 16) and worldsheet consistency (Module 25).

    Open questions

    • Deriving (7) from the Einstein–Hilbert action is deferred to the low-energy effective-action notes; the polarization count needs only the linearized equation and its gauge invariance.
    • The representation-theory meaning of "spin" is the massless little-group story. Light-cone gauge gives the same count without that machinery.
    • "Spin-2 implies GR" needs the interaction assumptions above; it is not proved by counting.

    Exercise

    A student proposes that a massless spin-3 field in DDD dimensions is a symmetric transverse tensor hIJKh^{IJK}hIJK (three transverse indices), traceless on every pair of indices. Using only the light-cone logic of this note, count its physical polarizations n3(D)n_3(D)n3​(D), then evaluate at D=4D=4D=4 and D=26D=26D=26. (Hint: the number of fully symmetric rank-sss tensors in nnn slots is (n+s−1s)\binom{n+s-1}{s}(sn+s−1​); a single trace condition removes one full lower-rank symmetric tensor.)

    Solution

    The physical field lives on the D−2D-2D−2 transverse directions, so set n=D−2n = D-2n=D−2 slots. A symmetric rank-3 tensor in nnn slots has (n+23)=n(n+1)(n+2)6\binom{n+2}{3} = \frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{6}(3n+2​)=6n(n+1)(n+2)​ independent components. Tracelessness "on every pair" for a symmetric tensor is a single symmetric-tensor condition: contracting any two indices with δIJ\delta_{IJ}δIJ​ gives one symmetric rank-1 tensor (a vector) that must vanish, removing (n1)=n\binom{n}{1} = n(1n​)=n components. So n3(D)=(n+23)−n=n(n+1)(n+2)6−n=n(n2+3n−4)6=n(n−1)(n+4)6,n=D−2.n_3(D) = \binom{n+2}{3} - n = \frac{n(n+1)(n+2)}{6} - n = \frac{n(n^2 + 3n - 4)}{6} = \frac{n(n-1)(n+4)}{6}, \qquad n = D-2.n3​(D)=(3n+2​)−n=6n(n+1)(n+2)​−n=6n(n2+3n−4)​=6n(n−1)(n+4)​,n=D−2. Check the pattern. For a general symmetric traceless transverse rank-sss tensor the count is (n+s−1s)−(n+s−3s−2)\binom{n+s-1}{s} - \binom{n+s-3}{s-2}(sn+s−1​)−(s−2n+s−3​). At s=1s=1s=1 this is n=D−2n = D-2n=D−2 (photon, no trace to remove) and at s=2s=2s=2 it is 12n(n+1)−1=12D(D−3)\tfrac12 n(n+1) - 1 = \tfrac12 D(D-3)21​n(n+1)−1=21​D(D−3) (the graviton, eq. (13)) — both reproduce the note. Evaluate. With n=D−2n = D-2n=D−2:

    • D=4D = 4D=4: n=2n = 2n=2, so n3=2⋅1⋅66=2n_3 = \frac{2\cdot 1\cdot 6}{6} = 2n3​=62⋅1⋅6​=2. (A massless field of any spin s≥1s \geq 1s≥1 has exactly 222 states in D=4D=4D=4 — the two helicities ±s\pm s±s — as this confirms.)
    • D=26D = 26D=26: n=24n = 24n=24, so n3=24⋅23⋅286=154566=2576n_3 = \frac{24\cdot 23\cdot 28}{6} = \frac{15456}{6} = 2576n3​=624⋅23⋅28​=615456​=2576.
