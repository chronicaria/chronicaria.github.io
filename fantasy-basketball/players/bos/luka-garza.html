Luka Garza

Luka Garza is a proven per-minute scoring and rebounding streamer buried behind two veteran centers; his fantasy season depends far more on Queta/Robinson availability than on his own rates.

Nine-category: waiver stash and immediate deep stream if either center is out. Points: per-minute scoring makes him more useful in points formats than the base table suggests. Dynasty: stable reserve-center value, but age 27 and defensive limitations make a long-term starter breakout unlikely.

Why the line looks like this

Garza was productive in 2025-26: 8.1 points, 4.1 rebounds and 0.8 combined steals/blocks in 16.2 minutes across 69 games, shooting 57.7% from the field and 43.3% from three. That stretch-big touch creates better category breadth than many third centers. It also came before Boston committed long term to both Neemias Queta and Mitchell Robinson.

The two-minute line is the healthy-roster competitive baseline and should not be read as a literal fixed nightly shift. Garza may record DNPs, then play 14 minutes when one center is unavailable. His per-appearance fantasy value in those games will be far above this seasonal role line.

Sources: Basketball Reference 2025-26 Celtics, NBA Queta re-signing, NBA Robinson signing.

Category shape

  • Wins with minutes: efficient points, rebounds, a three-point wrinkle and acceptable FT% for a center.
  • Neutral: low turnovers.
  • Costs: limited rim protection and steals compared with fantasy center specialists.
  • Build fit: points-league and scoring stream when a center sits; less attractive for blocks-only needs.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: healthy Queta/Robinson tandem and Cenac development leave Garza almost entirely out of the rotation.
  • Base: 35 appearances at two simultaneous minutes, mostly a reminder that he has no healthy-roster draft role.
  • Ceiling: Robinson misses extended time and Garza gets 16-20 minutes: roughly 8-10 points, 4-5 rebounds and one three, viable in deep points leagues.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Iowa, four seasons (2017-21): 12.1/6.4 as a freshman, 13.1/4.5 as a sophomore, then 23.9 PPG/9.8 RPG as a junior and 24.1 PPG/8.7 RPG as a senior; consensus national player of the year in 2020-21, two-time Big Ten Player of the Year, two-time consensus first-team All-American, Iowa's all-time leading scorer. Draft: 2021, round 2, pick 52, Detroit Pistons.

Durability: No injury history of note; the low GP totals from 2021-25 were role-driven (end-of-bench DNPs in Detroit and Minnesota), not health-driven. When given a real role in 2025-26 he answered the bell 69 times.

Trajectory: The record shows a college megastar whose per-minute NBA scoring has always been real — every stop, he produces when he plays — but whose defense capped him at third-center status until Boston's 2025-26 rotation opened up. The 2025-26 season (8.1/4.1 with 43.3% from three in 16.2 minutes over 69 games) is his proof of concept. In 2026-27 he competes for the third-center spot behind Queta and Robinson; he becomes fantasy-relevant only if one of those two misses extended time and Boston chooses him over rookie development, at which point he is an immediate deep-league points/FG% stream.
