    Riemann surfaces replace Feynman graphs

    The string action only sees the worldsheet up to a local rescaling, so the physical object is a Riemann surface — a 2d surface with angles and global shape but no intrinsic length — and a string "diagram" is one such surface, with external strings shrunk to marked missing points (punctures) instead of legs and vertices.

    Surface, not skeleton

    A Feynman graph is a skeleton: line segments (particles) glued at points (vertices), and the geometry is nothing but the combinatorics of what connects to what. A string diagram is not a skeleton — it is an actual surface, the sheet a string sweeps out in time. Two ideas turn that surface into a Riemann surface and dissolve the whole graph vocabulary:

    1. No length. The Polyakov action is Weyl-invariant: rescaling the worldsheet metric hαβ→Ω(τ,σ)2hαβh_{\alpha\beta}\to\Omega(\tau,\sigma)^2 h_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​→Ω(τ,σ)2hαβ​ changes nothing physical. So "how long is this piece of the surface" is not a question the theory answers. What survives rescaling is angles and global shape — exactly the data a Riemann surface carries. Think of a balloon: you can inflate or deflate any patch, and the string theory can't tell. Only the shape you can't remove by local stretching is real.

    2. Legs become points. Draw a free string as an infinitely long strip. Under a rescaling that shrinks the far past, the entire semi-infinite strip collapses to a single point. An incoming string is not an appendage sticking out to infinity; conformally it is one marked point on an otherwise closed surface — a puncture. "A string comes in here" becomes "delete this point and remember it."

    Put together: the free open string is a disk with two boundary punctures; the free closed string is a sphere with two punctures; and a four-string interaction is a disk (or sphere) with four punctures. There is no interaction vertex anywhere on the surface — locally every patch still looks like a free string (see Splitting and joining worldsheets). The interaction lives only in the global fact that the surface has four punctures, not two. That is the replacement of Feynman graphs: geometry stored in surface topology and shape, not in a graph of legs and vertices.

    Why "metric up to scale" = "complex structure"

    A 2d metric has three independent components hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​. Worldsheet diffeomorphisms (two functions) remove two of them; Weyl rescaling removes the third. Nothing is left of the metric locally — the surface has no intrinsic ruler. What is left is the equivalence class of metrics under rescaling, and in two dimensions (with an orientation) that class is precisely a complex structure: a consistent notion of angle and of which local coordinate is "holomorphic."

    Concretely, glue local complex coordinates zα:Uα→Cz_\alpha : U_\alpha \to \mathbb{C}zα​:Uα​→C on patches of the surface and demand that where patches overlap the change of coordinate is analytic. That defines a Riemann surface. Analyticity is exactly angle-preservation: writing f=u+ivf = u+ivf=u+iv, the Cauchy–Riemann equations \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}, \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}, \tag{1} say fff is conformal (rotations and uniform scalings only, no shear). Two surfaces related by an analytic one-to-one map are declared the same Riemann surface.

    Distance really is gone. If w=f(z)w=f(z)w=f(z) is such a map, then dw=f′(z) dzdw = f'(z)\,dzdw=f′(z)dz, so near z0z_0z0​ every length scales by the same factor ∣f′(z0)∣|f'(z_0)|∣f′(z0​)∣ — the map stretches uniformly at each point but by an amount that varies from point to point. You cannot ask "how far apart are these two points on a Riemann surface," for the same reason you cannot ask it on the Weyl-invariant worldsheet. The two "no ruler" statements are the same statement.

    One caveat is worth stating precisely. The physical worldsheet metric is Lorentzian, −dτ2+dσ2-d\tau^2 + d\sigma^2−dτ2+dσ2, whereas C\mathbb{C}C is Euclidean, dx2+dy2dx^2+dy^2dx2+dy2. So "the worldsheet is a Riemann surface" means: Wick-rotate to Euclidean signature, and then the conformal class is a Riemann surface. This is the standard Euclidean-continuation move of QFT; that it gives a consistent theory of string interactions is the justification, and the precise Lorentzian-to-complex-structure theorem is deferred (see Open questions).

    The conformal map that shrinks a string to a point

    Package the worldsheet coordinates into one complex coordinate, w = \tau + i\sigma. \tag{2} A free open string of light-cone momentum p+p^+p+ is the horizontal strip 0≤σ≤2πα′p+0 \le \sigma \le 2\pi\alpha' p^+0≤σ≤2πα′p+; strings are the vertical segments at fixed time τ\tauτ (the width 2πα′p+2\pi\alpha' p^+2πα′p+ is derived in Splitting and joining worldsheets).

    Now apply the exponential map z = \exp\!\Big(\frac{w}{2\alpha' p^+}\Big). \tag{3} This one map does two things at once:

    (i) The infinite past collapses to a single point. Take w=−τ0+iσw = -\tau_0 + i\sigmaw=−τ0​+iσ and send τ0→+∞\tau_0\to+\inftyτ0​→+∞: z = \underbrace{\exp\!\Big(\frac{-\tau_0}{2\alpha' p^+}\Big)}_{\to\, 0}\ \exp\!\Big(\frac{i\sigma}{2\alpha' p^+}\Big)\ \longrightarrow\ 0 \qquad\text{for every }\sigma. \tag{4} The radial factor kills everything; the angular factor is irrelevant. The entire incoming string — all values of σ\sigmaσ at τ=−∞\tau=-\inftyτ=−∞ — lands on the one point z=0z=0z=0. Symmetrically the far future τ→+∞\tau\to+\inftyτ→+∞ goes to z=∞z=\inftyz=∞. This is the "leg becomes a point" mechanism made explicit.

    (ii) The strip becomes the upper half-plane H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H. As σ\sigmaσ runs across the strip, arg⁡z=σ/(2α′p+)\arg z = \sigma/(2\alpha' p^+)argz=σ/(2α′p+) runs from 000 to π\piπ, so the image fills the upper half-plane. The two boundaries map to the two halves of the real axis: σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 to the positive real axis, σ=2πα′p+\sigma = 2\pi\alpha' p^+σ=2πα′p+ to the negative real axis. The strings, formerly vertical lines, become semicircles centered at z=0z=0z=0.

    To see the two boundary punctures cleanly, send H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H to the unit disk by \eta = \frac{1+iz}{1-iz}. \tag{5} The real axis maps to the unit circle. Check the interior: for z=x+iyz = x+iyz=x+iy with y>0y>0y>0, |\eta|^2 = \frac{|1+iz|^2}{|1-iz|^2} = \frac{x^2+(1-y)^2}{x^2+(1+y)^2} < 1, \tag{6} since (1−y)2<(1+y)2(1-y)^2 < (1+y)^2(1−y)2<(1+y)2 when y>0y>0y>0. So H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H maps onto the disk interior, its boundary onto the circle. Under this map the infinite past z=0z=0z=0 goes to η=1\eta=1η=1 and the infinite future z=∞z=\inftyz=∞ goes to η=−1\eta=-1η=−1: the free open string is a disk with two marked boundary points. Those two points are never reached by any finite-time string, so they are removed — they are the punctures where the incoming and outgoing strings are inserted.

    The dictionary

    Feynman graph Riemann surface
    external leg (line to infinity) puncture (a marked removed point)
    interaction vertex (point where lines meet) nothing local — the interaction is the surface's topology and shape
    propagator a tube or strip of the surface
    • Free open string: H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H (equivalently the disk) with two boundary punctures.
    • Free closed string: identify the top and bottom edges of the strip to get a cylinder of circumference 2πα′p+2\pi\alpha' p^+2πα′p+. The strip has the same width 2πα′p+2\pi\alpha' p^+2πα′p+ as before, but now its two long edges are glued, so the image must close up into a full circle rather than stop at a half-plane; the map z=exp⁡(w/α′p+)z=\exp(w/\alpha' p^+)z=exp(w/α′p+), whose denominator α′p+\alpha' p^+α′p+ is half the open-string 2α′p+2\alpha' p^+2α′p+, does exactly this and sends the cylinder to C\mathbb{C}C with the origin removed. The infinite past is the puncture at z=0z=0z=0; to treat the infinite future symmetrically, add the point at infinity, giving the Riemann sphere C^\hat{\mathbb{C}}C^ with two punctures at 000 and ∞\infty∞.

    The corner in the light-cone strip where a string splits (a −π-\pi−π turning angle in the polygon) looks like a special place, but it is not a term in the action and not an intrinsic feature of the surface. It is an artifact of the particular polygonal coordinate system; up to conformal equivalence it is packaged into puncture positions and moduli (this is what the Schwarz–Christoffel map does in The four-open-string modulus). Every part of the surface is conformally a free string — the whole content is which points are punctured and what shape remains.

    Open questions

    • The Euclidean continuation is used as a license, not proved. The precise theorem relating Lorentzian worldsheets, Wick rotation, and complex structures belongs in a later note.
    • That an interacting disk-like worldsheet can be conformally mapped to H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H with boundary punctures rests on the Riemann mapping / uniformization theorem. This is a complex-analysis prerequisite, not a string-specific derivation; see Moduli spaces for how the surviving shape data (moduli) are then counted.

    Exercise

    Repeat mechanism (i) for the closed string. Using the closed-string map z=exp⁡(w/α′p+)z = \exp(w/\alpha' p^+)z=exp(w/α′p+) with w=τ+iσw=\tau+i\sigmaw=τ+iσ and periodic σ∈[0,2πα′p+)\sigma\in[0,2\pi\alpha' p^+)σ∈[0,2πα′p+), show that (a) the infinite past τ→−∞\tau\to-\inftyτ→−∞ collapses to z=0z=0z=0 for every σ\sigmaσ, (b) a fixed-time string is a circle in the zzz-plane (not a semicircle), and (c) explain in one sentence why we must add the point at infinity to get two punctures, whereas the open string got both punctures already inside H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H.

    Solution

    Write w=τ+iσw = \tau + i\sigmaw=τ+iσ, so z=exp⁡ ⁣(τα′p+)exp⁡ ⁣(iσα′p+)z = \exp\!\big(\tfrac{\tau}{\alpha' p^+}\big)\exp\!\big(\tfrac{i\sigma}{\alpha' p^+}\big)z=exp(α′p+τ​)exp(α′p+iσ​).

    (a) As τ→−∞\tau\to-\inftyτ→−∞ the modulus ∣z∣=exp⁡(τ/α′p+)→0|z| = \exp(\tau/\alpha' p^+)\to 0∣z∣=exp(τ/α′p+)→0 for every σ\sigmaσ, so the whole incoming string collapses to z=0z=0z=0. (Same mechanism as the open case (4); only the constant in the exponent differs.)

    (b) At fixed τ\tauτ, ∣z∣=exp⁡(τ/α′p+)|z| = \exp(\tau/\alpha' p^+)∣z∣=exp(τ/α′p+) is a constant while arg⁡z=σ/(α′p+)\arg z = \sigma/(\alpha' p^+)argz=σ/(α′p+) runs over [0,2π)[0, 2\pi)[0,2π) as σ\sigmaσ sweeps its full period [0,2πα′p+)[0, 2\pi\alpha' p^+)[0,2πα′p+). A constant modulus with the argument covering a full 2π2\pi2π is a full circle centered at the origin. (The open string covered only arg⁡z∈[0,π]\arg z\in[0,\pi]argz∈[0,π], giving a semicircle. The σ\sigmaσ-ranges are in fact the same width 2πα′p+2\pi\alpha' p^+2πα′p+ in both cases; the difference is the map's denominator — 2α′p+2\alpha' p^+2α′p+ for the open string versus α′p+\alpha' p^+α′p+ here — so equal σ\sigmaσ-width sweeps out π\piπ there but 2π2\pi2π here. Geometrically, the open string's two edges stay distinct boundaries rather than being identified, so a fixed-time string is a semicircular arc with two endpoints on the real axis, not a closed loop.)

    (c) The map sends the infinite past to z=0z=0z=0 but the infinite future τ→+∞\tau\to+\inftyτ→+∞ to ∣z∣→∞|z|\to\infty∣z∣→∞, which is not a point of C\mathbb{C}C. To make the two ends symmetric — both are just "a string inserted here" — we compactify C\mathbb{C}C to the Riemann sphere C^=C∪{∞}\hat{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}C^=C∪{∞}, and then z=∞z=\inftyz=∞ is the second puncture. The open string needed no compactification because its exponential map already sent both the past (z=0z=0z=0) and future (z=∞z=\inftyz=∞) to boundary points of H‾\overline{\mathbb{H}}H, and the disk map (5) pulled both to finite points η=±1\eta=\pm1η=±1 on the unit circle.
