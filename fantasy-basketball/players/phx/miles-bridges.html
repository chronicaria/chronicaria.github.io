Miles Bridges

Player card

  • Team / role: Phoenix Suns starting power forward, rim attacker and secondary creator
  • Healthy rotation: 32 MPG
  • Season projection: 70 GP, 32 MPG
  • 9-cat line: .465 FG (6.1/13.1), .820 FT (2.4/2.9), 1.9 3PM, 16.5 PTS, 5.8 REB, 3.0 AST, 0.7 STL, 0.4 BLK, 1.5 TO
  • Projection confidence: Medium
  • Formats: Balanced mid-round points option; lower-ceiling nine-cat forward; fading dynasty asset unless the role surprises

Role and evidence

Phoenix officially acquired Bridges, a 2027 second, and a 2029 first for Grayson Allen, Royce O'Neale, and a 2033 first. Bridges arrives after 77 games at 17.1 points, 5.8 rebounds, 3.2 assists, and 46% shooting for Charlotte. Sources: NBA trade tracker, updated 2026-07-17, NBA trade report, 2026-07-13. Confidence: high.

The projected starting five—Devin Booker, Jalen Green, Dillon Brooks, Bridges, Mark Williams—offers four scorers but only one elite organizer. Bridges should add badly needed rim pressure and rebounding, yet he is unlikely to recover the high-volume Charlotte role. The base trims his field-goal attempts while holding assists near three because Phoenix needs secondary playmaking.

Bridges' 2025-26 category line included .460 FG, .822 FT, 1.9 threes, 0.6 steals, 0.4 blocks, and only 1.4 turnovers. That low-turnover balance is useful, but modest stocks make his overall rank dependent on scoring volume. Source: Hashtag Basketball, updated 2026-07-02. Confidence: medium-high on the historical line.

Category shape

  • Wins: Balanced points, rebounds, threes, and acceptable assists for a forward.
  • Neutral: Both percentages and turnovers should be manageable.
  • Costs: Low steals/blocks and reduced usage leave few category spikes.
  • Build fit: Points leagues and balanced builds needing a stable forward; less attractive in punt-points formats.

Points and dynasty outlook

ESPN placed Bridges No. 88 in April points rankings while he was still a Hornet. Lower usage in Phoenix argues against blindly carrying that rank forward, although better teammates may lift efficiency. Source: ESPN rankings, 2026-04-14. Confidence: medium-low after the trade.

Hashtag's July dynasty board ranked him No. 124 and its keeper crowd No. 146. At age 28, a role-driven decline matters more than long-run prospect growth. Sources: Hashtag dynasty, updated 2026-07-02, Hashtag keeper, updated 2026-07-18. Confidence: medium as market context.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: 14 points with two or fewer assists if Booker/Green dominate creation and the spacing fit compresses drives.
  • Base: 16.5 points, 5.8 rebounds, three assists, and low-end standard-league value.
  • Ceiling: 19 points, 6.5 rebounds, and 3.5 assists if Green misses time or Bridges anchors second units; roughly top-70 points value.

Draft verdict

Treat Bridges as a high-minutes glue scorer rather than a new star. His safest fantasy contribution is across-the-board competence; his risk is that the same balance produces no scarce category. A discount from his Charlotte counting stats is necessary until preseason usage proves otherwise.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Michigan State (2016-18), two seasons. As a freshman he averaged 16.9 points, 8.3 rebounds, and 2.1 assists in 32.0 minutes over 28 games on .486/.389/.685, missing seven games with an ankle injury, and was Big Ten Freshman of the Year. He returned rather than entering the draft and as a sophomore averaged 17.1 points, 7.0 rebounds, and 2.7 assists in 31.4 minutes over 34 games on .457/.364/.853, earning consensus second-team All-American and first-team All-Big Ten. Combined Michigan State line across 62 games: 17.0 points, 7.6 rebounds, 2.4 assists on .470/.375/.776. Draft: 2018, first round, 12th overall, Los Angeles Clippers — traded on draft night to Charlotte for the 11th pick, Shai Gilgeous-Alexander, plus a future second.

Bridges did not appear in an NBA game in 2022-23; the row is retained as a zero rather than omitted because the gap is the central fact of his career shape. The 2019-20 total of 65 is a full season by team games — Charlotte was not invited to the bubble and played only 65 — so it is not a games-missed year.

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-cat ranks are not verifiable from the sources reachable for this entry, and the secondary rankings that surfaced disagreed with one another, so no specific finishes are asserted. The shape is legible without them. Bridges has been a mid-round category asset in his three post-suspension seasons and a fringe-startable one before 2021-22, built on a narrow base: points, rebounds, threes, and a good free-throw percentage on real volume. He has never delivered a scarce category — steals have topped out at 0.9 and blocks at 0.8, and both were below 0.7 in 2025-26 — so his rank has always tracked his scoring volume almost linearly. His points-league value consistently runs ahead of his category value, and materially so: ESPN had him 88th in April 2026 points rankings while still a Hornet, against a Hashtag dynasty placement of 124. That gap is structural, not a market error — the free-throw percentage and low turnover count that help him in 9-cat are cheap in points formats, but the raw scoring and rebounding volume that carries points leagues is his actual skill.

Durability: GP by season: 80, 65, 66, 80, 0, 69, 64, 77. The zero is not medical. Bridges was arrested in June 2022 on domestic violence charges, pled no contest in November 2022, went unsigned through 2022-23, and received a 30-game NBA suspension in April 2023 with 20 games credited as time served; he then missed the first 10 games of 2023-24 serving the remainder. Setting that aside, his medical record is among the better ones at his position — no season-ending injury, no surgery of note, and 77 games in 2025-26 at age 27. The 2024-25 dip to 64 was an accumulation of minor absences rather than one event. For projection purposes he should be treated as a 70-plus-game player, with the caveat that the availability history has never been tested by an actual serious injury.

Trajectory: The arc has two discontinuities and neither is a skill leap. The first is 2021-22, when minutes went from 29.3 to 35.5 and scoring from 12.7 to 20.2 alongside LaMelo Ball's arrival as a full-time creator; per-36 scoring moved from 15.6 to 20.5, so some of it was real, but the shot volume was granted rather than earned. The second is the lost 2022-23 season, after which he returned at 26 to a rebuilding Charlotte team that handed him a 37.4-minute, high-usage role no contender would have. That role flattered the counting stats and hid the efficiency: .462 in 2023-24 and .431 in 2024-25 on rising volume is a player absorbing possessions, not creating value. The 2025-26 season is the useful one to read forward from, because it is the first in which he played a normal-sized role — 31.0 minutes, 17.1 points, .460, turnovers down to 1.4 — and his per-36 scoring held at 19.9. What did not hold was rebounding: 8.5 boards per 36 in 2024-25 fell to 6.7 in 2025-26, a rate decline rather than a minutes artifact, and the first genuine athletic signal in his profile. Entering his age-28 season in Phoenix behind Booker and Green, the reasonable expectation is that the per-minute scoring survives, the volume does not, and the rebounding continues to erode — which leaves a competent forward with no category he wins.
