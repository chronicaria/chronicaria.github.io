<h2 id="main-challenge-to-the-model">Main Challenge To The Model</h2>
<p>My best challenge is that the model is slightly too low because it may under-credit the interaction between elite frontcourt defense, rim pressure, and Spoelstra's scheme flexibility.</p>
<p>Giannis is not just another high-RAPM player being added to a depth chart. He changes the geometry of the team: he can protect the rim next to Bam instead of asking Bam to be the only back-line solution, he can ignite transition after defensive rebounds, and his downhill pressure creates the type of corner-three and scramble-pass ecosystem that helps lower-creation shooters look better. NBA.com notes that Giannis' recent impact has been enormous, including Milwaukee being 14.1 points per 100 better with him on the floor in 2025-26, albeit with a major minutes caveat. If Miami gets even a near-normal Giannis season, the 47.7-win prior may be a touch conservative.</p>
<p>The reason this is only a +1.8-win adjustment is that the counterweights are real. Powell's departure removes a 21.7 PPG, 38% three-point scorer from last season's Heat context. Herro, Ware, Jaquez, and Jakucionis are gone in the Giannis package. Miami is hard-capped/roster-spot constrained, and the depth chart leans on Hardaway at age 34, Portis in a new smaller role, young Larsson, low-sample Conwell, and several fringe or two-way names. If Giannis misses significant time, the floor is not a normal 48-win team's floor.</p>

<h2 id="adjustment-logic">Adjustment Logic</h2>
<p>I am applying a judgmental adjustment of +0.7 pts/100, roughly +1.8 wins, because I think the model may slightly under-credit the combined effect of:</p>
<ul>
<li>Giannis and Bam sharing back-line defensive responsibilities.</li>
<li>Spoelstra having enough defensive versatility to build elite regular-season shot defense.</li>
<li>Giannis creating easier offense for limited-creation guards through transition, roll gravity, and kickouts.</li>
<li>Hardaway and Wiggins giving just enough shooting to keep the regular-season offense above the scary version of the spacing floor.</li>
</ul>
<p>I am not applying a larger adjustment because:</p>
<ul>
<li>Powell's departure is a real shot-creation and shooting loss.</li>
<li>Herro/Ware/Jaquez/Jakucionis are gone, so this is not simply last year's Heat plus Giannis.</li>
<li>The roster has first-apron and open-spot constraints.</li>
<li>The non-authoritative roster context contains stale player rows and a lot of high-uncertainty/deep-bench value.</li>
<li>Giannis' 2025-26 minutes were low enough that 57 expected games is not obviously too conservative.</li>
</ul>
<p>The adjusted view is therefore 49.5 wins, not a new model output.</p>
<h2 id="counter-thesis">Counter-Thesis</h2>
<p>The strongest argument against <code>MODEL_TOO_LOW</code> is that 47.7 wins already bakes in a huge amount of Giannis value, and the roster around him may be worse for regular-season stability than the headline names imply. Powell left, Herro left, Ware left, and Miami no longer has a clean second perimeter scorer. If Mitchell and Larsson are treated as high-minute starters, opponents can load the paint against Giannis and Bam. If Hardaway is the required spacing release valve at age 34 and Portis is the only proven bench scorer, Miami may win because of defense but struggle to manufacture enough half-court offense against good teams. In that world, the model is not too low; it is right, or even a little too high.</p>
<p>There is also a data-quality counter-thesis. The authoritative standings prior and the non-authoritative roster-detail artifact disagree on team wins and strength, while the roster-context file includes Ament on Miami despite his rights being traded to Milwaukee. If similar roster-staleness exists elsewhere in the Miami input, my adjustment could be leaning on a moving target.</p>
<h2 id="what-would-change-my-mind">What Would Change My Mind</h2>
<ul>
<li>A confirmed opening-night depth chart showing Hardaway starting or playing 24-plus MPG would make me more comfortable with the positive adjustment.</li>
<li>A reliable update that Giannis will be load-managed below roughly 55 games or below 30 MPG would push the stance back toward <code>MODEL_ABOUT_RIGHT</code> or <code>MODEL_TOO_HIGH</code>.</li>
<li>A late veteran guard/wing signing under the apron would increase the upside case.</li>
<li>A preseason rotation where Mitchell, Larsson, and Bam all share the floor with limited shooting would reduce confidence.</li>
<li>A corrected roster/projection artifact that removes stale Ament minutes and reconciles the standings JSON with team-detail rows would sharpen the direction.</li>
<li>Early-season evidence that Conwell is a real rotation shooter would raise the depth floor.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="data-quality-and-uncertainty">Data Quality And Uncertainty</h2>
<ul>
<li>The authoritative team prior is only <code>private-model-workspace/andrewjparkus.github.io/projection_2026_27.json</code>. All team-level numbers in this memo come from that row.</li>
<li>The non-authoritative team-detail artifact has conflicting team-level numbers for Miami (<code>proj_wins</code> 45.7, <code>strength</code> 6.22), so I do not use those for prior, odds, or final adjustment base.</li>
<li>The same non-authoritative roster context includes Nate Ament as a Miami rotation player, while external draft/trade reporting says his rights went to Milwaukee.</li>
<li>NBA.com's Heat roster page still displays Norman Powell in the explore/roster area even though NBA.com News and Miami Herald report he agreed to join Chicago. I treat Powell as gone and flag the roster page as possibly lagged.</li>
<li>The local model prior reports <code>n_cold_start = 5</code>, and the roster-context file has many high-uncertainty players. The back half of the rotation should be treated cautiously until final camp rosters settle.</li>
<li>All external facts were checked on July 9, 2026, using public/free web sources only.</li>
</ul>
