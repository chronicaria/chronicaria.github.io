    On a circle of radius R , momentum is quantized: p = n/R , n\in\mathbb{Z} . The spacing 1/R is how you measure the size of a hidden circle.

    Momentum spacing is a ruler for the radius

    Roll one spatial direction into a circle of circumference 2πR2\pi R2πR. A wave living on that circle must close up on itself after one lap — you cannot fit half a wavelength around it. Only whole numbers of wavelengths survive, so momentum sits on a discrete ladder evenly spaced by 1/R1/R1/R.

    That spacing is a ruler. A large RRR packs the momentum levels close together, so they blur into the continuum of flat space — a big enough circle is indistinguishable from an infinite line. A small RRR spreads the levels apart, so the first excited momentum state is expensive and easy to spot. Read off the spacing and you have the radius. This is how a particle physicist would diagnose an extra dimension curled up too small to see, and it is exactly the intuition T-duality overturns.

    None of this is stringy yet. It is quantum mechanics on a circle, and a point particle obeys it too. The stringy twist, winding, comes in the sibling note.

    Setup: one direction curled up

    Pick a spatial coordinate xxx and make it a circle by declaring x∼x+2πR.x \sim x + 2\pi R.x∼x+2πR. The two points xxx and x+2πRx+2\pi Rx+2πR are literally the same point; circumference 2πR2\pi R2πR, radius RRR. Everything else stays ordinary non-compact Minkowski space. (In string theory this is one coordinate, x≡X25x \equiv X^{25}x≡X25, while X0,X1X^0, X^1X0,X1 stay non-compact so we can still fix Light-cone gauge with them.)

    Why momentum is quantized

    The argument is one line, and it turns on a single fact: momentum generates translations. The operator that slides a state a distance aaa along xxx is e−iap,e^{-iap},e−iap, where ppp is conjugate to the circle coordinate x0x_0x0​, formally [x0,p]=i[x_0,p]=i[x0​,p]=i. Exponentiating the generator of motion produces the finite shift.

    Now use the identification. A translation by one full circumference, a=2πRa = 2\pi Ra=2πR, sends every point back to itself — it is the identity map on the circle. So the operator implementing it must act as the identity on every physical state: e−i(2πR) p=1.e^{-i(2\pi R)\,p} = \mathbb{1}.e−i(2πR)p=1. Going once around must return every wavefunction to itself. For that exponential to equal 1\mathbb{1}1 its eigenvalues must all be 111, forcing 2πR p∈2πZ2\pi R\,p \in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}2πRp∈2πZ: \boxed{\,p = \frac{n}{R}, \qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.\,} \tag{1}

    The Fourier picture gives the same result and pins the factor down. A single-valued wavefunction on the circle is built from modes ψn(x)=einx/R,ψn(x+2πR)=ψn(x),\psi_n(x)=e^{inx/R}, \qquad \psi_n(x+2\pi R)=\psi_n(x),ψn​(x)=einx/R,ψn​(x+2πR)=ψn​(x), with −i∂xψn=(n/R)ψn-i\partial_x\psi_n = (n/R)\psi_n−i∂x​ψn​=(n/R)ψn​. The 2π2\pi2π lives in the circumference, but the momentum spacing is 1/R1/R1/R, not 1/(2πR)1/(2\pi R)1/(2πR).

    The integer nnn is the momentum number — the ruler from above, now with an equation attached.

    A subtlety: x0x_0x0​ is not a real operator

    The position zero mode x0x_0x0​ is not a well-defined operator: its eigenvalues are ambiguous under x0∼x0+2πRx_0 \sim x_0 + 2\pi Rx0​∼x0​+2πR, so which of the identified values would you report? The commutator [x0,p]=i[x_0,p]=i[x0​,p]=i is therefore a formal mnemonic. The well-defined objects are the exponentials eiℓx0/R,ℓ∈Z,e^{i\ell x_0/R}, \quad \ell\in\mathbb{Z},eiℓx0​/R,ℓ∈Z, which are invariant under x0→x0+2πRx_0 \to x_0+2\pi Rx0​→x0​+2πR (the shift multiplies them by e2πiℓ=1e^{2\pi i \ell}=1e2πiℓ=1). Feeding the formal [x0,p]=i[x_0,p]=i[x0​,p]=i into a Hadamard expansion gives their action unambiguously: e^{-i\ell x_0/R}\, p\, e^{i\ell x_0/R} = p + \frac{\ell}{R}. \tag{2} So eiℓx0/Re^{i\ell x_0/R}eiℓx0​/R raises the momentum eigenvalue by ℓ/R\ell/Rℓ/R, i.e. n→n+ℓn \to n+\elln→n+ℓ. These are the honest raising operators for momentum number, and being invariant under the identification, they never see the ambiguity in x0x_0x0​. On a circle, trust the exponentials; the commutator is shorthand for what they do.

    How this feeds the mass formula

    To an observer in the large non-compact directions, motion around the invisible circle looks like internal energy. A state with compact momentum p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R and nothing else has rest mass (natural units, ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1) M=∣p∣=∣n∣R.M = |p| = \frac{|n|}{R}.M=∣p∣=R∣n∣​. This is the 1/R1/R1/R term that trades places with the ∝R\propto R∝R winding energy under T-duality; see Compactified closed-string mass formula.

    For an open string on a space-filling brane, nnn is the only internal label: an open string cannot wind, since its endpoints slide off and let the loop shrink to a point. That asymmetry is exactly what T-duality exploits to change a brane's dimension in Dp to D(p minus 1) under T-duality.

    Open questions

    • Why the commutator is only formal. The load-bearing statement is the well-defined shift (2), not [x0,p]=i[x_0,p]=i[x0​,p]=i; the mass formula uses the spectrum of ppp, never an eigenvalue of x0x_0x0​. A fully rigorous compact-boson Hilbert space is set up later in the CFT viewpoint.

    Exercise

    A string theorist claims: "Compactify at radius RRR and the lightest nonzero-momentum state has mass M1=1/RM_1 = 1/RM1​=1/R. Halve the radius and that mass doubles." Confirm the claim, then decide whether it can be pushed arbitrarily far — is there a smallest circle for which "M1M_1M1​ grows as RRR shrinks" is the whole story? Answer using only this note plus what you know sits next door.

    Solution

    The lightest nonzero-momentum state has n=±1n=\pm 1n=±1, so from (1), p=1/Rp = 1/Rp=1/R and its rest mass is M1=∣p∣=1/RM_1 = |p| = 1/RM1​=∣p∣=1/R. Sending R→R/2R \to R/2R→R/2 gives M1=1/(R/2)=2/RM_1 = 1/(R/2) = 2/RM1​=1/(R/2)=2/R — doubled. The claim is correct as a statement about momentum modes.

    It is not the whole story, though, and this note flags why: momentum is the only internal label for an open string, not a closed one. A closed string can also wind, with winding energy ∝R\propto R∝R (a wound string is a stretched rubber band; see Winding modes). As RRR shrinks, momentum states get heavier but winding states get lighter, so the closed string has no runaway. The two effects cross, and the lightest states are momentum modes at large RRR but winding modes at small RRR. The crossover — where the shrinking circle stops looking small and starts looking large again — is the self-dual radius R∗=α′R^*=\sqrt{\alpha'}R∗=α′​, the seed of T-duality: shrinking a circle past R∗R^*R∗ is physically the same as growing it, with nnn and mmm swapped.
