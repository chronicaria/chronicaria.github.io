    Electric and magnetic fields on D-branes

    A background gauge field on a D-brane is the geometry of a T-dual brane in disguise: an electric field turns the dual brane into one that moves, so the speed limit v<c becomes a maximum electric field, while a magnetic field tilts it, so wrapping it on a torus quantizes the flux. Electric is a boost, magnetic is a rotation.

    This is the bridge from Maxwell fields coupled to open strings to Born-Infeld action: the relativistic square roots and the field bounds found here are exactly what the Born–Infeld Lagrangian must reproduce.

    The one dimensionless knob

    Everything runs on the dimensionless field strengths \mathcal E \equiv 2\pi\alpha'\,E, \qquad \mathcal B \equiv 2\pi\alpha'\,B. \tag{1} This is the same 2πα′F2\pi\alpha' F2πα′F that entered the mixed boundary condition ∂σXm−2πα′Fmn∂τXn=0\partial_\sigma X_m - 2\pi\alpha' F_{mn}\partial_\tau X^n = 0∂σ​Xm​−2πα′Fmn​∂τ​Xn=0 (from Maxwell fields coupled to open strings). These are the numbers that appear everywhere below, and E=1\mathcal E = 1E=1 is where the story breaks.

    Electric field = moving brane, and the critical field

    Take a Dppp-brane wrapped on a compact direction x25x^{25}x25 of radius RRR, carrying a constant electric field along it, F25,0=EF_{25,0} = EF25,0​=E. The only string coordinates that feel this are time X0X^0X0 and the wrapped direction X≡X25X\equiv X^{25}X≡X25; the mixed boundary condition couples them: \partial_\sigma X^0 - \mathcal E\,\partial_\tau X = 0, \qquad \partial_\sigma X - \mathcal E\,\partial_\tau X^0 = 0. \tag{2} Each ties the endpoint's σ\sigmaσ-slope in one direction to its τ\tauτ-velocity in the other: the field drags the string endpoint along the circle.

    To read off the physics, package (2) as a matrix. Switch to the worldsheet derivatives ∂±≡12(∂τ±∂σ)\partial_\pm \equiv \tfrac12(\partial_\tau \pm \partial_\sigma)∂±​≡21​(∂τ​±∂σ​) and solve for the ∂+\partial_+∂+​ data in terms of the ∂−\partial_-∂−​ data: \partial_+\begin{pmatrix} X^0\\ X\end{pmatrix} = \frac{1}{1-\mathcal E^2}\begin{pmatrix} 1+\mathcal E^2 & 2\mathcal E\\ 2\mathcal E & 1+\mathcal E^2\end{pmatrix}\partial_-\begin{pmatrix} X^0\\ X\end{pmatrix}. \tag{3} The matrix has unit determinant and blows up at E=±1\mathcal E=\pm1E=±1 — the first hint that E=1\mathcal E = 1E=1 is a wall.

    Compare this to a genuinely moving brane. A D(p−1)(p{-}1)(p−1)-brane on the dual circle has plain boundary conditions in its own rest frame: Neumann along the brane, Dirichlet across it. Boost to the lab frame with velocity β=v/c\beta = v/cβ=v/c and those conditions rotate into a matrix of exactly the form of (3), with β\betaβ wherever E\mathcal EE stood. The two configurations obey identical string boundary conditions precisely when \boxed{\;\mathcal E = 2\pi\alpha'\,E = \beta\;}. \tag{4} The endpoint dragged by the electric field is the endpoint sitting on a brane gliding past at speed β\betaβ. An electric field is a velocity.

    That dictionary turns a speed limit into a field limit. No massive brane reaches the speed of light, so β<1\beta<1β<1, so E<1\mathcal E<1E<1: |E| = \frac{|\beta|}{2\pi\alpha'} < \frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'} \equiv E_{\rm crit}. \tag{5} The electric field on a D-brane is bounded — unthinkable in Maxwell theory, where EEE can take any value. The ceiling is exactly the string tension: \boxed{\;E_{\rm crit} = T_0 = \frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\;}. \tag{6}

    The bound equals the tension for a reason visible at a single endpoint. Two forces act on a charged string endpoint in the field: the electric pull EEE along the brane, and the tension yanking it back. The endpoint moves at speed v⊥v_\perpv⊥​ perpendicular to the string, so the tension available to resist is Lorentz-reduced to T01−v⊥2T_0\sqrt{1-v_\perp^2}T0​1−v⊥2​​ — the same 1−v2\sqrt{1-v^2}1−v2​ that shrinks a relativistic string's effective tension. Balance demands E = T_0\sqrt{1-v_\perp^2}. \tag{7} For E<T0E<T_0E<T0​ this has a solution, v⊥=1−(E/T0)2=1−E2v_\perp = \sqrt{1-(E/T_0)^2} = \sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2}v⊥​=1−(E/T0​)2​=1−E2​; at E=T0E=T_0E=T0​ the endpoint must freeze (v⊥→0v_\perp\to 0v⊥​→0), the tension maxed out; for E>T0E>T_0E>T0​ no real speed balances the pull and the configuration runs away. Same wall, now seen from the endpoint rather than the dual brane. Maxwell has no scale at which EEE saturates, so a maximal field is already evidence that the brane gauge field obeys something nonlinear — Born–Infeld, whose built-in maximal field bbb is exactly this EcritE_{\rm crit}Ecrit​.

    Magnetic field = tilted brane, and flux quantization

    Now put a constant magnetic field F23=BF_{23}=BF23​=B on the brane. The mixed boundary condition ties X2X^2X2 and X3X^3X3: \partial_\sigma X^2 - \mathcal B\,\partial_\tau X^3 = 0, \qquad \partial_\sigma X^3 + \mathcal B\,\partial_\tau X^2 = 0. \tag{8} Compactify x3x^3x3 and T-dualize it. The clean N/D conditions of a tilted D(p−1)(p{-}1)(p−1)-brane on the dual cylinder — a brane rotated by angle α\alphaα — reproduce (8) exactly when \boxed{\;\mathcal B = 2\pi\alpha'\,B = -\tan\alpha\;}. \tag{9} An electric field is a boost; a magnetic field is a rotation. A weak field is a small tilt, and it takes an infinite field to tip the dual brane a full 90∘90^\circ90∘. Two consequences carry forward.

    • Flux quantization. Wrap the tilted brane on a two-torus. A tilted line closes up on the torus only after winding an integer number of times, so the tilt angle is locked to a discrete set and the total magnetic flux is quantized: \Phi = -2\pi n, \qquad n\in\mathbb Z. \tag{10} (The sign is orientation convention; the invariant statement is Φ/2π∈Z\Phi/2\pi\in\mathbb ZΦ/2π∈Z.) A constant BBB on a compact brane is not a free dial — the integer nnn is a topological winding number, pure stringy input that Maxwell on R4\mathbb R^4R4 never sees.

    • Dissolved branes. That integer counts branes. A Dppp-brane with flux 2πn2\pi n2πn is deformation-equivalent to a Dppp-brane carrying nnn dissolved D(p−2)(p{-}2)(p−2)-branes: a D2 with magnetic flux is a D2 with nnn D0-branes melted into it, the flux being the smeared-out D0 charge. The bound state has less energy than the pieces held apart — the energies add in quadrature, E=Mp2+(nMp−2)2<Mp+nMp−2E=\sqrt{M_p^2+(nM_{p-2})^2} < M_p + nM_{p-2}E=Mp2​+(nMp−2​)2​<Mp​+nMp−2​, the signature of a genuine bound state.

    The unifying square root

    Both dual Lagrangians have the same shape. The moving brane carries the relativistic factor 1−β2=1−E2\sqrt{1-\beta^2} = \sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2}1−β2​=1−E2​. The tilted brane is geometrically longer than the untilted one by 1/cos⁡α=1+tan⁡2α=1+B21/\cos\alpha = \sqrt{1+\tan^2\alpha} = \sqrt{1+\mathcal B^2}1/cosα=1+tan2α​=1+B2​, so its Lagrangian (energy ∝\propto∝ length) carries that root. Translated back to the Dppp-brane: L_{\rm electric} \propto -\sqrt{1-(2\pi\alpha' E)^2}, \qquad L_{\rm magnetic} \propto -\sqrt{1+(2\pi\alpha' B)^2}. \tag{11} These are the pure-electric and pure-magnetic limits of one object, −det⁡ ⁣(ηmn+2πα′Fmn),\sqrt{-\det\!\big(\eta_{mn}+2\pi\alpha' F_{mn}\big)},−det(ηmn​+2πα′Fmn​)​, the Born–Infeld determinant. The electric field sits in a time–space slot of ηmn+2πα′Fmn\eta_{mn}+2\pi\alpha' F_{mn}ηmn​+2πα′Fmn​, where the Minkowski minus sign (mostly-plus signature) turns into the 1−E21-\mathcal E^21−E2; the magnetic field sits in a space–space slot, all plus, giving 1+B21+\mathcal B^21+B2. The relative sign between the two bounds is nothing but the signature of spacetime. The full determinant is assembled in Born-Infeld action; the two limits derived here are the constant-field tests it must pass. They do not by themselves fix the determinant for arbitrary Fmn(x)F_{mn}(x)Fmn​(x) — that is a slowly-varying-field statement made in the sibling notes.

    Open questions

    • The endpoint force-balance in (7) uses the effective-tension factor T01−v⊥2T_0\sqrt{1-v_\perp^2}T0​1−v⊥2​​ as given; a full rotating-string solution would confirm v⊥=1−E2v_\perp=\sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2}v⊥​=1−E2​ directly, but the T-duality route already delivers the bound, so it is not reproduced here.
    • The dissolved-brane / bound-state energetics (EEE in quadrature) is a structural preview of D-brane charge, stated but not derived; it is not needed for the Born–Infeld determinant.

    Exercise

    The two boxed dictionary entries are E=β\mathcal E = \betaE=β (electric = boost) and B=−tan⁡α\mathcal B = -\tan\alphaB=−tanα (magnetic = rotation). Use them to make each square root in (11) predict its own physics.

    (a) From the electric identification, what is the endpoint speed v⊥v_\perpv⊥​ at the critical field E=EcritE=E_{\rm crit}E=Ecrit​, and what does the factor 1−E2\sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2}1−E2​ do there? (b) From the magnetic identification, show the tilted-brane length factor is 1+B2\sqrt{1+\mathcal B^2}1+B2​ and explain why it can grow without bound while the electric factor cannot.     Solution

    (a) At E=Ecrit=T0E=E_{\rm crit}=T_0E=Ecrit​=T0​ we have E=2πα′E=E/T0=1\mathcal E = 2\pi\alpha' E = E/T_0 = 1E=2πα′E=E/T0​=1, so from (7), v⊥=1−E2=0v_\perp = \sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2} = 0v⊥​=1−E2​=0: the endpoint freezes. The moving-brane Lagrangian Lelectric∝−1−E2L_{\rm electric}\propto -\sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2}Lelectric​∝−1−E2​ vanishes with it — the dual brane has hit the speed of light (β=E=1\beta=\mathcal E=1β=E=1), which is why the field cannot be pushed further. The bound E<EcritE<E_{\rm crit}E<Ecrit​ is the speed limit β<1\beta<1β<1 in disguise. (b) A segment that spans horizontal length ℓ\ellℓ but is tilted by angle α\alphaα is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with base ℓ\ellℓ, so its length is ℓ/cos⁡α\ell/\cos\alphaℓ/cosα. Then 1/cos⁡α=1+tan⁡2α=1+B21/\cos\alpha = \sqrt{1+\tan^2\alpha} = \sqrt{1+\mathcal B^2}1/cosα=1+tan2α​=1+B2​ using B=−tan⁡α\mathcal B=-\tan\alphaB=−tanα from (9), where tan⁡2α=B2\tan^2\alpha=\mathcal B^2tan2α=B2 kills the sign. As B→∞B\to\inftyB→∞, α→90∘\alpha\to 90^\circα→90∘ and 1+B2→∞\sqrt{1+\mathcal B^2}\to\infty1+B2​→∞: a rotation angle has no ceiling, so B\mathcal BB (hence BBB) is unbounded. The electric factor 1−E2\sqrt{1-\mathcal E^2}1−E2​ instead hits zero at E=1\mathcal E=1E=1 and goes imaginary beyond, because a velocity is capped at 111. Rotations run to infinity; boosts saturate at ccc. (c) The electric field occupies a time–space entry of ηmn+2πα′Fmn\eta_{mn}+2\pi\alpha' F_{mn}ηmn​+2πα′Fmn​, and the −1-1−1 from Minkowski time in that 2×22\times22×2 block yields 1−E21-\mathcal E^21−E2; the magnetic field occupies a space–space entry, all +1+1+1, yielding 1+B21+\mathcal B^21+B2.
