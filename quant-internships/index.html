How to read this

  • Type. QT quantitative trader, QR quantitative researcher, QD quantitative developer or analyst. Pure software, FPGA and hardware roles are left out even at quant firms.
  • Tier is a rough read on selectivity and exit value, not a ranking of the firms as businesses. T1 is the group that clears the highest bar and pays the most; T3 is still a real quant seat.
  • Multi-apply. Several firms want exactly one application per cycle. Where that column is red, choose your role before you submit, because the choice is usually irreversible for the season.
  • Intern pay is what the firm posts where pay-transparency law forces it, and what candidates report otherwise. Treat reported figures as approximate.
  • Track stores a local "applied" flag in this browser only. Nothing leaves the page.

The one-application traps

Twelve rows on this page restrict you, and the restriction is usually irreversible for the season. Decide before you submit:

  • Hudson River Trading. The posting says it outright: "We do not allow multiple applications. Please apply to the ONE role you are most interested in."
  • Virtu. "Please only apply to one internship position." Their QT and QR seats compete with each other.
  • IMC. One application per role per year, and they strongly urge a single best fit. QT and QR are separate reqs, so this is a real choice.
  • Akuna. Applying signals the role is your top Akuna preference and you will not be considered for the others.
  • Squarepoint. The req opens in bold with a one-application instruction.
  • Radix. Apply to only one Radix posting.
  • Quantic. Exactly one of the three Quantic roles. QR outranks QD for you.
  • Millennium. Two applications maximum, counted across every location.

By contrast, Jane Street takes multiple applications, D. E. Shaw lets you bundle several roles into one submission, and Citadel and Citadel Securities are separate firms that recruit independently, so both are fair game. Optiver has no per-role limit but does impose a global cooldown after a failed assessment, so do not burn it cold.

Applying

  • Almost every firm here reviews on a rolling basis. Applying in early August is materially better than applying in late September with the same résumé.
  • The online assessments are the real filter, not the résumé. Most are timed mental arithmetic and probability. Practise before you spend an application on a firm you care about.
  • Watch the graduation windows. Several postings name a range rather than a class year: DRW wants December 2027 to June 2028, IMC September 2027 to July 2028, Optiver December 2027 to June 2029, and Flow Traders says "Class of 2028 preferred".
