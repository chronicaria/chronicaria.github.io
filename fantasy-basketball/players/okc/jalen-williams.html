Why the line looks like this

Williams' 2025-26 rate—17.1 points, 4.6 rebounds, 5.5 assists, 1.2 steals and 48.4% FG in 28.4 minutes—came across only 33 games. He missed the first 19 while recovering from right-wrist surgery, then lost 30 more to right-hamstring trouble — an arithmetic residual (82 − 19 − 33) spanning a 10-game absence from January 17, a February 9 return and a reaggravation, not one continuous stretch. In the playoffs he suffered a Grade 1 left hamstring strain in Game 2 of the first round against Phoenix, missed the rest of that series and all four Lakers games, returned for West finals Game 1, reaggravated it in the first quarter of Game 2, and played only 10 minutes in Game 6 before sitting Game 7. He appeared in 5 of 15 playoff games.

The .500 FG and 1.2 3PM in the card sit deliberately between the 2025-26 collapse and the 2024-25 baseline, and the wrist is why. He shot .299 from three on roughly half his usual volume in the season immediately following ligament repair on his shooting hand, against .365 the year before; a repaired wrist finding its feel is a far better explanation than a lost jumper, so the projection assumes partial recovery rather than either extreme. If the hand is genuinely clean by camp, the three-point line is the most likely place this projection proves too low.

The base restores some scoring/efficiency toward his healthy 2024-25 level but refuses to project 70 games without public full-speed clearance. Oklahoma City went 45-10 without him in the combined regular/postseason sample cited during his May return, so the organization has little incentive to rush him. Ajay Mitchell, Jared McCain and Cason Wallace can absorb possessions.

Markets disagree sharply by horizon and format: SI ranked him 23rd, ESPN points 39th, NBC's eight-cat mock 45th, while one early Reddit ADP left him outside the first 60. Sources: NBA first-round Game 2 left-hamstring diagnosis, NBA May return context, NBA Game 7 absence, Basketball Reference, ESPN points.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Santa Clara, three seasons (2019-22), West Coast Conference. 2019-20: 33 games, 25.5 minutes, 7.7 points, 2.8 rebounds, 1.9 assists, 1.3 steals on .436/.352/.763. 2020-21 (COVID-shortened): 18 games, 31.6 minutes, 11.5 points, 4.1 rebounds, 2.3 assists on .399/.274/.757. 2021-22: 33 games, 34.8 minutes, 18.0 points, 4.4 rebounds, 4.2 assists, 1.2 steals on .513/.396/.809. Career 84 games, 12.6 points on .469, a figure Santa Clara's own records confirm. The junior year is the whole draft case — he finished second in the WCC in scoring, led the conference in total points, and was the only player in the country combining that scoring rate with 56 percent inside the arc and 41 percent outside it. First-team All-WCC, second-team NABC All-District, Lou Henson Award finalist. He was a three-star recruit out of Perry High School in Gilbert, Arizona, and the first Santa Clara player drafted since Steve Nash. Draft: 2022, first round, 12th overall, Oklahoma City Thunder — the pick originally belonged to the Clippers and reached OKC in the 2019 Paul George trade. Not traded on draft night.

Three-point percentage by season, which matters more than the makes column for reading this career: .356, .427, .365, .299. Honors: All-Star in 2025, All-NBA third team and All-Defensive second team for 2024-25, and an NBA championship the same year.

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-category ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag could not be retrieved for this dossier, and no verified season-end 9-cat finish was found for any of his four seasons, so none is asserted here. The one adjacent citation found is an ESPN in-season head-to-head points ranking during 2024-25 that listed him 24th overall, but that is a forward-looking ranking rather than a season-end result and should not be treated as a finish. The structural read: he is a category-league generalist whose value comes from having no holes rather than from any elite category — good field goal percentage, good free throw percentage on real volume, forward-scarce assists, above-average steals, and only blocks as a genuine weakness. That profile is worth more in category leagues than in points leagues, where his moderate scoring volume and low rebound total cap him. The exception is 2023-24, when .540 from the field and .427 from three made him a percentage weapon rather than merely percentage-neutral.

Durability: 75, 71, 69, 33. Causes: a right orbital bone fracture requiring surgery in October 2022 cost him the start of his rookie year without denting the total; 2023-24 and 2024-25 were routine. The break in the record is a torn ligament in his right wrist — his shooting hand — that he played through during the 2024-25 championship run, reportedly taking roughly 30 painkilling injections in the playoffs, and which was surgically repaired on 2025-06-30. That surgery cost him the first 19 games of 2025-26. He then strained his right hamstring on January 17 versus Miami, missed 10 games, returned February 9 and aggravated it, losing roughly 30 games in total across the stop-start pattern and finishing at 33. The postseason added a left hamstring strain suffered in Game 2 of the first round against Phoenix, which cost him the remainder of that series, the entire second-round sweep of the Lakers, and — after a return in West finals Game 1 and a first-quarter reaggravation in Game 2 — Games 3, 4, 5 and 7 against San Antonio. He played 5 of 15 playoff games and missed 10, with two failed returns. Two different hamstrings failing inside one season is the single most concerning entry in the file, and it is worth noting that both followed a long layoff and a compressed return.

Trajectory: The first three seasons are a clean, fast ascent with one wrinkle. He arrived as an efficient low-usage third option, jumped to 19.1 points on .540/.427 in year two, then to 21.6 on higher usage in year three — and the per-36 scoring rate confirms the second jump was real, rising from 22.0 to 24.0 rather than tracking minutes. The cost was efficiency: .540 to .484 from the field is the standard price of a usage increase, and the .427 from three in 2023-24 was always going to regress. Then 2025-26 breaks the pattern, and the interesting question is which parts of it are injury and which are signal. Per-36 points fell to 21.7 while per-36 assists rose sharply from 5.7 to 7.0, so the playmaking growth looks like genuine development in a smaller role; the shooting collapse to .299 from three on halved volume, alongside a career-high .837 from the line, is much more plausibly a repaired wrist finding its feel than a lost jumper. He turns 26 in April 2027, which places 2026-27 squarely in the prime window with no aging-curve drag. What the history implies is that the talent question is settled and the rate profile should revert close to the 2024-25 line if the wrist is clean; the entire investment case reduces to games played, and the record now contains one severe availability season out of four with a cause — bilateral hamstrings following a surgical layoff — that has a real recurrence rate.
