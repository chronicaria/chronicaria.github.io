Abstract

A body in hydrostatic equilibrium — a fast-rotating brown dwarf, a tidally distorted close binary — is bounded by an equipotential of its own effective potential, a closed surface that is not a sphere and that a standard sphere-based spherical-harmonic or latitude–longitude atmospheric core structurally cannot carry without new geometry. We present a surface-mesh-free, harmonic-free finite-difference method that solves the forced–damped shallow-water equations directly on such a figure, deep into the extreme rapid-rotation and near-Roche-fill regime, by embedding it as the zero level set S = {φ = 0} of a scalar on a background Cartesian grid and building difference operators at the cut points where S crosses grid edges. The method rests on a single structural identity: taking the level-set function to be the effective potential makes one ambient gradient supply the surface normal and metric, the Coriolis projection f = 2Ω·n̂, and the gravity-darkened forcing, because ‖∇φ‖|S = geff. We verify the surface operators to second order in space by manufactured solutions across a one-parameter figure family (sphere, two oblate spheroids, and a triaxial tidal “egg”), with fitted orders 1.998, 2.014, 2.005 and 2.001 (the time stepping is first order); the figure limits hold bit-exactly as the mass ratio vanishes and every control null collapses to machine zero. We then introduce a checkerboard-mode classifier, read as a refinement-trend dichotomy: a von Neumann analysis shows the classifier scales as O(h²) on a resolved field but O(1) on a grid-scale null mode — invisible to the scale-blind dissipation that damps resolved modes — so its decrease under refinement diagnoses a run as grid-resolved. Read this way as a sufficient empirical diagnostic, the re-measured admitted envelope reaches a qstab of about 0.24 at the coarsest grid and a measured 0.25 at the finer grids. As an exploratory forward model on a disclosed dimensionless layer-thickness proxy, the verified method then produces a companion-locked, even-dominated disk-integrated brightness-proxy lightcurve on the tidal egg that no axisymmetric figure can produce, scaling sharply toward Roche-lobe fill (odd-harmonic dominance appears only in a classifier-marginal high-fill regime). The operators and verification are rigorous and self-contained; the application is a forward model, never a measured figure, and the proxy is not a brightness.

Contributions

  1. The one-gradient identity. Taking the level-set function to be the body's effective potential makes a single ambient gradient supply the surface metric, the Coriolis projection, and the gravity-darkening forcing simultaneously, unifying geometry and forcing in one field (Figure 2).
  2. A cut-point discretization of the shallow-water operators. Beale's cut-point surface-gradient and surface-divergence operators, specialized to the forced–damped shallow-water system on the embedded figure — second order in space — with semi-Lagrangian departure-point transport, a conservative mass fixer, and the explicit stability treatment, all built from cut-point data on the Cartesian grid (Figure 3).
  3. Manufactured-solution verification across the figure family. Second-order convergence by the method of manufactured solutions on the sphere, two oblate figures, and the tidal egg alike (fitted orders 1.998, 2.014, 2.005 and 2.001), with the construction reducing bit-exactly to the sphere as the distortion vanishes.
  4. A checkerboard-mode stability diagnostic. A spectral classifier that distinguishes the grid-scale checkerboard artifact of the explicit core — whose amplitude decreases under refinement — from genuine physical instability, used as a sufficient empirical diagnostic that runs are grid-resolved out to a critical oblateness well beyond what a simple test of whether the simulation merely remains finite can establish (Figure 4).
  5. A demonstration with a figure-only signature. On the tidal egg, a companion-locked, even-dominated disk-integrated brightness-proxy lightcurve that arises from the broken axisymmetry of the figure alone, with no weather required, and that no axisymmetric figure can produce within the disclosed figure-only forward model. The discrimination is geometric only within that matched control: on a real body a spot or cloud pattern on an axisymmetric figure can mimic the same harmonic structure, a degeneracy the paper makes explicit.

The first four contributions are rigorous numerical analysis; the fifth opens the exploratory application that occupies the later sections. The paper separates the two explicitly in the scope note below, and so does this page.

The numerics of Sections 2–5 are rigorous: every operator is defined and every order and stability claim is backed by a derivation or a manufactured-solution measurement. The application of Sections 6–7 is, by contrast, an exploratory forward model. Throughout it, the dynamical field Φ, distinct from the figure's effective potential, is a dimensionless layer-thickness / figure proxy, never a brightness, and any quantity we call a “contrast”, a “rotational signal”, or a brightness map is built from Φ through a fixed, disclosed map B = exp[κ(Φ − Φ̄)] with κ = 2 held constant. We quote no absolute photometric amplitudes — no per cent of a real star, no ppm, no magnitudes — and we never invert a lightcurve to claim that a figure was measured. The shallow-water model is 1.5-layer (or, where stated, two-surface), not three-dimensional, and not magnetohydrodynamic.

2 · The model problem

Throughout this article the word “figure” is reserved for the lower-case effective-potential field φ and its level set: “the figure” is the surface S = {φ = 0}, never the prognostic field. The upper-case Φ is the dimensionless dynamical layer-thickness / geopotential proxy that evolves on S; it is a nondimensional code-unit geopotential (the layer thickness H scaled to its reference value, Φ = H/H₀, so that Φ and ∇sΦ are dimensionless), and it is not a brightness or a temperature. Every “contrast”, rotational fractional standard deviation, and gravity-darkened map reported later is a dimensionless single-layer dynamical quantity. Where a photometric quantity is needed it is obtained from Φ through the fixed, disclosed brightness proxy of the preprint; it is never an absolute photometric amplitude (no per cent, parts-per-million (ppm), or magnitude). The model is single-layer (“1.5-layer”), not three-dimensional and not magnetohydrodynamic. The distinction between the figure potential φ, which defines the surface, and the prognostic proxy Φ, which evolves on it, is maintained without exception.

Two figures carry everything below. The Roche oblate is the point-mass effective potential of a rotator with the polar radius scaled to exactly 1,

φob(x) = 1 − 1/r − ½ q ρ²,    ∇φob = x/r³ − q (x, y, 0)ᵀ,

with ρ² = x² + y² and q = Ω²R³pole/GM the centrifugal-to-gravity ratio at the pole. The tidal figure adds a companion of mass ratio μ at separation D, co-rotating at Ω² = (1+μ)/D³ about the centre of mass xcm = μD/(1+μ):

φtd(x) = C − 1/r − μ/rc − ½ Ω² [(x − xcm)² + y²].

Because φ is the effective potential rather than a signed distance, its gradient magnitude on the surface is the local effective gravity — ‖∇φ‖|S = geff/Ψ₀, and with lengths scaled by the polar radius and the potential by Ψ₀ = GM/Rpole the two are numerically equal. One field therefore supplies the normal, the metric, the Coriolis projection, and the gravity-darkened forcing target Φeq ∝ (‖∇φ‖/gpole)β. That is the identity the next figure is built to make obvious: switch the colouring to the forcing view and drag 0.1200 down to zero, and the forcing goes flat — not because it was turned off, but because a sphere has nothing for it to vary over.

3 · The cut-point discretization

The surface is never meshed. A background Cartesian grid is laid down once; the nodes are labelled by the sign of φ; every grid edge whose two ends disagree carries exactly one cut point, found by a safeguarded Newton iteration that falls back to bisection whenever a step would leave its bracket. Cuts whose surface normal lies too far from their own edge are inadmissible and thrown away — the parameter is η = 0.45 — because a grazing cut has no well-conditioned difference to offer. What survives is bucketed to the nearest grid node: the closest cut in each bucket is the primary, and the rest become secondaries that interpolate from it with three quadratic weights summing to one.

The figure below runs that construction rather than depicting it. The one thing to watch for is what the grid does when you move q: nothing. The cut points slide along fixed edges. That is the whole content of “no remeshing”, and it is not something a still can show.

5 · The checkerboard-mode classifier

“Did it blow up?” is the wrong question, and it mislabels the failure. The explicit core can support a grid-scale checkerboard mode that is invisible to a dissipation term acting equally on every wavenumber, and a run carrying one may stay perfectly finite. The classifier is the residual of a field against its own four-neighbour average,

(𝒜Φ)p = mean over the neighbours of p,   ℛ = I − 𝒜,   𝒞 = ‖ℛ Φ‖rms,

and its content is a trend, not a threshold. A von Neumann analysis gives ℛ̂ = ¼(kx² + ky²)h² + O((kh)⁴) = O(h²) on a resolved mode but ℛ̂(π/h, π/h) = 1 − (−1) = 2 = O(1) on the checkerboard, which is its maximal eigenvector. So at any single grid a clean field and a contaminated one are two numbers that look alike; only refinement separates them, and that is what the figure below is for.
