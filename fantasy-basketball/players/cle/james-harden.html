Contract gate

Harden declined a $42.3 million player option June 29 and was reported to be working with Cleveland on a multi-year contract. Reports described his return as expected, but no official deal appeared in the NBA transaction tracker by the cutoff. Fantasy profile

  • 9-cat: elite AST, FT% impact, and 3PM; strong PTS. High-volume FG% and TO are categorical drains. Draft range: pick 28-48 only after contract confirmation.
  • Points leagues: top-25-to-45 range, especially friendly in formats that reward double-doubles or do not punish turnovers heavily.
  • Dynasty: Hashtag Basketball ranked him 62nd in dynasty and 67th in keeper. RotoWire's June 29 contender-oriented board—built for a manager trying to win at least once over the next three years—ranked him 23rd. The 39-place disagreement is horizon/team-context evidence, not an error to average away: Harden is far more valuable to a contender than a rebuilder. Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: outside the top 60 per game if he lands near 28 minutes, the shooting declines, or LeBron creates a three-way usage squeeze.
  • Base: top 30-45 per game through assists, FT volume, and threes despite FG%/turnovers.
  • Ceiling: top 15-25 if Cleveland signs no additional primary creator and Harden again approaches 34 minutes and eight-plus assists.

Health and role uncertainty

Harden sustained a non-displaced right-thumb fracture February 25, missed only two games, and played through it. There was no public current limitation by July. NBA.com injury report

His role has more variance than his health: a LeBron signing could lower usage and assists, while a Mitchell absence would restore near-primary-engine volume. Dennis Schröder and Craig Porter Jr are the direct assist beneficiaries if Harden does not return.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Arizona State, two seasons (2007-09). He averaged 17.8 points, 5.3 rebounds and 3.2 assists as a freshman, then 20.1 points, 5.6 rebounds and 4.2 assists as a sophomore. Pac-10 Player of the Year and consensus All-American in 2009, first-team All-Pac-10 in both years, McDonald's All-American out of Artesia High in 2007. The 2025-26 split: 44 games with the Clippers at 35.4 minutes for 25.4 points, 4.8 rebounds, 8.1 assists and 1.3 steals on .419 shooting, then 26 games with Cleveland at 20.5 points and 7.7 assists after the February 4-5, 2026 trade that sent him east for Darius Garland and a second-round pick. The Cleveland stint traded volume for efficiency, which is the expected shape of a third option beside Mitchell and Mobley. The 2020-21 and 2021-22 rows are also multi-team seasons (Houston to Brooklyn in January 2021; Brooklyn to Philadelphia in February 2022); per-team game counts for those two are not verified here.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not verifiable from a rater source here and are not estimated. The arc is nonetheless one of the clearest in fantasy history. From roughly 2014-15 through 2019-20 Harden was a perennial top-three overall category player and often the No. 1 pick, because he supplied points, threes, assists, steals and enormous free-throw volume at a high percentage — five categories at elite scale — while costing only field-goal percentage and turnovers. The 2016-17 and 2017-18 seasons at eleven and nine assists reshaped the position entirely. Since 2020-21 the scoring has fallen and the assists have held, converting him from an overall-No.-1 profile into a specialist: assists, FT%, threes and points, with steals decaying and turnovers still high. Points leagues have consistently valued him above category leagues because turnovers are cheaper there and his assist and rebound volume compounds; the 2025-26 shape at .434 and 3.5 turnovers is a good top-40 points asset and a shakier category one.

Durability: GP by season: 76, 82, 62, 78, 73, 81, 82, 81, 72, 78, 68, 44, 65, 58, 72, 79, 70. Two contextual notes: 2011-12 was a 66-game lockout season and 2019-20 was a 72-game season for Houston, so both understate availability. The genuine losses cluster in 2020-21 through 2022-23. The 44-game 2020-21 was a right hamstring strain with Brooklyn that cost roughly twenty games and recurred in the playoffs; 2021-22 involved further left hamstring trouble; the 2022-23 absence is generally attributed to a right foot tendon strain but is not verified against a primary source here. He has since been notably available for his age: 72, 79 and 70 games in the last three years. In 2025-26 he sustained a non-displaced right thumb fracture on February 25, 2026, missed only two games and played through it; the causes of the remaining absences are not verified here.

Trajectory: Harden's career has two distinct engines. The first was scoring volume, which peaked at 36.1 points in 2018-19 and has been in monotonic decline since — 34.3, 24.6, 22.0, 21.0, 16.6, then a partial rebound to 22.8 and 23.6 as his role widened again in Los Angeles. The second is playmaking, and that has proved almost immune to aging: 10.8, 10.3, 10.7, 8.5, 8.7, 8.0 assists across his thirties, because it depends on decision-making and gravity rather than burst. The efficiency story is the one that matters for 2026-27: field-goal percentage has sat at or below .440 in five of the last six seasons, and the .434 mark in 2025-26 was propped up partly by the lower-usage Cleveland stint. He is 36 turning 37 and the physical decline is visible in the steal rate, which has slid from 2.0 to 1.1 in seven years, and in the block rate, which halved this season. Assume assists and free-throw percentage hold, assume points and minutes step down another notch behind Mitchell and Mobley, and treat any age-37 games-played projection above 65 as optimistic.
