Nine-category: pick 2-4 overall; no punt required. ESPN-default points: pick 2-5; the efficiency terms, assists and defensive events reinforce the real scoring. Dynasty: top-five cornerstone despite being older than Wembanyama, Flagg and Cunningham; production certainty outweighs a slightly shorter horizon.

Why the line looks like this

Gilgeous-Alexander averaged 31.1 points, 4.3 rebounds, 6.6 assists, 1.4 steals, 0.8 blocks and only 2.2 turnovers in 33.2 minutes over 68 games in 2025-26 while shooting 55.3% from the field and 87.9% from the line. He won a second straight MVP and drove a 64-win team. The base is intentionally boring: almost every rate remains near that elite observed season.

The games projection trims an 82-game ceiling because an abdominal strain cost him eight February games and right-oblique management appeared in April. There was no unresolved official injury designation at the July 18 cutoff, so the model does not invent a lingering diagnosis. Oklahoma City's blowout strength also keeps minutes near 33 even though close-game workloads can reach the high 30s.

Public markets agree on the tier. Yahoo/NBA ranked him third on April 14, ESPN's H2H points list ranked him third, Dynatyze displayed him second on July 18, Hashtag ranked him second in dynasty on July 2, and an NBC eight-category industry mock selected him fourth. Sources: 2025-26 team stats, Yahoo/NBA early first round, ESPN points rankings, Dynatyze July 18, Hashtag dynasty.

Category shape

  • Elite: points, FG impact, FT impact and steals.
  • Strong: assists, guard blocks and turnovers relative to usage.
  • Merely average: rebounds and threes; the midrange-heavy shot diet prevents an elite 3PM total.
  • Build fit: universal anchor; his two-percentage foundation makes later specialists easier to absorb.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: recurring core-muscle management limits him to roughly 60 games and 30-6-4 with slightly fewer defensive events; still first-round per game.
  • Base: 70 games at 31-4.5-6.7 with dual-percentage strength and 2.1 stocks.
  • Ceiling: 76-plus games, a small 3PM rise and 1.6-plus steals produce a credible No. 1 overall total-value season.

Career record

Pre-NBA: One season at Kentucky (2017-18): 14.4 points, 4.1 rebounds, 5.1 assists and 1.6 steals in 37 games with 24 starts, on 48.5% from the field, 40.4% from three and 81.7% from the line. He started the year off the bench and finished it as SEC Tournament MVP, plus second-team All-SEC and SEC All-Freshman. Draft: 2018, first round, 11th overall by Charlotte, traded to the Los Angeles Clippers on draft night. Oklahoma City acquired him on July 10, 2019, alongside Danilo Gallinari, five first-round picks and two pick swaps, in the Paul George trade.

Awards: MVP in 2024-25 and 2025-26; 2025 Finals MVP and champion; 2024-25 scoring champion at 32.7; four straight All-Star and All-NBA First Team selections, 2023 through 2026; 2026 Clutch Player of the Year; All-Rookie Second Team in 2019. In 2025-26 he finished second in scoring behind Luka Dončić and won 83 first-place MVP votes on a 64-win team.

Fantasy history: 2024-25: top-three in 8-cat per-game value at season's end, behind Jokić and alongside Wembanyama. 2025-26: second or third in both 9-cat and High Score per game, with Jokić comfortably ahead of him and Wembanyama in both formats; the exact ordering between him and Wembanyama is not verifiable from a primary ranker. Earlier seasons are not verifiable to a single source and no numbers are given for them. The points-league and category shapes are close for him, which is unusual for a 31-point scorer: in points formats the raw scoring and low turnovers carry it, and in 9-cat the same profile plus two elite percentage categories does. He is neutral-to-negative only in rebounds and threes.

Durability: 82, 70, 35, 56, 68, 75, 76, 68 games. The 2019-20 total reflects a pandemic-truncated season. The two bad years are old: 2020-21 ended with a significant tear of the right plantar fascia, and 2021-22 ended with a sore right ankle after a March sprain against Boston, which cost the last ten-plus games. Since then he has been reliable — 68, 75, 76, 68. The 2025-26 absence was a single cluster: an abdominal strain suffered against Orlando on February 3, 2026 that cost nine straight games and the All-Star Game, with a return on February 27. He was the league's No. 2 scorer at 31.8 per game and riding a 121-game streak of 20-plus points, second-longest in league history, when it happened. The remaining five missed games are not individually verified.

Trajectory: The scoring plateau arrived in 2022-23 and has held for four years, so the interesting movement is elsewhere. Efficiency is still climbing — .553 from the field in 2025-26 was a career high and the third straight year above .510 — and his per-36 assist rate rose again, from about 6.7 in 2024-25 to about 7.2, while per-36 scoring ticked down slightly. That is a player converting volume into quality at the exact age the curve predicts, and it is why the 1.6-point drop in per-game scoring should not be read as decline. The genuine warning sign is peripheral erosion: steals have fallen 2.0 to 1.7 to 1.4 across three seasons and rebounds 5.5 to 5.0 to 4.3 over the same span, so an increasing share of his 9-cat value now rests on points and the two percentages rather than defensive events. Entering an age-28 season on a team built to win blowouts, the base case is another 31-point, 33-minute year with elite efficiency; the fantasy risk is not a rate collapse but a further slide in steals and boards that quietly moves him from a No. 2 category anchor to a No. 4.
