Milos Uzan

Milos Uzan is an Exhibit 10 point-guard candidate with a clean two-way development profile, but he needs to win a contract before his passing and point-of-attack defense can matter in fantasy.

Uzan has zero simultaneous minutes in Boston's 240-minute healthy-roster model. The ten-game line applies only if he wins a two-way and is activated; those minutes replace unavailable rotation players.

Nine-category: no redraft value; assists/steals are the deep-league watch categories. Points: needs a dramatic role change. Dynasty: watch-list or taxi candidate in very deep formats because Boston lacks a young pure point guard behind Pritchard and White.

Why the line looks like this

Uzan averaged 11.1 points and about four assists in his final Houston season, improving as a shooter, defender and organizer. Boston gave him substantial Summer League ballhandling work; against Sacramento he produced 13 points, seven rebounds and two assists. The July 14 Exhibit 10 signing creates a camp pathway but no guaranteed NBA or two-way roster spot.

His competition is partly structural. Mike Conley supplies veteran emergency organization, while Derrick White and Payton Pritchard own the real guard minutes. Uzan's most plausible year-one development comes as Maine's lead guard, where ball security, point-of-attack defense and pull-up shooting can be tested at volume.

Sources: CelticsBlog rookie background on Uzan and DeVries, NBA Summer League Celtics–Kings recap, Yahoo report on both Exhibit 10 signings.

Category shape

  • Possible strengths: assists, low turnovers and steals if he handles point-of-attack assignments.
  • Swing skill: NBA-range shooting off the dribble and off the catch.
  • Costs: small scoring/rebounding ceiling in a low-minute reserve role.
  • Build fit: only deep dynasty until a two-way or standard deal exists.

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: waived after camp and either joins Maine as an affiliate player or signs elsewhere.
  • Base conditional: wins a two-way, appears in ten NBA games at four minutes and runs a larger G League role.
  • Ceiling: beats Conley for emergency point-guard work and reaches 12-15 minutes during injuries, offering 3-4 assists and a steal in deep formats.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons — Oklahoma 2022-24 (7.6 PPG/3.0 APG as a freshman, then 9.0/4.4 on weaker efficiency), Houston 2024-26. Junior year at Houston: 11.4 PTS, 3.1 REB, 4.3 AST, .453 FG/.428 3P/.783 FT. Senior year 2025-26: 37 GP, 33.0 MPG, 11.1 PTS, 2.7 REB, 4.0 AST, 1.0 STL, .380 FG/.343 3P/.741 FT, 1.3 TO (3.08 AST/TO). College career: 141 GP, 9.9 PTS, 3.9 AST, .364 3P. Awards: Big 12 All-Freshman (2023), Second-team All-Big 12 (2025). Draft: undrafted (2026).

No NBA record. Most recent season: 2025-26 at Houston (37 GP, all starts).

Durability: Durable in college — 141 games across four seasons, started all 77 of Houston's games over his two years there.

Trajectory: The record shows a low-usage organizer whose assist-to-turnover control is elite (1.3 TO on 4.0 AST as a senior) but whose scoring efficiency swung season to season — the .428 3P junior year looks like the outlier, not the .343 senior year. In 2026-27 he competes for one of Boston's two open two-way slots and Maine's lead-guard job, not NBA minutes. He becomes fantasy-relevant only if he wins a two-way conversion AND injuries clear both Pritchard and Conley from emergency point-guard duty — a deep-dynasty watch, nothing more.
