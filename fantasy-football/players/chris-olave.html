The role

He is the alpha X: the receiver a team throws to regardless of matchup, lined up wide and drawing the opponent's best defender. Targets — throws in his direction, counted whether or not he catches them (Fantasy football glossary) — are the most stable input in fantasy football, more stable than efficiency, which makes alphas the safest picks on the board that are not running backs. New Orleans has no other receiver in the file projected within thirty catches of him.

The number

94 catches projected (note column, Data/players.csv: alpha X; 94 catches for 1110 yards projected). In full PPR that is 94 points before a single yard is counted, roughly five and a half a game from receptions alone. It is a projection derived from FFToday stat lines dated 2026-07-23, not an observed total.

The risk

The CSV flags him low risk. What is left is a market/projection disagreement rather than a role problem: his ADP of 22.1 — Fantasy Football Calculator PPR 12-team, a labelled proxy for ESPN's own ADP, not ESPN's number — puts him ahead of two receivers the board projects behind him, so you may be paying the market's confidence rather than the model's. There is a second thing the label does not price: the projection assumes seventeen games and he has not played seventeen in any of the last three years, missing nine of them in 2024 alone — his modelled availability of 0.80 is the lowest of these six and below the receiver average, both of which are model outputs, not counts (Availability and injury risk).
