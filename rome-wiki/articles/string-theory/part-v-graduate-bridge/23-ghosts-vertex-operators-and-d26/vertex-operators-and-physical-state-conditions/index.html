    Vertex operators and physical-state conditions

    A vertex operator is the integrated local operator \int d^2z\,\mathcal{O} that stands in for an incoming or outgoing string; Weyl invariance forces it to have conformal weight (1,1) , and that condition is the mass-shell condition — it fixes the particle's mass.

    A vertex operator is an external leg

    In a scattering process, strings come in from infinity, interact, and leave. In field theory each external particle is an external leg of a Feynman diagram, labeled by a momentum ppp and a polarization. A vertex operator is the string version of an external leg: a local operator inserted on the worldsheet to say that a string of momentum ppp was created (or absorbed) at that point.

    The whole note turns on one demand: a physical amplitude must not depend on how we drew the coordinate grid on the worldsheet. Rescaling that grid is a Weyl transformation, and a physical amplitude cannot care about it. An inserted operator scales under a rescaling by a power set by its conformal weight (h,hˉ)(h,\bar h)(h,hˉ); the integration measure d2zd^2zd2z scales the opposite way. The two cancel — the insertion is Weyl-invariant — only if the operator has exactly weight (1,1)(1,1)(1,1). Computing when a momentum-ppp operator has weight (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) pins down p2p^2p2, hence the mass. Mass-shell falls out of pure worldsheet gauge invariance.

    Why an integrated operator, and why weight (1,1)(1,1)(1,1)

    By the State-operator correspondence, every physical string state ∣Ψ⟩|\Psi\rangle∣Ψ⟩ is a local primary operator O(z,zˉ)\mathcal{O}(z,\bar z)O(z,zˉ) on the worldsheet. But a bare operator sitting at a fixed point z0z_0z0​ is not gauge-invariant, because the worldsheet has a dynamical metric with two gauge symmetries we must respect:

    1. Reparameterization (diffeomorphism) invariance — no point on the worldsheet is special, so integrate over all of them: V∼∫d2z  O(z,zˉ).V\sim\int d^2z\;\mathcal{O}(z,\bar z).V∼∫d2zO(z,zˉ).
    2. Weyl invariance — under a rescaling z→λzz\to\lambda zz→λz the measure d2zd^2zd2z carries weight (−1,−1)(-1,-1)(−1,−1), so for VVV to be invariant the operator must carry the compensating weight \boxed{(h,\bar h)=(+1,+1)}.\tag{1}

    An integrated, weight-(1,1)(1,1)(1,1), gauge-invariant operator is a vertex operator. Because Weyl invariance is what forces (1), and (1) will fix the mass, we say the vertex operator is automatically on-shell — exactly like an external leg, which also carries an on-shell momentum.

    This is the covariant way to fix the normal-ordering constant. Old-covariant quantization imposes (L0−1)∣Ψ⟩=0(L_0-1)|\Psi\rangle=0(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0 on physical states; via the state-operator map the L0L_0L0​ eigenvalue equals hhh, so (L0−1)∣Ψ⟩=0(L_0-1)|\Psi\rangle=0(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0 is the same statement as h=1h=1h=1. The unit intercept — written as a shift in M2M^2M2 (open string α′M2=N⊥−1\alpha'M^2=N^\perp-1α′M2=N⊥−1, closed string α′4M2=N⊥−1\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2=N^\perp-14α′​M2=N⊥−1 per chiral sector) — reappears here as the required weight +1+1+1: the same number, moved to the other side of the equation.

    The core computation: weight of :eip⋅X::e^{ip\cdot X}::eip⋅X:

    A string of momentum pμp^\mupμ is created by the exponential : ⁣eip⋅X ⁣::\!e^{ip\cdot X}\!::eip⋅X: — in operator language it maps the zero-momentum vacuum to the momentum eigenstate ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩. Everything reduces to computing the weight of this exponential.

    The one input we need is the free-scalar propagator (mostly-plus target signature): X^\mu(z,\bar z)\,X^\nu(w,\bar w)\sim -\frac{\alpha'}{2}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}\ln|z-w|^2,\tag{2} and its holomorphic derivative ∂Xμ(z) Xν(w,wˉ)∼−α′2 ημν/(z−w)\partial X^\mu(z)\,X^\nu(w,\bar w)\sim -\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}/(z-w)∂Xμ(z)Xν(w,wˉ)∼−2α′​ημν/(z−w). Contracting ∂X\partial X∂X against every XXX in the exponential (each contraction pulls down a factor ipipip) gives the single most useful OPE: \partial X^\mu(z)\,:\!e^{ip\cdot X(w,\bar w)}\!:\;\sim\;-\frac{i\alpha'}{2}\,\frac{p^\mu}{z-w}\,:\!e^{ip\cdot X}\!:.\tag{3}

    Closed string (insertion in the bulk). The weight is read off the double pole in T(z): ⁣eip⋅X ⁣:T(z):\!e^{ip\cdot X}\!:T(z):eip⋅X:, with the stress tensor T(z)=−1α′: ⁣∂X⋅∂X ⁣:T(z)=-\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}:\!\partial X\cdot\partial X\!:T(z)=−α′1​:∂X⋅∂X:. Contract both ∂X\partial X∂X's in TTT into the exponential — a double contraction, each giving a factor from (3): −1α′(−iα′2 pμ) ⁣(−iα′2 pμ)=−1α′⋅(−α′24 p2)=α′p24.-\frac{1}{\alpha'}\left(-\frac{i\alpha'}{2}\,p^\mu\right)\!\left(-\frac{i\alpha'}{2}\,p_\mu\right)=-\frac{1}{\alpha'}\cdot\left(-\frac{\alpha'^2}{4}\,p^2\right)=\frac{\alpha' p^2}{4}.−α′1​(−2iα′​pμ)(−2iα′​pμ​)=−α′1​⋅(−4α′2​p2)=4α′p2​. The coefficient of the double pole 1/(z−w)21/(z-w)^21/(z−w)2 is the weight, so \boxed{\,h=\bar h=\frac{\alpha' p^2}{4}\,}.\tag{4} Notice the two minus signs — one from TTT's normalization, one from i2=−1i^2=-1i2=−1 — cancel, so a spacelike momentum (p2>0p^2>0p2>0) gives a positive weight; the antiholomorphic copy is identical, giving hˉ\bar hhˉ.

    Now impose Weyl invariance (1), h=hˉ=1h=\bar h=1h=hˉ=1: α′p24=1  ⟹  p2=4α′  ⟹  M2≡−p2=−4α′.\frac{\alpha'p^2}{4}=1\;\Longrightarrow\;p^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}\;\Longrightarrow\;M^2\equiv-p^2=-\frac{4}{\alpha'}.4α′p2​=1⟹p2=α′4​⟹M2≡−p2=−α′4​. The mass came out negative: this is the closed-string tachyon, the ground state, whose vertex operator is Vtach∼∫d2z : ⁣eip⋅X ⁣:V_{\text{tach}}\sim\int d^2z\,:\!e^{ip\cdot X}\!:Vtach​∼∫d2z:eip⋅X:. In mostly-plus, M2=−p2M^2=-p^2M2=−p2, so a spacelike ppp is exactly a tachyon — the ground state sits below the massless shell, and the operator formalism reproduces the same M2=−4/α′M^2=-4/\alpha'M2=−4/α′ found by light-cone quantization.

    Open string (insertion on the boundary). On a worldsheet with a boundary, a scalar field's propagator acquires an image charge across the boundary — the field at www also feels its mirror image at wˉ\bar wwˉ. So the derivative OPE (3) doubles up: the boundary version of ∂X: ⁣eipX ⁣:\partial X:\!e^{ipX}\!:∂X:eipX: has poles at both www and wˉ\bar wwˉ, −iα′p2(1z−w+1z−wˉ) ⁣: ⁣eipX ⁣:.-\frac{i\alpha'p}{2}\left(\frac{1}{z-w}+\frac{1}{z-\bar w}\right)\!:\!e^{ipX}\!:.−2iα′p​(z−w1​+z−wˉ1​):eipX:. Feed this into TTT and then set the operator on the boundary, w=wˉw=\bar ww=wˉ, so the two poles merge into one of twice the residue. The coefficient of 1/(z−w)21/(z-w)^21/(z−w)2 picks up a factor (1+1)2=4(1+1)^2=4(1+1)2=4 relative to before: T(z)\,:\!e^{ipX(w,\bar w)}\!:\;=\;\frac{\alpha'p^2\,:\!e^{ipX}\!:}{(z-w)^2}+\dots\;\Longrightarrow\;\boxed{\,h=\alpha'p^2\,}.\tag{5} A boundary operator has a single weight hhh, not a pair — there is no independent antiholomorphic sector on the boundary. Weyl invariance needs h=+1h=+1h=+1 (the line measure dsdsds has weight −1-1−1): α′p2=1  ⟹  M2=−p2=−1α′,\alpha'p^2=1\;\Longrightarrow\;M^2=-p^2=-\frac{1}{\alpha'},α′p2=1⟹M2=−p2=−α′1​, the open-string tachyon mass.

    Factor sanity check

    Closed: h=hˉ=α′p2/4h=\bar h=\alpha'p^2/4h=hˉ=α′p2/4, total scaling dimension h+hˉ=α′p2/2h+\bar h=\alpha'p^2/2h+hˉ=α′p2/2, mass-shell h+hˉ=2h+\bar h=2h+hˉ=2 at M2=−4/α′M^2=-4/\alpha'M2=−4/α′. Open: h=α′p2h=\alpha'p^2h=α′p2, mass-shell h=1h=1h=1 at M2=−1/α′M^2=-1/\alpha'M2=−1/α′. The closed tachyon mass is 4×4\times4× the open one in M2M^2M2 — because the boundary image doubled every contraction, so the same p2p^2p2 counts twice as much. Signs and α′\alpha'α′ powers are consistent.

    The first excited states: mass-shell and transversality

    Dress the exponential with one derivative from each sector: Vexc∼∫d2z  : ⁣eip⋅X ∂Xμ ∂ˉXν ⁣: ζμν,V_{\text{exc}}\sim\int d^2z\;:\!e^{ip\cdot X}\,\partial X^\mu\,\bar\partial X^\nu\!:\,\zeta_{\mu\nu},Vexc​∼∫d2z:eip⋅X∂Xμ∂ˉXν:ζμν​, where ∂Xμ\partial X^\mu∂Xμ supplies an α−1μ\alpha^\mu_{-1}α−1μ​ excitation, ∂ˉXν\bar\partial X^\nu∂ˉXν a αˉ−1ν\bar\alpha^\nu_{-1}αˉ−1ν​, and ζμν\zeta_{\mu\nu}ζμν​ is the polarization. Two conditions now drop out, because both physical constraints on a massless tensor particle appear as OPE conditions:

    • Mass-shell, from weight (1,1)(1,1)(1,1). Each of ∂X,∂ˉX\partial X,\bar\partial X∂X,∂ˉX adds weight (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), so the total weight is h=hˉ=1+α′p2/4h=\bar h=1+\alpha'p^2/4h=hˉ=1+α′p2/4. Setting this to 111 forces p2=0,p^2=0,p2=0, so the first excited states are massless — the graviton, the BBB-field, and the dilaton, matching the light-cone spectrum.
    • Transversality, from primarity. Being weight-(1,1)(1,1)(1,1) is not enough; the operator must also be primary (no worse-than-double pole with TTT). Sitting ∂X\partial X∂X and eip⋅Xe^{ip\cdot X}eip⋅X inside the same normal-ordering produces an extra double-contraction term with a 1/(z−w)31/(z-w)^31/(z−w)3 pole, proportional to pμζμνp^\mu\zeta_{\mu\nu}pμζμν​; the Tˉ\bar TTˉ contraction gives pνζμνp^\nu\zeta_{\mu\nu}pνζμν​. Killing both requires pμζμν=pνζμν=0.p^\mu\zeta_{\mu\nu}=p^\nu\zeta_{\mu\nu}=0.pμζμν​=pνζμν​=0. This is exactly the transverse polarization condition for a massless particle: the covariant way of throwing out the unphysical longitudinal polarizations. (In light-cone gauge you would have solved this by hand; here it is automatic.)

    So the two properties of a vertex operator — weight (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) and primary — are precisely mass-shell and transversality. That is the sense in which the operator is "on-shell": it can only exist for physical, correctly polarized momenta.

    The general lesson: spectrum = internal CFT, shifted by one

    Nothing above used flatness of all directions. Put the string on R1,3×(internal c=22 CFT)\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\times(\text{internal }c=22\text{ CFT})R1,3×(internal c=22 CFT) and dress an internal primary of weight (h,hˉ)(h,\bar h)(h,hˉ) with eip⋅Xe^{ip\cdot X}eip⋅X over the four flat directions. Weight-(1,1)(1,1)(1,1) demands α′p24+h=1,α′p24+hˉ=1.\frac{\alpha'p^2}{4}+h=1,\qquad \frac{\alpha'p^2}{4}+\bar h=1.4α′p2​+h=1,4α′p2​+hˉ=1. The two equations must agree, which forces h=hˉh=\bar hh=hˉ — this is the closed-string level-matching condition wearing CFT clothing. Solving, M2=4α′ (h−1).M^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}\,(h-1).M2=α′4​(h−1). Read off the whole spectrum by the weight of the internal operator:

    internal operator weight resulting particle
    relevant h<1h<1h<1 tachyon (M2<0M^2<0M2<0)
    marginal h=1h=1h=1 massless
    irrelevant h>1h>1h>1 massive

    The spectrum of the string is the spectrum of primary operators of its internal CFT, uniformly shifted down by one. "Which particles exist" becomes "which operators the CFT has" — the cleanest possible restatement of the string spectrum.

    Exercise

    The exponential : ⁣eip⋅X ⁣::\!e^{ip\cdot X}\!::eip⋅X: has closed-string weight h=hˉ=α′p2/4h=\bar h=\alpha'p^2/4h=hˉ=α′p2/4. Vertex operators for spacetime fermions in the superstring instead involve : ⁣eip⋅X ∂Xμ ∂ˉXν ∂Xρ ⁣::\!e^{ip\cdot X}\,\partial X^\mu\,\bar\partial X^\nu\,\partial X^\rho\!::eip⋅X∂Xμ∂ˉXν∂Xρ:-type dressings, but stay with the bosonic string and take the second closed excited level, dressed with two holomorphic derivatives on one side: V∼∫d2z  : ⁣eip⋅X ∂Xμ∂Xν ∂ˉXρ∂ˉXσ ⁣:.V\sim\int d^2z\;:\!e^{ip\cdot X}\,\partial X^\mu\partial X^\nu\,\bar\partial X^\rho\bar\partial X^\sigma\!:.V∼∫d2z:eip⋅X∂Xμ∂Xν∂ˉXρ∂ˉXσ:. Using only the additivity of weights, find M2M^2M2 from the Weyl condition. Is it a tachyon, massless, or massive? Then say in one sentence why level-matching is automatically satisfied here but would fail for a dressing with two holomorphic and one antiholomorphic derivative.

    Solution

    Weights are additive for a normal-ordered product of mutually-local factors. Each ∂X\partial X∂X carries weight (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and each ∂ˉX\bar\partial X∂ˉX carries (0,1)(0,1)(0,1); the exponential carries (α′p2/4,α′p2/4)(\alpha'p^2/4,\alpha'p^2/4)(α′p2/4,α′p2/4). So the two holomorphic derivatives add 222 to hhh and the two antiholomorphic add 222 to hˉ\bar hhˉ: h=2+α′p24,hˉ=2+α′p24.h=2+\frac{\alpha'p^2}{4},\qquad \bar h=2+\frac{\alpha'p^2}{4}.h=2+4α′p2​,hˉ=2+4α′p2​. Weyl invariance sets h=hˉ=1h=\bar h=1h=hˉ=1: 2+α′p24=1  ⟹  α′p24=−1  ⟹  p2=−4α′  ⟹  M2=−p2=4α′>0.2+\frac{\alpha'p^2}{4}=1\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{\alpha'p^2}{4}=-1\;\Longrightarrow\;p^2=-\frac{4}{\alpha'}\;\Longrightarrow\;M^2=-p^2=\frac{4}{\alpha'}>0.2+4α′p2​=1⟹4α′p2​=−1⟹p2=−α′4​⟹M2=−p2=α′4​>0. A positive M2M^2M2 — this is a massive state at N⊥=2N^\perp=2N⊥=2, so α′M2=4\alpha'M^2=4α′M2=4, one level above the massless graviton (N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1). The general formula M2=4α′(N⊥−1)M^2=\tfrac{4}{\alpha'}(N^\perp-1)M2=α′4​(N⊥−1) with N⊥=2N^\perp=2N⊥=2 agrees.

    Level-matching works here because the dressing is balanced: two holomorphic and two antiholomorphic derivatives give equal shifts to hhh and hˉ\bar hhˉ, so the two Weyl equations h=1h=1h=1 and hˉ=1\bar h=1hˉ=1 are the same equation and are consistent. With two holomorphic but only one antiholomorphic derivative we would need 2+α′p2/4=12+\alpha'p^2/4=12+α′p2/4=1 and 1+α′p2/4=11+\alpha'p^2/4=11+α′p2/4=1 simultaneously — two incompatible values of p2p^2p2. No momentum solves both, so that unbalanced operator is never Weyl-invariant. This is exactly closed-string level-matching (N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥) reappearing as "equal holomorphic and antiholomorphic weight."
