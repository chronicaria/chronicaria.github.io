Jaden McDaniels

Snapshot

  • Role: starting small forward, primary perimeter stopper, and likely 33-minute closer. Chris Finch has publicly resisted moving him to the four full time.
  • Current market: Alex Barutha ranked him 71st on July 14; Hashtag's crowdsourced keeper rank was 50th on July 18.
  • Team context: the departures of Julius Randle and Naz Reid create a real usage opening, but the rebounding opening is now smaller than this file previously assumed.

Correction 2026-07-18 Two changes, both against the previous 70 GP / 34 MPG / 14.2 PTS / 4.8 REB / 2.4 AST card. First, the position. Finch addressed it publicly on approximately 2026-07-15: "I don't immediately envision Jaden just moving over and playing the four. While I can see at times he'll play there and maybe play a good number of minutes there, I don't see that right off the bat and I don't necessarily, in my mind's eye, see that as a starting lineup with him there" (Hoops Rumors). The 4.8-rebound projection was, as the trajectory note below already conceded, an assumption about the Randle and Reid vacancies rather than an extrapolation of his own trend — and the vacancy is no longer his to absorb. It is cut to 4.4, just above his 4.2 baseline, and minutes to 33. Second, the arithmetic ran backwards. The old card raised his minutes by 2.3 while lowering his points and assists against a 14.8/4.2/2.7 line in 31.7 MPG — a roughly 13% per-minute scoring decline in the same season the desk says Randle's and Reid's frontcourt scoring vanished, with no mechanism offered. Per-minute production is now held flat, which is what 15.4 points and 2.8 assists at 33 minutes represents.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: 60-90. Two-plus stocks, efficient FG%, and manageable turnovers are the selling points.
  • Points: 80-120. Defensive value does not fully translate unless the scoring system rewards steals and blocks strongly.
  • Dynasty: 45-75. Prime-age role security and a scarce fantasy archetype make him more valuable than his scoring average.

Role, floor and ceiling

McDaniels should defend the best opposing forward while spacing beside Ball, Edwards, and Gobert. Floor: 13 points, four rebounds, and inconsistent threes, leaving him outside the top 100 when stocks dip. Ceiling: 17 points, six rebounds, 1.5 steals, and 1.2 blocks — but note that the six-rebound branch now requires the power-forward minutes Finch has said he does not plan to hand him at the start, so it is a mid-season contingency rather than an opening-night expectation.

Risk monitor

Minnesota listed left patellar tendinopathy and a bone bruise on 2026-03-30, which accounts for most of his nine missed games; no surgery was required. Finch's stated intent to keep him at small forward reduces the contact load this file previously flagged, which is a mild positive for availability and a mild negative for rebounds. The roster still lacks a settled starting four, so if the two open standard slots go unused he may end up at the position anyway by attrition.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Washington, one season (2019-20). He played 31 games (21 starts) at 31.1 minutes and averaged 13.0 points, 5.8 rebounds, 2.1 assists and 1.4 blocks on .405/.339/.763 shooting in the Pac-12 — a five-star recruit whose freshman efficiency never matched his tools, which is why he slid out of the lottery. Draft: 2020, first round, 28th overall by the Los Angeles Lakers; his rights moved to Oklahoma City alongside Danny Green in the Dennis Schröder trade and then on to Minnesota, so he has never played a game for the team that drafted him.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag are not verifiable from public sources at this writing, so no year-by-year ranking is asserted here. What the raw line supports: 2022-23 and 2024-25 were his first two seasons with a defensible top-100 category profile, and 2025-26 was clearly his best — career highs in points, assists, FG% and FT% with the stocks intact. The one contemporaneous marker found was a positional rank of roughly F-59 during 2025-26, which is consistent with a top-70 to top-90 overall finish but is not a season-end figure. In points leagues he grades materially worse than in categories: the steals and blocks that carry two of nine categories collapse into a small scoring bonus, and a 14.8-point, 4.2-rebound line with 1.8 turnovers has no other lever.

Durability: 63, 70, 79, 72, 82, 73 games. The rookie-year absences were rotation and health-and-safety related rather than a single injury. The costly one was 2022-23: he fractured his right hand punching a wall in the locker room on April 9, 2023, which ended his regular season and cost him the first four games of the playoffs. He then played 72 and a full 82. In 2025-26 he was diagnosed with left patellar tendinopathy and an accompanying bone bruise on March 30, 2026, which accounts for most of the nine games missed; no surgery was required. Across six seasons he has averaged 73 games and has never missed extended time to a soft-tissue lower-body issue, which makes the knee the only live flag.

Trajectory: The career is a straight line of accrued trust rather than one leap — minutes climbed from 24 to roughly 32 and stayed, and each rise in role came with the defense holding up. The genuine improvement seasons are 2022-23, when the shot diet shifted toward corner threes and rim finishes and FG% jumped from .460 to .517, and 2025-26, where the gain was not minutes at all: MPG was flat year over year, but points rose from 12.2 to 14.8 and assists from 2.0 to 2.7, meaning per-minute usage and playmaking actually grew for the first time since 2022. The counter-signal is rebounding, which fell from 5.7 to 4.2 even as he played more power forward, and turnovers, which jumped to a career-high 1.8 as he handled more — the dossier's projection of 4.8 rebounds is therefore an assumption about the Randle and Reid vacancies rather than an extrapolation of his own trend. At 25 turning 26 in September, he is entering the flat top of the aging curve, so the base case is repetition of 2025-26 rather than another step. The honest read for 2026-27: the scoring and efficiency gains look real and repeatable, the rebounding rebound is speculative, and the knee is the difference between 73 games and 80.
