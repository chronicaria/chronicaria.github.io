This note spends that freedom deliberately and counts what survives.</p>
<p>Film a wave traveling down a rope, and you get to choose how the camera sweeps through spacetime — which slices count as "one instant." A clumsy sweep, ordinary time, leaves the rope's motion tangled with constraints you can only stare at. Tilt the notion of "now" 45° onto a light ray instead: in those slices the leftover bookkeeping collapses, and the rope's genuine wiggling — always transverse, never along its own length — is laid bare. That tilt is light-cone gauge.</p>

<li><strong><span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>X</mi><mo>+</mo></msup></mrow><annotation <h2 id="why-it-is-powerful-and-dangerous">Why it is powerful and dangerous</h2>
<p>Powerful: it exhibits the physical modes directly and makes the classical string trivially solvable.
