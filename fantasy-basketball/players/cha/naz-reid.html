Snapshot

  • Role: starting PF and closing/small-ball C; the cleanest path on the roster to 30-33 minutes.
  • 2025-26: 77 GP, 26.1 MPG; .456 FG · .732 FT · 2.1 3PM · 13.6 PTS · 6.2 REB · 2.2 AST · 1.0 STL · 1.0 BLK · 1.6 TO.
  • Context: played through a painful shoulder issue and still set a career high in rebounding.
  • Acquisition: officially arrived July 10 in the LaMelo Ball/Josh Green four-team trade.

Role thesis

The Bridges trade opens the starting four, and Reid can also absorb center minutes when Charlotte wants five-out spacing. That dual pathway is why 30-32 minutes is sturdier than betting on any single Hornets center. Reid's 2025-26 one-steal/one-block line is especially valuable if it survives added minutes.

The caution is price. Alex Barutha's July 14 gut-check list ranked him No. 86; Josh Lloyd moved from a conservative 30-minute/top-50 case on June 25 to roughly 33 minutes and “almost certainly top 50” on July 12. The projection here splits those views because more volume can bring more scoring and rebounds without fixing mediocre FT% or creating a truly dominant category.

Range

  • Ceiling: 33-34 MPG, 19 PTS, 8 REB, 2.8 threes and two stocks—top 35-50 value.
  • Median: the balanced mid-round line above.
  • Floor: Charlotte closes with Diabaté/Steinbach more often, Reid stays near 27-28 minutes, or the shoulder recurs; 14/6 with an outside-top-110 finish.

Fantasy history: ESPN's in-season roto/category rankings had Reid around No. 66 during 2024-25, with the points-league version materially worse at roughly No. 83 — the standard gap for a player whose value leans on threes, stocks and percentages rather than raw scoring volume. Season-end nine-category finishes from Basketball Monster or Hashtag are not verifiable from the sources available here for any of his seven seasons, so no year-by-year rank line is given. The shape is stable regardless of the exact number: a mid-round category asset, a later-round points asset.

Durability: 30 / 70 / 77 / 68 / 81 / 80 / 77 games played, 2019-20 through 2025-26. The rookie total reflects two-way status in a 64-game COVID-shortened Minnesota season, not injury. The one genuinely lost stretch is 2022-23: a left scaphoid fracture suffered March 29, 2023 against Phoenix ended his regular season and cost him the playoffs. Otherwise the pattern is many short absences rather than long ones — foot (Nov 2021), back (Mar 2022 and Jan 2023), calf (Mar 2023), neck (Nov 2023), head (Mar 2024), shoulder (Oct 2024), ankle twice plus illness in early 2025, hamstring (May 2025). The 2025-26 shoulder problem the dossier flags is real and recurrent: left shoulder soreness forced him out after five minutes at San Antonio on January 18, 2026, and shoulder entries recur February 22-24, March 16-17 and April 10-17, the last of which cost him the final two regular-season games as maintenance before the playoffs. He still played 77 games through it.

Trajectory: The career shows one clear inflection and one quieter one. The first is 2023-24, when he added the three-point shot as a real weapon — 3PM jumped from 1.1 to 2.1 with minutes up only six per game — and won Sixth Man of the Year. The second is 2024-25, when Karl-Anthony Towns' departure pushed him to 27.5 MPG and his assists nearly doubled to 2.3, evidence of genuine offensive-hub reps rather than more of the same. What 2025-26 shows is that the per-minute production had plateaued: minutes fell 1.4, and PTS, FG% and FT% all slipped while REB, STL and BLK ticked up. That is a player who has found his level rather than one still ascending, which matters because he turns 27 in August 2026 and sits at the top of the aging curve, not below it. For 2026-27 the case is entirely about opportunity, not development — Charlotte can hand him 30-plus minutes, and the honest expectation is his 2024-25 per-minute rates scaled up, not a new skill. The FT% has never cleared 78 percent in a season and the shooting efficiency has now declined three straight years, so a minutes bump raises his counting-stat floor without fixing what caps his ceiling.
