Scouting-to-fantasy translation

Steinbach rebounds by position, timing, and relentless pursuit rather than length. He can roll, pop, short-roll pass, and score on putbacks, which gives him more offensive pathways than a pure rim-running center. The limiting questions are rim protection, lateral defense, and whether the three-pointer is real enough to share the floor with Diabaté or Kalkbrenner.

Charlotte used Steinbach and Kalkbrenner together in Summer League. That creates a plausible route to rotation minutes at both frontcourt spots, but it does not solve the regular-season crowd: Reid, Diabaté, Kalkbrenner, Grant Williams, and small-ball O'Neale are all present.

Range

  • Ceiling: wins a starting PF/C role by midseason; 26-28 MPG, 12-14 PTS, 9-10 REB, two assists and roughly 1.5 stocks—top-100 territory.
  • Median: 17-20 minutes, strong rebounding, and waiver-wire value when one veteran sits.
  • Floor: 12-15 developmental minutes; defensive mistakes and limited spacing keep him outside redraft relevance.

Career record

Pre-NBA: two stages, one professional and one college. Born May 1, 2006 in Würzburg, Germany, Steinbach came up through the Würzburg Baskets youth system, signed his first professional contract in the summer of 2024 and debuted in the Basketball Bundesliga that September. His 2024-25 BBL regular season was 31 games at 18.6 minutes for 7.2 points and 5.7 rebounds on .610 from the field — modest counting stats in a bench role, but the efficiency and the rebound rate per minute were already the tell. Counting all competitions that season he played 41 games at 9.1 points and 6.9 rebounds, and he raised his level in the BBL playoffs, including an 18-rebound game at Ratiopharm Ulm on June 6, 2025. He then reclassified to college for a single season at Washington in 2025-26: 30 games, 30 starts, 34.6 minutes, 18.5 points, 11.8 rebounds, 1.6 assists, 1.1 steals, 1.2 blocks and 2.0 turnovers on .577 from the field, .340 from three and .759 from the line. That rebounding average led all of NCAA Division I, and his 22 double-doubles tied for the national lead. He was the Big Ten's highest-scoring freshman, led the conference in total, offensive and defensive rebounds per game, ranked third in field-goal percentage, and took third-team All-Big Ten and All-Freshman honors. Internationally he won gold at the 2024 U18 EuroBasket and silver at the 2025 U19 World Cup, making the All-Star Five at both. Draft: 2026, first round, 14th overall, Charlotte. Not traded on draft night; signed his rookie-scale contract on July 6, 2026.

Steinbach has no NBA regular-season record yet — he has not played an NBA game, so there is no season table to report.

Summer League 2026 (Las Vegas): five games — 15 points, 11 rebounds and two blocks against Orlando; 7 and 6 against New Orleans; 8 and 5 against Boston; 27 points and 15 rebounds on 13-of-22 shooting with three assists in 27 minutes against Milwaukee; and 22 points and 11 rebounds on 7-of-10 against Sacramento, including his first two Summer League threes. That is roughly 15.8 points and 9.6 rebounds per game with three double-doubles in five outings. Note that the Kings game is dated July 17, 2026 in the box score, not July 18 as stated earlier in this dossier.

Fantasy history: none. He has never been rostered in an NBA fantasy season, so there is no category or points finish to report and none is estimated here. The first meaningful data point will be his 2026-27 opening-month usage.

Durability: no NBA availability record exists. What can be verified: 31 BBL regular-season appearances in 2024-25 and 41 games across all competitions that season, then 30 games at Washington in 2025-26 while carrying a 34.6-minute load. The reasons for any Washington games missed are not verified here, and no injury has been publicly reported that would affect his 2026-27 availability. He played five Summer League games in July 2026 without restriction.

Trajectory: The arc is short but unusually clean, and the sequencing is what makes it credible. A player who rebounded at a high rate in 18 bench minutes of a real professional league at 18, then did it as the national leader in 34 college minutes at 19, has demonstrated the skill at two levels against two very different kinds of opposition — that is much stronger evidence than a single dominant NCAA season would be. The scoring jump from 7.2 to 18.5 came with a field-goal percentage that stayed near .580, which means the added volume was absorbed at his existing efficiency rather than at a discount, and that is the signature of real translation rather than empty usage. The open questions are the ones the scouting section already names, and the numbers do not resolve them: .340 on low three-point volume is not yet a floor-spacing skill, and 1.2 blocks on a college schedule at his length is production from timing and effort rather than rim protection, so neither the spacing nor the defensive anchoring case is proven. At 20 he is at the front of the development curve with two years before an aging-related concern is relevant, and rebounding is historically the most translatable non-shooting skill for a young big. For 2026-27 the honest read is that the per-minute rebounding will show up immediately and the redraft value is a pure minutes question, exactly as the projection card frames it.
