Projection logic

Okereke averaged 9.6 points, 3.6 rebounds, and 2.0 assists over 36 games at Vanderbilt and made 40% of his threes before reportedly signing a two-way contract with Los Angeles. Those numbers support a credible spot-up wing pathway, not immediate NBA production. The Lakers' deep forward group and the normal rookie two-way development curve drive the 12-game median. Redraft, dynasty, and scenarios

  • Median: Primarily a developmental season away from the NBA rotation.
  • Ceiling: The shot translates immediately and his defense earns late-season call-ups, producing a 10-12-minute role.
  • Floor: Limited creation keeps him from separating from other development wings.
  • Draft stance: Undrafted in redraft, including most deep formats.
  • Dynasty: Monitor three-point volume, steal rate, and defensive assignments rather than raw scoring.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons. Walk-on turned All-Ivy forward at Cornell (2022-25, 9.8 PPG career); as a junior in 2024-25 he put up 13.9 PTS, 4.6 REB, 4.1 AST on .595 FG (led the Ivy League, 18th nationally) while leading Cornell in assists (120), steals (31) and blocks (31). Graduate year at Vanderbilt 2025-26: 36 GP (35 starts), 9.6 PTS, 3.6 REB, 2.0 AST, .481 FG/.400 3P for an NCAA-tournament team. Draft: undrafted (2026); signed a Lakers two-way on June 24, 2026.

No NBA record. Most recent season: 2025-26 at Vanderbilt.

Trajectory: The record shows an unusual profile — a connective big-wing who led a Division I team in assists, steals AND blocks, then proved the frame held up in the SEC while adding a legitimate three-point season (.400). The .321 college 3P the year before means the shot is the unproven part, not the feel or the defense. In 2026-27 he is a two-way development wing behind the Lakers' deep forward group; he becomes fantasy-relevant only if the 3P holds on NBA volume and injuries open a 3-and-D call-up late in the season — a deep-dynasty monitor, not a redraft pick.
