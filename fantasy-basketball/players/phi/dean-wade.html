Wade's 2025-26 baseline was 59 games and 22.3 minutes with roughly .439/.711 shooting, 1.2 threes, 5.8 points, 4.2 rebounds, 1.5 assists, 0.7 steals, 0.4 blocks, and 0.3 turnovers. Role and fantasy outlook

  • Philadelphia agreed with Wade at the July 1 opening of free agency and made it official July 6: four years and $39 million, signed with a portion of the non-taxpayer mid-level exception and carrying a partial guarantee in Year 4. Philadelphia beat out several other suitors. It was the first major signing by new president of basketball operations Mike Gansey, who was in Cleveland's front office when the Cavaliers first signed Wade in 2019 and championed him there. ESPN, July 1, 2026 · NBA.com · RealGM transaction history
  • RotoWire's July 12 depth chart lists him first at power forward; Dominick Barlow is the meaningful competition and started 59 of his 71 games in 2025-26. Full-MLE money on a four-year term for a career 5.8-point role player is an organizational statement about the starting job; Barlow's start count is the reason it is not settled. RotoWire depth chart, updated July 12, 2026
  • 9-cat: mild 3PM/rebound/steal help with extremely low turnovers. He does not supply enough scoring, blocks, or percentage volume for standard formats.
  • Points: waiver streamer only; low usage is punished in nearly every system.
  • Dynasty: Hashtag listed him 347th. He is a deep win-now role player, not a growth asset. Hashtag dynasty

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: 14-18 minutes if Barlow starts and Philadelphia uses Brown/Edwards at the 4. The floor is lifted slightly from a pure bench outcome because four guaranteed years at MLE money rarely coexist with a sub-15-minute role.
  • Base: 23 minutes, approximately 6/4/1.5 with one-plus three; deep-league glue.
  • Ceiling: 27 minutes and borderline top-180 value if injuries open wing/PF time and his threes approach two.

Health and uncertainty

Wade has a recurring history of lower-volume seasons and missed 23 games in 2025-26. No accessible July source reported an active restriction. Starting does not guarantee closing: Simons can replace him for offense, Bona for size, and Barlow for switching.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four years at Kansas State, 126 games and 12.0 points with 5.4 rebounds career. Big 12 All-Newcomer as a freshman in 2015-16 (9.9 points, 5.1 rebounds), 9.3 points as a sophomore, then a junior breakout in 2017-18 at 16.2 points and 6.2 rebounds with first-team All-Big 12 honors. His senior year in 2018-19 produced 12.9 points and a team-high 6.2 rebounds and a second first-team All-Big 12 selection before a foot injury cut it short — an injury that is a plausible reason he was not drafted. Draft: undrafted (2019). Signed a two-way deal with Cleveland on July 9, 2019 and spent most of his rookie year with the Canton Charge, averaging 14.2 points and 7.6 rebounds in the G League.

Career three-point percentage is .367 across 342 games, with 160 starts. All seven seasons came with Cleveland; 2026-27 is his first outside the organization.

Durability: The defining pattern. Wade has never played more than 63 games in a season and has cleared 55 only twice — 12, 63, 51, 44, 54, 59 and 59. A right knee sprain cost him the final 11 games of 2023-24 and kept him out of Game 1 of that playoff series; a right knee bone bruise in January 2025 cost him multiple weeks, and an ankle sprain took two more weeks earlier that same season. He missed 23 games in 2025-26. No active restriction was reported as of July 2026, but a roughly 55-to-60-game expectation is the honest planning number, not 70.

Trajectory: Seven seasons of a completely stable low-usage profile: about 20 minutes, five to six points, three to four rebounds, one-plus three, 0.6 steals and almost no turnovers. He has never averaged more than 6.0 points in a season and his usage has never expanded, which is why the four-year, $39 million non-taxpayer MLE deal Philadelphia gave him in July 2026 is a statement about role certainty rather than production. He is being paid to space the floor and defend multiple positions next to the stars, and that is exactly what the seven-year record says he does. In fantasy he is a threes-rebounds-steals streamer with elite turnover economy and no scoring or blocks, roughly top-200 at best. He becomes a 14-team specialist only if his minutes push toward 27 and the threes approach two per game — realistically requiring an injury to a Philadelphia wing plus Dominick Barlow, who started 59 games in 2025-26, losing the power forward competition outright.
