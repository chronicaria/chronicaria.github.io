    Polyakov action

    The Polyakov action rewrites string dynamics as D free scalar fields X^\mu living on a worldsheet whose metric h_{\alpha\beta} is itself a field to be varied — trading the ugly Nambu–Goto square root for a clean quadratic action, at the price of one extra (non-propagating) field.

    The one idea

    Think of the string's worldsheet as a rubber sheet swept out in spacetime. Nambu–Goto measures its area directly — geometrically honest, but the area formula has a square root of derivatives buried in it, and square roots are poison for quantization.

    Polyakov's move is to stop insisting the worldsheet metric equal the geometry the string actually has. Instead, lay down a separate, freely adjustable metric hαβ(τ,σ)h_{\alpha\beta}(\tau,\sigma)hαβ​(τ,σ) — a "reference grid" you paint on the sheet by hand — and let the action decide, through hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​'s equation of motion, how the grid should match the real geometry. The reward: with hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ carrying the geometric information, the XμX^\muXμ enter only quadratically. The worldsheet becomes DDD ordinary free fields, and the whole quantum machinery of 2D field theory opens up.

    This is the same trick as the einbein for the relativistic particle: introduce an auxiliary variable so the square root dissolves. The XμX^\muXμ are scalar fields; hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ is a metric with no dynamics of its own; varying hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ hands you the constraints for free.

    The action

    Let hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ be an independent Lorentzian metric on the worldsheet, coordinates σα=(τ,σ)\sigma^\alpha=(\tau,\sigma)σα=(τ,σ), with target-space dot product A⋅B=ημνAμBνA\cdot B=\eta_{\mu\nu}A^\mu B^\nuA⋅B=ημν​AμBν in mostly-plus signature η=diag(−,+,…,+)\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-,+,\dots,+)η=diag(−,+,…,+). Then

    SP=−14πα′∫d2σ −h  hαβ ∂αXμ ∂βXμ,(1)S_P=-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-h}\;h^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_\alpha X^\mu\,\partial_\beta X_\mu, \tag{1}SP​=−4πα′1​∫d2σ−h​hαβ∂α​Xμ∂β​Xμ​,(1)

    with h≡det⁡hαβ<0h\equiv\det h_{\alpha\beta}<0h≡dethαβ​<0 and hαβh^{\alpha\beta}hαβ the matrix inverse. Read it as a field theory: the Xμ(τ,σ)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)Xμ(τ,σ) are DDD scalar fields on the two-dimensional worldsheet, and −h hαβ\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}−h​hαβ is the machinery that tells those fields how to measure their own gradients. It is the flat free-scalar action −14πα′∫ηαβ∂αX⋅∂βX-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int \eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta X−4πα′1​∫ηαβ∂α​X⋅∂β​X with ηαβ\eta^{\alpha\beta}ηαβ promoted to the field −h hαβ\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}−h​hαβ.

    The coefficient 1/(4πα′)1/(4\pi\alpha')1/(4πα′) is half of Nambu–Goto's, and it has to be, so that eliminating hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ lands back exactly on SNG=−12πα′∫−γS_{\mathrm{NG}}=-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int\sqrt{-\gamma}SNG​=−2πα′1​∫−γ​. When hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ solves its equation of motion, the contraction hαβhαβ=2h^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}=2hαβhαβ​=2 (a 2×22\times22×2 metric) supplies the missing factor of two; see the Equivalence section. The coefficient looks wrong next to NG precisely because that 222 is waiting to appear.

    Its symmetries

    The action (1) has three local symmetries:

    • two reparametrizations (worldsheet diffeomorphisms) σα→σ~α(σ)\sigma^\alpha\to\tilde\sigma^\alpha(\sigma)σα→σ~α(σ);
    • one Weyl rescaling hαβ↦Ω2(σ) hαβh_{\alpha\beta}\mapsto\Omega^2(\sigma)\,h_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​↦Ω2(σ)hαβ​, with XμX^\muXμ untouched (see Weyl invariance).

    A symmetric 2×22\times22×2 metric has three independent components, and three local symmetries can kill all three. So hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ carries no local propagating degrees of freedom — it is pure gauge, and can be fixed to a flat reference metric ("conformal gauge"). The counting is the subject of Independent worldsheet metric; here it suffices that this is why the extra field costs nothing physical.

    Equation of motion for XμX^\muXμ

    This is the action's own dynamics. Vary XμX^\muXμ with hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ held fixed. Only ∂αX\partial_\alpha X∂α​X carries the variation, and each of the two ∂X\partial X∂X factors contributes equally:

    δSP=−14πα′∫d2σ −h hαβ(2 ∂αδXμ)∂βXμ=−12πα′∫d2σ −h hαβ ∂αδXμ ∂βXμ.\delta S_P=-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}\big(2\,\partial_\alpha\delta X^\mu\big)\partial_\beta X_\mu =-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_\alpha\delta X^\mu\,\partial_\beta X_\mu.δSP​=−4πα′1​∫d2σ−h​hαβ(2∂α​δXμ)∂β​Xμ​=−2πα′1​∫d2σ−h​hαβ∂α​δXμ∂β​Xμ​.

    Integrate by parts to strip the derivative off δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ:

    δSP=12πα′∫d2σ δXμ ∂α ⁣(−h hαβ∂βXμ)−12πα′∫d2σ ∂α ⁣(−h hαβ δXμ ∂βXμ).\delta S_P=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\delta X^\mu\,\partial_\alpha\!\big(\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\beta X_\mu\big) -\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\partial_\alpha\!\big(\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}\,\delta X^\mu\,\partial_\beta X_\mu\big).δSP​=2πα′1​∫d2σδXμ∂α​(−h​hαβ∂β​Xμ​)−2πα′1​∫d2σ∂α​(−h​hαβδXμ∂β​Xμ​).

    The second term is a total derivative — a boundary term. It vanishes for closed strings (periodicity in σ\sigmaσ) and, for open strings, is killed by imposing Neumann or Dirichlet conditions at the endpoints (worked in the Exercise). Demanding δSP=0\delta S_P=0δSP​=0 for arbitrary δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ in the bulk gives

    ∂α ⁣(−h hαβ ∂βXμ)=0.(2)\partial_\alpha\!\big(\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}\,\partial_\beta X^\mu\big)=0. \tag{2}∂α​(−h​hαβ∂β​Xμ)=0.(2)

    This is the covariant free-field equation: a massless scalar on the curved worldsheet. In conformal gauge −h hαβ=ηαβ\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}−h​hαβ=ηαβ, the metric factor is constant and (2) collapses to the flat 2D wave equation (−∂τ2+∂σ2)Xμ=0(-\partial_\tau^2+\partial_\sigma^2)X^\mu=0(−∂τ2​+∂σ2​)Xμ=0 — the Weyl factor drops out because −h hαβ\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}−h​hαβ is Weyl-invariant.

    Equation of motion for hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​: the constraints, for free

    Now vary hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ (equivalently hαβh^{\alpha\beta}hαβ). Because hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ appears in (1) with no derivatives, its equation of motion is purely algebraic — it constrains rather than propagates. Varying −h\sqrt{-h}−h​ (via δ−h=−12−h hαβ δhαβ\delta\sqrt{-h}=-\tfrac12\sqrt{-h}\,h_{\alpha\beta}\,\delta h^{\alpha\beta}δ−h​=−21​−h​hαβ​δhαβ) and the explicit hαβh^{\alpha\beta}hαβ gives

    Tαβ  ≡  ∂αX⋅∂βX−12 hαβ hγδ ∂γX⋅∂δX=0.(3)T_{\alpha\beta}\;\equiv\;\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta X-\tfrac12\,h_{\alpha\beta}\,h^{\gamma\delta}\,\partial_\gamma X\cdot\partial_\delta X=0. \tag{3}Tαβ​≡∂α​X⋅∂β​X−21​hαβ​hγδ∂γ​X⋅∂δ​X=0.(3)

    The full metric variation is carried out in Worldsheet stress tensor; TαβT_{\alpha\beta}Tαβ​ is the worldsheet stress tensor. Two things to see immediately:

    • The constraints are not an extra postulate: Tαβ=0T_{\alpha\beta}=0Tαβ​=0 is the metric's equation of motion. In conformal gauge (3) reads X˙2+X′2=0\dot X^2+X'^2=0X˙2+X′2=0 and X˙⋅X′=0\dot X\cdot X'=0X˙⋅X′=0, i.e. (X˙±X′)2=0(\dot X\pm X')^2=0(X˙±X′)2=0 — the Virasoro constraints, which in light-cone quantization had to be imposed by hand.
    • The trace vanishes identically. Contracting (3) with hαβh^{\alpha\beta}hαβ and using hαβhαβ=2h^{\alpha\beta}h_{\alpha\beta}=2hαβhαβ​=2 gives hαβTαβ=0h^{\alpha\beta}T_{\alpha\beta}=0hαβTαβ​=0 with no reference to the equations of motion. A traceless stress tensor is the local signature of Weyl invariance, and the hallmark of a conformal theory.

    Equivalence to Nambu–Goto

    Equation (3) says hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ is proportional, point by point, to the induced metric γαβ=∂αX⋅∂βX\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta Xγαβ​=∂α​X⋅∂β​X: any solution has the form hαβ=f2(σ) γαβh_{\alpha\beta}=f^2(\sigma)\,\gamma_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​=f2(σ)γαβ​ for some positive function f2f^2f2 (its indices must match γαβ\gamma_{\alpha\beta}γαβ​, and only the scale is left free). In two dimensions the scale then cancels inside the action's combination,

    −h hαβ=f2−γ⋅f−2γαβ=−γ γαβ,\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}=f^2\sqrt{-\gamma}\cdot f^{-2}\gamma^{\alpha\beta}=\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\gamma^{\alpha\beta},−h​hαβ=f2−γ​⋅f−2γαβ=−γ​γαβ,

    so substituting back into (1), with γαβγαβ=2\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=2γαβγαβ​=2,

    SP  ⟶  −14πα′∫d2σ −γ γαβγαβ=−12πα′∫d2σ −γ=SNG.S_P\;\longrightarrow\;-\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-\gamma}\,\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\gamma_{\alpha\beta} =-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{-\gamma} =S_{\mathrm{NG}}.SP​⟶−4πα′1​∫d2σ−γ​γαβγαβ​=−2πα′1​∫d2σ−γ​=SNG​.

    There is the promised factor of two: 14πα′⋅2=12πα′\tfrac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\cdot2=\tfrac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}4πα′1​⋅2=2πα′1​. Classically, Polyakov and Nambu–Goto are the same theory; the cancellation of the arbitrary scale f2f^2f2 is Weyl invariance in action. The full accounting lives in Polyakov vs Nambu-Goto.

    To restate the payoff: you paid for one auxiliary metric and bought three things: a quadratic (hence quantizable) action for the XμX^\muXμ, the Virasoro constraints delivered automatically as hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​'s equation of motion, and a new gauge symmetry (Weyl) that makes the gauge-fixed worldsheet a conformal field theory. That is the whole reason every modern treatment starts from SPS_PSP​.

    Open questions

    • Classical equivalence is settled above; quantum equivalence needs the path-integral measure and the Faddeev–Popov ghost determinant, the gateway to Faddeev-Popov ghosts and the critical dimension D=26D=26D=26.
    • Fixing hαβh_{\alpha\beta}hαβ​ to a flat reference is a local statement. On higher-genus worldsheets a finite set of moduli survive gauge-fixing; see Moduli spaces.

    Exercise

    For an open string, σ∈[0,π]\sigma\in[0,\pi]σ∈[0,π] with no periodicity, the boundary term in δSP\delta S_PδSP​ does not automatically vanish. Working in conformal gauge (−h hαβ=ηαβ\sqrt{-h}\,h^{\alpha\beta}=\eta^{\alpha\beta}−h​hαβ=ηαβ), extract the boundary term from the XXX-variation and determine the condition on the endpoints that kills it while leaving the string ends free to move (a Neumann condition). What is the geometric meaning of that condition?

    Solution

    In conformal gauge the XXX-variation is δSP=12πα′∫dτ dσ δXμ (−∂τ2+∂σ2)Xμ−12πα′∫dτ dσ ∂α(ηαβ δXμ ∂βXμ).\delta S_P=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d\tau\,d\sigma\,\delta X^\mu\,(-\partial_\tau^2+\partial_\sigma^2)X_\mu-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d\tau\,d\sigma\,\partial_\alpha\big(\eta^{\alpha\beta}\,\delta X^\mu\,\partial_\beta X_\mu\big).δSP​=2πα′1​∫dτdσδXμ(−∂τ2​+∂σ2​)Xμ​−2πα′1​∫dτdσ∂α​(ηαβδXμ∂β​Xμ​). The total-derivative term is a sum over α=τ,σ\alpha=\tau,\sigmaα=τ,σ. The τ\tauτ piece integrates to a τ\tauτ-boundary (initial/final time slices), where we hold δXμ=0\delta X^\mu=0δXμ=0 as usual. The surviving piece is the σ\sigmaσ-derivative, evaluated at the two spatial endpoints: −12πα′∫dτ [δXμ ∂σXμ]σ=0σ=π-\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\int d\tau\,\Big[\delta X^\mu\,\partial_\sigma X_\mu\Big]_{\sigma=0}^{\sigma=\pi}−2πα′1​∫dτ[δXμ∂σ​Xμ​]σ=0σ=π​ (the +++ sign from ησσ=+1\eta^{\sigma\sigma}=+1ησσ=+1 absorbed into the bracket orientation). For this to vanish for arbitrary bulk variations δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ, we need at each end ∂σXμ∣σ=0=∂σXμ∣σ=π=0,\partial_\sigma X^\mu\big|_{\sigma=0}=\partial_\sigma X^\mu\big|_{\sigma=\pi}=0,∂σ​Xμ​σ=0​=∂σ​Xμ​σ=π​=0, the free (Neumann) boundary condition. (Alternatively δXμ=0\delta X^\mu=0δXμ=0 at the ends — a Dirichlet condition pinning the endpoint, the seed of D-branes — but that fixes the endpoint rather than freeing it.)

    Geometric meaning: ∂σXμ\partial_\sigma X^\mu∂σ​Xμ is the spatial tangent to the string. Setting it to zero at the ends says no momentum flows off the end of the string — the endpoints are free and move at the speed of light, transverse to the string. The endpoints are not tied to anything.
