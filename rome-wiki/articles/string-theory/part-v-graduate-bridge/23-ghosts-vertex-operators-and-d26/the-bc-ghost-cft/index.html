    The ghosts came from Faddeev-Popov ghosts as bookkeeping: a Jacobian for slicing out the gauge redundancy of the worldsheet metric. Here we quantize them and read off one number, cgh=−26c_{\text{gh}}=-26cgh​=−26. That number is the tax the gauge symmetry charges, expressed as negative central charge. It has to be paid by exactly 262626 free scalars, and that is where D=26D=26D=26 comes from (Total central charge and D = 26). Everything in this note is in service of extracting −26-26−26 honestly, sign by sign.

    From covariant to conformal gauge

    The ghost action from the Faddeev–Popov procedure is Sghost=12π∫d2σ g  bαβ∇αcβ,S_{\text{ghost}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^2\sigma\,\sqrt{g}\;b_{\alpha\beta}\nabla^\alpha c^\beta ,Sghost​=2π1​∫d2σg​bαβ​∇αcβ, with bαβb_{\alpha\beta}bαβ​ a symmetric traceless tensor and cαc^\alphacα a vector — they inherited these shapes from the gauge parameters they replaced (a traceless symmetric βαβ\beta^{\alpha\beta}βαβ and the diffeo vector vαv^\alphavα). Go to conformal gauge g^αβ=e2ωδαβ\hat g_{\alpha\beta}=e^{2\omega}\delta_{\alpha\beta}g^​αβ​=e2ωδαβ​ in complex coordinates z=σ1+iσ2z=\sigma^1+i\sigma^2z=σ1+iσ2, where g=e2ω\sqrt{g}=e^{2\omega}g​=e2ω and d2σ=12d2zd^2\sigma=\tfrac12 d^2zd2σ=21​d2z.

    Two simplifications happen at once, and they are the whole reason the ghost CFT is easy:

    Equations of motion and OPEs

    Varying bbb and ccc gives ∂zˉb=∂zˉc=0\partial_{\bar z}b=\partial_{\bar z}c=0∂zˉ​b=∂zˉ​c=0: both fields are holomorphic, functions of zzz alone. This is the defining feature of a chiral CFT — the dynamics is entirely in how singular the fields get as they collide.

    Those collisions are the OPE. There is no singularity in b bb\,bbb or c cc\,ccc: like fields do not contract.

    One warning that runs through every computation below: bbb and ccc anticommute (they are Grassmann). Sliding one past the other flips a sign, so the order in which fields are written is load-bearing. Two things generate signs: differentiating the pole 1/(z−w)1/(z-w)1/(z−w), and hopping Grassmann fields past each other to bring a contracted pair adjacent. We will see the first does all the work in the central charge and the second, for this system, quietly lands on +++ — but keeping both explicit is the only way to trust the result.

    A negative central charge is not a problem here. The central charge is the Weyl anomaly coefficient of the CFT — the quantum failure of the trace TααT^\alpha{}_\alphaTαα​ to vanish on a curved worldsheet. A unitary CFT has c>0c>0c>0; the ghosts are non-unitary by construction (anticommuting integer-spin fields are precisely the spin-statistics-violating objects), so c<0c<0c<0 is allowed and there is no contradiction. This negative −26-26−26 is the whole point: it is what cancels the positive c=Dc=Dc=D of the matter sector.

    Where −26-26−26 really comes from

    The −26-26−26 is not an arithmetic coincidence. It is forced by the weights (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1), which are forced by the index structure bzz,czb_{zz},c^zbzz​,cz, which is forced by what the ghosts replaced — a traceless-symmetric tensor βαβ\beta^{\alpha\beta}βαβ and a vector vαv^\alphavα, i.e. the parameters of diffeomorphisms and Weyl rescalings. The chain is: diffeo+Weyl gauge fixing → (βαβ,vα) → (bzz,cz) → (h=2, h=−1) → cgh=−26.\text{diffeo+Weyl gauge fixing}\ \to\ (\beta^{\alpha\beta},v^\alpha)\ \to\ (b_{zz},c^z)\ \to\ (h=2,\,h=-1)\ \to\ c_{\text{gh}}=-26 .diffeo+Weyl gauge fixing → (βαβ,vα) → (bzz​,cz) → (h=2,h=−1) → cgh​=−26. Traced back to Faddeev-Popov ghosts, the −26-26−26 is a fingerprint of two-dimensional diffeomorphism invariance itself. The ghosts are the gauge symmetry made into fields, and −26-26−26 is the price tag it stamps on the theory.

    Exercise

    A tempting but wrong story says the minus in cgh=−26c_{\text{gh}}=-26cgh​=−26 comes from the Grassmann hop sign. Test it.     Solution

    B(z)B(w)=: ⁣c(z)∂b(z) ⁣:: ⁣c(w)∂b(w) ⁣:B(z)B(w)=:\!c(z)\partial b(z)\!::\!c(w)\partial b(w)\!:B(z)B(w)=:c(z)∂b(z)::c(w)∂b(w):. Dropping the Grassmann statistics changes nothing: the crossed pairing that the hop sign would have weighted still pairs cccccc and ∂b ∂b\partial b\,\partial b∂b∂b and vanishes, and the surviving nested pairing carries a +++ under both statistics. The whole minus is already there in the derivative on the propagator. The same is true term by term, so this commuting toy — same propagators, no hop — still totals −13-13−13 and gives c=−26c=-26c=−26, not +26+26+26. The Grassmann hop is a genuine part of the machinery but is not the origin of the sign. Where the real flip lives. A true bosonic ghost system is the βγ\beta\gammaβγ system, and its propagator is antisymmetric: ⟨β(z)γ(w)⟩=+1z−w\langle\beta(z)\gamma(w)\rangle=+\tfrac{1}{z-w}⟨β(z)γ(w)⟩=+z−w1​ but ⟨γ(z)β(w)⟩=−1z−w\langle\gamma(z)\beta(w)\rangle=-\tfrac{1}{z-w}⟨γ(z)β(w)⟩=−z−w1​, whereas the fermionic bcbcbc propagator is symmetric (⟨b(z)c(w)⟩=⟨c(z)b(w)⟩=+1z−w\langle b(z)c(w)\rangle=\langle c(z)b(w)\rangle=+\tfrac{1}{z-w}⟨b(z)c(w)⟩=⟨c(z)b(w)⟩=+z−w1​). Feeding that antisymmetry through the identical TTTTTT computation flips the overall sign of every double contraction, turning −26-26−26 into +26+26+26 for a (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) bosonic system. So the sign is carried by the propagator's symmetry — the (−1)(-1)(−1) of a closed fermion loop — not by the Wick hop. Why fermionic is correct. The ghosts exist to reproduce the Faddeev–Popov determinant ΔFP\Delta_{\text{FP}}ΔFP​, not its inverse, and only anticommuting fields put a determinant in the numerator of a Gaussian integral (commuting fields give 1/ΔFP1/\Delta_{\text{FP}}1/ΔFP​). That statistical choice is the same choice that fixes the propagator symmetry above. Had the ghosts been bosonic, cgh=+26c_{\text{gh}}=+26cgh​=+26 would force cmatter=−26c_{\text{matter}}=-26cmatter​=−26, i.e. D=−26D=-26D=−26 scalars — nonsense. The minus in cgh=−26c_{\text{gh}}=-26cgh​=−26 and the anticommuting statistics are one fact.
