Ryan Nembhard

Ryan Nembhard is a clean low-usage organizer whose trade to Atlanta buries him in the NBA's most crowded guard room — a roster-hold question now, not a draftable player.

Nembhard's rookie baseline was 6.6 points, 2.2 rebounds, and 5.3 assists with 1.4 turnovers in 19.5 minutes across 60 games. His assist-to-turnover shape is much more fantasy-relevant than his 41.5% FG or low stocks.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: undraftable in Atlanta; an assists-only streamer if injuries or a re-route ever hand him 20-plus minutes.
  • Points: no value without minutes, and Atlanta has none to give.
  • Dynasty: deep hold at age 23. The passing is NBA-useful, but size and scoring pressure limit the ceiling, and the trade removed the one depth chart where he had a lane.

Role, floor, ceiling, uncertainty

  • Median role: third-string organizer behind McCollum and Flemings, with G League (College Park) reps a live possibility.
  • Floor: waived or re-routed before opening night — Atlanta sits at 17 standard contracts against a 15-man limit, and his $2.15M is the easiest salary to move.
  • Ceiling: an injury to McCollum or a Flemings stumble makes him a 18-20-minute assists specialist; his 27-start Dallas template (25.7 MPG, 6.8 AST) is the proof of concept.
  • Contract: $2.15M guaranteed for 2026-27; Dallas exercised the option June 29 before including him in the July 19 trade.

He shot .356 from three on low volume. The rookie season split cleanly by role: in the 27 games he started he averaged 25.7 MPG, 9.4 PTS, 2.9 REB, 6.8 AST and 0.4 STL, which is a different and materially more valuable player than the aggregate line. Dallas waived Tyus Jones and converted him from his two-way to a standard multiyear deal on March 1, 2026, then exercised the 2026-27 option on June 29.

Durability: 60 of 82 games. Early-season absences were two-way roster mechanics rather than health; a left knee sprain accounted for later missed time, though the specific game count is not verified.

Trajectory: An undrafted rookie who produced a 5.3-assist season at 19.5 minutes and forced his way onto a standard contract inside four months — that is a real outcome, not a fluke of opportunity. The problem is everything other than passing: .415 from the field, zero blocks, 0.4 steals and no size mean he cannot be on the floor for scoring reasons alone. The July 19 trade to Atlanta took away the clear first-guard-off-the-bench lane he had in Dallas; he becomes a genuine nine-cat asset only if attrition clears Atlanta's guard room, at which point his 27-start line — near seven assists in 26 minutes — is the template. Otherwise he is roster filler with an NBA skill.
