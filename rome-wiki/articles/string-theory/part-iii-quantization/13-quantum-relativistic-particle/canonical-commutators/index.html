    Canonical commutators

    Quantize only the variables that survive the classical constraint, and postulate one commutator for each coordinate–momentum pair: [x^I,p^J]=i\eta^{IJ} and [x_0^-,p^+]=-i , everything else zero.

    Ordinary quantum mechanics runs on one relation — a coordinate and its conjugate momentum fail to commute, [x,p]=i[x,p]=i[x,p]=i (units with ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1) — repeated for every independent coordinate. The work here is bookkeeping: identify which variables remain independent after light-cone gauge has done its cutting, then pair each with its momentum. The one surprise is that the partner of x−x^-x− is not p−p^-p− but p+p^+p+, dragging a minus sign along. The sign is forced by the light-cone metric, not chosen, and it resurfaces correctly in every commutator built downstream.

    Which variables are independent?

    Quantization is mechanical once you know what to quantize; deciding that is classical work, already done in Light-cone Hamiltonian. There, solving the light-cone particle showed the entire motion is fixed by four pieces of data:

    • the transverse trajectory xI(τ)=x0I+pIm2τx^I(\tau)=x_0^I+\tfrac{p^I}{m^2}\tauxI(τ)=x0I​+m2pI​τ — needs the constants x0Ix_0^Ix0I​ and the momenta pIp^IpI;
    • the x−x^-x− trajectory x−(τ)=x0−+p−m2τx^-(\tau)=x_0^-+\tfrac{p^-}{m^2}\taux−(τ)=x0−​+m2p−​τ — needs the constant x0−x_0^-x0−​ and p−p^-p−;
    • p−p^-p− itself, which is not free: the mass shell fixes it, p−=12p+(pIpI+m2)p^-=\tfrac{1}{2p^+}(p^Ip^I+m^2)p−=2p+1​(pIpI+m2);
    • x+(τ)=p+m2τx^+(\tau)=\tfrac{p^+}{m^2}\taux+(τ)=m2p+​τ — no free constant, because the gauge condition nailed x+x^+x+ to τ\tauτ.

    Strip out everything determined by the rest, and the independent dynamical variables are

    \big(\,x^I,\; x_0^-,\; p^I,\; p^+\,\big). \tag{1}

    Here xIx^IxI (no explicit τ\tauτ) is the constant transverse position — the same object written x0Ix_0^Ix0I​ when emphasizing the trajectory. Both p−p^-p− and x+x^+x+ are absent: they are consequences, not inputs.

    What pairs with what — and why the minus sign

    Independent coordinates come with conjugate momenta. Read them off the natural pairing that sits inside the relativistic scalar product p⋅x=pμxμp\cdot x = p_\mu x^\mup⋅x=pμ​xμ. In light-cone components (see Light-cone coordinates),

    pμxμ=pIxI+p+x++p−x−,p+=−p−,p−=−p+. p_\mu x^\mu = p_I x^I + p_+ x^+ + p_- x^- , \qquad p_+ = -p^-,\quad p_- = -p^+ .pμ​xμ=pI​xI+p+​x++p−​x−,p+​=−p−,p−​=−p+.

    The last two identities are the light-cone lowering rule: since η+−=η−+=−1\eta_{+-}=\eta_{-+}=-1η+−​=η−+​=−1 are the only nonvanishing longitudinal components, lowering an index swaps the labels +↔−+\leftrightarrow-+↔− and brings a minus sign. So the momentum multiplying the coordinate x−x^-x− in p⋅xp\cdot xp⋅x is p−=−p+p_- = -p^+p−​=−p+. The conjugate of x−x^-x− is therefore p+p^+p+ (up to the sign), not p−p^-p−.

    That is the entire origin of the surprise: lowering swaps plus and minus, so the momentum conjugate to x−x^-x− is p+p^+p+, and the swap drags a minus sign along. Two conjugate pairs result:

    xI pairs with pI(ordinary),x0− pairs with p+(the light-cone twist). x^I \text{ pairs with } p^I \quad(\text{ordinary}), \qquad x_0^- \text{ pairs with } p^+ \quad(\text{the light-cone twist}).xI pairs with pI(ordinary),x0−​ pairs with p+(the light-cone twist).

    The postulated commutators

    Canonical quantization is one rule: for each conjugate pair, the coordinate and its momentum fail to commute by iii times the metric that connects their indices. Applied to (1):

    \boxed{\;[\,x^I,\,p^J\,] = i\,\eta^{IJ} = i\,\delta^{IJ},\qquad [\,x_0^-,\,p^+\,] = i\,\eta^{-+} = -\,i\;}\tag{2}

    with every other commutator zero. One index comes from the coordinate, one from the momentum, and η\etaη ties them together.

    1. Transverse sector. The transverse metric is Euclidean (mostly-plus signature), ηIJ=δIJ\eta^{IJ}=\delta^{IJ}ηIJ=δIJ, so this is the textbook [x,p]=i[x,p]=i[x,p]=i, one copy per transverse direction. No physics beyond first-year QM.

    2. Longitudinal sector. The coordinate is x0−x_0^-x0−​ (upper −-−), the momentum is p+p^+p+ (upper +++), so the metric on the right is η−+\eta^{-+}η−+. With η+−=η−+=−1\eta^{+-}=\eta^{-+}=-1η+−=η−+=−1 this is −i-i−i — the same minus sign that appeared in the pairing above, the light-cone metric showing through.

    The "all others zero" clause is not a throwaway. In full it reads [xI,xJ]=[pI,pJ]=[xI,x0−]=[xI,p+]=[x0−,pI]=[pI,p+]=0. [x^I,x^J]=[p^I,p^J]=[x^I,x_0^-]=[x^I,p^+]=[x_0^-,p^I]=[p^I,p^+]=0 .[xI,xJ]=[pI,pJ]=[xI,x0−​]=[xI,p+]=[x0−​,pI]=[pI,p+]=0. In particular every momentum commutes with every other momentum — the fact that makes p−p^-p− and the Hamiltonian free of ordering ambiguities.

    The commutators hold at every τ\tauτ

    Passing to the Heisenberg picture conjugates each operator by eiHτe^{iH\tau}eiHτ (see Schrodinger and Heisenberg pictures). A commutator whose right-hand side is a pure number — like iδIJi\delta^{IJ}iδIJ or −i-i−i — is unchanged by conjugation, since numbers commute with everything. So the same relations hold for the time-dependent operators:

    [ xI(τ), pJ(τ) ]=i δIJ,[ x0−(τ), p+(τ) ]=−i.[\,x^I(\tau),\,p^J(\tau)\,]=i\,\delta^{IJ},\qquad [\,x_0^-(\tau),\,p^+(\tau)\,]=-i.[xI(τ),pJ(τ)]=iδIJ,[x0−​(τ),p+(τ)]=−i.

    Derived operators inherit their commutators

    The operators p−p^-p−, x−(τ)x^-(\tau)x−(τ), x+(τ)x^+(\tau)x+(τ) were not postulated — they are defined by the quantum versions of the classical relations:

    p^-\equiv \tfrac{1}{2p^+}\big(p^Ip^I+m^2\big),\qquad x^-(\tau)\equiv x_0^- + \tfrac{p^-}{m^2}\tau,\qquad x^+(\tau)\equiv \tfrac{p^+}{m^2}\tau. \tag{3}

    Their commutators are consequences of (2), computed with the product rule [A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C][A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C][A,BC]=[A,B]C+B[A,C]. Two we will need:

    [ xI, p− ]=i pIp+,[ x0−, p− ]=i p−p+.[\,x^I,\,p^-\,]=\frac{i\,p^I}{p^+},\qquad [\,x_0^-,\,p^-\,]=i\,\frac{p^-}{p^+}.[xI,p−]=p+ipI​,[x0−​,p−]=ip+p−​.

    The first: [xI,p−]=12p+[xI,pJpJ]=12p+(2ipI)=ipI/p+[x^I,p^-]=\tfrac{1}{2p^+}[x^I,p^Jp^J]=\tfrac{1}{2p^+}(2ip^I)=ip^I/p^+[xI,p−]=2p+1​[xI,pJpJ]=2p+1​(2ipI)=ipI/p+, using [xI,pJpJ]=2iδIJpJ=2ipI[x^I,p^Jp^J]=2i\delta^{IJ}p^J=2ip^I[xI,pJpJ]=2iδIJpJ=2ipI. The second is nonzero only because p−p^-p− contains 1/p+1/p^+1/p+ and x0−x_0^-x0−​ fails to commute with p+p^+p+; it is derived in Momentum and Lorentz generators and again in the exercise below. Each is one postulate of (2) reaching through the definition of p−p^-p− — the transverse pair for the first, the longitudinal minus sign for the second. The first matters downstream: because [xI,p−]≠0[x^I,p^-]\neq0[xI,p−]=0, the boost generator M−IM^{-I}M−I built from xIx^IxI and p−p^-p− is not Hermitian as written and must be symmetrized.

    The definition of p−p^-p− carries no ordering ambiguity: 1/p+1/p^+1/p+ commutes with the polynomial pIpI+m2p^Ip^I+m^2pIpI+m2 because all momenta commute. This is the gentle feature of the point particle. The string's mass operator is an infinite oscillator sum where the analogous ordering is a genuine calculation — that is where D=26D=26D=26 and the normal-ordering constant enter.

    Why not quantize x+x^+x+ and p−p^-p− instead?

    A tempting alternative: treat x+x^+x+ as a coordinate and p−p^-p− as its momentum, then write [x+,p−]=iη+−=−i[x^+,p^-]=i\eta^{+-}=-i[x+,p−]=iη+−=−i. In covariant quantization (where even x0x^0x0 is an operator) that is exactly right. But in our light-cone gauge x+=p+m2τx^+=\tfrac{p^+}{m^2}\taux+=m2p+​τ is built from a momentum, and all momenta commute, so

    [x+,p−]=[p+m2τ, p−]=0.[x^+,p^-]=\Big[\tfrac{p^+}{m^2}\tau,\,p^-\Big]=0.[x+,p−]=[m2p+​τ,p−]=0.

    Choosing the wrong independent set would have baked in a commutator that our framework does not have. That is the discipline: quantize what is actually independent, and let everything else follow.

    Open questions

    • The inverse 1/p+1/p^+1/p+ presumes states supported away from p+=0p^+=0p+=0. Standard in light-cone gauge; for strings the p+=0p^+=0p+=0 sector falls outside this coordinate patch and needs covariant methods.
    • The index III runs over D−2D-2D−2 transverse directions for a particle in DDD dimensions. Nothing in the point-particle commutators fixes DDD — the critical dimension only appears once string oscillator ordering feeds into the Lorentz algebra.

    Exercise

    Using only the boxed relations (2) and the definitions (3), evaluate the commutator [ x+, p+ ][\,x^+,\,p^+\,][x+,p+] and the commutator [ x0−, p− ][\,x_0^-,\,p^-\,][x0−​,p−]. For the second, you may use [x0−,1/p+]=i/p+2[x_0^-,1/p^+]=i/p^{+2}[x0−​,1/p+]=i/p+2. State in one sentence why the first is zero but the second is not.

    Solution

    First. x+=p+m2τx^+=\tfrac{p^+}{m^2}\taux+=m2p+​τ is a multiple of the momentum p+p^+p+, and all momenta commute, so [x+,p+]=[p+m2τ, p+]=τm2 [p+,p+]=0.[x^+,p^+]=\Big[\tfrac{p^+}{m^2}\tau,\,p^+\Big]=\tfrac{\tau}{m^2}\,[p^+,p^+]=0.[x+,p+]=[m2p+​τ,p+]=m2τ​[p+,p+]=0.

    Second. Since p−=12p+(pIpI+m2)p^-=\tfrac{1}{2p^+}(p^Ip^I+m^2)p−=2p+1​(pIpI+m2) and x0−x_0^-x0−​ commutes with every pIp^IpI, only the 1/p+1/p^+1/p+ factor contributes: [x0−,p−]=12(pIpI+m2) [x0−,1p+]=12(pIpI+m2) ip+2=i 1p+⋅pIpI+m22p+=i p−p+.[x_0^-,p^-]=\tfrac12(p^Ip^I+m^2)\,[x_0^-,\tfrac{1}{p^+}]=\tfrac12(p^Ip^I+m^2)\,\frac{i}{p^{+2}}=i\,\frac{1}{p^+}\cdot\frac{p^Ip^I+m^2}{2p^+}=i\,\frac{p^-}{p^+}.[x0−​,p−]=21​(pIpI+m2)[x0−​,p+1​]=21​(pIpI+m2)p+2i​=ip+1​⋅2p+pIpI+m2​=ip+p−​. (Provided check: [x0−,1/p+]=1p+[p+,x0−]1p+=1p+(+i)1p+=i/p+2[x_0^-,1/p^+]=\tfrac{1}{p^+}[p^+,x_0^-]\tfrac{1}{p^+}=\tfrac{1}{p^+}(+i)\tfrac{1}{p^+}=i/p^{+2}[x0−​,1/p+]=p+1​[p+,x0−​]p+1​=p+1​(+i)p+1​=i/p+2, using [x0−,p+]=−i⇒[p+,x0−]=+i[x_0^-,p^+]=-i\Rightarrow[p^+,x_0^-]=+i[x0−​,p+]=−i⇒[p+,x0−​]=+i.)

    Why one vanishes and the other doesn't. x+x^+x+ is made purely of a momentum, so it commutes with all momenta including p+p^+p+; but x0−x_0^-x0−​ is a genuine coordinate conjugate to p+p^+p+, and p−p^-p− secretly depends on p+p^+p+, so the nonzero pair [x0−,p+]=−i[x_0^-,p^+]=-i[x0−​,p+]=−i leaks through.
