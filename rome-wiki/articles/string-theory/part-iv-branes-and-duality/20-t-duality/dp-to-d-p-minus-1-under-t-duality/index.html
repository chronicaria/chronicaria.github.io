    Dp to D(p minus 1) under T-duality

    T-duality along a compact circle swaps Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions. A D p -brane that wraps the circle turns into a D (p\!-\!1) -brane stuck at a point on the dual circle: the brane loses one dimension, and the open string's momentum around the circle becomes its winding.

    An open string on a wrapped brane slides freely and carries momentum. View the same physics on the dual circle and that sliding freedom is gone: the endpoints are nailed down, and what was momentum is now the number of times the string wraps. Nailing down the endpoints is exactly what it means for the brane to shrink away in that direction.

    (Here ppp counts the brane's spatial dimensions, not momentum. A D24-brane fills 24 of the 25 spatial directions, so it is localized in exactly one — a wall in 25-dimensional space.)

    The puzzle: open strings seem to break the duality

    Spectrum invariance under R to alpha-prime over R established that a circle of radius RRR and one of radius R~=α′/R\tilde R = \alpha'/RR~=α′/R are the same physics for closed strings, once you swap momentum and winding. Now add a space-filling D25-brane so open strings can move everywhere; their endpoints are Neumann in all directions, including the compact X25≡XX^{25}\equiv XX25≡X.

    An open string on a space-filling brane cannot wind. Its endpoints slide freely, so any loop it makes around the circle can be pulled off. Its only circle label is momentum, p=n/Rp = n/Rp=n/R, contributing n2/R2n^2/R^2n2/R2 to M2M^2M2. If we kept the space-filling brane and merely replaced RRR by R~=α′/R\tilde R=\alpha'/RR~=α′/R, the momentum tower would become p~=nR~=nRα′,p~ 2=n2R2α′2≠n2R2.\tilde p=\frac{n}{\tilde R}=\frac{nR}{\alpha'}, \qquad \tilde p^{\,2}=\frac{n^2R^2}{\alpha'^2} \neq \frac{n^2}{R^2}.p~​=R~n​=α′nR​,p~​2=α′2n2R2​=R2n2​. That is a different spectrum. Open strings on an unchanged D25-brane could tell RRR from α′/R\alpha'/Rα′/R by measuring their momentum spacing — so naive T-duality is broken.

    The escape is that the brane is not a fixed backdrop; it transforms too. Fix the duality and the brane is forced to change type.

    The resolution: the brane changes type

    In the dual description, replace the D25-brane by a D24-brane with XXX Dirichlet — the endpoints pinned at a fixed value, say X=0X=0X=0. Two things flip at once:

    • The open string can now wind. A string running from X=0X=0X=0 to X=2πR~X=2\pi\tilde RX=2πR~ (once around) cannot contract, because both endpoints are nailed to the brane. The Neumann string that couldn't wind becomes a Dirichlet string that must.
    • The momentum tower of the space-filling theory matches the winding tower of the D24 theory, level for level, precisely when R~=α′/R\tilde R=\alpha'/RR~=α′/R.

     T-duality along X25:(D25; R) ⟶ (D24; R~=α′/R). \boxed{\ \text{T-duality along } X^{25}:\quad (\text{D}25;\, R) \ \longrightarrow\ (\text{D}24;\, \tilde R=\alpha'/R).\ } T-duality along X25:(D25;R) ⟶ (D24;R~=α′/R). ​

    The rest of this note derives this from the mode expansion, which shows the boundary-condition flip is automatic — not a choice we impose.

    The mechanism: boundary conditions get swapped

    Start from the Neumann–Neumann (NN) open-string expansion on σ∈[0,π]\sigma\in[0,\pi]σ∈[0,π]: X(\tau,\sigma) = x_0 + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0\,\tau + i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\ne0}\frac{1}{n}\,\alpha_n\cos n\sigma\, e^{-in\tau}, \qquad \alpha_0 = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\frac{n}{R}. \tag{1} The cos⁡nσ\cos n\sigmacosnσ is what makes it Neumann: ∂σX∝sin⁡nσ\partial_\sigma X \propto \sin n\sigma∂σ​X∝sinnσ vanishes at both ends σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π, so no momentum flows off the string. The α0τ\alpha_0\tauα0​τ term is the center-of-mass motion; its zero mode α0=2α′ p\alpha_0=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,pα0​=2α′​p encodes the momentum p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R.

    Split into left- and right-movers, X=XL(τ+σ)+XR(τ−σ)X = X_L(\tau+\sigma)+X_R(\tau-\sigma)X=XL​(τ+σ)+XR​(τ−σ). Copying the closed-string trick, define the dual coordinate by flipping the sign of the right-mover only: \tilde X \equiv X_L - X_R. \tag{2} Because cos⁡nσ\cos n\sigmacosnσ came from adding the two movers, subtracting them turns every cosine into a sine: \tilde X(\tau,\sigma) = q_0 + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0\,\sigma + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\ne0}\frac{1}{n}\,\alpha_n\,\sin n\sigma\, e^{-in\tau}. \tag{3} Read off what changed. The τ\tauτ-linear (momentum) term is gone; a σ\sigmaσ-linear (winding-like) term α0σ\alpha_0\sigmaα0​σ takes its place. And sin⁡nσ\sin n\sigmasinnσ makes ∂τX~∝sin⁡nσ\partial_\tau\tilde X \propto \sin n\sigma∂τ​X~∝sinnσ vanish at the ends: ∂τX~=0\partial_\tau\tilde X=0∂τ​X~=0 at σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π. That is the Dirichlet condition — the endpoints do not move in τ\tauτ, so they sit at fixed positions. The dual string's endpoints are pinned.

    The two statements combine into the master swap: \boxed{\ X = X_L + X_R,\quad \tilde X = X_L - X_R, \qquad \partial_\sigma X = \partial_\tau \tilde X, \qquad \partial_\tau X = \partial_\sigma \tilde X.\ } \tag{4} (These follow instantly from (2): ∂σX=XL′−XR′=∂τX~\partial_\sigma X = X_L' - X_R' = \partial_\tau\tilde X∂σ​X=XL′​−XR′​=∂τ​X~, and ∂τX=XL′+XR′=∂σX~\partial_\tau X = X_L' + X_R' = \partial_\sigma\tilde X∂τ​X=XL′​+XR′​=∂σ​X~, since XLX_LXL​ and XRX_RXR​ each depend on a single variable.)

    The left relation is the whole story. Neumann for XXX (∂σX=0\partial_\sigma X=0∂σ​X=0) is Dirichlet for X~\tilde XX~ (∂τX~=0\partial_\tau\tilde X=0∂τ​X~=0), and vice versa. T-duality literally exchanges N and D. Since "Dirichlet in a direction" means "the brane does not extend there," passing to X~\tilde XX~ drops that direction from the brane and its dimension falls by one.

    Momentum becomes winding, made quantitative

    Because X~\tilde XX~ is Dirichlet, its endpoints are fixed — but how far apart are they? Integrate the σ\sigmaσ-linear term across the string: \tilde X(\tau,\pi) - \tilde X(\tau,0) = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0\,(\pi-0) = 2\pi\alpha'\, p = 2\pi\,\frac{\alpha'}{R}\,n = 2\pi\tilde R\,n. \tag{5} So the dual string's ends are separated by nnn full circumferences of the dual circle: the original momentum number nnn has become the winding number. The clean picture is an infinite ladder of identical D24-branes on the covering line, spaced 2πR~2\pi\tilde R2πR~ apart (the images of one brane on the dual circle). A string whose ends land nnn rungs apart wraps the dual circle nnn times. Momentum quantization p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R has become winding quantization in units of R~\tilde RR~.

    This also passes the energy check. A stretched string costs (tension)×\times×(length). With tension T0=1/(2πα′)T_0=1/(2\pi\alpha')T0​=1/(2πα′) and dual length 2π∣n∣R~2\pi|n|\tilde R2π∣n∣R~, T_0\cdot 2\pi|n|\tilde R = \frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\cdot 2\pi|n|\,\frac{\alpha'}{R} = \frac{|n|}{R}, \tag{6} which is exactly the original momentum-mode energy ∣p∣=∣n∣/R|p| = |n|/R∣p∣=∣n∣/R. The stretch of the Dirichlet string weighs precisely what the momentum did — the 2π2\pi2π and α′\alpha'α′ cancel, just as they did for closed-string winding. Same spectrum, relabelled.

    The general rule (and the direction that goes the other way)

    The argument touches only one direction, so it iterates. If a Dppp-brane wraps a compact direction (Neumann there), T-duality along it gives a D(p ⁣− ⁣1)(p\!-\!1)(p−1)-brane at a point on the dual circle. Conversely, dualizing a direction transverse to the brane — where the brane already sits at a point, i.e. Dirichlet there — turns that pinned point into a wrapped Neumann direction and raises the dimension: wrap the circle:Dp→D(p−1);transverse circle:Dp→D(p+1).\text{wrap the circle:}\quad \text{D}p \to \text{D}(p-1); \qquad\qquad \text{transverse circle:}\quad \text{D}p \to \text{D}(p+1).wrap the circle:Dp→D(p−1);transverse circle:Dp→D(p+1). The rule is symmetric because (4) is: N↔\leftrightarrow↔D runs both ways.

    Closed strings do not end on the brane, so nothing in their Hilbert space cares whether we call it D25 or D24 — their R↔α′/RR\leftrightarrow\alpha'/RR↔α′/R equivalence is untouched. The brane transformation is demanded only by the open sector, and once made, everything is consistent.

    Step back: a D-brane is nothing but "where open strings are allowed to end." T-duality is a change of variables that swaps the string's two ways of noticing the circle — sliding around it (momentum) versus wrapping it (winding). Swapping those swaps "endpoints free to slide" for "endpoints nailed down," which is the same as the brane losing a dimension. This is how D-branes were first discovered: by asking what a moving open string turns into on the dual circle.

    The convention above pins the dual D(p ⁣− ⁣1)(p\!-\!1)(p−1)-brane at X~=0\tilde X=0X~=0; the arbitrary constant q0q_0q0​ just chooses which point. A Wilson line on the original wrapped brane slides that point around the dual circle — the holonomy angle is the dual brane's position.

    Open questions

    • Why only p≥2p\ge 2p≥2 here? Light-cone gauge builds X±=(X0±X1)/2X^\pm=(X^0\pm X^1)/\sqrt2X±=(X0±X1)/2​ from the time direction X0X^0X0 and a spatial direction X1X^1X1, both of which must be Neumann (on the brane); together with the wrapped compact direction XXX also being Neumann, the brane must fill at least the two spatial directions X1X^1X1 and XXX, so this explicit derivation covers Dp→p\top→D(p ⁣− ⁣1)(p\!-\!1)(p−1) only for p≥2p\ge 2p≥2. The p=1→0p=1\to0p=1→0 case (a D1-brane to a D0-brane) is true but needs covariant quantization — asserted here, not derived.
    • Superstrings. Everything above is the bosonic string in 26 dimensions. In Type II, T-duality additionally exchanges IIA↔\leftrightarrow↔IIB (consistent with IIA carrying even-ppp branes and IIB odd-ppp), so a wrapped brane in one theory becomes a lower brane in the other. Same spirit, out of scope for this module.

    Exercise

    You know that dualizing a wrapped direction sends Dp→p\top→D(p ⁣− ⁣1)(p\!-\!1)(p−1). Show the reverse claim quantitatively: dualizing a direction transverse to the brane sends Dp→p\top→D(p ⁣+ ⁣1)(p\!+\!1)(p+1), and the string's original stretch (a Dirichlet separation) becomes momentum on the dual circle. Concretely, take a Dppp-brane sitting at the point X=x1X=x_1X=x1​ on the circle of radius RRR, so XXX is Dirichlet with the DD expansion X(τ,σ)=x1+2α′ α0 σ+2α′∑n≠01nαnsin⁡nσ e−inτ,2α′ α0=x2−x1π,X(\tau,\sigma) = x_1 + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0\,\sigma + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\ne0}\tfrac{1}{n}\alpha_n\sin n\sigma\, e^{-in\tau}, \qquad \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0 = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{\pi},X(τ,σ)=x1​+2α′​α0​σ+2α′​∑n=0​n1​αn​sinnσe−inτ,2α′​α0​=πx2​−x1​​, where the string stretches from x1x_1x1​ to a brane image at x2=x1+2πR wx_2 = x_1 + 2\pi R\,wx2​=x1​+2πRw (w∈Zw\in\mathbb Zw∈Z windings around the covering line).     Solution

    Splitting the DD expansion into movers and forming X~=XL−XR\tilde X = X_L - X_RX~=XL​−XR​ flips sin⁡nσ→cos⁡nσ\sin n\sigma \to \cos n\sigmasinnσ→cosnσ and turns the σ\sigmaσ-linear term into a τ\tauτ-linear term — the exact inverse of the wrapped-case algebra (1)→\to→(3): X~(τ,σ)=x~0+2α′ α0 τ+i2α′∑n≠01nαncos⁡nσ e−inτ.\tilde X(\tau,\sigma) = \tilde x_0 + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0\,\tau + i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\ne0}\tfrac{1}{n}\alpha_n\cos n\sigma\, e^{-in\tau}.X~(τ,σ)=x~0​+2α′​α0​τ+i2α′​∑n=0​n1​αn​cosnσe−inτ. Now ∂σX~∝sin⁡nσ\partial_\sigma\tilde X \propto \sin n\sigma∂σ​X~∝sinnσ vanishes at σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π: X~\tilde XX~ is Neumann. The brane that was pinned to a point in XXX now extends along X~\tilde XX~, so its dimension rises: Dp→p\top→D(p ⁣+ ⁣1)(p\!+\!1)(p+1).

    The dual momentum is the coefficient of τ\tauτ: p~=α0/2α′\tilde p = \alpha_0/\sqrt{2\alpha'}p~​=α0​/2α′​. From the given zero mode, 2α′ α0=x2−x1π=2πR wπ=2R w⟹p~=α02α′=2α′ α02α′=2Rw2α′=wRα′=wR~.\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0 = \frac{x_2-x_1}{\pi} = \frac{2\pi R\,w}{\pi} = 2 R\,w \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \tilde p = \frac{\alpha_0}{\sqrt{2\alpha'}} = \frac{\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0}{2\alpha'} = \frac{2Rw}{2\alpha'} = \frac{wR}{\alpha'} = \frac{w}{\tilde R}.2α′​α0​=πx2​−x1​​=π2πRw​=2Rw⟹p~​=2α′​α0​​=2α′2α′​α0​​=2α′2Rw​=α′wR​=R~w​. So p~=w/R~\tilde p = w/\tilde Rp~​=w/R~ with w∈Zw\in\mathbb Zw∈Z: exactly momentum quantized in units of 1/R~1/\tilde R1/R~ on the dual circle. The Dirichlet stretch www (how many times the string wound the covering line between brane images) has become the momentum number on the dual circle — the mirror image of (5), where momentum nnn became stretch. Boundary condition, brane dimension, and the winding↔\leftrightarrow↔momentum roles all flip together, confirming Dp→p\top→D(p ⁣+ ⁣1)(p\!+\!1)(p+1) for a transverse circle.
