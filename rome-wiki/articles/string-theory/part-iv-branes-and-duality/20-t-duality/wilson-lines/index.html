    A vector potential wrapping a circle is a phase the circle can never shake off — an Aharonov–Bohm flux with no solenoid inside — and T-duality reveals that this phase was secretly a place: the point where the dual brane sits.

    A vector potential with no magnetic field that still matters

    Let the spatial direction xxx be a circle, x∼x+2πRx\sim x+2\pi Rx∼x+2πR, with only one nonzero gauge-field component, a constant AxA_xAx​ along it. The field strength vanishes everywhere, Fμν=∂μAν−∂νAμ=0,F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu = 0,Fμν​=∂μ​Aν​−∂ν​Aμ​=0, so there is no magnetic field anywhere. On a line, a pure-gauge AAA like this would be physically empty.

    The circle changes that. The gauge-invariant quantity is the holonomy around the loop: ω≡q∮dx Ax,W≡exp⁡ ⁣(iq∮dx Ax)=eiω.\omega \equiv q\oint dx\,A_x,\qquad W\equiv \exp\!\Big(iq\oint dx\,A_x\Big)=e^{i\omega}.ω≡q∮dxAx​,W≡exp(iq∮dxAx​)=eiω. WWW is the Wilson line. On a non-compact line the loop bounds a surface, and Stokes' theorem with F=0F=0F=0 forces ω\omegaω to vanish. On a circle there is no bounding surface, so ∮Ax dx\oint A_x\,dx∮Ax​dx can be nonzero while F≡0F\equiv 0F≡0. The circle remembers the gauge field as a phase — exactly the Aharonov–Bohm effect, where an electron circling a solenoid picks up a phase from a magnetic flux it never touches. Here there isn't even a solenoid; the flux lives in a region that doesn't exist.

    (The symbol ω\omegaω denotes only this gauge holonomy. It is not the winding momentum w=mR/α′w=mR/\alpha'w=mR/α′ of Winding modes.)

    Why the holonomy is an angle

    Under a gauge transformation Ax→Ax+∂xχA_x\to A_x+\partial_x\chiAx​→Ax​+∂x​χ, the holonomy shifts by the total change in χ\chiχ around the loop: ω→ω+q(χ(x0+2πR)−χ(x0)).\omega \to \omega + q\big(\chi(x_0+2\pi R)-\chi(x_0)\big).ω→ω+q(χ(x0​+2πR)−χ(x0​)). So whether ω\omegaω is physical hinges on one question: must χ\chiχ come back to itself after one lap?

    The naive answer, χ(x+2πR)=χ(x)\chi(x+2\pi R)=\chi(x)χ(x+2πR)=χ(x), would make ω\omegaω flatly gauge-invariant. It is wrong. The object that acts on charged states is not χ\chiχ but the phase U=eiqχU=e^{iq\chi}U=eiqχ: a charged wavefunction transforms as ψ→eiqχψ\psi\to e^{iq\chi}\psiψ→eiqχψ, and that is what makes the Schrödinger operator transform covariantly, \Big(\tfrac1i\partial_x-q(A_x+\partial_x\chi)\Big)\big(e^{iq\chi}\psi\big)=e^{iq\chi}\Big(\tfrac1i\partial_x-qA_x\Big)\psi. \tag{1} The transformed wavefunction solves the transformed equation at the same energy, so the physical gauge transformation is the single-valued phase UUU, not χ\chiχ itself. To check (1), expand ∂x(eiqχψ)=eiqχ(iq χ′ψ+ψ′)\partial_x(e^{iq\chi}\psi)=e^{iq\chi}(iq\,\chi'\psi+\psi')∂x​(eiqχψ)=eiqχ(iqχ′ψ+ψ′); the two qχ′q\chi'qχ′ terms cancel.

    What must be single-valued is therefore UUU, not χ\chiχ: U(x+2πR)=U(x) ⟹ q(χ(x+2πR)−χ(x))=2πm,m∈Z.U(x+2\pi R)=U(x)\ \Longrightarrow\ q\big(\chi(x+2\pi R)-\chi(x)\big)=2\pi m,\quad m\in\mathbb Z.U(x+2πR)=U(x) ⟹ q(χ(x+2πR)−χ(x))=2πm,m∈Z. So χ\chiχ need only be quasi-periodic — it may jump by an integer multiple of 2π/q2\pi/q2π/q per lap. Feeding this back, ω→ω+2πm.\omega \to \omega + 2\pi m.ω→ω+2πm. The holonomy is defined only modulo 2π2\pi2π: it is an angle. Physically inequivalent constant gauge fields are labeled by \boxed{\;\theta \equiv \omega\!\!\pmod{2\pi}=q\oint dx\,A_x\in[0,2\pi).\;} \tag{2} For a constant AxA_xAx​ on a circle of circumference 2πR2\pi R2πR, (2) reads \boxed{\;qA_x=\frac{\theta}{2\pi R}.\;} \tag{3} The factor is fixed by units: θ\thetaθ is dimensionless, so dividing by the circumference 2πR2\pi R2πR gives inverse length — the units of qAxqA_xqAx​, and of momentum.

    What the Wilson line does: it shifts momentum

    Put a charged particle of mass μ\muμ on the circle. Its Schrödinger equation, with the minimal coupling 1i∂x→1i∂x−qAx\tfrac1i\partial_x\to\tfrac1i\partial_x-qA_xi1​∂x​→i1​∂x​−qAx​, is 12μ(1i∂x−qAx)2ψ=Eψ,qAx=θ2πR.\frac{1}{2\mu}\Big(\frac1i\partial_x-qA_x\Big)^2\psi=E\psi,\qquad qA_x=\frac{\theta}{2\pi R}.2μ1​(i1​∂x​−qAx​)2ψ=Eψ,qAx​=2πRθ​. Single-valuedness still forces ψℓ∼eiℓx/R\psi_\ell\sim e^{i\ell x/R}ψℓ​∼eiℓx/R with ℓ∈Z\ell\in\mathbb Zℓ∈Z — the Wilson line does not touch the boundary condition on ψ\psiψ. It shows up only inside the covariant derivative, giving energies \boxed{\;E_\ell=\frac{1}{2\mu}\Big(\frac{\ell}{R}-\frac{\theta}{2\pi R}\Big)^2.\;} \tag{4} The effective momentum is shifted, ℓ/R→ℓ/R−θ/(2πR)\ell/R\to \ell/R-\theta/(2\pi R)ℓ/R→ℓ/R−θ/(2πR): clean integer momenta become fractional. This is real physics — it splits the ±ℓ\pm\ell±ℓ degeneracy for any θ≠0\theta\neq0θ=0. It also re-confirms that θ\thetaθ is an angle: the set of levels is unchanged under θ→θ+2π\theta\to\theta+2\piθ→θ+2π once you relabel ℓ→ℓ+1\ell\to\ell+1ℓ→ℓ+1.

    Why θ\thetaθ is a brane position

    This is what puts the Wilson line in a T-duality module. Take a Dppp-brane wrapping the circle, carrying a Wilson line with q∮Ax dx=θq\oint A_x\,dx=\thetaq∮Ax​dx=θ, and T-dualize. From Dp to D(p minus 1) under T-duality the brane becomes a D(p ⁣− ⁣1)(p\!-\!1)(p−1)-brane sitting at a point on the dual circle of radius R~=α′/R\tilde R=\alpha'/RR~=α′/R. That point is the one at angle θ\thetaθ: the gauge phase becomes a literal location, arc-length coordinate y=θ R~y=\theta\,\tilde Ry=θR~, read modulo the circumference 2πR~2\pi\tilde R2πR~.

    On the original brane, how much gauge field is smeared around the loop is invisible as a field — undetectable locally, only as a phase. T-duality turns the loop inside out: winding becomes position, and the phase you couldn't see becomes the spot where the dualized brane sits. A quantity with no local meaning on one side is pure geometry on the other.

    Three checks that the dictionary is right:

    • Periodicity. A brane at angle θ\thetaθ equals a brane at θ+2π\theta+2\piθ+2π — the same identification the Wilson line already obeys.
    • A neutral string feels nothing. For a string with both endpoints on the same brane, the two endpoints carry opposite charge, so the holonomy contributions cancel: p→p−qA+qA=pp\to p-qA+qA=pp→p−qA+qA=p. Correspondingly, a single brane's absolute position never affects strings ending on it. Only differences can matter.
    • Two branes, and only the difference counts. Put the negatively charged endpoint on brane 1 (angle θ1\theta_1θ1​) and the positive endpoint on brane 2 (angle θ2\theta_2θ2​). The momentum shifts by qA1−qA2qA_1-qA_2qA1​−qA2​: ℓR⟼ℓR−θ22πR+θ12πR=2πℓ−(θ2−θ1)2πR.\frac{\ell}{R}\longmapsto \frac{\ell}{R}-\frac{\theta_2}{2\pi R}+\frac{\theta_1}{2\pi R}=\frac{2\pi\ell-(\theta_2-\theta_1)}{2\pi R}.Rℓ​⟼Rℓ​−2πRθ2​​+2πRθ1​​=2πR2πℓ−(θ2​−θ1​)​. Only the relative angle θ2−θ1\theta_2-\theta_1θ2​−θ1​ survives — just as only the relative brane position matters after T-duality. The open-string mass formula becomes M2=(2πℓ−(θ2−θ1)2πR)2+1α′(N⊥−1),M^2=\Big(\frac{2\pi\ell-(\theta_2-\theta_1)}{2\pi R}\Big)^2+\frac{1}{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp-1\big),M2=(2πR2πℓ−(θ2​−θ1​)​)2+α′1​(N⊥−1), and for the lowest sector ℓ=0\ell=0ℓ=0, M^2=\Big(\frac{\theta_2-\theta_1}{2\pi R}\Big)^2+\frac{1}{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp-1\big). \tag{5}

    One normalization check seals the dictionary. On the dual circle the two branes are separated by arc length L=∣θ2−θ1∣ R~L=|\theta_2-\theta_1|\,\tilde RL=∣θ2​−θ1​∣R~. A fundamental string stretched between them costs energy = tension ×\times× length, with tension T0=1/(2πα′)T_0=1/(2\pi\alpha')T0​=1/(2πα′): (LT0)2=(∣θ2−θ1∣ α′R⋅12πα′)2=(θ2−θ12πR)2.(LT_0)^2=\Big(|\theta_2-\theta_1|\,\frac{\alpha'}{R}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\Big)^2=\Big(\frac{\theta_2-\theta_1}{2\pi R}\Big)^2.(LT0​)2=(∣θ2​−θ1​∣Rα′​⋅2πα′1​)2=(2πRθ2​−θ1​​)2. The α′\alpha'α′ cancels exactly, matching the ℓ=0\ell=0ℓ=0 term of (5). The stretched-string mass computed from gauge theory — a Wilson-line difference — equals the one computed from geometry — a physical brane separation. That agreement is the proof that θ\thetaθ is the angular position of the dual brane.

    Open questions

    • For coincident branes the holonomy becomes matrix-valued (non-abelian Wilson line); θ\thetaθ generalizes to the eigenvalues of a matrix, and the dual picture is a stack of branes at those eigenvalue positions. This is beyond the abelian case here.
    • In the dynamical theory, Wilson-line moduli and transverse brane positions land in the same open-string gauge multiplet after T-duality. This note fixes the background brane; Born–Infeld dynamics comes later.

    Exercise

    A Dppp-brane wraps the circle with Wilson-line angle θ\thetaθ; a second, identical brane wraps it with angle θ′=θ+π\theta'=\theta+\piθ′=θ+π. (a) Where do the two dual D(p ⁣− ⁣1)(p\!-\!1)(p−1)-branes sit relative to each other on the dual circle? (b) Using (5), find the mass of the lightest string stretched between them (take N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1, for which the oscillator term 1α′(N⊥−1)\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}(N^\perp-1)α′1​(N⊥−1) vanishes, isolating the pure stretch energy), and express it as tension ×\times× length to confirm the geometry. (c) If instead both endpoints sat on the same brane, what would this mass be, and why?

    Solution

    (a) The dual positions are the angles themselves, θ\thetaθ and θ+π\theta+\piθ+π. They differ by π\piπ: the two branes sit at antipodal points (diametrically opposite) on the dual circle of radius R~=α′/R\tilde R=\alpha'/RR~=α′/R.

    (b) Here θ′−θ=π\theta'-\theta=\piθ′−θ=π. From (5) with N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1 the oscillator term 1α′(N⊥−1)\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}(N^\perp-1)α′1​(N⊥−1) vanishes, leaving M2=(θ′−θ2πR)2=(π2πR)2=14R2,M=12R.M^2=\Big(\frac{\theta'-\theta}{2\pi R}\Big)^2=\Big(\frac{\pi}{2\pi R}\Big)^2=\frac{1}{4R^2}, \qquad M=\frac{1}{2R}.M2=(2πRθ′−θ​)2=(2πRπ​)2=4R21​,M=2R1​. Geometry check: antipodal points are separated by half the circumference, L=πR~=πα′/RL=\pi\tilde R=\pi\alpha'/RL=πR~=πα′/R. With tension T0=1/(2πα′)T_0=1/(2\pi\alpha')T0​=1/(2πα′), LT0=πα′R⋅12πα′=12R=M. ✓LT_0=\pi\frac{\alpha'}{R}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}=\frac{1}{2R}=M.\ \checkmarkLT0​=πRα′​⋅2πα′1​=2R1​=M. ✓ The α′\alpha'α′ cancels and the two routes agree — a stretched string across half the dual circle. (The genuine ground state is N⊥=0N^\perp=0N⊥=0, with M2=14R2−1α′M^2=\tfrac{1}{4R^2}-\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}M2=4R21​−α′1​ — lighter, and tachyonic when R>α′/2R>\sqrt{\alpha'}/2R>α′​/2.     (c) Both endpoints on one brane means θ′=θ\theta'=\thetaθ′=θ, so the relative angle is zero and the M2M^2M2 term (θ′−θ2πR)2\big(\tfrac{\theta'-\theta}{2\pi R}\big)^2(2πRθ′−θ​)2 vanishes: M2=1α′(N⊥−1)M^2=\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}(N^\perp-1)M2=α′1​(N⊥−1), giving M=0M=0M=0 at N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1. Physically the string has zero length to stretch — its two endpoints sit on the same dual brane — and, because the endpoints carry opposite charge, the single brane's Wilson line cancels out entirely (p→p−qA+qA=pp\to p-qA+qA=pp→p−qA+qA=p). Only relative angles are ever physical.
