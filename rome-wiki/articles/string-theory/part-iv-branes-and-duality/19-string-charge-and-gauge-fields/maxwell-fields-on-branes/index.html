    Maxwell fields on branes

    Every D-brane carries its own photon — a Maxwell field A_m living only on the brane. When a string ends on the brane, this photon's electric field lines carry away the string charge that the string was transporting, and gauge invariance forces F and the background B to appear only in the combination \mathcal F = F + B .

    The puzzle from the endpoint

    Endpoint charge left a puzzle: string charge flows down a string like current in a wire, but an open string ends on a D-brane, and current cannot just stop. The resolution is that the brane has its own electromagnetism and the endpoint is a point charge in it. The current does not vanish at the endpoint — it converts into brane electric flux and keeps flowing, now spread across the worldvolume. Picture a wire soldered to a metal sheet: the current fans out as a surface current on the sheet rather than disappearing at the joint.

    Two facts do the work:

    1. The brane carries a photon, which drops out of quantizing the open string.
    2. That photon's field strength is not gauge invariant on its own; only F=F+B\mathcal F = F + BF=F+B is. The F⋅BF\cdot BF⋅B cross term in the brane Lagrangian is what ties electric field lines to string charge.

    Where the brane photon comes from

    Quantize an open string whose endpoints lie on a Dppp-brane. Its lowest massless state is α−1m ∣0;p⟩\alpha_{-1}^m\,|0;p\rangleα−1m​∣0;p⟩ — a single oscillator acting on the ground state ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ (momentum ppp, no oscillators), carrying one spacetime index mmm. Because the endpoints obey Neumann conditions only along the brane, that index runs over the (p+1)(p{+}1)(p+1) worldvolume directions, and a one-index massless field is a Maxwell gauge field AmA_mAm​.

    The closed string handed us the bulk fields — graviton gμνg_{\mu\nu}gμν​, Kalb–Ramond BμνB_{\mu\nu}Bμν​, dilaton — that fill all of spacetime. The open string hands us a gauge field trapped on the brane: this photon cannot roam, it lives only where the brane is. Every D-brane is a little universe with its own electromagnetism.

    FFF alone is not gauge invariant on a brane

    Two independent gauge redundancies now act on the brane. Keep them separate:

    • Ordinary Maxwell, Am↦Am+∂mλA_m \mapsto A_m + \partial_m\lambdaAm​↦Am​+∂m​λ, with parameter λ\lambdaλ. This leaves Fmn=∂mAn−∂nAmF_{mn}=\partial_m A_n - \partial_n A_mFmn​=∂m​An​−∂n​Am​ untouched, as always.
    • The bulk BBB-field redundancy, inherited from Endpoint charge, with parameter Λm\Lambda_mΛm​. Restoring gauge invariance for the string endpoint forced AmA_mAm​ to shift when BBB shifts:

    \delta B_{mn}=\partial_m\Lambda_n-\partial_n\Lambda_m,\qquad \delta A_m=-\Lambda_m. \tag{1}

    The second rule is the surprise: the brane photon must transform under the bulk two-form's gauge symmetry. Watch what that does to the ordinary field strength:

    \delta F_{mn}=\partial_m\delta A_n-\partial_n\delta A_m = -\partial_m\Lambda_n+\partial_n\Lambda_m = -\delta B_{mn}. \tag{2}

    Under the BBB-symmetry FFF is not invariant — it shifts by exactly −δB-\delta B−δB. The familiar slogan "FFF is gauge invariant" holds only for the ordinary Maxwell parameter λ\lambdaλ, not for this second redundancy. The minus sign in (2) is what makes F+BF+BF+B invariant:

    \delta(F_{mn}+B_{mn})=0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;\mathcal F_{mn}\equiv F_{mn}+B_{mn},\qquad \delta\mathcal F_{mn}=0\;} \tag{3}

    F\mathcal FF is the physically meaningful field strength on the brane: every FFF in the brane's Maxwell theory gets replaced by F+BF+BF+B. When the background has B=0B=0B=0, F=F\mathcal F=FF=F and ordinary electromagnetism is recovered, so the modification is invisible in most settings even though it carries the whole story. The same F\mathcal FF reappears nonlinearly in the Born-Infeld action for brane dynamics.

    Electric field lines carry the string charge

    This resolves Endpoint charge's puzzle. The gauge-invariant brane Lagrangian density is the Maxwell density built from F\mathcal FF — its overall constant is fixed later by the string coupling and does not affect the structure. Expand it:

    -\tfrac14\,\mathcal F^{mn}\mathcal F_{mn} = -\tfrac14 F^{mn}F_{mn} -\tfrac14 B^{mn}B_{mn} -\tfrac12 F^{mn}B_{mn}. \tag{4}

    The coefficient −12-\tfrac12−21​ on the cross term is not a typo: (F+B)2(F+B)^2(F+B)2 produces two equal cross terms F ⁣⋅ ⁣BF\!\cdot\!BF⋅B and B ⁣⋅ ⁣FB\!\cdot\!FB⋅F, each −14-\tfrac14−41​. That cross term carries the physics. Pull out its time–space part:

    -\tfrac12 F^{mn}B_{mn} = -F^{0k}B_{0k}+\cdots. \tag{5}

    (The block m=0,n=km=0,n=km=0,n=k and m=k,n=0m=k,n=0m=k,n=0 each give F0kB0kF^{0k}B_{0k}F0kB0k​ by antisymmetry, so −12⋅2=−1-\tfrac12 \cdot 2 = -1−21​⋅2=−1; the dots are the purely spatial FijBijF^{ij}B_{ij}FijBij​.)

    Here is the identification that closes the argument. Recall from Fundamental string charge that the string-charge density j0kj^{0k}j0k is defined as whatever couples to B0kB_{0k}B0k​ — it is the source on the right of the BBB field equation. In (5), the object multiplying B0kB_{0k}B0k​ on the brane is F0kF^{0k}F0k. In mostly-plus signature ημν=diag(−1,+1,…,+1)\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,\dots,+1)ημν​=diag(−1,+1,…,+1),

    F^{0k}=\eta^{00}\eta^{kk}F_{0k}=-F_{0k}=E_k, \tag{6}

    the ordinary electric field; the sign flip from η00=−1\eta^{00}=-1η00=−1 cancels the sign in F0k=−EkF_{0k}=-E_kF0k​=−Ek​. So F0kF^{0k}F0k is not a convention — the signature forces it to be the electric field. Chaining these together:

    Ek=F0k couples to B0k as string charge does⟹brane electric field lines carry string charge.E_k = F^{0k}\ \text{couples to}\ B_{0k}\ \text{as string charge does}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \textbf{brane electric field lines carry string charge}.Ek​=F0k couples to B0k​ as string charge does⟹brane electric field lines carry string charge.

    String-charge current and brane electric flux are the same thing at the charged endpoint, converting into each other. At the σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π endpoint (charge +1+1+1) the string current surfaces as electric flux fanning out inside the brane; at the σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 endpoint (charge −1-1−1) that flux converges and feeds the current back into the string. The oriented string runs from −-− to +++, the brane flux runs from +++ to −-−, and the loop closes. Because the photon lives only on the brane, the field lines cannot escape into the bulk — the continuation of string charge is confined to the worldvolume. That is why current can end on a brane without ever stopping.

    Open questions

    • The absolute normalization of the brane F2F^2F2 term — the constant that fixes the physical strength of the endpoint charges — depends on the string coupling and α′\alpha'α′, and is deferred to the later string-coupling discussion. The structure above is normalization-independent; only the magnitude of the charge is not.
    • The non-abelian generalization: NNN coincident branes carry a U(N)U(N)U(N) gauge field, not a single AmA_mAm​, which is where Standard Model gauge groups could come from. Not developed here — see D-branes and the Standard Model.
    • The full low-energy brane action is the Born-Infeld action, in which F\mathcal FF appears nonlinearly; this note needs only its quadratic Maxwell limit.

    Exercise

    The invariant field strength on a brane came out to F=F+B\mathcal F = F + BF=F+B with a plus sign. Suppose you had instead been careless and written the endpoint transformation rule in (1) as δAm=+Λm\delta A_m = +\Lambda_mδAm​=+Λm​. Redo the one-line computation (2) and determine which combination, F+BF+BF+B or F−BF-BF−B, is gauge invariant now. Then explain in one sentence why the actual sign in (1) is not free to choose — what fixes it?

    Solution

    With δAm=+Λm\delta A_m = +\Lambda_mδAm​=+Λm​, the field strength varies as δFmn=∂mδAn−∂nδAm=+∂mΛn−∂nΛm=+δBmn.\delta F_{mn}=\partial_m\delta A_n-\partial_n\delta A_m = +\partial_m\Lambda_n-\partial_n\Lambda_m = +\delta B_{mn}.δFmn​=∂m​δAn​−∂n​δAm​=+∂m​Λn​−∂n​Λm​=+δBmn​. Now δ(Fmn−Bmn)=δFmn−δBmn=0\delta(F_{mn}-B_{mn}) = \delta F_{mn}-\delta B_{mn}=0δ(Fmn​−Bmn​)=δFmn​−δBmn​=0, so the invariant combination would be F=F−B\mathcal F = F - BF=F−B. The sign of δAm\delta A_mδAm​ directly flips the relative sign of BBB in F\mathcal FF.

    But the sign is not free. It is fixed back in Endpoint charge: δAm=−Λm\delta A_m = -\Lambda_mδAm​=−Λm​ is precisely the choice that makes the endpoint coupling ±∫Am ∂τXm\pm\int A_m\,\partial_\tau X^m±∫Am​∂τ​Xm cancel the leftover boundary term ±∫Λm ∂τXm\pm\int \Lambda_m\,\partial_\tau X^m±∫Λm​∂τ​Xm in δSB\delta S_BδSB​. Choosing +Λm+\Lambda_m+Λm​ would double the boundary term instead of cancelling it, breaking gauge invariance of the full string action. So the plus sign in F=F+B\mathcal F = F + BF=F+B is inherited from the requirement that string charge be conserved at the endpoint — it is not a convention.
