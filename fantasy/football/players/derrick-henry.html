A goal-line back with the largest projected carry load in the file — enormous volume and touchdown equity, bought at the cost of the receiving points this format pays best.

The role

He is the goal-line back in Baltimore: the runner they hand it to inside the five, where a touchdown pays six points against a rushing yard's tenth of a point. He is not a three-down back — the passing-down work goes elsewhere, and the backup listed behind him on the same roster actually projects for more catches than he does. So his scoring is two things stacked: a very large pile of carries, and a share of his offense's short touchdowns.

The number

16 catches, from the note column of Data/players.csv — a projection dated 2026-07-23 from FFToday stat lines, not an observed total. In this league a reception is worth a full point, so that is about one point a game from the receiving side. Every other back on the board in this range collects a meaningful second income stream through the air; he collects almost none, and the case for him has to be carried entirely by carries and scores.

The risk

The risk column says low, which reflects the security of the role, not the shape of the weekly output. A player whose value concentrates in touchdowns has a wide range of outcomes, because touchdowns are rare events and rare events are noisy over a 17-game sample — see Touchdown variance. That variance is not automatically bad here: this league's prizes pay first place more than second and third combined, so ceiling is worth something from the middle rounds on. It is bad in weeks 1–14, where you need a high mean to finish top six. What the risk is not is availability: he is the only player in this pick window who has played every game of the last three seasons without appearing on the injury report once.

The context

He is 32.7 on the September 9 opener — the oldest player inside the first thirty picks of the market — and was the 45th pick of the 2016 draft, by Tennessee rather than Baltimore, so the signal in Draft capital dominance was spent by a team he no longer plays for. The availability record is the surprise: 17, 17 and 17 games across 2023–2025, every game his team played, and across three years of official injury reports never listed Out, never even Questionable, with no body part recorded at all. In 2025 he played only 54.5% of Baltimore's snaps yet took 307 carries against 21 targets — a concentrated early-down and goal-line role. The projection asks for 321 carries, more than he just had, in his age-33 season. He is paid $30 million, $25 million guaranteed, voiding 2028.
