    Two roads to D = 26

    Two independent derivations force the bosonic string into exactly 26 spacetime dimensions — one by fixing a spacetime gauge and demanding Lorentz symmetry survive, one by fixing a worldsheet gauge and demanding the Weyl anomaly cancel — and the same number falls out of both because they are two symptoms of one quantum defect.

    Underneath both roads is a single quantum defect: a would-be symmetry of the classical string that fails to close once we quantize. We can push the defect into the spacetime Lorentz algebra or into the worldsheet Weyl symmetry, but we cannot make it vanish except at D=26D=26D=26.

    This note ties together Faddeev-Popov ghosts, The bc ghost CFT, Total central charge and D = 26, the Light-cone gauge quantization of Modules 14–16, and the Light-cone Lorentz anomaly calculation whose result we quote below.

    Road 1 — Light-cone: fix a spacetime gauge, then demand Lorentz invariance back

    A string worldsheet has more gauge freedom than we need. Light-cone gauge spends all of it at once: set X+∝τX^+\propto\tauX+∝τ and use the constraints to solve for X−X^-X− entirely in terms of the D−2D-2D−2 transverse oscillators αnI\alpha^I_nαnI​. Nothing unphysical is left — no ghosts, no negative-norm states, a clean positive Fock space. The bill comes due elsewhere. Singling out the two null directions X±X^\pmX± hides manifest Lorentz invariance: rotating a transverse direction into X−X^-X− now acts on a complicated nonlinear function of the oscillators, so it is no longer obvious that boosts still close into the Lorentz algebra. Road 1 is the demand that they do.

    Step 1 — the zero-point energy. Quantizing reorders the transverse Hamiltonian L0⊥L_0^\perpL0⊥​ into normal-ordered form, and the reordering leaves a c-number: the sum of ground-state energies of all the transverse oscillators, a_{\text{lc}}=\frac{1}{2}(D-2)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac{1}{24}(D-2).\tag{1} The factor 12(D−2)\tfrac12(D-2)21​(D−2) counts 12\tfrac1221​ per oscillator times the D−2D-2D−2 transverse directions. The sum ∑n\sum n∑n is divergent and must be regulated honestly: with a cutoff, ∑n≥1n e−ϵn=1ϵ2−112+O(ϵ2),\sum_{n\ge1}n\,e^{-\epsilon n}=\frac{1}{\epsilon^2}-\frac{1}{12}+O(\epsilon^2),∑n≥1​ne−ϵn=ϵ21​−121​+O(ϵ2), and the 1/ϵ21/\epsilon^21/ϵ2 piece is a local (length-dependent) divergence removed by a counterterm, leaving the finite −112-\tfrac{1}{12}−121​.     Step 2 — the Lorentz anomaly. Build the quantum boost generators M−IM^{-I}M−I out of the oscillators (every hidden αn−\alpha_n^-αn−​ replaced by the transverse Virasoro operator Ln⊥/p+L_n^\perp/p^+Ln⊥​/p+, with the zero-mode term symmetrized so M−IM^{-I}M−I is Hermitian). The two brackets in CmC_mCm​ come from different sources: the mmm term is the Virasoro central charge of the D−2D-2D−2 transverse bosons leaking into the boost algebra; the 1/m1/m1/m term measures the mismatch between that ordering constant and the transverse zero-point value 124(D−2)\tfrac{1}{24}(D-2)241​(D−2).     Step 3 — the answer. The first equation fixes the dimension; the second then fixes the mass shift: D=26,alc=−124(D−2)=−2424=−1.\boxed{D=26},\qquad a_{\text{lc}}=-\frac{1}{24}(D-2)=-\frac{24}{24}=-1.D=26​,alc​=−241​(D−2)=−2424​=−1. With alc=−1a_{\text{lc}}=-1alc​=−1, the open-string formula α′M2=N⊥−1\alpha'M^2=N^\perp-1α′M2=N⊥−1 puts a massless vector (the photon) at N⊥=1N^\perp=1N⊥=1; the closed string's first level gives the massless graviton/BBB-field/dilaton. String theory does not accept DDD as input — it predicts D=26D=26D=26.

    Road 2 — Covariant: keep Lorentz manifest, pay with ghosts

    Now do the opposite. Keep all DDD coordinates XμX^\muXμ so Lorentz invariance stays manifest, and spend the gauge freedom on the worldsheet symmetries instead — the diffeomorphisms and Weyl rescalings of the Polyakov metric. Gauge-fixing the metric integral is not free: the Faddeev–Popov change of variables produces a Jacobian, and that Jacobian is itself a path integral over anticommuting ghost fields b,cb,cb,c. Now the defect has nowhere to hide in the Lorentz algebra (which is manifest) — it resurfaces as a quantum anomaly in the Weyl symmetry.

    Step 1 — ghosts from gauge-fixing. The FP procedure (Faddeev-Popov ghosts) replaces the metric Jacobian with a free action for a symmetric-traceless anticommuting field bαβb_{\alpha\beta}bαβ​ (from the gauge-fixing multiplier) and a vector cαc^\alphacα (from the diffeomorphism parameter). In conformal gauge it factorizes into holomorphic and antiholomorphic copies of Sghost=12π∫d2z  b ∂zˉc + (antihol.).S_{\text{ghost}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^2z\;b\,\partial_{\bar z}c\ +\ \text{(antihol.)}.Sghost​=2π1​∫d2zb∂zˉ​c + (antihol.).

    Step 2 — the ghost central charge. The bcbcbc system (The bc ghost CFT) is a CFT with weights (hb,hc)=(2,−1)(h_b,h_c)=(2,-1)(hb​,hc​)=(2,−1) — forced by the index structure bzz,czb_{zz},c^zbzz​,cz — and its TTTTTT OPE has a quartic pole −13/(z−w)4-13/(z-w)^4−13/(z−w)4, i.e. cgh=−26.c_{\text{gh}}=-26.cgh​=−26. It is negative: anticommuting integer-weight fields are exactly the objects that violate the unitarity bound c>0c>0c>0, so there is no contradiction. This −26-26−26 is not "minus two coordinates worth of central charge." It is the anomaly coefficient of a specific first-order Grassmann system whose weights were dictated by what it replaced (a rank-2 symmetric-traceless tensor and a vector). "Ghosts remove two gauge directions" is a slogan, not this number.

    Step 3 — cancel the Weyl anomaly. On a curved worldsheet the trace of the stress tensor picks up a quantum piece ⟨Tαα⟩=−c12R,c=cmatter+cgh.\langle T^\alpha{}_\alpha\rangle=-\frac{c}{12}R,\qquad c=c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}.⟨Tαα​⟩=−12c​R,c=cmatter​+cgh​. Weyl symmetry is a gauge redundancy of the Polyakov string, and a gauge anomaly is fatal — it means the degrees of freedom you divided out do not actually decouple. So we need c=0c=0c=0. The matter sector is DDD free scalars, one per coordinate, so cmatter=Dc_{\text{matter}}=Dcmatter​=D, and (Total central charge and D = 26) ctotal=D−26=!0⟹D=26.c_{\text{total}}=D-26\overset{!}{=}0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\boxed{D=26}.ctotal​=D−26=!0⟹D=26​. The dimension is the value that makes the conformal anomaly cancel between matter and ghosts. More precisely, 262626 is a critical central charge, and "dimension" is merely the name it takes when the c=26c=26c=26 CFT happens to be 262626 free scalars — any c=26c=26c=26 CFT is an equally valid string background.

    Why the two roads agree

    The agreement is not luck. Both roads are computing the same number in different disguises.

    Same constraints underneath. In both pictures the physics is the Virasoro constraints. Light-cone gauge solves them (for X−X^-X−, keeping D−2D-2D−2 transverse towers). Covariant gauge imposes them as operator conditions on states, Ln>0∣Ψ⟩=0L_{n>0}|\Psi\rangle=0Ln>0​∣Ψ⟩=0 and (L0−1)∣Ψ⟩=0(L_0-1)|\Psi\rangle=0(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0. The single intercept appears in two costumes:

    • light-cone: α′M2=N⊥+alc\alpha'M^2=N^\perp+a_{\text{lc}}α′M2=N⊥+alc​ with alc=−1a_{\text{lc}}=-1alc​=−1;
    • covariant: (L0−1)∣Ψ⟩=0(L_0-1)|\Psi\rangle=0(L0​−1)∣Ψ⟩=0, so a physical matter operator must have weight +1+1+1 (and (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) for closed strings; see Vertex operators and physical-state conditions).

    The counting matches — carefully. In the covariant Hilbert space the matter sector has DDD oscillator towers, of which two (X+X^+X+ and X−X^-X−, or equivalently a timelike and a longitudinal combination) are unphysical. The bcbcbc ghosts are precisely engineered so that their contribution to the anomaly, together with these two unphysical matter directions, cancels; what carries physical states is the same transverse count as light-cone gauge. At the level of central charge this reads cmatter+cgh=D−26,c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}=D-26,cmatter​+cgh​=D−26, and D−26=0D-26=0D−26=0 is the same statement as alc=−124(D−2)=−124⋅24=−1.a_{\text{lc}}=-\frac{1}{24}(D-2)=-\frac{1}{24}\cdot 24=-1.alc​=−241​(D−2)=−241​⋅24=−1. Both say "242424 transverse oscillators." One caveat keeps this honest: turning "two unphysical matter directions" into "cgh=−26c_{\text{gh}}=-26cgh​=−26" is not a naive subtraction (−26≠−2-26\neq -2−26=−2); it goes through the weights (2,−1)(2,-1)(2,−1) of the specific ghost system, and the clean statement of the cancellation is the BRST cohomology theorem, deferred below.

    Lorentz anomaly ⇔\Leftrightarrow⇔ Weyl anomaly. This is the core equivalence: there is one obstruction to consistently quantizing the string, and the gauge choice only decides where it surfaces. Light-cone gauge fixes the Weyl symmetry away by hand, so the obstruction cannot appear there — it is forced to resurface as a Lorentz anomaly in the boost algebra. Covariant gauge keeps Lorentz manifest, so the obstruction has nowhere to go but the Weyl (conformal) anomaly. Same defect, two gauges, two names. Both are healed at, and only at, D=26D=26D=26.

    Fixing all the gauge freedom the cheap way breaks a spacetime symmetry; fixing it the honest way breaks a worldsheet symmetry. Exactly one dimension — twenty-six — breaks neither, and that is where the bosonic string has to live.

    Open questions

    • The boost-commutator coefficient (2) is quoted, not expanded term-by-term; the full oscillator bookkeeping lives in Light-cone Lorentz anomaly.
    • "Ghosts cancel two unphysical matter directions" is stated here as arithmetic. The rigorous version — that physical states are the BRST cohomology, and that this is gauge-independent — is deeper than the central-charge match and belongs with BRST (revisit in Module 24 / old covariant vs. BRST quantization).

    Exercise

    Both roads secretly agree that D=26D=26D=26 leaves D−2=24D-2=24D−2=24 physical transverse oscillators. Superstring warm-up: repeat both consistency conditions for the superstring, where each coordinate XμX^\muXμ is accompanied by a worldsheet fermion ψμ\psi^\muψμ, and predict DDD.

    Use two facts: a single free worldsheet fermion has c=12c=\tfrac12c=21​, and the superstring's diffeomorphism+superconformal gauge-fixing produces a ghost system with total central charge cgh=−15c_{\text{gh}}=-15cgh​=−15 (the bcbcbc ghosts at −26-26−26 plus a commuting βγ\beta\gammaβγ superghost system at +11+11+11). Find DDD from the anomaly-cancellation condition cmatter+cgh=0c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}=0cmatter​+cgh​=0, and state the superstring analogue of "D−2=24D-2=24D−2=24."

    Solution

    Central-charge road. Each coordinate contributes one free boson (c=1c=1c=1) and one free fermion (c=12c=\tfrac12c=21​), so the matter central charge per dimension is 1+12=321+\tfrac12=\tfrac321+21​=23​, giving cmatter=32D.c_{\text{matter}}=\frac{3}{2}D.cmatter​=23​D. Weyl (now super-Weyl) invariance demands cmatter+cgh=0c_{\text{matter}}+c_{\text{gh}}=0cmatter​+cgh​=0 with cgh=−15c_{\text{gh}}=-15cgh​=−15: 32D−15=0⟹D=10.\frac{3}{2}D-15=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{D=10}.23​D−15=0⟹D=10​. The analogue of "D−2=24D-2=24D−2=24." In light-cone gauge the transverse count is D−2=8D-2=8D−2=8, and each transverse direction carries a boson and a fermion. The critical central charge 151515 splits as 32⋅10\tfrac32\cdot 1023​⋅10, mirroring the bosonic 26=1⋅2626=1\cdot 2626=1⋅26; the "twenty-four transverse bosons" of the bosonic string become "eight transverse bosons plus eight transverse fermions" for the superstring. Both roads again agree — the superstring lives in D=10D=10D=10. (Sanity check on the ghost number: bosonic bcbcbc gives −26-26−26; adding the commuting superghost βγ\beta\gammaβγ system with c=+11c=+11c=+11 gives −15-15−15, so the superstring needs less matter, 32D=15\tfrac32 D=1523​D=15 vs D=26D=26D=26, and lands lower.)
