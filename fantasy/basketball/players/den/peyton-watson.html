Team rights: Denver Nuggets · Position: SG/SF/PF · Age during season: 24

Reported baseline

Watson broke out over 54 games (40 starts), averaging 14.6 points, 4.9 rebounds, 2.1 assists, 1.5 threes, 0.9 steals and 1.1 blocks in 29.6 minutes on 49.1% FG and 73.0% FT.1 Denver issued a qualifying offer, making him a restricted free agent, but he remained unsigned at the July 18 cutoff.2 A right hamstring strain kept him out for the entire first-round playoff series.3

ESPN's dynamic snapshot ranked him only 239th standard but 136th roto, a sensible divergence because blocks are more valuable in categories than many generic points systems.4

The upside was visible before the season ended: NBA Fantasy's January High Score list moved Watson from No. 115 to No. 90 after a month above 34 fantasy points per game, including a 22/6/3 line with two stocks while Jokić was out. That is evidence of the injury-created ceiling, not a forecast of his healthy-team role.5

Fantasy profile

  • Helps: blocks from a wing, usable FG%, rebounds, and enough threes/steals to create a multi-category path.
  • Hurts: FT% and modest assists; usage can collapse behind four established starters.
  • Floor: bench role below 22 MPG or a slow hamstring ramp, outside top 200.
  • Ceiling: top 75 if he receives 31-plus minutes and holds 40% three-point shooting near one block/steal each.

Role, market, and health uncertainty

February fantasy-community discussion treated Watson as must-roster during his hot stretch but correctly identified Gordon, Johnson and Braun returning as the usage/minutes test; that is sentiment, not evidence that a 40-fantasy-point run is sustainable.6 Denver said it wanted him long-term, while July reporting discussed matching offers and sign-and-trade possibilities.7 NBC Sports reported July 17 that Milwaukee had joined the Clippers and Hawks as potential sign-and-trade suitors; interest is not a completed move.8 Hoops Rumors reported the same day that Denver values Watson "significantly," believes it is positioned well to retain him, and is comfortable letting the restricted process run all offseason, noting that all three interested teams are over the cap and would need a sign-and-trade.9 Roster confidence medium-high, minutes medium, rate medium, availability medium-low.

Correction 2026-07-18: roster confidence raised from low to medium-high and minutes from low to medium, and the team desk's primary 240-minute model now carries Watson at 26 minutes rather than holding him in a conditional table. The prior read treated an unsigned RFA as a coin flip; the July 17 Hoops Rumors report that Denver expects to keep him, combined with the cap position of every reported suitor, makes retention the modal outcome. This is a change in belief about an unresolved situation, not a reported signing — Watson was still unsigned at the cutoff. https://www.hoopsrumors.com/2026/07/nuggets-rumors-watson-joerger-roddy-diallo.html

Career record

Pre-NBA: Long Beach Polytechnic HS (23.2 PTS, 8.0 REB as a junior; Moore League MVP, McDonald's All-American). One season at UCLA (2021-22): 32 games, 12.7 MPG, 3.3 PTS, 2.9 REB, 0.8 AST, 0.6 BLK on .322/.226/.688 — a bench freshman who was drafted on tools rather than production, with the article record noting he arrived out of shape after a COVID-disrupted senior high school season. Draft: 2022, first round, No. 30 overall, Oklahoma City Thunder; traded on draft night to Denver with two future second-rounders for JaMychal Green and a protected 2027 first. He also spent his rookie year developing in the G League with the Grand Rapids Gold, averaging 19.0 PTS, 7.5 REB, 2.9 AST and 1.7 STL in 32.7 minutes over 15 games.

Three-point percentage by season: .429 on negligible volume (2022-23), .296, .353, .411. No season split across two teams — Watson has played only for Denver.

Fantasy history: verifiable season-end nine-category ranks could not be located for any Watson season, so none is asserted. Two in-season data points are on the record and are cited above in this dossier: NBA Fantasy's High Score list moved him from No. 115 to No. 90 in January 2026 during a stretch above 34 fantasy points per game, and ESPN's July 2026 forward-looking feed had him 239th standard against 136th roto. That gap is the whole profile — the blocks and steals that carry him in categories are close to worthless in most points formats, and the poor FT% that categories punish is largely invisible there. Before 2025-26 he was a specialist streamer whose only standalone category was blocks; 2025-26 is the first year the counting stats were broad enough to hold roster spots on merit, and it was cut short.

Durability: 23, 80, 68, 54. The rookie total is a development choice, not health — he was on G League assignment. The 2023-24 season of 80 games is real evidence he can hold up. The 2025-26 total is the problem: 28 games missed, driven by a Grade 2 right hamstring strain suffered against New York on 2026-02-05 that cost him 19 straight games across February and March, then a re-aggravation against Utah in early April after only five games back, which ended his regular season and kept him out of the entire first-round series against Minnesota. One report frames it as missing 31 of Denver's final 36 games, which does not reconcile with a 54-game total in an 82-game season; the 28-games-missed figure from the GP column is the number to trust. A re-aggravated Grade 2 hamstring is the specific injury type with the highest recurrence rate, and he had not been cleared to a normal offseason program as of the July 18 cutoff.

Trajectory: The per-minute story is genuinely better than the raw progression, which matters because the raw progression looks like a pure minutes ramp. Minutes went 8.1 to 18.6 to 24.4 to 29.6 and points went 3.3 to 6.7 to 8.1 to 14.6 — but the 2025-26 scoring jump outran the minutes increase by a wide margin, which means efficiency and role changed rather than just opportunity. The mechanism is identifiable: three-point rate and accuracy both moved (0.7 makes at .353 to 1.5 makes at .411) while FG% still rose to .491, and free-throw shooting climbed for the fourth straight year from .550 to .730. That is a shooting development curve, not noise, though a 54-game sample at .411 from three is exactly the size where regression toward his .361 career mark should be assumed. The one category that went backwards is the one that made him rosterable before — blocks fell from 1.4 to 1.1 despite five more minutes, consistent with a move to more perimeter defensive assignments. At 23 he is squarely pre-peak and every skill trend points up, so 2026-27 is a bet on two things unrelated to talent: whether the hamstring holds, and whether the restricted free agency resolves into a job with 29-plus minutes rather than a return to the fourth-option wing slot behind Gordon, Johnson and Braun.
