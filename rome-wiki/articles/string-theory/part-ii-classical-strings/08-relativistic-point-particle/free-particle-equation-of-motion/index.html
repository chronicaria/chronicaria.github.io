    Free particle equation of motion

    Extremizing the geometric action S=-mc\int ds gives dp_\mu/d\tau=0 for any worldline parameter — a free particle's four-momentum is frozen along its worldline — and in the proper-length gauge this reads d^2x^\mu/ds^2=0 , the relativistic Newton's first law.

    Straightness from extremal length

    A free particle sweeps out a worldline, and its action measures one thing: the proper length ∫ds\int ds∫ds of that curve (equivalently the proper time ∫ds/c\int ds/c∫ds/c its onboard clock ticks off). Nature picks the worldline that extremizes this length, and the extremal worldline turns out to be straight — where "straight in spacetime" is exactly the statement that the four-momentum never changes. That is Newton's first law — a free body moves in a straight line at constant velocity — written so that every inertial observer sees the same equation.

    One deliberate choice before the algebra: we refuse to privilege time as the clock ticking off the motion. We use an arbitrary label τ\tauτ along the curve, so space and time enter symmetrically, and the answer comes out parameter-blind — it holds no matter which ruler we lay along the worldline. Why the parameter drops out is proved once and for all in Reparameterization invariance; this note is the derivation half of that story.

    Vary the action

    Deform the worldline by δxμ(τ)\delta x^\mu(\tau)δxμ(τ), pinning the endpoints. The action changes by

    \delta S = -mc\int \delta(ds). \tag{1}

    We need δ(ds)\delta(ds)δ(ds). It is cleaner to vary ds2ds^2ds2, then divide. Starting from ds2=−ημν dxμdxνds^2 = -\eta_{\mu\nu}\,dx^\mu dx^\nuds2=−ημν​dxμdxν (mostly-plus signature; the proper-length element from Proper length and proper time),

    2 ds δ(ds)=−ημν δ(dxμdxν)=−2 ημν δ(dxμ) dxν.2\,ds\,\delta(ds) = -\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\delta(dx^\mu dx^\nu) = -2\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\delta(dx^\mu)\,dx^\nu.2dsδ(ds)=−ημν​δ(dxμdxν)=−2ημν​δ(dxμ)dxν.

    Two factors of 2 appear and cancel. On the left, δ(ds2)=2 ds δ(ds)\delta(ds^2)=2\,ds\,\delta(ds)δ(ds2)=2dsδ(ds) by the chain rule. On the right, varying the product gives δ(dxμ) dxν+dxμ δ(dxν)\delta(dx^\mu)\,dx^\nu + dx^\mu\,\delta(dx^\nu)δ(dxμ)dxν+dxμδ(dxν), and these two terms are equal after contracting with the symmetric ημν\eta_{\mu\nu}ημν​ — so they combine into a single factor of 2.

    Dividing by 2 ds2\,ds2ds,

    \delta(ds) = -\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\delta(dx^\mu)\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}. \tag{2}

    The one lemma: variation commutes with ddd

    \delta(dx^\mu) = d(\delta x^\mu). \tag{3}

    The plain-language version: the wiggle in a small step equals the small step of the wiggle. Proof: a step is a difference, dxμ=xμ(τ+dτ)−xμ(τ)dx^\mu = x^\mu(\tau+d\tau)-x^\mu(\tau)dxμ=xμ(τ+dτ)−xμ(τ), and δ\deltaδ is linear, so it passes straight through the subtraction:

    δ[xμ(τ+dτ)−xμ(τ)]=δxμ(τ+dτ)−δxμ(τ).\delta\big[x^\mu(\tau+d\tau)-x^\mu(\tau)\big] = \delta x^\mu(\tau+d\tau) - \delta x^\mu(\tau).δ[xμ(τ+dτ)−xμ(τ)]=δxμ(τ+dτ)−δxμ(τ).

    The left side is δ(dxμ)\delta(dx^\mu)δ(dxμ); the right side is d(δxμ)d(\delta x^\mu)d(δxμ). This is the covariant cousin of "the change in velocity is the time-derivative of the change in position." Insert (3) into (2) and write the step as a τ\tauτ-derivative:

    \delta(ds) = -\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{d(\delta x^\mu)}{d\tau}\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}\,d\tau. \tag{4}

    Integrate by parts

    Feed (4) into (1). The two minus signs make the prefactor +mc+mc+mc:

    δS=mc∫τiτfημν d(δxμ)dτ dxνds dτ.\delta S = mc\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{d(\delta x^\mu)}{d\tau}\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}\,d\tau.δS=mc∫τi​τf​​ημν​dτd(δxμ)​dsdxν​dτ.

    To read off an equation of motion we need δxμ\delta x^\muδxμ standing bare, not differentiated. Move the derivative off it by parts — write the integrand as a total derivative minus the leftover:

    \delta S = mc\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f} d\tau\,\frac{d}{d\tau}\Big(\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\delta x^\mu\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}\Big) - mc\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f} d\tau\;\delta x^\mu\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{d}{d\tau}\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}. \tag{5}

    The first integral is a total derivative, so it collapses to a boundary term evaluated at τi,τf\tau_i,\tau_fτi​,τf​. We pinned the endpoints, δxμ(τi)=δxμ(τf)=0\delta x^\mu(\tau_i)=\delta x^\mu(\tau_f)=0δxμ(τi​)=δxμ(τf​)=0, so it dies:

    δS=−∫τiτfdτ  δxμ(τ) ημν ddτ(mc dxνds).\delta S = -\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f} d\tau\;\delta x^\mu(\tau)\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{d}{d\tau}\Big(mc\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}\Big).δS=−∫τi​τf​​dτδxμ(τ)ημν​dτd​(mcdsdxν​).

    Read off the momentum and conclude

    The object inside the derivative is the four-momentum:

    mc dxνds=m Uν=pν,Uν≡dxνd(s/c)=c dxνds.mc\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds} = m\,U^\nu = p^\nu, \qquad U^\nu \equiv \frac{dx^\nu}{d(s/c)} = c\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}.mcdsdxν​=mUν=pν,Uν≡d(s/c)dxν​=cdsdxν​.

    Here UνU^\nuUν is the four-velocity — position differentiated with respect to the physical proper time s/cs/cs/c — so it carries units of velocity, while the bare dxν/dsdx^\nu/dsdxν/ds is the dimensionless unit tangent. Sanity check on the spatial part: with ds=c dt/γds=c\,dt/\gammads=cdt/γ, we get pi=mc dxi/ds=γm vip^i = mc\,dx^i/ds = \gamma m\,v^ipi=mcdxi/ds=γmvi — the relativistic three-momentum from Proper-time action. The full units dictionary is in Proper length and proper time.

    Pull ημν\eta_{\mu\nu}ημν​ through the derivative to lower the index, ημν dpν/dτ=dpμ/dτ\eta_{\mu\nu}\,dp^\nu/d\tau = dp_\mu/d\tauημν​dpν/dτ=dpμ​/dτ:

    δS=−∫τiτfdτ  δxμ(τ) dpμdτ.\delta S = -\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f} d\tau\;\delta x^\mu(\tau)\,\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau}.δS=−∫τi​τf​​dτδxμ(τ)dτdpμ​​.

    The variation δxμ\delta x^\muδxμ is arbitrary in the interior, so δS=0\delta S=0δS=0 for every wiggle forces the coefficient to vanish pointwise:

    \boxed{\,\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau} = 0\,} \tag{6}

    A free particle's four-momentum is constant along its worldline. The statement is automatically parameter-blind: a quantity constant along a curve has vanishing derivative with respect to any label, so we never had to say what τ\tauτ was — exactly as Reparameterization invariance promised. All four components are conserved: the energy component p0=E/cp^0=E/cp0=E/c and the three-momentum pip^ipi alike.

    The straight-line form

    Choose the parameter to be the proper length itself, τ=s\tau=sτ=s (see Gauge choices for the worldline parameter). Then (6) becomes dpμ/ds=0dp^\mu/ds=0dpμ/ds=0, and with pμ=mc dxμ/dsp^\mu = mc\,dx^\mu/dspμ=mcdxμ/ds and constant mass,

    \boxed{\,\frac{d^2 x^\mu}{ds^2} = 0\,}. \tag{7}

    The worldline advances by equal four-vector steps per equal proper-length interval — that is what "straight in spacetime" means, Newton's first law in relativistic dress. But (7) is gauge-specific: an arbitrary τ\tauτ spaces its tick marks unevenly, so d2xμ/dτ2d^2x^\mu/d\tau^2d2xμ/dτ2 need not vanish even though dpμ/dτdp_\mu/d\taudpμ​/dτ does. The frame- and parameter-independent statement is (6), not (7).

    Arbitrary-parameter form

    There is a version of (7) that keeps an arbitrary τ\tauτ and is manifestly reparameterization invariant. Start from the parameterized action

    S=−mc∫dτ −x˙2,x˙2≡ημνx˙μx˙ν,x˙μ≡dxμdτ.S=-mc\int d\tau\,\sqrt{-\dot x^2},\qquad \dot x^2\equiv \eta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu\dot x^\nu,\quad \dot x^\mu\equiv\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}.S=−mc∫dτ−x˙2​,x˙2≡ημν​x˙μx˙ν,x˙μ≡dτdxμ​.

    The momentum conjugate to xμx^\muxμ is

    ∂L∂x˙μ=mc x˙μ−x˙2.\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot x^\mu} = mc\,\frac{\dot x_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}.∂x˙μ∂L​=mc−x˙2​x˙μ​​.

    Since ds=−x˙2 dτds=\sqrt{-\dot x^2}\,d\tauds=−x˙2​dτ, the fractions of dτd\taudτ cancel and this is exactly pμ=mc dxμ/dsp_\mu=mc\,dx_\mu/dspμ​=mcdxμ​/ds again. Euler–Lagrange, ddτ∂L∂x˙μ−∂L∂xμ=0\frac{d}{d\tau}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x^\mu}=0dτd​∂x˙μ∂L​−∂xμ∂L​=0, has no ∂L/∂xμ\partial L/\partial x^\mu∂L/∂xμ term (the Lagrangian has no bare xxx), so after dropping the nonzero constant mcmcmc:

    \boxed{\;\frac{d}{d\tau}\left(\frac{\dot x_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}\right)=0\;}. \tag{8}

    This is constant four-momentum wearing arbitrary-τ\tauτ clothes: the bracket is the unit tangent, and mcmcmc times it is pμp_\mupμ​. Setting τ=s\tau=sτ=s makes −x˙2=1\sqrt{-\dot x^2}=1−x˙2​=1 and collapses (8) back to d2xμ/ds2=0d^2x_\mu/ds^2=0d2xμ​/ds2=0; the dividing −x˙2\sqrt{-\dot x^2}−x˙2​ is what soaks up any uneven spacing of the parameter, restoring the invariance that (7) gave up.

    The parameter-independence of (6) is now a one-liner. Under any good reparameterization τ′(τ)\tau'(\tau)τ′(τ) (with dτ′/dτ≠0d\tau'/d\tau\neq0dτ′/dτ=0),

    dpμdτ′=dpμdτ dτdτ′=0.\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau'}=\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau}\,\frac{d\tau}{d\tau'}=0.dτ′dpμ​​=dτdpμ​​dτ′dτ​=0.

    Requiring dτ′/dτ>0d\tau'/d\tau>0dτ′/dτ>0 also keeps the new parameter running from the initial endpoint to the final one.

    Lorentz invariance, checked

    The final test the geometric-action strategy was built to pass: the equation must look identical to every inertial observer. Under a Lorentz transformation x′μ=Lμνxνx'^\mu = L^\mu{}_\nu x^\nux′μ=Lμν​xν — constant and η\etaη-preserving, so dsdsds is unchanged —

    0=d2xμds2  ⟹  d2x′μds2=Lμν d2xνds2=0,0 = \frac{d^2 x^\mu}{ds^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{d^2 x'^\mu}{ds^2} = L^\mu{}_\nu\,\frac{d^2 x^\nu}{ds^2} = 0,0=ds2d2xμ​⟹ds2d2x′μ​=Lμν​ds2d2xν​=0,

    and invertibility of LLL (any η\etaη-preserving matrix has (det⁡L)2=1(\det L)^2=1(detL)2=1) runs the implication backward. Physical in one frame ⇔\Leftrightarrow⇔ physical in all frames — the payoff a scalar action was chosen for in Proper-time action.

    Open questions

    • Curved space: the same action −mc∫ds-mc\int ds−mc∫ds with gμν(x)g_{\mu\nu}(x)gμν​(x) in place of ημν\eta_{\mu\nu}ημν​ yields the geodesic equation d2xμds2+Γνρμdxνdsdxρds=0\frac{d^2x^\mu}{ds^2}+\Gamma^\mu_{\nu\rho}\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}\frac{dx^\rho}{ds}=0ds2d2xμ​+Γνρμ​dsdxν​dsdxρ​=0. The extra Christoffel term is the position-dependence of the metric leaking into the variation; it needs geodesic machinery beyond this flat-space module, picked up when curved backgrounds appear in Metric, B-field, and dilaton as low-energy fields.

    Exercise

    The boundary term in equation (5) was thrown away because we pinned both endpoints. Suppose instead we free the final endpoint: δxμ(τi)=0\delta x^\mu(\tau_i)=0δxμ(τi​)=0 but δxμ(τf)\delta x^\mu(\tau_f)δxμ(τf​) arbitrary, while still demanding δS=0\delta S=0δS=0 for all such variations. What extra condition does this impose at τf\tau_fτf​? Show that a massive particle cannot satisfy it, then find the modified free-endpoint problem that does have a solution.

    Solution

    Redo the integration by parts (5) keeping the boundary term. The total-derivative piece evaluates the bracket at the two endpoints: δS=mc [ημν δxμ dxνds]τiτf−∫τiτfdτ  δxμ dpμdτ.\delta S = mc\,\Big[\eta_{\mu\nu}\,\delta x^\mu\,\frac{dx^\nu}{ds}\Big]_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f} - \int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f} d\tau\;\delta x^\mu\,\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau}.δS=mc[ημν​δxμdsdxν​]τi​τf​​−∫τi​τf​​dτδxμdτdpμ​​. With pν=mc dxν/dsp_\nu = mc\,dx_\nu/dspν​=mcdxν​/ds the boundary piece is [δxμ pμ]τiτf\big[\delta x^\mu\, p_\mu\big]_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}[δxμpμ​]τi​τf​​; it vanishes at τi\tau_iτi​ because δxμ(τi)=0\delta x^\mu(\tau_i)=0δxμ(τi​)=0. Arbitrary interior variations still force the bulk equation dpμ/dτ=0dp_\mu/d\tau=0dpμ​/dτ=0. What remains is δxμ(τf) pμ(τf)\delta x^\mu(\tau_f)\,p_\mu(\tau_f)δxμ(τf​)pμ​(τf​), and since all four components of δxμ(τf)\delta x^\mu(\tau_f)δxμ(τf​) are now arbitrary, stationarity demands the natural (free-endpoint) boundary condition pμ(τf)=0.p_\mu(\tau_f)=0.pμ​(τf​)=0. A massive particle cannot obey this. The momentum is mcmcmc times the unit tangent, so pμpμ=m2c2 ημνdxμdsdxνds=−m2c2≠0p^\mu p_\mu = m^2c^2\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\frac{dx^\mu}{ds}\frac{dx^\nu}{ds} = -m^2c^2 \neq 0pμpμ​=m2c2ημν​dsdxμ​dsdxν​=−m2c2=0 identically along any timelike worldline — the Mass-shell relation holds by the definition of sss, equations of motion or not. In particular p0=γmc≥mc>0p^0 = \gamma mc \geq mc > 0p0=γmc≥mc>0: even a particle at rest carries rest energy, so the four components can never vanish simultaneously. The fully-free-endpoint problem therefore has no extremal at all. Geometrically: once the bulk equation holds, δS=δxμ(τf) pμ(τf)\delta S = \delta x^\mu(\tau_f)\,p_\mu(\tau_f)δS=δxμ(τf​)pμ​(τf​), and choosing δxμ(τf)=−ϵ pμ\delta x^\mu(\tau_f) = -\epsilon\,p^\muδxμ(τf​)=−ϵpμ gives δS=+ϵ m2c2≠0\delta S = +\epsilon\,m^2c^2 \neq 0δS=+ϵm2c2=0 — sliding the endpoint along the worldline always changes the length at first order. This is why the equation of motion is stated for pinned endpoints. The version that works frees only where the particle ends, not when: hold δx0(τf)=0\delta x^0(\tau_f)=0δx0(τf​)=0 and let the spatial δxi(τf)\delta x^i(\tau_f)δxi(τf​) range freely over the slice t=tft=t_ft=tf​. Then only pi(τf)=0p_i(\tau_f)=0pi​(τf​)=0 is forced — the particle must end at rest — and since momentum is conserved, it is at rest throughout. Check: proper time from a fixed starting event to anywhere on the slice t=tft=t_ft=tf​ is ∫titf1−v2/c2 dt\int_{t_i}^{t_f}\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}\,dt∫ti​tf​​1−v2/c2​dt, maximized by v≡0v\equiv0v≡0; the stay-at-home worldline of Proper-time action wins. On the string worldsheet the same free-endpoint variation produces a boundary condition at each end of an open string — but there it constrains the flow of momentum off the end, not the endpoint's total momentum, so it can be satisfied. Choosing which directions are freed versus pinned is Neumann versus Dirichlet, the seed of D-branes.
