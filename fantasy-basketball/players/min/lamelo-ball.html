LaMelo Ball

Snapshot

  • Role: starting point guard and primary organizer beside Anthony Edwards.
  • 2025-26 baseline: 72 games, 20.1 PTS, 7.1 AST, 4.8 REB, and 272 made threes.
  • Current market: Alex Barutha ranked him 21st on July 14; Dynatyze showed No. 25 on July 18 but retained a stale Charlotte label.
  • Transaction: officially acquired from Charlotte in the four-team trade finalized July 10.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: 18-35. FT%, threes, assists, and steals can produce second-round per-game value; FG% and missed games create the downside.
  • Points: 20-35. Minnesota may reduce scoring volume but increase assist quality and overall efficiency.
  • Dynasty: 15-30. The ceiling remains elite, but availability prevents a stable top-15 valuation.

Role, floor and ceiling

Ball should own transition initiation and much of the half-court pick-and-roll work while Edwards closes possessions. Floor: another injury-interrupted season with roughly 17 points, seven assists, and damaging FG%. Ceiling: 68-72 games, nine assists, more than three threes, and top-15 per-game value if his shot quality improves beside Edwards.

Risk and transaction monitor

The 72-game rebound followed 141 missed games across the previous three seasons. Ankle and foot history remains the dominant risk. Minnesota's defense can cover some of his weaknesses through Jaden McDaniels and Rudy Gobert, but a reduced closing role would lower his assist and scoring ceiling.

Career record

Pre-NBA: No college. Chino Hills High School, then a professional stint in Lithuania with BC Prienai (Vytautas) in 2017-18 at age 16 alongside LiAngelo Ball, then a post-graduate year at SPIRE Academy in Ohio in 2018-19. His only meaningful pre-draft professional sample is the 2019-20 NBL season with the Illawarra Hawks in Australia: 12 games, 17.0 points, 7.4 rebounds, 6.8 assists on .377 shooting, and NBL Rookie of the Year over Kouat Noi by a 49-44 vote. That season also ended early — he bruised a foot in practice in December 2019, was ruled out roughly four weeks, elected on January 16, 2020 to sit the rest of the year, and parted with the Hawks on January 28 to prepare for the draft. The pattern of a foot or ankle problem truncating a season predates his NBA career. Draft: 2020, round 1, pick 3, Charlotte.

Fantasy history: Rookie of the Year in 2020-21 and an All-Star in 2021-22. His per-game category value has been in first-to-second-round territory in every season since 2021-22 on the strength of three punt-proof categories — threes, assists, and free throw percentage — while field goal percentage has been an active negative in five of six seasons and has never once cleared .436. Exact season-end 9-cat ranks from Basketball Monster or Hashtag are not verifiable from public sources, so no per-season rank is asserted. The material shape difference is that points leagues have historically valued him lower than category leagues do, because his .418 career field goal percentage costs nothing in a points format while his 3.2 turnovers cost real points, and because the elite FT% that carries a full roto category is nearly worthless in scoring formats. The 2025-26 season inverted his usual profile: he set a career high in FT% (.899) and a career low in turnovers (2.8) while scoring 5.1 fewer points than the year before.

Durability: 51 of 72 as a rookie (right wrist fracture, missed 21 games). 75 in 2021-22, his only near-full season until now. 36 in 2022-23 (repeated left and right ankle sprains, then a season-ending right ankle fracture in February 2023). 22 in 2023-24 (right ankle sprain in November, a re-aggravation, then season-ending ankle surgery). 47 in 2024-25 (right ankle and right wrist problems; shut down in late March). 72 in 2025-26, missing roughly ten games split between a right ankle impingement in November 2025 and a left ankle bone bruise, with no absence after December 14. That is 141 games missed across 2022-23 through 2024-25 and then the healthiest season of his career. Every significant absence except the rookie wrist fracture has been an ankle.

Trajectory: The arc is unusual because his per-game production peaked at 25.2 points in his worst availability stretch and then fell in his best. Minutes explain part of it — 28.0 in 2025-26 is a career low, down four from 2024-25 — but not all of it: his per-minute scoring dropped too, and Charlotte visibly traded shot volume for durability, which worked. The 2025-26 line is the first evidence that his body can hold up when the workload is capped, and that is the single most important fact in his file. What it does not show is a shooting fix; .407 from the field at age 24 across a 72-game sample is now a five-season trend, not variance, and it is driven by a heavy diet of pull-up threes and contested drives rather than by bad luck. Moving to Minnesota changes the inputs in his favor — Edwards absorbs the hardest on-ball scoring load, Gobert supplies a roll target that raises assist quality, and Ball should see cleaner catch-and-shoot looks than he ever got in Charlotte — but it also caps his usage, so the realistic 2026-27 outcome is fewer points on better efficiency with assists roughly flat. At 25 he is entering his physical prime with the injury history mostly behind him rather than ahead; the correct risk framing is now shot-quality upside rather than availability terror, though one more ankle event would reverse that in a week.
