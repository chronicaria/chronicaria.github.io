Terrence Shannon Jr

Terrence Shannon Jr. is the clearest early-season minutes beneficiary of Donte DiVincenzo's injury, but his scoring-first profile needs more threes, assists, or stocks to become a standard-league asset.

Snapshot

  • Role: projected 19-minute scoring wing; possible spot starter when Minnesota wants Dosunmu to lead the bench.
  • 2025-26 baseline: 43 games at 12.5 MPG for 5.6 PTS. He could not hold a rotation spot on a deeper roster under the same coach, which is the number every projection of him has to start from.
  • Opportunity: DiVincenzo is unavailable and the roster has only 13 standard contracts. Minnesota has also been reported to be weighing Shannon and Josh Green as power-forward-sized lineup answers, which is a different and less certain role than bench scoring wing.
  • Market: Hashtag's July keeper crowd ranked him 510th, showing that dynasty managers still discount his non-scoring categories.

Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: 140-200; waiver watch in standard leagues.
  • Points: 130-180 because his shot volume translates more cleanly than his category profile.
  • Dynasty: 225+ unless the role expands beyond 25 minutes.

Role, floor and ceiling

Floor: 12-14 minutes as an athletic bench scorer behind Edwards, Dosunmu, and Green — essentially a repeat of 2025-26 — if Minnesota fills either open roster slot with a wing. Ceiling: 28-plus minutes, 13-15 points, and top-120 points-league value if his threes or defensive activity improve. Scoring alone will not create a strong nine-category return.

Risk monitor

Shannon's early role is linked to DiVincenzo's rehab and Minnesota's two open standard slots. A veteran wing signing or a faster DiVincenzo return would reduce his minutes. Conversely, Ball or Edwards absences would increase his shot volume more than his playmaking.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Five college seasons — Texas Tech (2019-20 through 2021-22) then Illinois (2022-23, 2023-24). Texas Tech: 9.8 points and 4.1 rebounds on .470/.257/.829 as a freshman across 29 games; 12.9 and 4.0 on .448/.357/.756 across 28 games as a sophomore; 10.4 and 2.6 on .455/.384/.784 across 26 games as a junior. He transferred to Illinois and jumped immediately: 17.2 points, 4.6 rebounds, 2.8 assists on .442/.321/.790 in 31 games, then 23.0 points, 4.0 rebounds, 2.3 assists on .475/.362/.801 in 32 games as a fifth-year senior. Honors: Big 12 All-Freshman (2020), third-team All-Big 12 (2021), two-time first-team All-Big Ten (2023 and 2024, coaches), Big Ten Tournament Most Outstanding Player (2024), and AP third-team All-American (2024). Draft: 2024, first round, 27th overall, Minnesota. Not traded on draft night.

He also appeared in nine games of the 2026 playoffs with three starts, averaging 11.8 points — roughly double his regular-season scoring, on a materially larger role. That is the single most important line in his professional record so far and it is not in the regular-season table.

Fantasy history: 2024-25: unrostered in any standard format; 10.6 minutes and a 4.3-point line produce no category value. 2025-26: same, and the 43 games make even streaming impractical. Season-end 9-cat ranks for either year were not locatable and are not worth estimating — both seasons are comfortably outside the top 250 in any format. The points-league shape is modestly better than the category shape because his production is concentrated in scoring and free throws while he contributes essentially nothing in steals, blocks, assists or rebounds; his 2025-26 blocks figure rounds to 0.0. Hashtag's July 2026 keeper crowd at 510th is a reasonable reflection of that record.

Durability: GP by season — 32 of 82 in 2024-25, 43 of 82 in 2025-26. The 2024-25 absences were overwhelmingly coach's decisions and G League assignments to the Iowa Wolves rather than injury; he was a 27th pick on a contending roster. The 2025-26 absences were injury and they were serious. He missed nine games in November 2025 with a left foot fifth metatarsal bone bruise, returned, then left a game against Denver in late December with left foot soreness; an MRI at the Mayo Clinic diagnosed a left foot abductor hallucis strain that cost him roughly 24 consecutive games into February 2026. Two separate left-foot injuries in one season on a player whose entire value proposition is athleticism is the most important negative in this file. For completeness, his 2023-24 Illinois season also included a non-injury absence of about six games from a suspension that was lifted by court order, and he was acquitted in June 2024.

Trajectory: The college arc was a genuine late bloom — three ordinary Texas Tech seasons, then a transfer that produced 17.2 and then 23.0 points per game and an All-America selection at 23. That is unusual and it is why Minnesota spent a first-round pick on a player who would be 24 as a rookie. The NBA record so far shows almost none of it: two seasons, 75 games, 11.7 minutes, and a per-minute scoring rate that is fine without any secondary category to support it. He is not a young player developing on schedule — he turns 26 in July 2026 and is already past the age at which most wings have settled into their role — so the 2026-27 case does not rest on improvement, it rests on opportunity. The 2026 playoff run is the one piece of evidence that he can hold a real role when handed one: 11.8 points across nine games with three starts is the first NBA sample where his usage resembled his college usage. Against that, the twin foot injuries and a career three-point rate that has never established itself at NBA volume are the reasons the projection card sits at 22 minutes rather than 28. For fantasy purposes he remains a scoring-only profile in a format that pays for everything else, and DiVincenzo's health is a larger input to his value than anything Shannon himself controls.
