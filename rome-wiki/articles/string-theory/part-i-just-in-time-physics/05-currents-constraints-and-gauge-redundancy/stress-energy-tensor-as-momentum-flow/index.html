    Stress-energy tensor as momentum flow

    The stress-energy tensor is what you get when you run Noether's machine on translation symmetry: it is the bookkeeping ledger for energy and momentum, tracking how much sits at each point and how fast it flows past each surface.

    Prereq: Current versus charge — currents, charges, and the total-derivative Noether rule used below.

    Why two indices

    Electric charge has one conserved number and so one current jαj^\alphajα: a density j0j^0j0 plus a spatial flux ȷ⃗\vec\jmath​. Energy-momentum is a whole vector of conserved numbers — one for energy, one for each momentum component — so it needs a whole vector of currents, stacked into a two-index object Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​. Read it as a table: the lower index picks which conserved quantity, the upper index picks which direction it flows:

    Θαβ="flow of β-momentum in the α direction."\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = \text{"flow of }\beta\text{-momentum in the }\alpha\text{ direction."}Θαβ​="flow of β-momentum in the α direction."

    Everything below either derives this object or reads entries off the table.

    Why a whole vector? Translation is not one symmetry but a family: shifting along x0x^0x0, along x1x^1x1, and so on are all separate symmetries, each with its own conserved charge. Label the charge by β\betaβ. Each charge, being conserved, obeys its own continuity equation — its own current with a flow index α\alphaα. Bolt the flow index on top of the charge label and you get Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​, a stack of continuity equations ∂αΘαβ=0\partial_\alpha\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = 0∂α​Θαβ​=0, one per β\betaβ.

    The rows and columns have plain meanings. Fix the conserved quantity β\betaβ and scan the flow index:

    Θ0β=density of β-momentum,Θiβ=flux of β-momentum through a surface normal to i.(1)\Theta^0{}_\beta = \text{density of }\beta\text{-momentum}, \qquad \Theta^i{}_\beta = \text{flux of }\beta\text{-momentum through a surface normal to }i. \tag{1}Θ0β​=density of β-momentum,Θiβ​=flux of β-momentum through a surface normal to i.(1)

    The upper index is a spacetime direction; the lower index is a label, not a direction. Continuum mechanics stacks conservation laws the same way: a column of densities and a matrix of fluxes, one flux row per conserved quantity.

    Fix the sign convention once. Under a translation by ϵ\epsilonϵ we compare the field with its own value one step ahead, \delta\phi(x) = \phi(x+\epsilon) - \phi(x) = \epsilon^\beta\partial_\beta\phi, \tag{2} which is the same as dragging the whole field configuration by −ϵ-\epsilon−ϵ. The signature is mostly-plus throughout, ηαβ=diag(−1,+1,…,+1)\eta_{\alpha\beta} = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,\dots,+1)ηαβ​=diag(−1,+1,…,+1). The opposite choice — dragging by +ϵ+\epsilon+ϵ, so δϕ=−ϵβ∂βϕ\delta\phi = -\epsilon^\beta\partial_\beta\phiδϕ=−ϵβ∂β​ϕ — flips the overall sign of Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​ and nothing physical. We take (2) because it makes the energy density Θ00\Theta^0{}_0Θ00​ come out positive; the price shows up in the momentum entries, and the exercise reads it off exactly.

    Deriving Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​

    Take L=L(∂αϕ)\mathcal L = \mathcal L(\partial_\alpha\phi)L=L(∂α​ϕ) with no explicit coordinate dependence — the field enters only through its derivatives. The variation (2) commutes with derivatives: δ(∂αϕ)=∂α(δϕ)=ϵβ ∂α∂βϕ.\delta(\partial_\alpha\phi) = \partial_\alpha(\delta\phi) = \epsilon^\beta\,\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta\phi.δ(∂α​ϕ)=∂α​(δϕ)=ϵβ∂α​∂β​ϕ.

    Now vary L\mathcal LL. Because it depends on position only through ∂ϕ\partial\phi∂ϕ, the chain rule collapses to a plain gradient of L\mathcal LL:

    δL=∂L∂(∂αϕ) ϵβ∂α∂βϕ=ϵβ∂βL=∂α ⁣(ϵβ δβα L).\delta\mathcal L = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)}\,\epsilon^\beta\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta\phi = \epsilon^\beta\partial_\beta\mathcal L = \partial_\alpha\!\big(\epsilon^\beta\,\delta^\alpha_\beta\,\mathcal L\big).δL=∂(∂α​ϕ)∂L​ϵβ∂α​∂β​ϕ=ϵβ∂β​L=∂α​(ϵβδβα​L).

    So L\mathcal LL is not strictly invariant — it changes by a total derivative. This is the key move: translation shifts the Lagrangian's value from point to point, and that shift is exactly a divergence, δL=∂α(ϵβKαβ)\delta\mathcal L = \partial_\alpha(\epsilon^\beta K^\alpha{}_\beta)δL=∂α​(ϵβKαβ​) with Kαβ=δβαLK^\alpha{}_\beta = \delta^\alpha_\beta\mathcal LKαβ​=δβα​L. The action still changes only by a boundary term, so it is still a symmetry — and the total-derivative Noether rule (Current versus charge) says the honest conserved current is the naive current minus KKK:

    ϵβΘαβ=∂L∂(∂αϕ) δϕ−ϵβ δβα L.\epsilon^\beta\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)}\,\delta\phi - \epsilon^\beta\,\delta^\alpha_\beta\,\mathcal L.ϵβΘαβ​=∂(∂α​ϕ)∂L​δϕ−ϵβδβα​L.

    Insert δϕ=ϵβ∂βϕ\delta\phi = \epsilon^\beta\partial_\beta\phiδϕ=ϵβ∂β​ϕ and strip the arbitrary ϵβ\epsilon^\betaϵβ:

      Θαβ=∂L∂(∂αϕ) ∂βϕ−δβα L,∂αΘαβ=0.  (3)\boxed{\; \Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)}\,\partial_\beta\phi - \delta^\alpha_\beta\,\mathcal L, \qquad \partial_\alpha\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = 0. \;} \tag{3}Θαβ​=∂(∂α​ϕ)∂L​∂β​ϕ−δβα​L,∂α​Θαβ​=0.​(3)

    The subtracted −δβαL-\delta^\alpha_\beta\mathcal L−δβα​L is the whole reason a stress tensor differs from the naive "momentum times field-gradient" guess. Drop it and conservation fails — it is the single most common mistake here.

    Conservation follows in one line. Differentiate (3):

    ∂αΘαβ=∂α ⁣(∂L∂(∂αϕ))⏟= ∂L/∂ϕ ∂βϕ+∂L∂(∂αϕ) ∂α∂βϕ−∂βL.\partial_\alpha\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = \underbrace{\partial_\alpha\!\Big(\tfrac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)}\Big)}_{=\,\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi}\,\partial_\beta\phi + \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)}\,\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta\phi - \partial_\beta\mathcal L.∂α​Θαβ​==∂L/∂ϕ∂α​(∂(∂α​ϕ)∂L​)​​∂β​ϕ+∂(∂α​ϕ)∂L​∂α​∂β​ϕ−∂β​L.

    The underbraced combination equals ∂L/∂ϕ\partial\mathcal L/\partial\phi∂L/∂ϕ by the Euler–Lagrange equation — and that is 000 here, since L\mathcal LL has no bare ϕ\phiϕ. The middle term is exactly ∂βL\partial_\beta\mathcal L∂β​L (the chain rule run backward), cancelling the last term. So ∂αΘαβ=0\partial_\alpha\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = 0∂α​Θαβ​=0 on shell. The conservation of energy-momentum is the equation of motion in disguise.

    Worked example: the free massless scalar

    L=−12 ηρλ∂ρϕ ∂λϕ=−12 ∂ρϕ ∂ρϕ.\mathcal L = -\tfrac12\,\eta^{\rho\lambda}\partial_\rho\phi\,\partial_\lambda\phi = -\tfrac12\,\partial^\rho\phi\,\partial_\rho\phi.L=−21​ηρλ∂ρ​ϕ∂λ​ϕ=−21​∂ρϕ∂ρ​ϕ.

    Units are c=1c=1c=1 from here on, so x0=tx^0 = tx0=t and ϕ˙≡∂0ϕ\dot\phi \equiv \partial_0\phiϕ˙​≡∂0​ϕ; we work in four spacetime dimensions for concreteness. The −12-\tfrac12−21​ is not arbitrary: in mostly-plus the time-derivative term carries a +12ϕ˙2+\tfrac12\dot\phi^2+21​ϕ˙​2, giving positive kinetic energy (checked below). Then

    ∂L∂(∂αϕ)=− ∂αϕ,Θαβ=− ∂αϕ ∂βϕ+12 δβα ∂ρϕ ∂ρϕ.(4)\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial(\partial_\alpha\phi)} = -\,\partial^\alpha\phi, \qquad \Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = -\,\partial^\alpha\phi\,\partial_\beta\phi + \tfrac12\,\delta^\alpha_\beta\,\partial^\rho\phi\,\partial_\rho\phi. \tag{4}∂(∂α​ϕ)∂L​=−∂αϕ,Θαβ​=−∂αϕ∂β​ϕ+21​δβα​∂ρϕ∂ρ​ϕ.(4)

    The energy density is the top-left entry, Θ00\Theta^0{}_0Θ00​. In mostly-plus, ∂0=η00∂0=−∂0\partial^0 = \eta^{00}\partial_0 = -\partial_0∂0=η00∂0​=−∂0​, so the first term is −∂0ϕ ∂0ϕ=+ϕ˙2-\partial^0\phi\,\partial_0\phi = +\dot\phi^2−∂0ϕ∂0​ϕ=+ϕ˙​2, and 12∂ρϕ∂ρϕ=12(−ϕ˙2+∣∇ϕ∣2)\tfrac12\partial^\rho\phi\partial_\rho\phi = \tfrac12(-\dot\phi^2 + |\nabla\phi|^2)21​∂ρϕ∂ρ​ϕ=21​(−ϕ˙​2+∣∇ϕ∣2). Adding:

    Θ00=ϕ˙2−12ϕ˙2+12∣∇ϕ∣2=12ϕ˙2+12∣∇ϕ∣2  ≥  0.(5)\Theta^0{}_0 = \dot\phi^2 - \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 = \tfrac12\dot\phi^2 + \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2 \;\ge\; 0. \tag{5}Θ00​=ϕ˙​2−21​ϕ˙​2+21​∣∇ϕ∣2=21​ϕ˙​2+21​∣∇ϕ∣2≥0.(5)

    Kinetic plus gradient (potential) energy, both positive — exactly what a field energy density should be. Both ingredients matter: without the −δβαL-\delta^\alpha_\beta\mathcal L−δβα​L subtraction the entry would be ϕ˙2\dot\phi^2ϕ˙​2 — positive, but not the energy, the gradient term lost — and the sign of the kinetic term rests on the metric factor ∂0=−∂0\partial^0 = -\partial_0∂0=−∂0​.

    Conservation for this L\mathcal LL:

    ∂αΘαβ=−(□ϕ) ∂βϕ  − ∂αϕ ∂α∂βϕ+∂ρϕ ∂β∂ρϕ⏟= 0=−(□ϕ) ∂βϕ.\partial_\alpha\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = -(\Box\phi)\,\partial_\beta\phi \;\underbrace{-\,\partial^\alpha\phi\,\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta\phi + \partial^\rho\phi\,\partial_\beta\partial_\rho\phi}_{=\,0} = -(\Box\phi)\,\partial_\beta\phi.∂α​Θαβ​=−(□ϕ)∂β​ϕ=0−∂αϕ∂α​∂β​ϕ+∂ρϕ∂β​∂ρ​ϕ​​=−(□ϕ)∂β​ϕ.

    The two derivative terms cancel: relabel the dummy ρ→α\rho\to\alphaρ→α and use ∂α∂β=∂β∂α\partial_\alpha\partial_\beta = \partial_\beta\partial_\alpha∂α​∂β​=∂β​∂α​. Here □≡∂α∂α\Box \equiv \partial_\alpha\partial^\alpha□≡∂α​∂α, and the Euler–Lagrange equation for this L\mathcal LL is exactly the wave equation □ϕ=0\Box\phi = 0□ϕ=0 — so on shell ∂αΘαβ=0\partial_\alpha\Theta^\alpha{}_\beta = 0∂α​Θαβ​=0 for every β\betaβ. The scalar's energy and all three momenta are conserved.

    What "stress" means

    Nothing mystical. Momentum can cross a surface just as charge can, and a spatial-upper entry Θij\Theta^i{}_jΘij​ counts the flux of jjj-momentum through a surface normal to iii. In continuum mechanics those entries are the stress tensor — pressure on the diagonal, shear off it — up to the same overall sign that convention (2) locks into every entry. The name "stress-energy tensor" is literal: the Θ0\Theta^0Θ0 row holds energy and momentum densities, the Θi\Theta^iΘi rows hold their mechanical stresses.

    One thing to keep straight for the string chapters ahead. This Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​ is the canonical Noether tensor for translating the coordinates a field theory lives on. The string's World-sheet momentum current instead translates the target-space coordinates Xμ↦Xμ+ϵμX^\mu\mapsto X^\mu + \epsilon^\muXμ↦Xμ+ϵμ and yields Pμα\mathcal P^\alpha_\muPμα​ — a related but distinct object, whose lower index is a spacetime label and whose upper index is a worldsheet direction.

    Open questions

    • The canonical Θαβ\Theta^\alpha{}_\betaΘαβ​ from (3) need not come out symmetric (Θαβ≠Θβα\Theta^{\alpha\beta}\ne\Theta^{\beta\alpha}Θαβ=Θβα) for fields with spin; a "Belinfante improvement" fixes this. Harmless for the scalar, but it matters when comparing to the symmetric Polyakov worldsheet tensor TαβT_{\alpha\beta}Tαβ​ and the Virasoro constraints later.
    • Most QFT texts use mostly-minus signature, where the same formula (3) gives a positive Θ00\Theta^0{}_0Θ00​ and charges ∫Θ0β d3x=+pβ\int\Theta^0{}_\beta\,d^3x = +p_\beta∫Θ0β​d3x=+pβ​ simultaneously. Rerun the exercise in that signature and watch the sign tension it exposes dissolve.

    Exercise

    Using (4) and mostly-plus, compute the momentum-density entry Θ0i\Theta^0{}_iΘ0i​ in terms of ϕ˙≡∂0ϕ\dot\phi \equiv \partial_0\phiϕ˙​≡∂0​ϕ and ∂iϕ\partial_i\phi∂i​ϕ. Then evaluate it on a right-moving wave ϕ=f(x1−x0)\phi = f(x^1 - x^0)ϕ=f(x1−x0) and decide: does the sign of Θ01\Theta^0{}_1Θ01​ point along or against the direction the wave travels? What does that tell you about reading Θ0i\Theta^0{}_iΘ0i​ as "the" momentum density?

    Solution

    Set α=0\alpha=0α=0, β=i\beta=iβ=i in (4). Since iii is spatial, δi0=0\delta^0_i = 0δi0​=0 kills the second term, leaving Θ0i=− ∂0ϕ ∂iϕ.\Theta^0{}_i = -\,\partial^0\phi\,\partial_i\phi.Θ0i​=−∂0ϕ∂i​ϕ. In mostly-plus, ∂0ϕ=η00∂0ϕ=−∂0ϕ=−ϕ˙\partial^0\phi = \eta^{00}\partial_0\phi = -\partial_0\phi = -\dot\phi∂0ϕ=η00∂0​ϕ=−∂0​ϕ=−ϕ˙​, so   Θ0i=ϕ˙ ∂iϕ.  \boxed{\;\Theta^0{}_i = \dot\phi\,\partial_i\phi.\;}Θ0i​=ϕ˙​∂i​ϕ.​ That single metric sign is the entire computation: (1) fixes each entry's role, but the overall sign is locked by convention (2).

    Now the sign check. For the right-mover ϕ=f(x1−x0)\phi = f(x^1 - x^0)ϕ=f(x1−x0): ∂0ϕ=−f′\partial_0\phi = -f'∂0​ϕ=−f′ and ∂1ϕ=+f′\partial_1\phi = +f'∂1​ϕ=+f′, so Θ01=ϕ˙ ∂1ϕ=(−f′)(f′)=−[f′]2≤0.\Theta^0{}_1 = \dot\phi\,\partial_1\phi = (-f')(f') = -[f']^2 \le 0.Θ01​=ϕ˙​∂1​ϕ=(−f′)(f′)=−[f′]2≤0. Meanwhile (5) gives the wave's energy density Θ00=12[f′]2+12[f′]2=[f′]2\Theta^0{}_0 = \tfrac12[f']^2 + \tfrac12[f']^2 = [f']^2Θ00​=21​[f′]2+21​[f′]2=[f′]2, so the packet carries energy E=∫[f′]2 d3xE = \int[f']^2\,d^3xE=∫[f′]2d3x; a massless packet moving in +x1+x^1+x1 carries momentum p1=E>0p^1 = E > 0p1=E>0, along its motion. So Θ01\Theta^0{}_1Θ01​ points against the physical momentum, with exactly matching magnitude: ∫Θ01 d3x=−E=−p1=−p1\int\Theta^0{}_1\,d^3x = -E = -p^1 = -p_1∫Θ01​d3x=−E=−p1=−p1​ (mostly-plus leaves spatial indices alone).

    This is the price of convention (2). The charges conserved by (3) assemble into ∫Θ0β d3x=−pβ\int\Theta^0{}_\beta\,d^3x = -p_\beta∫Θ0β​d3x=−pβ​: for β=0\beta=0β=0 that is −p0=+E-p_0 = +E−p0​=+E — exactly why Θ00\Theta^0{}_0Θ00​ came out positive in (5) — and for β=i\beta=iβ=i it is −pi-p_i−pi​, the flipped sign found above. Flipping the sign of (2) would make ∫Θ0i d3x\int\Theta^0{}_i\,d^3x∫Θ0i​d3x the momentum directly, but would turn Θ00\Theta^0{}_0Θ00​ into −12ϕ˙2−12∣∇ϕ∣2-\tfrac12\dot\phi^2 - \tfrac12|\nabla\phi|^2−21​ϕ˙​2−21​∣∇ϕ∣2. In mostly-plus no overall sign makes both entries look nice, because lowering an index on pβp^\betapβ costs a sign for β=0\beta=0β=0 but not for β=i\beta=iβ=i. The reliable reading is covariant: with (2), Θ0β\Theta^0{}_\betaΘ0β​ is the density of −pβ-p_\beta−pβ​, all components at once. So Θ0i\Theta^0{}_iΘ0i​ is the momentum density up to a convention-locked overall sign — and when the sign matters, test it on a wave, exactly as here.
