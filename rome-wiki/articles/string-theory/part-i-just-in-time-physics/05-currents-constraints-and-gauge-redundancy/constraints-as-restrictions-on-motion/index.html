    A constraint does not tell your data how to move; it tells you which data were legal to begin with.

    Evolution laws versus constraints

    Most equations of physics are evolution laws: hand one the state now, it returns the state a moment later. A constraint never mentions the future. It is a bouncer at the door: it inspects a candidate state, pronounces it allowed or forbidden, and only allowed states are ever admitted to the dynamics.

    Two ways to tell them apart at a glance:

    • Evolution law — can be solved for an acceleration ("x¨=…\ddot x = \dotsx¨=…"). It predicts.
    • Constraint — an algebraic relation among positions, velocities, or momenta at one instant, with no acceleration in sight. It filters.

    The claim this note develops: wherever a physical history can be described in more than one way — the same worldline traced at any speed, the same string surface gridded by any coordinates — the description carries more variables than the physics does, and the surplus is pinned down by constraints. Redundancy in the description is the source of the constraints. That single principle produces both the relativistic particle's mass shell and the string's two Virasoro conditions below.

    Warm-up: a bead on a wire

    A bead threaded on a fixed wire obeys f(q)=0f(q)=0f(q)=0, the equation of the wire. Notice what this equation is not: it says nothing about the bead's acceleration. It only declares that the configuration must sit on the wire. Dynamics happens along the allowed surface; the constraint just says which surface. Every constraint below is this same move, promoted to phase space: an algebraic surface the pair (position,momentum)(\text{position},\text{momentum})(position,momentum) must lie on before any evolution is even discussed.

    Relativistic particle: the mass shell is a constraint

    The cleanest example. A point particle sweeps a worldline; its action is the invariant length of that line (mostly-plus signature; ccc kept throughout):

    S=−mc∫dτ −x˙μx˙μ,x˙μ=dxμdτ.S=-mc\int d\tau\,\sqrt{-\dot x^\mu\dot x_\mu}, \qquad \dot x^\mu=\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau}.S=−mc∫dτ−x˙μx˙μ​​,x˙μ=dτdxμ​.

    The integrand dτ−x˙2d\tau\sqrt{-\dot x^2}dτ−x˙2​ is invariant arc length along the curve (ccc times the elapsed proper time) — reparameterizing τ\tauτ retraces the same curve at a different speed, so τ\tauτ is a fake label. That fakeness is the redundancy that will force a constraint.

    The canonical momentum is

    pμ=∂L∂x˙μ=mc x˙μ−x˙2.p_\mu=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot x^\mu} =mc\,\frac{\dot x_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}.pμ​=∂x˙μ∂L​=mc−x˙2​x˙μ​​.

    It points along the worldline's tangent, but the arbitrary magnitude of x˙μ\dot x^\mux˙μ has divided out — pμp_\mupμ​ knows the direction of travel, not the (meaningless) speed in τ\tauτ. That erasure of scale is what leaves a relation behind. Square it:

    pμpμ=m2c2 x˙μx˙μ−x˙νx˙ν=−m2c2.p^\mu p_\mu =m^2c^2\,\frac{\dot x^\mu\dot x_\mu}{-\dot x^\nu\dot x_\nu} =-m^2c^2.pμpμ​=m2c2−x˙νx˙ν​x˙μx˙μ​​=−m2c2.

    Therefore \boxed{\;p^\mu p_\mu+m^2c^2=0.\;} \tag{1} This is the Mass-shell relation. Read it correctly: it is not an equation for p˙μ\dot p_\mup˙​μ​ or x¨μ\ddot x^\mux¨μ — no time derivative of the momentum appears. It is the bouncer. Every allowed momentum must already satisfy it; a pμp_\mupμ​ off the mass shell was never a legal particle state.

    The same redundancy shows up as a vanishing Hamiltonian:

    Hcan=pμx˙μ−L=0.H_{\rm can}=p_\mu\dot x^\mu-L=0.Hcan​=pμ​x˙μ−L=0.

    Why zero? Because LLL is homogeneous of degree one in x˙μ\dot x^\mux˙μ, Euler's theorem gives pμx˙μ=Lp_\mu\dot x^\mu=Lpμ​x˙μ=L and the difference vanishes identically. So "evolution in τ\tauτ" generates no real change — τ\tauτ is a gauge label, not a clock, until you pick a gauge. A vanishing canonical Hamiltonian is the signature of reparameterization redundancy.

    Nambu–Goto string: two constraints at every point

    The string repeats the particle's trick — twice, because a surface has two coordinate labels (τ,σ)(\tau,\sigma)(τ,σ) to relabel instead of one. The Nambu–Goto density is

    L=−T0c(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2,\mathcal L=-\frac{T_0}{c}\sqrt{(\dot X\cdot X')^2-\dot X^2 X'^2},L=−cT0​​(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2​,

    with X˙=∂τX\dot X=\partial_\tau XX˙=∂τ​X, X′=∂σXX'=\partial_\sigma XX′=∂σ​X, and tension T0T_0T0​. Abbreviate the three invariants and the discriminant:

    A=X˙2,B=X˙⋅X′,C=X′2,Δ=B2−AC.A=\dot X^2,\qquad B=\dot X\cdot X',\qquad C=X'^2,\qquad \Delta=B^2-AC.A=X˙2,B=X˙⋅X′,C=X′2,Δ=B2−AC.

    For a physical worldsheet the τ\tauτ-direction is timelike and the σ\sigmaσ-direction spacelike, so A<0A<0A<0, C>0C>0C>0, and Δ>0\Delta>0Δ>0: the square root is real.

    The momentum density conjugate to XμX^\muXμ follows by differentiating L=−T0cΔ\mathcal L=-\frac{T_0}{c}\sqrt\DeltaL=−cT0​​Δ​, using ∂Δ/∂X˙μ=2BXμ′−2CX˙μ\partial\Delta/\partial\dot X^\mu = 2B X'_\mu - 2C\dot X_\mu∂Δ/∂X˙μ=2BXμ′​−2CX˙μ​:

    Pμτ=∂L∂X˙μ=T0c CX˙μ−BXμ′Δ.\mathcal P^\tau_\mu =\frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot X^\mu} =\frac{T_0}{c}\,\frac{C\dot X_\mu-B X'_\mu}{\sqrt\Delta}.Pμτ​=∂X˙μ∂L​=cT0​​Δ​CX˙μ​−BXμ′​​.

    This is the σ\sigmaσ-density of spacetime momentum built in World-sheet momentum current. The two constraints now fall out by pure algebra — no equations of motion needed, which is the tell that they are constraints, not dynamics.

    Constraint 1 — dot Pτ\mathcal P^\tauPτ into X′X'X′:

    Pτ⋅X′=T0c C(X˙⋅X′)−B(X′2)Δ=T0c CB−BCΔ=0.\mathcal P^\tau\cdot X' =\frac{T_0}{c}\,\frac{C(\dot X\cdot X')-B(X'^2)}{\sqrt\Delta} =\frac{T_0}{c}\,\frac{CB-BC}{\sqrt\Delta}=0.Pτ⋅X′=cT0​​Δ​C(X˙⋅X′)−B(X′2)​=cT0​​Δ​CB−BC​=0.

    Momentum density has zero component along the string's own tangent. Sliding grid points along the string is not motion of the string — it is renaming, and renaming carries no momentum. All real motion is transverse.

    Constraint 2 — square Pμτ\mathcal P^\tau_\muPμτ​:

    (Pτ)2=T02c2 C2A−2CB2+B2CΔ=T02c2 C(AC−B2)Δ=−T02c2 C,(\mathcal P^\tau)^2 =\frac{T_0^2}{c^2}\,\frac{C^2A-2CB^2+B^2C}{\Delta} =\frac{T_0^2}{c^2}\,\frac{C(AC-B^2)}{\Delta} =-\frac{T_0^2}{c^2}\,C,(Pτ)2=c2T02​​ΔC2A−2CB2+B2C​=c2T02​​ΔC(AC−B2)​=−c2T02​​C,

    using AC−B2=−ΔAC-B^2=-\DeltaAC−B2=−Δ. Since C=X′2C=X'^2C=X′2,

      Pτ⋅X′=0,(Pτ)2+T02c2 X′2=0.  (2)\boxed{\; \mathcal P^\tau\cdot X'=0,\qquad (\mathcal P^\tau)^2+\frac{T_0^2}{c^2}\,X'^2=0. \;} \tag{2}Pτ⋅X′=0,(Pτ)2+c2T02​​X′2=0.​(2)

    These are two local conditions on the phase-space data (Xμ,Pμτ)(X^\mu,\mathcal P^\tau_\mu)(Xμ,Pμτ​) at each point σ\sigmaσ — the string version of the particle's single mass shell (1)(1)(1). Before you evolve a string, its data must already sit on this surface. The family resemblance is no accident: (1)(1)(1) ties p2p^2p2 to a mass, (2)(2)(2) ties (Pτ)2(\mathcal P^\tau)^2(Pτ)2 to the local stretch X′2X'^2X′2, and each is the fingerprint of a fake label being quotiented away.

    Conformal-gauge form: the classical Virasoro seeds

    The redundancy lets us choose worldsheet coordinates (τ\tauτ and σ\sigmaσ in common units) in which the induced metric γαβ=∂αX⋅∂βX\gamma_{\alpha\beta}=\partial_\alpha X\cdot\partial_\beta Xγαβ​=∂α​X⋅∂β​X is conformally flat: a positive function times diag(−1,1)\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)diag(−1,1). Then B=0B=0B=0 and A=−CA=-CA=−C, so Δ=C\sqrt\Delta=CΔ​=C and the momentum density collapses to Pμτ=(T0/c) X˙μ\mathcal P^\tau_\mu=(T_0/c)\,\dot X_\muPμτ​=(T0​/c)X˙μ​. In these coordinates the pair (2)(2)(2) reads

    X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.\dot X\cdot X'=0,\qquad \dot X^2+X'^2=0.X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.

    The first says the τ\tauτ- and σ\sigmaσ-tangents are orthogonal; the second says they have equal and opposite norm. Add and subtract them:

    (X˙+X′)2=0,(X˙−X′)2=0,(\dot X+X')^2=0,\qquad (\dot X-X')^2=0,(X˙+X′)2=0,(X˙−X′)2=0,

    since (X˙±X′)2=(X˙2+X′2)±2 X˙⋅X′(\dot X\pm X')^2 = (\dot X^2+X'^2)\pm 2\,\dot X\cdot X'(X˙±X′)2=(X˙2+X′2)±2X˙⋅X′ and both pieces vanish. The left- and right-moving tangent directions on the string are null in spacetime — a wave running along the string traces a light-like track in the target. These two null conditions are the classical seeds of the Virasoro constraints: mode-expanding them yields the Virasoro operators, and demanding that they survive quantization is what later fixes the critical dimension.

    Symmetry versus redundancy: the same origin, opposite meanings

    Both conserved charges and constraints are born from invariance — but invariance of two different kinds, and confusing them is the classic beginner error.

    maps between... produces example
    Physical symmetry different physical states a conserved charge spacetime translation →\to→ momentum pμp_\mupμ​
    Gauge redundancy the same physical state a constraint worldline / worldsheet reparameterization →\to→ mass shell, Virasoro

    An invariance that carries you to a genuinely new state hands you a conserved quantity; an invariance that merely renames the state you are already in hands you a constraint. Momentum is real physics you can measure; the mass shell is bookkeeping that removes fiction. See Gauge fixing as choosing a representative for how one then chooses a representative and solves the constraints away.

    Open questions

    • Dirac's first-class / second-class classification is the systematic Hamiltonian language for these constraints; worth a note only once quantization needs it.
    • Boundary conditions can turn string-only momentum conservation into momentum exchange with a brane — that belongs with D-branes, not this structural note.

    Exercise

    A student, reasoning by analogy with the ordinary particle Hamiltonian, claims the relativistic particle's canonical Hamiltonian should be the familiar relativistic energy H=c2pipi+m2c4H=\sqrt{c^2 p^ip_i+m^2c^4}H=c2pipi​+m2c4​ (spatial momenta only) and is puzzled why the boxed result (1)(1)(1) instead gives a constraint. Resolve the tension in two short steps:

    1. Compute Hcan=pμx˙μ−LH_{\rm can}=p_\mu\dot x^\mu-LHcan​=pμ​x˙μ−L directly for S=−mc∫dτ−x˙2S=-mc\int d\tau\sqrt{-\dot x^2}S=−mc∫dτ−x˙2​ and show it vanishes identically.
    2. Explain, in one sentence each, (a) why it vanishes and (b) what the mass-shell relation (1)(1)(1) is doing if it is not serving as the Hamiltonian.
    Solution

    Step 1. With pμ=mc x˙μ/−x˙2p_\mu = mc\,\dot x_\mu/\sqrt{-\dot x^2}pμ​=mcx˙μ​/−x˙2​,

    pμx˙μ=mc x˙μx˙μ−x˙2=mc x˙2−x˙2=−mc −x˙2=L.p_\mu\dot x^\mu = mc\,\frac{\dot x_\mu\dot x^\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}} = mc\,\frac{\dot x^2}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}} = -mc\,\sqrt{-\dot x^2} = L.pμ​x˙μ=mc−x˙2​x˙μ​x˙μ​=mc−x˙2​x˙2​=−mc−x˙2​=L.

    Hence Hcan=pμx˙μ−L=L−L=0H_{\rm can}=p_\mu\dot x^\mu-L=L-L=0Hcan​=pμ​x˙μ−L=L−L=0 identically, for every trajectory — not only on shell.

    Step 2. (a) It vanishes because LLL is homogeneous of degree one in x˙μ\dot x^\mux˙μ (rescaling x˙μ→λx˙μ\dot x^\mu\to\lambda\dot x^\mux˙μ→λx˙μ scales LLL by λ\lambdaλ), so Euler's theorem forces pμx˙μ=Lp_\mu\dot x^\mu=Lpμ​x˙μ=L. This homogeneity is exactly the τ\tauτ-reparameterization redundancy: τ\tauτ is a fake label, so "energy conjugate to τ\tauτ" is zero. (b) With Hcan≡0H_{\rm can}\equiv 0Hcan​≡0 there is no clock and no evolution law from it; the physical content lives entirely in the constraint (1)(1)(1), p2+m2c2=0p^2+m^2c^2=0p2+m2c2=0, which filters allowed momenta rather than evolving them. The student's HHH appears only after gauge-fixing τ\tauτ to a physical time (static gauge x0=cτx^0=c\taux0=cτ): solving (1)(1)(1) for the positive-energy root, p0=−pipi+m2c2p_0=-\sqrt{p^ip_i+m^2c^2}p0​=−pipi​+m2c2​, gives E=−cp0=c2pipi+m2c4E=-cp_0=\sqrt{c^2p^ip_i+m^2c^4}E=−cp0​=c2pipi​+m2c4​ (mostly-plus: E=cp0=−cp0E=cp^0=-cp_0E=cp0=−cp0​), turning the constraint into exactly that gauge-fixed Hamiltonian. Constraint first, Hamiltonian only after a choice of clock.
