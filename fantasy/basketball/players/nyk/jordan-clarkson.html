Jordan Clarkson

His neutral healthy allocation is five minutes, while 10 MPG per appearance reflects nights when the rotation needs scoring or is missing a guard. Nine-cat: outside top 300. Points: D. Dynasty: 1.5/10.

Role and evidence

Clarkson averaged 8.6 points, 1.8 rebounds, 1.3 assists, 1.0 three, and 0.9 turnovers in 17.8 minutes across 72 games in 2025-26, shooting .451/.830 (Hashtag Basketball). That role already represented a major fall from his peak sixth-man volume.

New York brought him back on a reported one-year deal in the NBA's 2026 offseason transaction tracker. McBride, Alvarado, and Shamet have clearer two-way or spacing functions, so Clarkson's minutes are most likely to arrive through injury, rest, or a bench unit needing self-created shots.

Role branches

  • Ceiling: a multi-week guard absence restores 20 minutes and 10-12 points with two threes.
  • Median: spot scorer and healthy-night 10th/11th man.
  • Floor: frequent DNP-CDs and a midseason roster move.

Market and confidence

Hashtag ranked Clarkson 391st in analyst dynasty and 615th on the keeper crowd board. Contract status is high confidence; rotation minutes are low; scoring rate medium; defensive categories low.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Tulsa 2010-11 (11.5 PPG) and 2011-12 (16.5 PPG, first-team All-Conference USA); transferred to Missouri, sat out 2012-13, then 17.5 PPG/3.8 RPG/3.4 APG in 2013-14 (second-team All-SEC). Draft: 2014, round 2, pick 46, Washington Wizards; sold to the Lakers on draft night.

Durability: Iron-man early (82 games in 2016-17, 81 twice), eroding in Utah: 61, 55, then 37 in 2024-25, when plantar fasciitis in his left foot required a procedure that ended his season in March 2025. Bounced back to 72 games in a light 17.8-minute role for the Knicks in 2025-26.

Trajectory: Twelve seasons as one archetype — the microwave bench scorer, peaking with the 2021 Sixth Man of the Year award and a 20.8 PPG pseudo-starter year in 2022-23. The 2025-26 Knicks season cut his usage roughly in half from his Utah norms, but the rates survived intact (.451 FG, .830 FT, 1.0 threes in 17.8 minutes), and he won a ring in the role. At 34 he competes with McBride, Alvarado and Shamet for New York's discretionary bench-scoring minutes on a one-year return. He becomes fantasy-relevant only during a multi-week Knicks guard absence, when 20+ minutes would restore a 10-12 point, two-three line with strong FT% — a points-league stream, not a nine-cat asset.
