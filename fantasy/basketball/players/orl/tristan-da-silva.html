Tristan da Silva

Tristan da Silva is Orlando's most dependable reserve forward, offering threes, excellent free throws, steals, and low turnovers without enough base minutes for standard-league volume.

2025-26 baseline

Da Silva played 77 games at 24.7 minutes and averaged 9.9 points, 3.7 rebounds, 1.6 assists, 0.9 steals, 0.3 blocks, 1.6 threes, and 0.9 turnovers. He shot .450 from the field and .884 from the line.

The clean category line did not require much usage. The 2026-27 projection reduces minutes because Wagner is assumed to play more than 34 games and the forward/center depth remains crowded.

Role and pathway

Da Silva is the first reserve at both forward spots and the cleanest spot starter for Wagner or Banchero. He helps lineups function without demanding touches, which should keep him ahead of Penda and Cain when the team is healthy. He can also play with either star in smaller units.

His path to standard-league relevance is straightforward: 25-plus minutes through an injury or a decisive coaching preference for shooting over a second center. In that role, threes and steals become streamable while the FT/turnover profile does no harm.

Category shape

  • Strong: FT percentage, low turnovers.
  • Helpful: threes and steals.
  • Modest: points and rebounds.
  • Weak: blocks and assists.

Low-volume FT excellence is supportive rather than transformative. Da Silva is best used as a schedule streamer when his minutes are visible.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Born May 15, 2001 in Munich to a Brazilian father and a German mother; older brother Oscar played at Stanford and professionally for Bayern Munich. Played 2019 simultaneously for Schwabing in Germany's Regionalliga and IBA München in the NBBL, leading the latter at 16.9 points on 53.2% shooting across 12 games. Four seasons at Colorado (Pac-12), with a steady four-year climb.

Durability: 74 and 77 games in his two NBA seasons, 151 of a possible 164. No significant injury on record, and he has been available for effectively every rotation decision Orlando has had to make.

Trajectory: Two seasons of clean, monotonic improvement in every category that matters to his role — field goal percentage from .412 to .450, three-point rate from 1.1 to 1.6 makes at .374, steals more than doubling to 0.9 — while the turnover rate stayed at 0.9 and the free throw percentage stayed near .880. That is the profile of a low-usage connector who does not damage a fantasy roster anywhere, and it is also why he is not a standard-league asset: nothing in the line is large enough to carry a category. The 24.7 minutes in 2025-26 were partly a Wagner-absence artifact, and Penda's arrival crowds the backup power forward slot, so the base case is a step back toward 14-18 minutes. He becomes rosterable if Wagner or Banchero misses extended time and he holds 27-plus minutes, where the threes and steals scale into streamer territory; short of that he is a deep-roto glue piece and a schedule stream.
