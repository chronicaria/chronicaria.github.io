Fantasy dossier

  • 9-cat: top 60-85 base. Points, free-throw attempts, rebounds and steals arrive first; three-point volume, rookie turnovers and NBA FG% are the swing categories.
  • Points: top 50-70 because raw usage and minutes matter more than category efficiency.
  • Dynasty: top-15 overall and a top-three 2026 rookie asset. He has the roster's best long-term first-option path.
  • Context: RotoWire expects immediate points/free throws and specifically notes that Young's gravity should open driving lanes.

Floor, median and ceiling

  • Floor: inefficient rookie shooting, crowded veteran usage and a finish outside the top 120.
  • Median: approximately 19-6-3 with useful steals, top-75 overall.
  • Ceiling: NBA-ready jumper, more on-ball work and top-40 value immediately.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Saint Sebastian's (Needham, MA), then Prolific Prep (Napa, CA), then Utah Prep (Hurricane, UT) for 2024-25 — a three-school prep path that made him the consensus No. 1 recruit in the 2025 class. One season at BYU (2025-26, Big 12): 35 games, all starts, 34.8 MPG, 25.5 points, 6.8 rebounds, 3.7 assists, 1.1 steals, 0.3 blocks on .510 FG, .331 3P, .774 FT. He led Division I in scoring, totaled 894 points (third-most by a freshman in D-I history), scored in double figures in all 35 games with 28 twenty-point games and eight thirty-point games, and became the first freshman with multiple 40-point games. Awards: Julius Erving Award, consensus first-team All-American, Big 12 Freshman of the Year, NCAA scoring champion; also FIBA U19 World Cup MVP. Draft: 2026, round 1, pick 1, Washington Wizards — no draft-night trade; signed a four-year rookie-scale deal reported at $66.91M on 2026-07-02.

NBA record: none. Dybantsa has not played an NBA regular-season game; his first will come in 2026-27.

2026 Summer League (Las Vegas): two games before Washington shut him down, 25.0 points, 7.0 rebounds, 2.0 assists on 40% shooting — 27/7/2stl/1blk against Utah, 23/7/2ast/3stl/2blk against Sacramento. Reported as the highest scoring average by a No. 1 pick in Las Vegas Summer League history. The shutdown was precautionary rather than injury-driven per the reporting.

Fantasy history: none. No NBA season has been played, so there is no category or points-league finish to cite. College production does not map cleanly to 9-cat value — the .331 college three-point rate and a turnover load consistent with a 30%-plus usage freshman are the two inputs that will decide whether his rookie line is rosterable in category leagues before it is in points leagues.

Durability: BYU 2025-26: 35 of 35 games, every one a start, at 34.8 MPG — no missed games in his college season. Summer League 2026: played 2 of 4, held out of the last two by team decision after he had established himself, not because of a reported injury. There is no professional durability record to draw on yet.

Trajectory: The only real datapoint is one college season, and it was an outlier one: no freshman had previously averaged 25/6/3, and he did it as a 6-9 wing carrying a top-25 team's entire half-court offense. The scoring came from volume plus rim pressure and a strong free-throw diet rather than from shooting efficiency — .510 FG is good for a high-usage wing but .331 from three is the number that has to move. That single split is the fulcrum for 2026-27: in Washington he will be asked to be a secondary creator next to Trae Young rather than the sole engine, which should lower his usage from BYU levels while raising his catch-and-shoot volume, exactly the diet that punishes a .331 shooter. He is 19 and at the very bottom of the aging curve, so the dynasty case does not depend on the rookie year resolving well. For redraft, the honest read is that his floor is a high-volume, low-efficiency rookie whose steals and free throws keep him near the top-100 line, and the ceiling requires the jumper to arrive a year early.
