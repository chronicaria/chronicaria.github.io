The role

Harrison is a field-stretching Z in Arizona — the deep threat, not the volume target. He catches fewer passes than a slot receiver but gains far more on each one, which makes his week-to-week scoring lumpy: three quiet games and then a 25-point Sunday. He is also not his offense's first read; the same team's move TE (a tight end used as a receiver) is projected for far more catches, so Harrison is the second mouth at the table.

The number

933 receiving yards projected, from the note column of Data/players.csv. It is a projection dated 2026-07-23, not an observation. At 0.1 points a yard that is about 93 points from yardage alone — more than his projected catches pay him at a point each. For a receiver, yardage outweighing receptions is the whole Z profile: he needs long gains to score, and long gains do not arrive evenly.

The risk

The CSV rates his risk as low, which understates one thing: the risk here is not that the role disappears, it is that the distribution is wide. A receiver who needs long gains has more weeks near zero than a receiver who needs short ones. Given the two-stage payout — top six over weeks 1–14, then a bracket in weeks 15–17 — that variance is a cost in the first stage and an asset in the second. The availability record sharpens it further: the projection pays him for 17 games, and he has played 17 once in two seasons — see Availability and injury risk.

The context

He is 24.1 on the September 9 opener and was the fourth pick of the 2024 draft out of Ohio State — about as much draft capital as a receiver ever carries, and by Draft capital dominance that alone usually guarantees him the role. What he has not done is play. Seventeen games as a rookie, then 12 in 2025: five missed across two years, with the official injury report listing him Out four weeks and Questionable or Doubtful three, against a heel and an illness. In 2025 he was on the field for 71 percent of snaps and drew 73 targets for 41 catches — an 11.8 percent target share, a second option's number rather than a fourth pick's. His fully guaranteed rookie deal runs to free agency in 2028.

Open questions

  • Does the deep role survive if Arizona's offense is throwing short to its tight end all year? The projection has both happening at once.
  • His ADP sits in the mid-60s (Fantasy Football Calculator PPR proxy, not ESPN's own) — is the market paying for the archetype or for the name?
