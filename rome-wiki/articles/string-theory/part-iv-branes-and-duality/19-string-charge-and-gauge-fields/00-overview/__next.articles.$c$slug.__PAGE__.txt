3:T286c9,<h2 id="the-picture">The picture</h2>
<p>The string is a wire carrying a current, not a rod smeared with static charge. The current is charge under the closed-string 19.1 <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-iv-branes-and-duality/19-string-charge-and-gauge-fields/kalb-ramond-field/" class="wiki-link">Kalb-Ramond field</a> <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>B</mi><mrow><mi>μ</mi><mi>ν</mi></mrow></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">B_{\mu\nu}</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.9694em;vertical-align:-0.2861em;"></span><span class="mord"><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span><span class="msupsub"><span class="vlist-t vlist-t2"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.1514em;"><span style="top:-2.55em;margin-left:-0.0502em;margin-right:0.05em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:2.7em;"></span><span class="sizing reset-size6 size3 mtight"><span class="mord mtight"><span class="mord mathnormal mtight" style="margin-right:0.0637em;">μν</span></span></span></span></span><span class="vlist-s">​</span></span><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.2861em;"><span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span></span>, and it flows along the string. A steady current in a wire has no loose ends: it loops back on itself or runs to infinity. Cut the wire in mid-air and the current stops, which is impossible.</p>
<p>So what happens when an open string ends on a D-brane, its endpoint stopping in the middle of the brane? The current must go somewhere. The module builds to the answer: the endpoint becomes an electric point charge, and the missing current continues as electric field lines on the brane. The string is the wire; the brane completes the circuit. A current can't stop, so the string's endpoint becomes a charge and hands its current off to the brane.</p>

<p>Every result in this module comes from watching that <strong>boundary term</strong>. For a closed string there is no boundary and everything is conserved. For an open string the boundary is in <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>σ</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sigma</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.4306em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0359em;">σ</span></span></span></span> — the two endpoints — and the boundary term <em>survives</em>. That surviving term is the entire physics: it is non-conservation made visible, and cancelling it is what forces the endpoint charge. This is the same "boundary terms carry the physics" theme as <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-i-just-in-time-physics/01-action-principles/boundary-terms-and-allowed-variations/" class="wiki-link">Boundary terms and allowed variations</a>, now with the boundary at the string's ends.</p>
