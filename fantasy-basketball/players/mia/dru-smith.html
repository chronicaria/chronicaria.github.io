Dru Smith

Smith is an unusually productive steals specialist per minute, but a 12-minute base role and possible veteran-guard addition keep him out of normal redraft plans.

Why this shape

  • Smith's 1.4 steals in only 16.3 minutes is a real fantasy mechanism; prorating the entire line to starter minutes would be a mistake.
  • Giannis and Bam can organize non-Mitchell minutes, allowing Miami to choose defense over traditional point-guard creation.
  • Miami still has open standard roster spots and an obvious need for another handler. The projection cuts his role before any such signing is official.
  • Tre Donaldson is on a two-way and is more developmental than immediate competition.

Format outlook

  • Nine-category: steals streamer in 16-team leagues; watchlist in 12-team formats.
  • Points: generally not draftable without a Mitchell injury.
  • Dynasty: deep roster hold only; the per-minute steals keep him relevant in 30-team formats.
  • Best use: short-term category specialist when his playing time projects above 18 minutes.

Range and watchpoints

  • Ceiling: 20 MPG, 3–4 assists and 1.5+ steals if Miami makes no guard addition or Mitchell misses time.
  • Median: 10–14 minutes and occasional matchup DNPs.
  • Floor: displaced from the regular rotation by a veteran signing or Conwell/Larsson handling development.
  • Efficiency: low shot volume limits both the harm and the upside of his percentages.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Two years at Evansville, a redshirt transfer year, then two at Missouri. He broke out as an Evansville sophomore (13.7 points, 4.6 assists, 2.0 steals on .578/.482/.862) and remained a high-steal lead guard in the SEC: 12.7 points, 3.9 assists and 2.1 steals in 2019-20, then 14.3 points, 3.8 assists and 2.1 steals on .442/.398/.833 in 2020-21. Honors: first-team All-SEC (coaches) and SEC All-Defensive Team in 2021, MVC Most Improved (2018). Born December 30, 1997. Draft: undrafted (2021). Miami signed him in September 2021; he then spent three seasons cycling between two-way deals, waivers and the Sioux Falls Skyforce before a three-year, $7.9 million contract in August 2025.

Durability: The defining feature of the career. A season-ending knee injury ended 2021-22 before he played an NBA game; an ACL tear suffered November 22, 2023 ended 2023-24 after nine games; a torn Achilles ended 2024-25 after 14 games, announced December 24, 2024. He then played 70 of 82 in 2025-26 — by an enormous margin his healthiest season and the first evidence that the body has held.

Trajectory: Five NBA seasons producing 108 games, three of them cut short by major lower-body injuries, and one full season that finally showed what the profile is: a low-usage backup point guard who organizes an offense without turning it over and gets steals at a rate almost nobody at 16 minutes matches. The 2025-26 shooting was the weak point — .295 from three on light volume gives him no scoring floor. He enters 2026-27 as the nominal backup behind Davion Mitchell with Miami still holding roster spots and an acknowledged need for another handler, so his role is not secure. He becomes fantasy-relevant if Miami adds no veteran guard and Mitchell misses time, pushing him toward 20 minutes where the steal rate alone makes him a category streamer.
