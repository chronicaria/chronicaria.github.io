Positional scarcity and eligibility in 2026-27

No major fantasy basketball platform publishes a numeric games-played threshold for position eligibility, and on the encyclopedia's own July 18 board the only genuinely scarce position is centre — and only above roughly the twenty-third centre.

1. How eligibility actually works — and why basketball is the loose sport

Fantasy baseball has hard thresholds (20 games at a position to carry it into the next season, 10 in-season to gain it). Basketball has none. Every major platform runs position eligibility as an editorial judgement, not a rule you can compute in advance. This is the single most misunderstood thing about the topic.

Platform Preseason assignment In-season gain Loss Published numeric threshold
ESPN NBA supplies an updated position list for every player, rookies included, before the season; ESPN reserves the right to override it. Once the season starts the primary position is frozen.2 A player "can gain additional eligibility for other positions during the season if circumstances warrant it" — no criteria stated.2 Not stated; secondary positions accrue None
Yahoo "A combination of box score data, past performance, outside websites that track positional data, and a few of our trusted reporters."3 Commissioners may add positions via Custom Position Eligibility, never remove or edit them.3 Continuous; Yahoo publishes a running position-change feed.4 Not stated None
Fantrax Initial eligibility set by Fantrax. A player may gain eligibility at other positions in-season; "he will never lose eligibility at any position he initially qualified for or earned."5 Explicitly never None
Sleeper Official team designations set the primary position.6 Case-by-case using past and projected usage, role consistency, team utilisation, and stage of season. "We do not rely on strict minimum thresholds for games played or minutes logged."6 Not stated None, stated explicitly

Three practical consequences.

  1. You cannot pre-draft an eligibility edge. Because gain is discretionary, no public tracker can tell you in August that a given player will pick up centre in December. Anyone selling you that is guessing.
  2. Eligibility is sticky and accretive on every platform. Fantrax says so outright; ESPN and Yahoo behave the same way in practice. So a player who already carries two positions carries a permanent, riskless asset. A player who might gain one carries a lottery ticket.
  3. Platform choice changes the board more than most people think. ESPN's frozen-primary rule matters because on ESPN, league roster minimums and maximums key off the primary position. Yahoo's commissioner-add tool means a friendly league can manufacture eligibility that Fantrax never would. If Andrew's league is on Yahoo, confirm whether Custom Position Eligibility is enabled before valuing dual-eligibility at all.

One more platform detail that is not eligibility but is often confused with it: Yahoo caps games per roster slot in rotisserie — public free and prize leagues default to 82 games per position — while head-to-head leagues have no such cap.1 In roto, a thin centre slot means you burn 82 centre games on whoever you have; in H2H it means nothing.

Two things jump out. First, 65% of the top 100 is multi-eligible — which is why guard and forward "scarcity" is fictional. There are 48 guard-eligible and 49 forward-eligible players in the top 100 for a 12-team league that needs roughly 24–36 of each. Supply exceeds demand by a wide margin at every non-centre slot.

Second, centre is the only bucket where supply is genuinely tight, and it is tight for a structural reason: centre is where the sole-position players live. Thirteen of the 27 centre-eligible top-100 players can only be played at centre or UTIL. That rigidity is the real cost, not the raw count.

The shape of the curve. The gap from C2 to C3 is 6.35 z — the largest single step anywhere on the board. The gap from C3 to C4 is 1.64. Then the curve goes almost flat: C4 through C23 spans 5.52 down to 2.06, i.e. 3.46 z across twenty players, or 0.18 z per step. Spread over nine categories that is roughly \(0.019\) z per category per rung. The true cliff is after C23, where the next four names give back 1.28 z in four steps and Gobert's FT% turns him into a build constraint rather than a player.

So: is waiting on centre viable? Do the demand arithmetic rather than trusting instinct. Let \(T\) be teams and \(s\) the number of dedicated C slots.

\[D = T \times s \quad\text{(guaranteed centre starts required)}\]

  • 12 teams, 2 C slots: \(D = 24\). Supply of playable centres is 23 (through Şengün) before the cliff at C24. Demand and supply are exactly balanced, and UTIL slots push real demand above 24. Waiting is dangerous: if two rivals roster three centres each, the last team to move ends up on Gobert or Turner and eats a category loss.
  • 12 teams, 1 C slot: \(D = 12\). Supply 23. Comfortable surplus of 11. Waiting works.
  • 10 teams, 2 C slots: \(D = 20\). Supply 23. Surplus of 3. Workable but thin.
  • 10 teams, 1 C slot: \(D = 10\). Supply 23. Surplus of 13. Waiting is clearly correct — the C11–C20 block (Adebayo through Mark Williams) carries a mean z of ≈ +2.8, and every one of those is available well after the point where the top guards are gone.

The single decision-relevant number: the mean z of C4–C10 is +4.88; the mean of C14–C20 is +2.71. You pay roughly forty board slots for 2.17 z, or about a quarter of a z per category. In a 10-team league that is a bad trade. In 12-team with two C slots it is close to fair. Take the elite big only if he is genuinely the best player available — Jokić and Wembanyama are, and Kessler's +7.16 is a modelled outlier the board itself has already calibrated down to No. 39 for build-fit reasons.

4. Multi-position eligibility as an asset

Multi-eligibility is worth something concrete and something imaginary. The concrete part: in a daily-lineup league it raises the number of games you can actually start, which in a 9-cat H2H is a direct volume gain. The imaginary part: in weekly-lock leagues with generous UTIL counts, it is nearly worthless.

How much is it worth? Honest answer: less than the discourse implies, and it is a tiebreaker rather than a driver. A defensible way to price it is as an expected-starts gain:

\[\Delta V \approx \frac{\text{extra startable games per week}}{\text{roster starts per week}} \times V_{\text{player}}\]

In a daily league with tight slots, one extra start per week on a 40-start week is a \(\approx 2.5\%\) value bump. That is real but it is not a round. Never move a player up more than a few spots for eligibility alone. The exception is PF/C in a 12-team, two-C league, where dual eligibility resolves the exact structural bind identified in §3 — there, a Towns or a Sabonis is doing work no single-slot player can do.

5. The positionless caveat

In 9-cat, position is nearly a distraction. The categories do not care what slot a player occupies; they care about his statistical shape. Two worked cores from the vault's own projections, eight players each, per-game team totals:

Build 3PM PTS REB AST STL BLK TO mean FG% mean FT%
Guard-heavy (SGA, Maxey, Curry, Brunson, Herro, Sheppard, Daniels, Melton) 19.2 167.7 33.2 41.9 10.5 3.4 17.1 .473 .838
Big-heavy (Wembanyama, Holmgren, Duren, Kessler, Clingan, Edey, Gafford, Zubac) 4.8 127.6 83.6 16.4 5.7 14.5 13.6 .602 .736
Balanced (Jokić, Cunningham, Murphy, Mobley, D. White, Okongwu, Daniels, Reid) 15.0 151.4 59.3 44.1 10.0 7.5 17.9 .495 .782

The guard core wins 3PM, PTS, AST, STL and FT% by enormous margins and loses REB, BLK and FG% by equally enormous ones — a 5–4 or 4–5 weekly coin flip depending on TO. The big core is the mirror image: +50.4 REB and +11.1 BLK, but only 4.8 threes across eight players, which in a 12-team league is a guaranteed weekly zero in 3PM. The balanced core wins nothing by a landslide but is competitive in eight of nine.

This is the point: the position labels are downstream of the category shape. A "guard-heavy build" is really a 3PM/AST/STL/FT% build, and a "big-heavy build" is really a FG%/REB/BLK build. If you are drafting a punt (see Punt strategies for nine-category leagues), you should be selecting on shape and letting position fall out — and then checking, once, that you can legally field a lineup.

Position scarcity is a genuine constraint when: (a) the league uses two dedicated C slots with 12 teams; (b) the format is roto with Yahoo's 82-games-per-position cap, where a thin slot is a hard ceiling on accumulation;1 (c) the roster has few UTIL slots, so shape cannot be laundered through flex.

It is a distraction when: (a) the league runs three or more UTIL slots, which is the modern default and which dissolves nearly all positional pressure; (b) the format is points, where 2026-27 points-league rankings shows that position correlates with nothing except minutes and usage — Şengün's points rank of 19 versus his 9-cat rank of 72 is the cleanest illustration in the pool; (c) you are in a 10-team league of any configuration.

The correct order of operations at the draft: shape first, scarcity second, eligibility third. Only the two-C, 12-team case ever promotes scarcity above shape.

Open questions

  • ESPN's support article could not be fetched directly (the page returns HTTP 403 to automated requests); its eligibility language here comes from search-engine extraction of that page. Before the draft, open the article in a browser and confirm the frozen-primary-position rule verbatim.
  • No platform has published 2026-27 position assignments as of the July 18 cutoff. Every position label in this note is a dossier label or an official NBA roster label, not verified platform eligibility. The dual-eligibility table in §4 is the highest-risk section and must be re-verified in October — 2026-27 fantasy basketball rankings flags "sourced platform position eligibility" as an outstanding board repair.
  • The z-scores driving §3 inherit the projection pipeline's known ratio limitation: many dossiers still lack explicit FGA/FTA, so FG%/FT% impact is proxied. This most affects the low-volume centres (Gafford, Lively, Zubac), whose FG% edge may be overstated relative to true attempt volume. See 2026-27 fantasy basketball projection methodology.
  • The multi-eligibility value formula in §4 is a construction, not a measured effect. The 2.5% figure is an illustrative calculation, not an empirical finding; no public study quantifying the fantasy-basketball value of dual eligibility was located.
