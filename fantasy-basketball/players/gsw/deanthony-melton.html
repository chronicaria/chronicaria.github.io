Correction 2026-07-18: Melton's allocation is reduced from 28 minutes to 22 and his median MPG from 27.5 to 24.0. ESPN's Anthony Slater reported July 10 that Golden State and Gary Payton II have mutual interest in another one-year veteran minimum (SI). Payton is the same archetype — point-of-attack defense, low usage — and the previous 28-minute figure assumed he was gone. The steals rate is unchanged; only the minutes carrying it moved.

Role and evidence

Melton averaged 12.3 points, 3.2 rebounds, 2.6 assists, 1.6 steals, and 1.5 threes in 23.0 minutes across 49 games in 2025-26, shooting .407 from the field and .826 at the line (ESPN). The Warriors then reached a reported two-year, $11 million agreement with a second-year player option.

The base depth chart uses Melton for 18 shooting-guard minutes and four backup-point minutes. Butler and Moody are not opening-night options, but an expected Payton return supplies a second point-of-attack defender and caps the upside case. Butler's return lowers Melton's scoring ceiling but need not erase his closing role because defense and low-usage spacing fit the veteran core.

Steals are the draft reason; poor FG% on double-digit attempts is the cost. The projection regresses his 29.4% three-point season upward without restoring an old peak wholesale. His prior ACL history and recent 49-game season justify a 62-game median rather than a full durability assumption.

Range

  • Ceiling: 30 MPG, 2.0 steals, 2.2 threes, and top-65 nine-cat value while Butler ramps slowly.
  • Median: rosterable in 12-team category leagues, more valuable to punt-FG% builds than balanced teams.
  • Floor: the agreement changes, health interrupts the season, or a healthy Butler compresses him below 23 minutes.

Career record

Pre-NBA: USC, one season played. As a 2016-17 freshman he appeared in 36 games with 25 starts at 27.0 MPG, producing 8.3 points, 4.7 rebounds, 3.5 assists, 1.9 steals and 1.0 blocks — he led the Pac-12 and tied a USC freshman record with 69 steals, earned honorable-mention Pac-12 All-Defensive honors, and became the first Division I freshman since Dwyane Wade in 2001-02 to reach 300 points, 150 rebounds, 100 assists, 60 steals and 35 blocks in a season. Shooting splits for that year are not carried by the sources I used, so none are asserted. He never played a second college season: USC suspended him in 2017-18 over a family member's involvement in the federal college-basketball fraud investigation, and he withdrew from the school and declared for the draft on 2018-02-21, sitting out an entire year of competitive basketball. Draft: 2018, round 2, pick 46, Houston — traded to Phoenix on 2018-08-31 with Ryan Anderson for Brandon Knight and Marquese Chriss, before ever appearing for the Rockets.

No season here is a multi-team split — he was traded to Brooklyn on 2024-12-15 while already shut down after ACL surgery and never appeared for them, so the six-game 2024-25 line is entirely Golden State.

Fantasy history: Season-end 9-category ranks from a rank provider (Basketball Monster / Hashtag / ESPN Player Rater) were not verifiable for this entry, so no per-season numbers are asserted. What the box score supports: 2022-23 was the best full-season category year, because it is the only one that paired the elite steal rate with 77 games and 2.0 threes — availability, not rate, is what made it. The shape has been remarkably stable for eight seasons and it is lopsided by design: steals at 1.2 to 1.6 every single year are the entire draft case, threes on real volume are the second column, turnovers around 1.3 are quietly a third, and field-goal percentage in the .386-.438 band is a persistent drag that only a punt build absorbs comfortably. Rebounds, assists and blocks have never been more than filler. He grades materially worse in points leagues than in categories, and the reason is structural rather than situational: steals and low turnovers are worth a great deal across nine columns and very little in a format that mostly counts scoring volume, which he has never had.

Durability: GP by season — 50, 60, 52, 73, 77, 38, 6, 49. Context matters for three of those: 2019-20 was a 73-game Memphis season and 2020-21 a 72-game league season, so 60 and 52 are less alarming than they read, and the rookie year mixed a right ankle sprain on 2019-01-24 with ordinary rotation limits. The genuinely healthy stretch is 2021-22 and 2022-23, 150 games across two years. Then the injuries get serious. 2023-24 was destroyed by recurring lumbar spine bone stress: roughly six weeks out, a late-February return, three games, and another shutdown, though he needed no offseason surgery. 2024-25 ended after six games — a partially torn left ACL suffered on 2024-11-12 against Dallas, with season-ending surgery in Los Angeles in early December 2024. 2025-26 was the rehab year: he re-signed with Golden State on 2025-10-01 while still recovering, did not debut until 2025-12-04 against Philadelphia, and the Warriors then held him out of one leg of essentially every back-to-back for the rest of the season, plus a three-game absence between 2026-03-07 and 2026-03-15. He finished with 49 games and 24 starts and, importantly, his season did not end early. He declined a $3.45 million player option on 2026-06-29 and re-signed for two years and $11 million with a player option for 2027-28.

Trajectory: The counterintuitive fact in this file is that 2025-26, the season that looks worst by raw totals, was his best per-minute season ever. Per 36 minutes he scored 19.3 points, comfortably above the 17.1 of his 2021-22 peak and far above the 13.0 of the 77-game 2022-23 season, and his steal rate of roughly 2.5 per 36 was also a career high — a year removed from ACL surgery, on a knee the team was actively protecting. The 23.0 minutes were a medical cap, not a verdict on the player, which is the single most important thing to understand before pricing him. What went wrong was the three-point percentage: .294 against a .358 career mark and .371-.412 in his good years, on volume he never reduced, which is the kind of outlier that regresses rather than persists. He turns 29 in May, so he is at the back edge of prime but not past it, and the second year removed from an ACL repair is historically when the explosiveness that drives a steal rate comes back rather than continues declining. For 2026-27 the entire projection is a minutes question. The rate profile says a healthy 28-minute Melton is a top-70 nine-cat player in the right build; the history says he has cleared 70 games twice in eight seasons, and a spine problem plus an ACL in consecutive years is a durability record you discount, not one you talk yourself out of. Draft him as a category specialist with a real ceiling and an availability distribution wide enough that he should never be the load-bearing piece.
