    Gauge fixing is picking one convenient description out of the many that all mean the same physical thing.

    Lowest terms

    A gauge redundancy is like the difference between "3/6" and "1/2": two symbols, one number. Gauge fixing is the rule "always write fractions in lowest terms" — it changes no number, it just stops you from carrying the same fact around in five disguises. For the string, the disguises are the coordinate grids (τ,σ)(\tau,\sigma)(τ,σ) you can paint on a worldsheet; the number is the physical surface the string sweeps out. Fix a gauge and you compute with one grid instead of infinitely many.

    Everything below obeys one rule: gauge fixing removes fake variables, never real physics. If a quantity changes when you re-fix the gauge, it was never a physical quantity to begin with.

    Redundancy as an equivalence relation

    Gauge redundancy is not uncertainty about the system. It is surplus notation. To make that precise, let D\mathcal DD be a space of descriptions — for a string, all maps Xμ(τ,σ)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)Xμ(τ,σ) that trace out a worldsheet. A gauge group GGG acts on D\mathcal DD by relabeling, and two descriptions related by GGG are declared equivalent:

    d1∼d2⟺d2=g⋅d1 for some g∈G.d_1\sim d_2 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad d_2=g\cdot d_1\ \text{for some }g\in G.d1​∼d2​⟺d2​=g⋅d1​ for some g∈G.

    The physical object is the whole equivalence class (the orbit), not any single member, so the real physical space is the quotient D/G\mathcal D/GD/G. The prototype from applied math is a curve with many parameterizations: as functions, γ(s)\gamma(s)γ(s) and γ(f(s))\gamma(f(s))γ(f(s)) look different, yet they trace the same geometric curve whenever fff is an allowed reparameterization. The curve is the orbit; the choice of sss is a representative.

    Gauge fixing picks one representative from each orbit — a section of the quotient:

    gauge fixing  :  D/G→D.\text{gauge fixing}\;:\;\mathcal D/G\to\mathcal D .gauge fixing:D/G→D.

    Nothing physical is lost: the section deletes only coordinates that never encoded a physical distinction. It need not be global, though — a single gauge can miss some orbits, hit an orbit twice, or leave leftover freedom. That is a limitation of the chosen slice, not of the idea.

    Two rules follow immediately:

    • Observables are orbit-constant. Anything physical must take the same value on every representative of an orbit.
    • Constraints are the phase-space shadow of the redundancy. The redundancy acts on descriptions; in the Hamiltonian picture its fingerprint is a set of constraints, as in Constraints as restrictions on motion.

    Worldsheet reparameterization is the string's gauge group

    The Nambu–Goto action depends only on the geometric surface swept out in spacetime, not on the grid you draw on it. Swapping coordinates (τ,σ)→(τ~,σ~)(\tau,\sigma)\to(\tilde\tau,\tilde\sigma)(τ,σ)→(τ~,σ~) rewrites the functions Xμ(τ,σ)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)Xμ(τ,σ) but leaves the embedded worldsheet untouched. Reparameterization is the gauge group GGG here.

    This is why string momentum is naturally integrated over a curve rather than pinned to one favorite time-slice. The momentum

    pμ(γ)=∫γ(Pμτ dσ−Pμσ dτ)(1)p_\mu(\gamma)=\int_\gamma \left(\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,d\sigma-\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,d\tau\right) \tag{1}pμ​(γ)=∫γ​(Pμτ​dσ−Pμσ​dτ)(1)

    returns the same physical answer for any curve γ\gammaγ that slices across the worldsheet once, so long as deforming one such curve into another sweeps across no momentum-leaking boundary (derived in World-sheet momentum current). The curve and the coordinates are description data; pμp_\mupμ​ is orbit-constant, hence physical.

    Static gauge: worldsheet time is the observer's clock

    Static gauge ties worldsheet time to target-space time for one chosen Lorentz observer (keeping ccc explicit for the moment):

    X0(τ,σ)=c τ.X^0(\tau,\sigma)=c\,\tau .X0(τ,σ)=cτ.

    Every constant-τ\tauτ slice is now a literal snapshot of the string at one instant of the observer's clock — in this gauge τ\tauτ is the observer's time t=X0/ct=X^0/ct=X0/c. The worldsheet conservation law becomes the everyday one,

    dpμdτ=0⟺dpμdt=0.\frac{dp_\mu}{d\tau}=0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \frac{dp_\mu}{dt}=0 .dτdpμ​​=0⟺dtdpμ​​=0.

    Nothing new was proven — the same conserved pμp_\mupμ​ existed before the gauge choice (that is the content of (1)). Static gauge merely relabels the clock so the bookkeeping reads like Newton's.

    Static gauge is partial: it spends the τ\tauτ-relabeling freedom but still lets you relabel σ\sigmaσ along each slice, and it is tied to one observer's frame. Light-cone gauge, next, spends both functions at once — a different clock, plus a σ\sigmaσ-rule to match.

    Light-cone gauge: spend the freedom to expose the real degrees of freedom

    Light-cone gauge is the workhorse for quantization. Build light-cone coordinates from time and one chosen spatial direction,

    X±=12 (X0±X1),X^\pm=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\,(X^0\pm X^1),X±=2​1​(X0±X1),

    so a target-space vector is described by (X+,X−,XI)(X^+,X^-,X^I)(X+,X−,XI) with I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1 the transverse directions. The gauge makes X+X^+X+ run linearly with worldsheet time, at a rate set by the string's own momentum:

    X+(τ,σ)=β α′p+ τ,β={2,open string1,closed string,(2)X^+(\tau,\sigma)=\beta\,\alpha' p^+\,\tau , \qquad \beta=\begin{cases}2,&\text{open string}\\[2pt]1,&\text{closed string,}\end{cases} \tag{2}X+(τ,σ)=βα′p+τ,β={2,1,​open stringclosed string,​(2)

    Here and below we adopt the natural units ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1 of the light-cone modules. The factor of 2 tracks the σ\sigmaσ-interval convention (open σ∈[0,π]\sigma\in[0,\pi]σ∈[0,π], closed σ∈[0,2π]\sigma\in[0,2\pi]σ∈[0,2π]), and Light-cone gauge fixes the constant from the mode expansion. The choice is legal exactly when p+=12(p0+p1)≠0p^+=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(p^0+p^1)\neq0p+=2​1​(p0+p1)=0 — automatic for every massive state, and failing only for a massless string aimed exactly along the negative X1X^1X1 axis.

    The payoff is structural. Condition (2) spends only the τ\tauτ-relabeling; the companion σ\sigmaσ-choice spreads the p+p^+p+-density uniformly along each slice, and together the two conditions put the constraints of Constraints as restrictions on motion in the form

    X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.\dot X\cdot X'=0,\qquad \dot X^2+X'^2=0 .X˙⋅X′=0,X˙2+X′2=0.

    With X+X^+X+ frozen, these can be solved algebraically for X−X^-X−. The light-cone dot product a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbIa\cdot b=-a^+b^--a^-b^++a^Ib^Ia⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI (mostly-plus signature) has no (a−)2(a^-)^2(a−)2 term, so both constraints are linear in the derivatives of X−X^-X−: dividing by the constant X˙+=βα′p+\dot X^+=\beta\alpha'p^+X˙+=βα′p+ hands you X˙−\dot X^-X˙− and X′−X'^-X′− in terms of the transverse fields, which determines X−X^-X− up to one constant x0−x_0^-x0−​. The bookkeeping is now settled:

    • X+X^+X+ is frozen by the gauge,
    • X−X^-X− is determined by the constraints (up to the zero mode x0−x_0^-x0−​),
    • only the transverse XIX^IXI are free.

    The independent physical vibrations are the D−2D-2D−2 transverse modes. In quotient language: the gauge chose a slice through the orbits, the constraints solved for the dependent coordinates on it, and what parameterizes D/G\mathcal D/GD/G is the transverse fields XIX^IXI plus two longitudinal constants, p+p^+p+ and x0−x_0^-x0−​. A string in DDD spacetime dimensions vibrates in only D−2D-2D−2 transverse ways.

    What you must re-check after fixing a gauge

    Fixing a gauge can hide a symmetry that was obvious before. Light-cone gauge singles out X0X^0X0 and X1X^1X1, so Lorentz invariance — which rotates all directions into each other — is no longer manifest. Classically the symmetry is still there, just disguised. Quantum-mechanically it is not automatic: it survives only if an anomaly cancels, and demanding that cancellation is precisely what pins the bosonic critical dimension to D=26D=26D=26.

    The moral generalizes. A gauge-fixed calculation is only trustworthy if its answers are independent of which representative you picked. If they are not, the gauge has not created a problem — it has exposed a real inconsistency hiding in the quantized theory.

    Open questions

    • The constant in (2) — the open/closed factor of 2 and its convention dependence — is fixed by the mode expansion in Light-cone gauge, together with the σ\sigmaσ-parameterization that spends the second function of gauge freedom.
    • Global gauge-fixing failures (a section that misses or double-counts orbits) and residual/Gribov-type freedom become real issues beyond the classical, contractible-worldsheet setting; they are not needed before the classical light-cone module.

    Exercise

    Count the physical degrees of freedom of a classical bosonic string in DDD spacetime dimensions, tracking exactly where each gauge choice and each constraint removes one. You start with the DDD fields Xμ(τ,σ)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)Xμ(τ,σ). Show the surviving count is D−2D-2D−2, and name the field that each step eliminates.

    Solution

    Start with DDD oscillating fields XμX^\muXμ, μ=0,…,D−1\mu=0,\dots,D-1μ=0,…,D−1.

    1. Worldsheet reparameterization is a gauge redundancy with two functions' worth of freedom (τ\tauτ- and σ\sigmaσ-relabeling), so we may impose two gauge conditions. Light-cone gauge spends the τ\tauτ-function on X+=β α′p+τX^+=\beta\,\alpha'p^+\tauX+=βα′p+τ (2): X+X^+X+ carries no independent oscillations — it is frozen. The σ\sigmaσ-function goes into fixing the grid along each slice (a uniform p+p^+p+-density); that removes labeling freedom, not a field. Fields removed: one (X+X^+X+). Count: D−1D-1D−1.
    2. The two constraints X˙⋅X′=0\dot X\cdot X'=0X˙⋅X′=0 and X˙2+X′2=0\dot X^2+X'^2=0X˙2+X′2=0 are not evolution equations. With X+X^+X+ frozen, X′+=0X'^+=0X′+=0 and X˙+=βα′p+\dot X^+=\beta\alpha'p^+X˙+=βα′p+, and the light-cone dot product a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbIa\cdot b=-a^+b^--a^-b^++a^Ib^Ia⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI makes each constraint linear in a minus-derivative: the first solves for X′−=X˙IX′I/(βα′p+)X'^-=\dot X^IX'^I/(\beta\alpha'p^+)X′−=X˙IX′I/(βα′p+), the second for X˙−=(X˙IX˙I+X′IX′I)/(2βα′p+)\dot X^-=\big(\dot X^I\dot X^I+X'^IX'^I\big)/(2\beta\alpha'p^+)X˙−=(X˙IX˙I+X′IX′I)/(2βα′p+) — the division by p+p^+p+ is where p+≠0p^+\neq0p+=0 is used. Both derivatives fixed means X−X^-X− is determined up to its constant zero mode x0−x_0^-x0−​: its oscillators αn−\alpha_n^-αn−​ and momentum p−p^-p− are all composites of transverse data. Fields removed: one more (X−X^-X−). Count: D−2D-2D−2.
    3. What is left are the transverse coordinates XIX^IXI, I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1 — exactly D−2D-2D−2 independent fields.

    One field killed by the gauge choice (X+X^+X+), one killed by the constraints the redundancy left behind (X−X^-X−); the D−2D-2D−2 transverse vibrations are the only physical degrees of freedom. (Demanding that the quantized transverse theory still close into the Lorentz algebra is what later forces D=26D=26D=26.)
