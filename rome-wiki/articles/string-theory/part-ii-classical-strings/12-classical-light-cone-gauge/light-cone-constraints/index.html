    The shadow determines the string

    Each point of the string has a velocity, and the string through that point has a local stretch. The constraints lock the two together and, crucially, lock the direction: the physical motion is transverse — perpendicular to the string. Nothing genuinely new happens along the string; sliding a point down its own length just relabels which σ\sigmaσ sits where.

    Think of the transverse wiggling XIX^IXI as the string's shadow on the screen perpendicular to the light-cone axis. In ordinary coordinates, reconstructing the full motion from a shadow needs a square root (depth is ambiguous). In light-cone coordinates the "depth" coordinate X−X^-X− comes back with no square root at all — you just divide. One algebraic accident is responsible: the light-cone metric has no (X−)2(X^-)^2(X−)2 term, so the constraint, which looks quadratic, is secretly linear in X−X^-X−. Given the shadow, the whole string follows.

    Where the constraints come from

    Choosing τ\tauτ as worldsheet time (in Fixing X-plus as worldsheet time) and the matching uniform-density σ\sigmaσ ruler forces two conditions on the worldsheet (natural units, ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1):

    \dot X \cdot X' = 0, \qquad \dot X^2 + X'^2 = 0. \tag{1}

    The first says lines of constant τ\tauτ and constant σ\sigmaσ are orthogonal on the worldsheet — geometrically, the string's velocity is perpendicular to the string, no sloshing along its own length. The second ties the local velocity to the local stretching. These are the constraints met in static gauge (Wave equation and constraints, Module 11), with units now folded into α′\alpha'α′.

    The useful repackaging is to add and subtract them:

    \boxed{\,(\dot X \pm X')^2 = 0\,}. \tag{2}

    Expand: (X˙±X′)2=X˙2±2 X˙⋅X′+X′2(\dot X \pm X')^2 = \dot X^2 \pm 2\,\dot X\cdot X' + X'^2(X˙±X′)2=X˙2±2X˙⋅X′+X′2. The cross term dies by the first constraint in (1); what remains is X˙2+X′2\dot X^2 + X'^2X˙2+X′2, which dies by the second. Both signs together carry exactly the two original constraints.

    Reading the constraint in light-cone coordinates

    From Light-cone coordinates, the dot product in mostly-plus signature is off-diagonal in the (+,−)(+,-)(+,−) sector:

    a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI.a\cdot b = -a^+ b^- - a^- b^+ + a^I b^I.a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI.

    Write Vμ≡X˙μ±Xμ′V^\mu \equiv \dot X^\mu \pm X^{\mu\prime}Vμ≡X˙μ±Xμ′, so the constraint (2) is just V⋅V=0V\cdot V = 0V⋅V=0. Applying the dot product with a=b=Va=b=Va=b=V:

    V⋅V=−2 V+V−+VIVI=0.V\cdot V = -2\,V^+ V^- + V^I V^I = 0.V⋅V=−2V+V−+VIVI=0.

    Spelling out V±=X˙±±X±′V^\pm = \dot X^\pm \pm X^{\pm\prime}V±=X˙±±X±′ and VI=X˙I±XI′V^I = \dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}VI=X˙I±XI′:

    -2\big(\dot X^+ \pm X^{+\prime}\big)\big(\dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime}\big) + \big(\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2 = 0. \tag{3}

    Look at what is missing: there is no (V−)2(V^-)^2(V−)2 term. That term would need η−−\eta_{--}η−−​, and η−−=0\eta_{--}=0η−−​=0. So (3), though it came from a quadratic V⋅VV\cdot VV⋅V, is linear in V−=X˙−±X−′V^- = \dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime}V−=X˙−±X−′. The off-diagonal metric is doing all the work.

    Solving for the longitudinal motion

    Now use the gauge condition. From Fixing X-plus as worldsheet time, X+=βα′p+τX^+ = \beta\alpha' p^+\tauX+=βα′p+τ with β=2\beta=2β=2 for open strings and β=1\beta=1β=1 for closed, so

    X˙+=βα′p+,X+′=0⟹V+=X˙+±X+′=βα′p+,\dot X^+ = \beta\alpha' p^+, \qquad X^{+\prime} = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V^+ = \dot X^+ \pm X^{+\prime} = \beta\alpha' p^+,X˙+=βα′p+,X+′=0⟹V+=X˙+±X+′=βα′p+,

    a constant, the same for both signs. Equation (3) is linear in V−V^-V− with a constant coefficient, so solving it is a division:

    \boxed{\;\dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime} = \frac{1}{2\beta\alpha' p^+}\,\big(\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2\;}. \tag{4}

    This one line is the module. The two longitudinal combinations X˙−±X−′\dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime}X˙−±X−′ are completely determined by the transverse data X˙I±XI′\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}X˙I±XI′. No square root was taken — we divided by the constant X˙+\dot X^+X˙+. Contrast static gauge (Module 11), where the constraints could only be imposed, never inverted.

    For open strings β=2\beta = 2β=2:

    \dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime} = \frac{1}{4\alpha' p^+}\big(\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2 \qquad \text{(open string)}. \tag{5}

    The 444 is where the module's normalization is most often misremembered. It is 2×22\times 22×2: one 222 from the light-cone dot product −2V+V−-2V^+V^-−2V+V−, the other from the open-string value X˙+=2α′p+\dot X^+ = 2\alpha' p^+X˙+=2α′p+.

    Two ingredients were both essential:

    1. Light-cone coordinates — the off-diagonal (+,−)(+,-)(+,−) metric killed the (V−)2(V^-)^2(V−)2 term, making the constraint linear in X−X^-X−.
    2. Light-cone gauge — it made X˙+=βα′p+\dot X^+ = \beta\alpha' p^+X˙+=βα′p+ a constant we could divide by.

    Neither alone cracks it; together they do.

    What this buys: X−X^-X− up to one number

    Adding and subtracting the two signs of (4) gives both partial derivatives of X−X^-X−:

    X˙−=A+B2,X−′=A−B2,A≡X˙−+X−′,  B≡X˙−−X−′,\dot X^- = \frac{A+B}{2}, \qquad X^{-\prime} = \frac{A-B}{2}, \qquad A \equiv \dot X^-+X^{-\prime},\ \ B \equiv \dot X^--X^{-\prime},X˙−=2A+B​,X−′=2A−B​,A≡X˙−+X−′,  B≡X˙−−X−′,

    and by (4) the right-hand sides are known functions of the transverse fields. Knowing both partials of a field on the worldsheet pins it down up to a single additive constant x0−x_0^-x0−​:

    X−(τ,σ) is fixed by the XI, up to one number x0−.X^-(\tau,\sigma) \text{ is fixed by the } X^I, \text{ up to one number } x_0^-.X−(τ,σ) is fixed by the XI, up to one number x0−​.

    This is the precise sense in which X−X^-X− is not an independent dynamical field. The constraints are not extra equations the transverse waves must satisfy after the fact — once XIX^IXI solves the wave equation, these formulas define X−X^-X−. See Transverse coordinates as physical degrees of freedom for the mode-by-mode version; the zero mode of (4), fed through the Mass-shell relation, becomes the mass formula in Mode expansions in light-cone gauge.

    We divided by p+p^+p+, so the formulas need p+≠0p^+ \neq 0p+=0. Physical momentum has p0=E≥∣p⃗∣p^0 = E \geq |\vec p|p0=E≥∣p​∣ (classically M2≥0M^2 \geq 0M2≥0), so p+=(p0+p1)/2≥0p^+ = (p^0+p^1)/\sqrt2 \geq 0p+=(p0+p1)/2​≥0, with equality only when E=−p1E = -p^1E=−p1. Then E2=(p1)2+pIpI+M2E^2 = (p^1)^2 + p^I p^I + M^2E2=(p1)2+pIpI+M2 collapses to pIpI+M2=0p^I p^I + M^2 = 0pIpI+M2=0, forcing M=0M = 0M=0 and pI=0p^I = 0pI=0: a massless object moving exactly along −x1-x^1−x1. Anything else has p+>0p^+ > 0p+>0, and the formalism applies.

    Consistency: the reconstructed X−X^-X− is a genuine field

    Two derivatives don't automatically come from one function. We assigned X˙−\dot X^-X˙− and X−′X^{-\prime}X−′ independently through AAA and BBB; for a single field X−(τ,σ)X^-(\tau,\sigma)X−(τ,σ) to exist, they must satisfy the mixed-partials condition

    ∂σX˙−=∂τX−′.\partial_\sigma \dot X^- = \partial_\tau X^{-\prime}.∂σ​X˙−=∂τ​X−′.

    Check it. With the open-string coefficient κ≡1/(4α′p+)\kappa \equiv 1/(4\alpha' p^+)κ≡1/(4α′p+), we have A=κ(X˙I+XI′)2A = \kappa(\dot X^I + X^{I\prime})^2A=κ(X˙I+XI′)2 and B=κ(X˙I−XI′)2B = \kappa(\dot X^I - X^{I\prime})^2B=κ(X˙I−XI′)2. The transverse fields obey the wave equation X¨I−XI′′=0\ddot X^I - X^{I\prime\prime}=0X¨I−XI′′=0, which factors neatly on the chiral combinations:

    (∂τ−∂σ)(X˙I+XI′)=X¨I−XI′′=0,(∂τ+∂σ)(X˙I−XI′)=X¨I−XI′′=0.(\partial_\tau - \partial_\sigma)(\dot X^I + X^{I\prime}) = \ddot X^I - X^{I\prime\prime} = 0, \qquad (\partial_\tau + \partial_\sigma)(\dot X^I - X^{I\prime}) = \ddot X^I - X^{I\prime\prime} = 0.(∂τ​−∂σ​)(X˙I+XI′)=X¨I−XI′′=0,(∂τ​+∂σ​)(X˙I−XI′)=X¨I−XI′′=0.

    So AAA is purely left-moving (a function of τ+σ\tau+\sigmaτ+σ) and BBB is purely right-moving (a function of τ−σ\tau-\sigmaτ−σ):

    (∂σ−∂τ)A=0,(∂σ+∂τ)B=0.(\partial_\sigma - \partial_\tau)A = 0, \qquad (\partial_\sigma + \partial_\tau)B = 0.(∂σ​−∂τ​)A=0,(∂σ​+∂τ​)B=0.

    Feeding these into ∂σX˙−−∂τX−′=12[(∂σ−∂τ)A+(∂σ+∂τ)B]\partial_\sigma\dot X^- - \partial_\tau X^{-\prime} = \tfrac12[(\partial_\sigma-\partial_\tau)A + (\partial_\sigma+\partial_\tau)B]∂σ​X˙−−∂τ​X−′=21​[(∂σ​−∂τ​)A+(∂σ​+∂τ​)B] gives zero.

    The mixed partials agree, so the two formulas reconstruct one honest field X−X^-X− from the transverse solution, up to the single constant x0−x_0^-x0−​. The boundary conditions cooperate too: at a free endpoint XI′=0X^{I\prime}=0XI′=0, so A=BA=BA=B and X−′=0X^{-\prime}=0X−′=0 — the reconstructed X−X^-X− satisfies its own Neumann condition automatically.

    One notational trap

    Two different ±\pm±'s live near this material. There are worldsheet light-cone coordinates σ±=τ±σ\sigma^\pm = \tau \pm \sigmaσ±=τ±σ (used to write the constraint as (∂+X)2=(∂−X)2=0(\partial_+X)^2 = (\partial_-X)^2 = 0(∂+​X)2=(∂−​X)2=0 and to define the Virasoro modes LnL_nLn​ — see Virasoro constraints), and there are spacetime light-cone coordinates x±=(x0±x1)/2x^\pm = (x^0\pm x^1)/\sqrt2x±=(x0±x1)/2​ (the X±X^\pmX± of this note). They share the symbol ±\pm± and nothing else. The two-stage story: first impose the constraints covariantly, Ln=0L_n = 0Ln​=0 with every component of XμX^\muXμ in play; then, after choosing spacetime light-cone gauge, solve them for the −-− components — equation (4). Same physics, different bookkeeping.

    Open questions

    • For closed strings the same local solution (4) holds with β=1\beta = 1β=1, but wrapping the σ\sigmaσ circle adds a global consistency condition (level matching). That belongs with the closed-string modules.

    Exercise

    The open-string coefficient in (5) is 1/(4α′p+)1/(4\alpha' p^+)1/(4α′p+). Redo the two-line argument for a closed string, where the gauge fixes X+=α′p+τX^+ = \alpha' p^+\tauX+=α′p+τ (i.e. β=1\beta = 1β=1), and confirm the coefficient becomes 1/(2α′p+)1/(2\alpha' p^+)1/(2α′p+) — twice as large. Then say in one sentence which factor changed and why, so you could never misremember the open-string 444.

    Solution

    Start from the light-cone form of the constraint (3), which holds for any β\betaβ: −2(X˙+±X+′)(X˙−±X−′)+(X˙I±XI′)2=0.-2\big(\dot X^+ \pm X^{+\prime}\big)\big(\dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime}\big) + \big(\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2 = 0.−2(X˙+±X+′)(X˙−±X−′)+(X˙I±XI′)2=0. The gauge choice enters only through X˙+\dot X^+X˙+. For the closed string X+=α′p+τX^+ = \alpha' p^+\tauX+=α′p+τ, so X˙+=α′p+\dot X^+ = \alpha' p^+X˙+=α′p+ and X+′=0X^{+\prime}=0X+′=0, giving V+=α′p+V^+ = \alpha' p^+V+=α′p+. Dividing: X˙−±X−′=12 α′p+(X˙I±XI′)2.\dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime} = \frac{1}{2\,\alpha' p^+}\big(\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2.X˙−±X−′=2α′p+1​(X˙I±XI′)2. This matches the general formula (4), 1/(2βα′p+)1/(2\beta\alpha' p^+)1/(2βα′p+), at β=1\beta = 1β=1. Compared with open (β=2\beta = 2β=2, coefficient 1/(4α′p+)1/(4\alpha' p^+)1/(4α′p+)) it is larger by a factor of 222.

    The one factor that changed: the 222 inside X˙+=2α′p+\dot X^+ = 2\alpha' p^+X˙+=2α′p+ (open) versus X˙+=α′p+\dot X^+ = \alpha' p^+X˙+=α′p+ (closed) — i.e. β\betaβ itself. The other factor of 222, from the −2V+V−-2V^+V^-−2V+V− light-cone dot product, is present in both cases. So the open-string denominator is 2βα′p+=2⋅2⋅α′p+=4α′p+2\beta\alpha'p^+ = 2\cdot 2\cdot\alpha'p^+ = 4\alpha'p^+2βα′p+=2⋅2⋅α′p+=4α′p+: one 222 is universal (the metric), the other 222 is β\betaβ (open vs closed).
