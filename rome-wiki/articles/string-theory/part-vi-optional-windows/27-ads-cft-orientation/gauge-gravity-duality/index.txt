20:T1ff4c,<h2 id="two-descriptions-of-one-object">Two descriptions of one object</h2>
<p>The same object has two honest low-energy descriptions; they share one identical piece, so the remaining pieces have to be the same theory.</p>
<p>The object is a stack of <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.109em;">N</span></span></span></span> coincident D3-branes (<a href="/rome-wiki/articles/string-theory/part-iv-branes-and-duality/18-d-branes-as-boundary-geometry/definition-of-a-dp-brane/" class="wiki-link">Definition of a Dp-brane</a>) — a 3+1 dimensional sheet sitting inside 9+1 dimensional spacetime. There are two legitimate ways to describe its low-energy physics:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>As a place where open strings end.</strong> Open strings attached to the branes give a gauge theory <em>on</em> the branes.</li>
<li><strong>As a heavy charged object.</strong> The branes carry mass and charge, so like a black hole they bend spacetime around themselves, digging an infinite "throat."</li>
</ul>
<p>Neither description is wrong. They are the same physics. Take the low-energy limit of each: both split into a decoupled free-gravity piece plus one interesting piece. The free-gravity pieces are <em>identical</em>. So the two interesting pieces must be the same theory — and that is the duality.</p>

<h2 id="description-b--the-branes-as-a-curved-throat">Description B — the branes as a curved throat</h2>
<p>The same <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>N</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">N</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.109em;">N</span></span></span></span> branes carry energy and Ramond–Ramond charge, so in the closed-string theory they source a nontrivial solution of the gravitational field equations, much like a charged black hole. The solution has an <strong>infinite throat</strong>: transverse spheres around the branes, whose circumference would shrink to zero near a point mass in flat space, instead approach a <em>constant</em> size as you descend, and the branes sit an infinite proper distance down the tube (schematically below).</p>
<pre><code>   flat asymptotic region
   ────────────────────────
            │  throat
            │  (spheres → constant radius R)
            │
            ▼  D3-branes (infinitely far down)
</code></pre>
<p>In the limit (1), low-energy excitations as seen from infinity split into two kinds:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Far from the branes:</strong> ordinary low-energy excitations in the asymptotically flat region — again free closed strings in flat space.</li>
<li><strong>Deep in the throat (near-horizon):</strong> an excitation of any energy sitting deep down is <strong>gravitationally red-shifted</strong> to arbitrarily low energy as measured from infinity. So even non-negligible near-horizon physics <em>survives</em> the low-energy limit — this is why the interesting piece here is a full string theory, not just massless modes.</li>
</ul>
<p>These two kinds decouple: far excitations are almost never captured by the throat, and throat excitations cannot climb out to infinity. (This decoupling is supported by evidence — vanishing absorption in the limit — not proven here.)</p>
<p><strong>Result B:</strong> type IIB strings in the near-horizon throat <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mtext>  </mtext><mo>⊕</mo><mtext>  </mtext></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\;\oplus\;</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6667em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span><span class="mord">⊕</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2778em;"></span></span></span></span> free closed strings in flat 10D.</p>
