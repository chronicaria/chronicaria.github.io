Otega Oweh

Otega Oweh is a scoring-and-steals two-way guard whose power and production make him the most interesting of Oklahoma City's two-way rookies, but the standard guard room leaves no base minutes.

Oweh receives zero simultaneous minutes in the healthy 240. Redraft/points: no value. Dynasty: very-deep watch list; steals and rim pressure offer more paths than a one-skill two-way.

Why the line looks like this

Oklahoma City drafted Oweh 41st after he averaged 18.6 points, 4.8 rebounds, 2.7 assists and 1.8 steals across 36 Kentucky starts, then signed him to a two-way July 3. The official release says he led Kentucky in scoring and steals. That is stronger pre-NBA production than a typical activation-only player.

The landing is brutal: Wallace, Mitchell, McCain, Topic and Stirtz all sit behind the core creators on standard deals. Oweh needs Blue shooting/playmaking growth or a consolidation trade before fantasy volume exists.

Source: official two-way release and Kentucky stats. Community Summer League discussion identified him as a possible pleasant surprise, but that remains sentiment rather than role evidence. July 17 discussion

Floor, base, ceiling

  • Floor: inefficient Blue scorer without enough shooting to convert.
  • Base: 12 NBA appearances at five appearance minutes.
  • Ceiling: becomes the preferred two-way, reaches 15 minutes during guard injuries and supplies points/steals in deep leagues.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons — Oklahoma 2022-23: 4.8 PTS, 2.1 REB, 1.2 STL in 13.2 MPG on .443/.250/.650 (28 GP); Oklahoma 2023-24: 11.4/3.8/1.0, 1.5 STL in 24.9 MPG on .493/.377/.643 (32 GP); Kentucky 2024-25: 16.2/4.7/1.7, 1.6 STL in 28.3 MPG on .492/.355/.776 (36 GP); Kentucky 2025-26: 18.6/4.8/2.7, 1.8 STL in 32.8 MPG on .465/.333/.731 (36 GP). Second Team All-SEC 2025 and 2026; fastest Wildcat ever to 1,000 points; led Kentucky in scoring and steals as a senior. Draft: 2026, Round 2, Pick 41 — selected by Miami, draft rights traded to Oklahoma City.

No NBA record — 2026 two-way rookie; the 2025-26 Kentucky season above is the most recent competitive sample.

Durability: 132 college games across four seasons (28, 32, 36, 36) with rising minutes every year — no significant injury history. Trajectory: The record is a linear four-year climb from Big 12 role player to SEC lead guard: scoring rose every season, and the steal rate rose right alongside it, though efficiency dipped when Kentucky made him the primary option (.465 FG, .333 3P as a senior). In 2026-27 he competes for the preferred two-way activation slot (against Barnhizer) and Blue lead-guard reps, with zero healthy-night NBA minutes behind OKC's standard guard depth. He becomes fantasy-relevant only in the injury-cascade scenario — a converted contract plus 15+ minutes — where his points/steals combination would surface first in deep category leagues. Career sources: Wikipedia · Sports-Reference college page · OKC two-way release · Draft-night trade report
