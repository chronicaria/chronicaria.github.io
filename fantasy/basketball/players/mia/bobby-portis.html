Why this shape

  • Portis arrived in the same official trade as Giannis, preserving the familiar pairing but placing Bam ahead of him at center.
  • Miami's current roster has no established traditional backup center. That supports 20–24 minutes and makes Portis the clearest frontcourt injury beneficiary.
  • His 45.6% three-point season was exceptional and should regress; volume remains valuable because it is exactly what Miami needs around the two stars.
  • He does not solve blocks. Managers who draft Portis as a second center must source rim protection elsewhere.

Format outlook

  • Nine-category: picks 115–155; attractive late when points/rebounds/threes matter more than blocks.
  • Points: picks 115–150, with a much higher weekly ceiling whenever Giannis or Bam sits.
  • Dynasty: veteran contender depth, not a rebuild cornerstone.
  • Streaming: automatic short-term add for any announced Giannis/Bam absence; useful even at 22 minutes in favorable rebounding matchups.

Range and watchpoints

  • Ceiling: 27–30 MPG and roughly 15/8 with 2+ threes if one starting big misses extended time.
  • Median: strong per-minute bench scorer whose thin stocks keep him around the roster line in shallow categories leagues.
  • Floor: 16–18 MPG if Miami signs a conventional backup center and prefers smaller closing groups.
  • Efficiency: the FT projection remains below his longer career norms because the 2025-26 .706 mark deserves some weight but not full carry-forward.

Career record

Pre-NBA: Arkansas, two seasons (2013-14, 2014-15). As a freshman he went 12.3 PTS / 6.8 REB on .509 FG and .273 3PT, earning SEC All-Freshman and second-team All-SEC; as a sophomore he jumped to 17.5 PTS / 8.9 REB on .536 FG and .467 3PT, winning SEC Player of the Year — the first Razorback to do so since Corliss Williamson in 1994-95 — and consensus second-team All-American. Draft: 2015, first round, 22nd overall, Chicago Bulls.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category ranks are not reliably verifiable from public sources for this player — the rankings that surface in search are preseason projections, not final per-game finishes, so no year-by-year rank is asserted here. What the raw lines support: Portis has never been a top-100 nine-category asset, because his profile is structurally capped. He supplies points, rebounds, threes and low turnovers while contributing almost nothing in steals or blocks, which are the two categories that carry mid-round bigs. His best statistical seasons (2021-22, 2022-23) paired 14-15 points with 9+ rebounds and still only reached late-round territory on a per-game basis. Points leagues treat him materially better than categories do: the scoring-plus-rebounding bulk with few turnovers is efficient in that format, and the missing stocks cost him nothing there.

Durability: GP by season — 62, 64, 73, 50, 66, 66, 72, 70, 82, 49, 67. The two conspicuous dips are not the same kind of event. The 49 games in 2024-25 were a 25-game suspension announced February 20, 2025 for testing positive for Tramadol, a banned substance, not an injury. The 2025-26 shortfall of 15 games traces to wrist problems: he missed Milwaukee's final six games with a sprained right wrist and separately dealt with a left wrist sprain earlier in the year. Outside those two seasons his floor has been 62 games and he played all 82 in 2023-24. The availability record is good; the 2024-25 gap is a compliance event that should not be priced as injury risk.

Trajectory: Portis's arc is a bench specialist who found his level early and has held it with unusual stability — six of the last seven seasons land between 13.7 and 14.6 points, and the per-36 scoring rate has sat near 20 for most of that stretch. The one leap that mattered came in 2017-18, when Chicago's minutes opened up and he doubled his scoring; everything since has been role-driven variance around a fixed skill, not development. The genuine 2025-26 change is shot diet, not ability: 2.0 threes per game on .456 is a career high in both volume and accuracy, and both halves of that will regress, though the volume shift toward the arc is a real and probably permanent adaptation. The number that should worry a drafter is rebounding rate, which fell to roughly 9.5 per 36 in 2025-26 from 11.9 the prior year and 13.3 in 2022-23 — a three-year slide that predates the Miami move and reads as an aging signal rather than a minutes artifact. At 31 in 2026-27, sharing a frontcourt with Bam Adebayo and Giannis Antetokounmpo, he is on the declining side of the curve in exactly the category that gave him his floor, which argues for the conservative rebounding projection already in this dossier.
