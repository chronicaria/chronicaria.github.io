    From standing waves to oscillators

    Pluck a guitar string and it rings in a superposition of standing waves — a fundamental, an octave, and so on. A relativistic string is the same, except each standing wave, once quantized, becomes a quantum harmonic oscillator: it holds 0,1,2,…0,1,2,\dots0,1,2,… quanta of vibration, nothing in between. A single quantum string is therefore not one oscillator but a whole warehouse of them — one for every mode number nnn and every transverse direction III the string can wiggle in, all independent. Establishing that, and fixing the normalization, is the work of this note.

    The quanta are what become particles. Adding one quantum to the nnn-th oscillator raises the string's energy by a fixed amount, and a state with a definite set of quanta is a definite particle. The oscillator algebra derived here is the machinery that later manufactures the tachyon, the photon, and the whole mass tower (Open-string Fock space, Mass formula and excitation level).

    The canonical field commutator

    Quantizing the point particle means [x^,p^]=i[\hat x,\hat p]=i[x^,p^​]=i (units ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1 throughout). Quantizing a field means the same thing at every point of space at once. Here the "space" is the string itself, parametrized by σ∈[0,π]\sigma\in[0,\pi]σ∈[0,π], and the field is the set of transverse coordinates XI(τ,σ)X^I(\tau,\sigma)XI(τ,σ), I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1 — the only independent directions left after light-cone gauge eliminates X+X^+X+ and X−X^-X− (see Fixing X-plus as worldsheet time). With conjugate momentum density PτI=X˙I/(2πα′)\mathcal P^{\tau I}=\dot X^I/(2\pi\alpha')PτI=X˙I/(2πα′), the equal-time postulate is

    [\,X^I(\tau,\sigma),\,\mathcal P^{\tau J}(\tau,\sigma')\,]=i\,\delta^{IJ}\,\delta(\sigma-\sigma'),\tag{1}

    all other equal-time commutators zero. The Kronecker delta of ordinary QM has become a Dirac δ(σ−σ′)\delta(\sigma-\sigma')δ(σ−σ′): there is one independent degree of freedom at each point along the string, and separated points do not disturb one another. The index factor is δIJ\delta^{IJ}δIJ: covariantly this slot holds the metric ημν\eta^{\mu\nu}ημν, but the transverse directions are purely spacelike, so its restriction to them is the identity — no minus signs, and (as we will see) no negative-norm states. The center of mass carries its own conjugate pair, [x0I,pJ]=i δIJ[x_0^I,p^J]=i\,\delta^{IJ}[x0I​,pJ]=iδIJ, recovered at the end.

    The mode expansion we are quantizing

    Read as an operator equation, the Neumann mode expansion is

    X^I(\tau,\sigma)=x_0^I+\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0^I\,\tau+i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac1n\,\alpha_n^I\cos(n\sigma)\,e^{-in\tau},\qquad \alpha_0^I=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^I.\tag{2}

    Term by term: x0Ix_0^Ix0I​ is the center-of-mass position; the τ\tauτ-linear term carries the momentum (α0I∝pI\alpha_0^I\propto p^Iα0I​∝pI); each αnI\alpha_n^IαnI​ is the amplitude of the nnn-th standing wave. The cos⁡(nσ)\cos(n\sigma)cos(nσ) (not sine) is what enforces the open-string Neumann condition ∂σXI=0\partial_\sigma X^I=0∂σ​XI=0 at the free ends σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π. Classical reality of XIX^IXI becomes Hermiticity (αnI)†=α−nI(\alpha_n^I)^\dagger=\alpha_{-n}^I(αnI​)†=α−nI​; the explicit factor of iii out front is exactly what makes this consistent.

    Deriving the mode algebra

    The trick is to package the modes into the combinations X˙I±X′I\dot X^I\pm X'^IX˙I±X′I, which depend on τ\tauτ and σ\sigmaσ only through τ±σ\tau\pm\sigmaτ±σ and carry no 1/n1/n1/n and no cosine:

    \dot X^I\pm X'^I=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_n^I\,e^{-in(\tau\pm\sigma)}.\tag{3}

    This follows from (2) term by term: the τ\tauτ-derivative cancels the 1/n1/n1/n, the σ\sigmaσ-derivative turns the cosine into a sine, and cos⁡(nσ)∓isin⁡(nσ)=e∓inσ\cos(n\sigma)\mp i\sin(n\sigma)=e^{\mp in\sigma}cos(nσ)∓isin(nσ)=e∓inσ reassembles a single exponential. These are the natural variables because their commutator is clean. Rewriting (1) with PτI=X˙I/(2πα′)\mathcal P^{\tau I}=\dot X^I/(2\pi\alpha')PτI=X˙I/(2πα′) gives [XI,X˙J]=2πα′ i δIJδ(σ−σ′)[X^I,\dot X^J]=2\pi\alpha'\,i\,\delta^{IJ}\delta(\sigma-\sigma')[XI,X˙J]=2πα′iδIJδ(σ−σ′); differentiating with respect to σ\sigmaσ and σ′\sigma'σ′ and using [XI,XJ]=[X˙I,X˙J]=[X′I,X′J]=0[X^I,X^J]=[\dot X^I,\dot X^J]=[X'^I,X'^J]=0[XI,XJ]=[X˙I,X˙J]=[X′I,X′J]=0 at equal time, the two surviving cross terms add (because ∂σ′δ(σ′−σ)=−∂σδ(σ−σ′)\partial_{\sigma'}\delta(\sigma'-\sigma)=-\partial_\sigma\delta(\sigma-\sigma')∂σ′​δ(σ′−σ)=−∂σ​δ(σ−σ′)) to

    \big[(\dot X^I\pm X'^I)(\tau,\sigma),\,(\dot X^J\pm X'^J)(\tau,\sigma')\big]=\pm 4\pi\alpha'\,i\,\delta^{IJ}\,\frac{d}{d\sigma}\delta(\sigma-\sigma').\tag{4}

    The right-hand side carries a derivative of the delta function, not a bare delta. This is the structural fact to carry away: the derivative is what becomes the mode-number factor mmm in the algebra below. A bare delta would have produced a plain Kronecker delta — the commutators of finitely many ordinary coordinates — with no weighting by mode number and no infinite tower.

    Now extract a single [αmI,αnJ][\alpha_m^I,\alpha_n^J][αmI​,αnJ​]. The sum (3) defines X˙I+X′I\dot X^I+X'^IX˙I+X′I for all σ\sigmaσ with period 2π2\pi2π, and for σ∈[−π,0]\sigma\in[-\pi,0]σ∈[−π,0] it reproduces (X˙I−X′I)(τ,−σ)(\dot X^I-X'^I)(\tau,-\sigma)(X˙I−X′I)(τ,−σ) — the standard doubling of the open string. The "+++" case of (4) then holds on all of [−π,π][-\pi,\pi][−π,π]: the one new case is the mixed commutator of X˙+X′\dot X+X'X˙+X′ with X˙−X′\dot X-X'X˙−X′, whose cross terms cancel rather than add — as they must, since ddσδ(σ−σ′)\frac{d}{d\sigma}\delta(\sigma-\sigma')dσd​δ(σ−σ′) has no support when the two arguments sit in opposite halves of the interval. So hit both sides with the Fourier projectors 12π∫−ππdσ eimσ\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\sigma\,e^{im\sigma}2π1​∫−ππ​dσeimσ and 12π∫−ππdσ′ einσ′\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}d\sigma'\,e^{in\sigma'}2π1​∫−ππ​dσ′einσ′.     2α′ [αmI,αnJ] e−i(m+n)τ=4πα′ i δIJ(2π)2∫dσ dσ′ eimσeinσ′ ddσδ(σ−σ′)=2α′ i (in) δIJ δm+n,0.2\alpha'\,[\alpha_m^I,\alpha_n^J]\,e^{-i(m+n)\tau}=\frac{4\pi\alpha'\,i\,\delta^{IJ}}{(2\pi)^2}\int d\sigma\,d\sigma'\,e^{im\sigma}e^{in\sigma'}\,\frac{d}{d\sigma}\delta(\sigma-\sigma')=2\alpha'\,i\,(in)\,\delta^{IJ}\,\delta_{m+n,0}.2α′[αmI​,αnJ​]e−i(m+n)τ=(2π)24πα′iδIJ​∫dσdσ′eimσeinσ′dσd​δ(σ−σ′)=2α′i(in)δIJδm+n,0​.

    On the support of the Kronecker delta m+n=0m+n=0m+n=0, so the leftover phase e−i(m+n)τe^{-i(m+n)\tau}e−i(m+n)τ is 111 and i (in)=−n=mi\,(in)=-n=mi(in)=−n=m. Cancel the common 2α′2\alpha'2α′:

    \boxed{\;[\alpha_m^I,\alpha_n^J]=m\,\delta^{IJ}\,\delta_{m+n,0}\;}\tag{5}

    Three features to read off:

    • Nonzero only when m+n=0m+n=0m+n=0: a mode and its opposite fail to commute; the bracket conserves total mode number.
    • The factor mmm, not 111: these are not yet canonically normalized — the next section fixes that.
    • δIJ\delta^{IJ}δIJ, hence a plus sign: only spacelike directions appear, and this positivity is the whole payoff of light-cone gauge — it guarantees positive-norm states. A covariant treatment keeps the timelike αn0\alpha_n^0αn0​, whose η00=−1\eta^{00}=-1η00=−1 (mostly-plus signature) produces negative-norm ghosts; light-cone gauge has already thrown those away.

    Setting m=0m=0m=0 shows α0I∝pI\alpha_0^I\propto p^Iα0I​∝pI commutes with every oscillator — right, since momentum should. Its nontrivial partner is x0Ix_0^Ix0I​: since pJ=∫0πdσ′ PτJp^J=\int_0^\pi d\sigma'\,\mathcal P^{\tau J}pJ=∫0π​dσ′PτJ, integrating (1) over σ′\sigma'σ′ gives [XI(τ,σ),pJ]=i δIJ[X^I(\tau,\sigma),p^J]=i\,\delta^{IJ}[XI(τ,σ),pJ]=iδIJ, and every term of (2) except x0Ix_0^Ix0I​ commutes with pJp^JpJ, leaving [x0I,pJ]=i δIJ[x_0^I,p^J]=i\,\delta^{IJ}[x0I​,pJ]=iδIJ. The center of mass is just a free quantum particle riding along.

    Rescaling to canonical ladder operators

    The factor mmm in (5) is ugly. Absorb it. For n≥1n\ge1n≥1 define

    αnI=n anI,α−nI=n anI†.\alpha_n^I=\sqrt n\,a_n^I,\qquad \alpha_{-n}^I=\sqrt n\,a_n^{I\dagger}.αnI​=n​anI​,α−nI​=n​anI†​.

    Hermiticity (αnI)†=α−nI(\alpha_n^I)^\dagger=\alpha_{-n}^I(αnI​)†=α−nI​ makes anIa_n^IanI​ and anI†a_n^{I\dagger}anI†​ genuine adjoints. For two positive modes, (5) becomes

    [m amI,n anJ†]=m δmn δIJ  ⟹  [amI,anJ†]=mmn δmn δIJ,[\sqrt m\,a_m^I,\sqrt n\,a_n^{J\dagger}]=m\,\delta_{mn}\,\delta^{IJ}\;\Longrightarrow\;[a_m^I,a_n^{J\dagger}]=\frac{m}{\sqrt{mn}}\,\delta_{mn}\,\delta^{IJ},[m​amI​,n​anJ†​]=mδmn​δIJ⟹[amI​,anJ†​]=mn​m​δmn​δIJ,

    and since the delta forces m=nm=nm=n the prefactor is exactly 111:

      [amI,anJ†]=δmn δIJ,[amI,anJ]=[amI†,anJ†]=0  \boxed{\;[a_m^I,a_n^{J\dagger}]=\delta_{mn}\,\delta^{IJ},\qquad [a_m^I,a_n^J]=[a_m^{I\dagger},a_n^{J\dagger}]=0\;}[amI​,anJ†​]=δmn​δIJ,[amI​,anJ​]=[amI†​,anJ†​]=0​

    For each mode number n≥1n\ge1n≥1 and each transverse direction III this is one independent copy of the textbook algebra [a,a†]=1[a,a^\dagger]=1[a,a†]=1: a quantized open string is a countably infinite collection of decoupled harmonic oscillators. (The closed string carries a second, independent tower for its left-movers; the open string's Neumann ends tie left- and right-movers together, so it has only this one.)

    Which raise, which lower

    By convention the ground state is annihilated by every anI=αnI/na_n^I=\alpha_n^I/\sqrt nanI​=αnI​/n​, n≥1n\ge1n≥1 — this is what defines the vacuum (see Open-string Fock space). Therefore

    αnI (n≥1) annihilate,α−nI (n≥1) create.\alpha_n^I\ (n\ge1)\ \text{annihilate},\qquad \alpha_{-n}^I\ (n\ge1)\ \text{create}.αnI​ (n≥1) annihilate,α−nI​ (n≥1) create.

    Mnemonic: positive mode number lowers, negative mode number raises.

    Open questions

    • Strictly, light-cone quantization should quantize only the physical degrees of freedom, and the transverse commutators are not automatically inherited from a covariant field postulate. Here the algebra follows from the canonical postulate (1) — quick and correct, but not the last word. The honest, fully constrained-phase-space justification (checking that no correction sneaks in when the dependent modes αn−\alpha_n^-αn−​ are rebuilt from αnI\alpha_n^IαnI​) belongs with a later treatment of gauge fixing.

    Exercise

    The n\sqrt nn​ in αnI=n anI\alpha_n^I=\sqrt n\,a_n^IαnI​=n​anI​ looks like a free choice.     Insert the ansatz into the mode algebra (5) for two positive modes m,n≥1m,n\ge1m,n≥1.
