<h2 id="the-comparison-that-the-offer-screen-hides">The comparison that the offer screen hides</h2>
<p>Suppose one manager would send players <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">A</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">A</span></span></span></span> and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">B</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span></span></span></span> for player <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span>. Calling this “two players for one” is descriptively correct but analytically incomplete. After processing, the manager receiving <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span> also owns a newly available roster slot. That slot can hold a free agent, support a sequence of streams, activate a player from an injured slot, accept a later trade, or remain open while the manager waits for news. The other manager receives two players and must have, or create, enough legal roster capacity.</p>

<p>This note turns that principle into a reproducible valuation worksheet. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/consolidation-trades-and-roster-spots/" class="wiki-link">Consolidation trades and roster spots</a> explains the broader strategy of converting depth into concentration; this article owns the narrower question: how to calculate and audit a particular two-for-one comparison without pretending that public rank sums settle it.</p>
<p>No real trade can be recommended from this public method alone. A decision requires current league settings, both relevant rosters, the live player pool, deadline and review rules, scoring horizon, playoff schedule, keeper terms, and risk preference. Offering or accepting a trade still requires Andrew’s explicit authorization.</p>
<h2 id="freeze-the-rules-before-projecting-players">Freeze the rules before projecting players</h2>
<p>The league, not a generic analyst, defines value. ESPN’s official support material says League Manager leagues can customize scoring, roster size, waiver rules, trade rules, review process, and playoff format. Its rules also permit trade limits, deadlines, review periods, positional limits, acquisition limits, and games-played limits. These are not administrative details. They determine whether the open slot exists when expected and what it can produce.</p>

<p>Platform mechanics can create non-obvious path dependence. Its lineup documentation says a player must occupy an eligible slot and roster moves generally lock when that player’s NBA game begins. Thus “I will add the best free agent afterward” needs an exact sequence: trade accepted, review completed, roster legal, claim submitted, claim won, and player eligible before the intended game.</p>
<h2 id="choose-one-horizon-and-one-objective">Choose one horizon and one objective</h2>

<blockquote>
<p>Maximize expected head-to-head matchup win probability through the next three scoring periods, subject to five remaining acquisitions and the actual daily lineup slots.</p>

Then estimate the change in the relevant payoff: category win probability for head-to-head or expected standings points for rotisserie. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/percentage-categories-and-volume/" class="wiki-link">Percentage categories and volume</a> explains why averaging player percentages is wrong, while <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/fantasy-basketball-category-scarcity/" class="wiki-link">Fantasy basketball category scarcity</a> explains why the same stat increment has different marginal value across rosters and standings states.</p>

<p>This is where shallow and deep leagues diverge. In a shallow league, several unrostered players may offer near-starter production, so the open slot creates a meaningful dividend. In a deep league, the best available player may contribute little or actively harm percentage or turnover categories. A daily league with generous acquisitions can make a stream valuable; a weekly-lock league or tight acquisition cap can make the same empty slot nearly inert.</p>
<p>The pool must be captured at decision time. Public roster percentages and generic “waiver wire” columns cannot prove availability. Record several plausible candidates, their waiver state, eligibility, schedule, role range, and the time at which each can first score. A candidate band is more robust than one name because the preferred player may be claimed or lose a temporary role before the trade clears.</p>
<h2 id="optimize-playable-lineups-not-roster-totals">Optimize playable lineups, not roster totals</h2>
<p>Two outgoing players may look additive on paper while competing for the same active slot on the same nights. Conversely, the acquired star and replacement may have complementary positions and schedules. The correct unit is the optimized lineup path.</p>
<p>Create a day-by-day grid for the evaluation horizon. For each roster state, place eligible players into active slots to maximize the stated objective. Mark games that become bench games because all eligible positions are full. Repeat under availability scenarios. The difference between scheduled and counted games is the congestion loss.</p>

When <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">A</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">A</span></span></span></span> and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">B</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span></span></span></span> regularly fit the lineup, their combined loss is small. <p>Position eligibility must be evaluated jointly. A multi-position player may unlock more combinations than a slightly better but narrow replacement. A center cap or roster maximum can make an otherwise attractive incoming player illegal. ESPN’s official trade help confirms that League Manager leagues may impose positional limits; the actual platform state, rather than an assumed standard configuration, controls.</p>
<h2 id="measure-category-fit-at-the-roster-margin">Measure category fit at the roster margin</h2>
<p>In points formats, one scoring formula often permits a reasonably direct comparison after schedule and risk adjustments. In categories, player values are non-additive. A two-player package can spread production across categories; a star-plus-replacement path may concentrate it. Either can be preferable depending on the team’s marginal needs.</p>
<p>For head-to-head categories, simulate or scenario-test the full roster against a relevant opponent distribution. Convert the trade into changes in the probability of winning each category and then the matchup. A trade that loses public “value” can improve the objective by converting redundant points and threes into scarce steals and blocks. The reverse can occur if the replacement’s apparent counting stats bring destructive free-throw volume or turnovers.</p>

<p>A replacement shooting 50% on two attempts is not equivalent to one shooting 50% on twelve. Evaluate both sides against the existing attempt base and horizon. Turnovers also need an explicit sign: added usage may help points and assists while making turnovers worse. Do not silently omit the category to flatter the side with more volume.</p>
<p>For rotisserie, translate projected category totals into standings movement. A large statistical gain that crosses no competitor has little near-term standings value; a small gain inside a tight cluster may earn multiple points. For punt builds, value only the categories the strategy genuinely targets, but test whether the trade makes the build more fragile under projection error. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/category-covariance-in-fantasy-basketball-roster-construction/" class="wiki-link">Category covariance in fantasy basketball roster construction</a> explains why correlated category changes and portfolio structure matter.</p>
<h2 id="price-concentration-depth-and-optionality-separately">Price concentration, depth, and optionality separately</h2>
<p>A two-for-one usually moves production into fewer high-value roster spots. This has benefits: fewer difficult lineup choices, less congestion, and a higher ceiling when stars play. It also changes the risk distribution.</p>
<p>If <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span> misses time, a larger fraction of post-trade surplus disappears at once. The outgoing players may be exposed to different teams, roles, and injury states, providing diversification. Do not value this by subtracting an arbitrary “injury-prone” penalty. Use scenarios for availability and role, preferably informed by official NBA injury reports and public role evidence, and show the roster payoff in each branch.</p>
<p>The NBA’s official injury-report process requires teams to disclose affected participation statuses on defined timelines and updates reports throughout the day. Those reports establish current public status; they do not establish a medical prognosis or a fantasy probability by themselves. Preserve uncertainty instead of turning “questionable” into a fabricated fixed absence rate. Define the next report, lineup announcement, or completed game that triggers revaluation through <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/how-nba-injury-reports-should-change-fantasy-decisions/" class="wiki-link">How NBA injury reports should change fantasy decisions</a>.</p>
<p>Depth also has option value. Keeping <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">A</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">A</span></span></span></span> and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">B</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span></span></span></span> may allow one to cover an injury, exploit a future schedule, or become a trade asset. But “depth” should not receive a vague premium when one player will usually sit and comparable players are freely available. Measure it as the difference between future feasible roster paths, net of the probability and cost of using them.</p>
<p>The open slot has its own option value. Filling it immediately can sacrifice the ability to react to tomorrow’s news. In an acquisition-capped league, every stream consumes a move that cannot be used later. <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/faab-and-waiver-priority-as-option-value/" class="wiki-link">FAAB and waiver priority as option value</a> supplies the same logic for claim resources; <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/roster-slot-liquidity-in-fantasy-basketball/" class="wiki-link">Roster-slot liquidity in fantasy basketball</a> extends it to how quickly and reversibly capacity can change uses.</p>

<h2 id="a-complete-two-for-one-worksheet">A complete two-for-one worksheet</h2>
<h3 id="1-state-and-timestamp">1. State and timestamp</h3>
<p>Record scoring, categories, rosters, eligibility, caps, waiver state, transaction resources, deadlines, review time, horizon, and objective. Timestamp the player pool and every volatile status claim.</p>
<h3 id="2-legal-roster-states">2. Legal roster states</h3>
<p>Write the exact post-trade rosters for both sides. Confirm that incoming players fit positional and roster maximums. Identify any required drop, IL activation, or pending claim conflict.</p>
<h3 id="3-common-projections">3. Common projections</h3>
<p>Project <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span>, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">A</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">A</span></span></span></span>, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">B</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span></span></span></span>, and replacement candidates with the same availability, minutes, role, schedule, and scoring assumptions. Use ranges rather than mixing one source’s ceiling for one side with another source’s median for the other.</p>
<h3 id="4-replacement-candidates-and-paths">4. Replacement candidates and paths</h3>
<p>List at least three feasible slot uses when available, including no immediate action. Attach acquisition probabilities, effective dates, playable games, move costs, and expiry triggers. Reject infeasible paths explicitly.</p>
<h3 id="5-lineup-optimization">5. Lineup optimization</h3>
<p>Map each side across the horizon. Count only games that can enter legal active slots and remain below caps. Record congestion losses and positional bottlenecks.</p>
<h3 id="6-objective-conversion">6. Objective conversion</h3>
<p>For points, calculate expected counted fantasy points and distribution. For categories, aggregate rosters and estimate category or standings payoff, including attempts and turnovers. For keeper formats, display current and future components separately.</p>
<h3 id="7-risk-scenarios">7. Risk scenarios</h3>
<p>At minimum, test base, upside, and downside branches for star availability, outgoing-player roles, and replacement acquisition. Identify correlated risks. Do not make every uncertain event independently favorable.</p>
<h3 id="8-costs-and-alternatives">8. Costs and alternatives</h3>
<p>Subtract waiver/FAAB cost, acquisition scarcity, later forced drops, lost flexibility, and any contract obligation. Compare with holding, a one-for-one offer, a different replacement plan, and no trade.</p>
<h3 id="9-decision-card">9. Decision card</h3>
<p>Report the preferred path, range of advantage, main driver, main risk, deadline, evidence timestamps, and exact trigger that would reverse it. Confidence should reflect input quality and decision margin, not rhetorical conviction.</p>
<h2 id="worked-abstract-example">Worked abstract example</h2>
<p>Assume a points league over four weeks. After active and playable probabilities, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span> projects for 430 counted points, <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">A</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">A</span></span></span></span> for 270, and <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">B</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0502em;">B</span></span></span></span> for 245. The naive sum says the package wins 515–430. But the best feasible replacement path projects for 125 counted points after two crowded-slate games are removed. The star side reaches 555.</p>
<p>Now price costs. The replacement path spends two of four remaining acquisitions, valued at 12 expected points of future option cost. A later IL activation is likely to force a drop worth 18 points. The star side falls to 525. Its mean edge is only 10 points, far smaller than the naive 85-point deficit and probably inside projection error.</p>
<p>Then test concentration. In a downside branch where <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>S</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">S</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6833em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0576em;">S</span></span></span></span> misses four games, the star side loses 72 expected counted points; the package loses only 25 under its joint availability scenario. If the downside probability is material, a manager protecting a lead may prefer the package even though the star side has the higher base mean. A manager needing upside may prefer the concentration. This is a risk-preference choice, not proof that one public rank is wrong.</p>
<p>In a category league, the same point totals are irrelevant. If <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi><mo>+</mo><mi>B</mi></mrow><annotation <h2 id="failure-modes">Failure modes</h2>
<p><strong>Adding ranks.</strong> Rank is format- and population-dependent and usually ignores the new roster slot.</p>
<p><strong>Calling the slot “free.”</strong> It has value only through a legal, obtainable, playable use; filling it can spend scarce resources.</p>
<p><strong>Choosing the replacement after the outcome.</strong> Freeze the candidate set and acquisition probabilities at the decision deadline.</p>
<p><strong>Counting scheduled games.</strong> Congestion, locks, eligibility, caps, injury, and transaction timing determine counted games.</p>
<p><strong>Valuing only the receiver of one player.</strong> The two-player receiver may need to drop someone or lose lineup utility. Model both complete rosters.</p>
<p><strong>Using points logic in categories.</strong> Percentages, turnovers, scarcity, standings clusters, and punts make contributions non-additive.</p>
<p><strong>Ignoring review time.</strong> The deal and subsequent waiver move may become effective after important games or deadlines.</p>
<p><strong>Projecting one role forever.</strong> Use role and availability branches with explicit revaluation triggers.</p>
<p><strong>Treating variance as automatically bad.</strong> Concentration can be desirable when chasing upside and undesirable when protecting a lead; state the objective.</p>
<p><strong>Blending redraft and keeper value.</strong> Separate present production, future surplus, and obligations.</p>
<p><strong>Executing from an educational model.</strong> This method does not authorize an offer, acceptance, message, or transaction.</p>
<h2 id="why-it-matters">Why it matters</h2>
<p>Two-for-one trades are a stress test for fantasy reasoning because every hidden assumption becomes consequential: what replacement means, whether games are playable, which categories matter at the margin, how much flexibility is worth, and how uncertainty interacts with the manager’s objective. Public trade calculators often compress those questions into one number. The compression is convenient but can conceal the very mechanism deciding the trade.</p>
<p>A complete-roster comparison makes disagreement diagnosable. Managers can discover whether they disagree about a player projection, waiver access, lineup congestion, category needs, playoff horizon, or tolerance for concentrated risk. That is more useful than declaring one side the winner by rank sum. It also produces an auditable forecast that can later be graded through <a href="/rome-wiki/articles/basketball/fantasy-basketball/fantasy-basketball-decision-calibration/" class="wiki-link">Fantasy basketball decision calibration</a> without rewriting the assumptions after results arrive.</p>
