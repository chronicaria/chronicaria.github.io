Walter Clayton Jr

Walter Clayton Jr. is Memphis's co-lead guard and its cheapest real assists source: he averaged 5.7 assists in 25 minutes across 24 games after the trade, and the Grizzlies acquired him as the headline return for Jaren Jackson Jr.

Player card

  • Team/status: standard-roster guard acquired from Utah on 3 February 2026 in the Jaren Jackson Jr. trade; high-confidence roster core on a rebuilding team.
  • Position: official roster label G; PG/SG platform eligibility must be checked.
  • Age/experience: 23 during the season; entering NBA Year 2.
  • Depth role: co-lead guard with Jerome, 20-minute base role.
  • 2025-26: 69 total games/6 starts, 20.4 MPG; .384 FG, .901 FT, 1.1 3PM, 7.8 PTS, 2.0 REB, 4.0 AST, 0.6 STL, 0.3 BLK, 1.7 TO. The Memphis split was materially better: 24 games, 25.0 MPG, 9.7 PTS, 5.7 AST, 2.1 REB on .366 FG and .864 FT, including a 14-assist game against Indiana on 1 March 2026 that tied Ja Morant's franchise single-game rookie record. Correction 2026-07-18 This dossier previously gave Clayton zero base minutes and a conditional-only line triggered by a lead guard leaving, which ranked him below three guards with more roster risk and less recent production. That was wrong on the evidence available in July: he played 24 Memphis games at 25.0 MPG with 5.7 assists after arriving as the headline return in the Jackson trade, he is 23 on a roster that has since shed Bane, Jackson, Morant and Aldama, and he is under contract at roughly $4.0 million. He is now carried at 20 base minutes as the co-lead guard, and the guard-consolidation risk has been moved onto Pippen instead. ESPN trade report, 2026-02-03

Nine-category: roughly top 150-200 per game; assists and elite FT% carry the line, FG% is the clear drag. Points: playable streamer given the assist volume. Dynasty: genuine hold, with the strongest value in assists-scarce formats.

Range and risks

  • Strengths: passing, FT%, pull-up threes and usable turnovers relative to assists.
  • Liabilities: FG%, defensive events and roster security.
  • Floor/base/ceiling: Memphis keeps Jerome and Pippen healthy and Clayton settles at 14 minutes / 20-minute co-lead guard with 4.6 assists / Jerome is moved at the deadline and Clayton runs the offense at 28-30 minutes for roughly 13 points and seven assists.
  • Branches: a Jerome trade is the ceiling trigger. His .384 rookie FG% — and the worse .366 mark after the trade — could improve on better shot quality, but should not be assumed away.

Roster reporting did identify a Jerome/Pippen/Clayton choice as a likely consolidation move, but Clayton is the least plausible of the three to be moved: he is the youngest, the cheapest, the most recently acquired, and the only one Memphis actively traded for. Sports Illustrated / Memphis On SI, 2026-07-09

Career record

Pre-NBA: Four college seasons, born March 6, 2003, listed at 6-4. Two at Iona — a 7.3-point reserve year, then 16.8 points, 4.3 rebounds and 3.2 assists on .455/.431/.953 as MAAC Player of the Year in 2022-23. He transferred to Florida and scored immediately: 17.6 points on 36.5% from three in 2023-24, then 18.3 points, 3.7 rebounds and 4.2 assists on .448/.386/.875 in 2024-25, a season that ended with a national title, Final Four Most Outstanding Player, consensus first-team All-America and SEC Tournament MVP. Draft: 2025, first round, 18th overall by Washington; rights traded to Utah on draft night.

Durability: 69 of a possible 82 as a rookie across two teams, with no reported injury shutdown; the missed games are consistent with a rookie in and out of a rotation plus the February 3 trade. Six starts, all in Memphis.

Trajectory: The two halves of the rookie year say different things. In Utah he was a bench scorer at 18 minutes with 3.2 assists; in Memphis, on a roster stripped of Jaren Jackson Jr, Ja Morant, Desmond Bane and Santi Aldama, he ran an offense at 25 minutes with 5.7 assists and a 14-assist game against Indiana. The shooting is the problem and it did not improve with the bigger role — .384 for the year, .366 after the trade, and 30.7% from three across both stops, which is well below the 38.6% he shot at Florida. He is a real assists-and-FT% source at 20-plus minutes, and the FG% is the cost of admission. The ceiling trigger is a Ty Jerome trade, which would push him to 28-30 minutes as the primary handler; the floor is Memphis deciding a 30% three-point shooter cannot be a lead guard and consolidating the room around Jerome and Scotty Pippen Jr.
