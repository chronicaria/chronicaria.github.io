    Hagedorn temperature

    A string has so many excited states that heating it never gets it hotter than one fixed temperature kT_H = 1/(4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}) — pour in more energy and it just makes longer strings, not a hotter gas.

    Two racing exponentials

    In statistical mechanics a temperature TTT weights each state of energy EEE by the Boltzmann factor e−E/kTe^{-E/kT}e−E/kT: high-energy states are exponentially suppressed. This is a race between two exponentials:

    • how many states there are at energy EEE — the density of states ρ(E)\rho(E)ρ(E),
    • and how hard the Boltzmann factor e−βEe^{-\beta E}e−βE (with β=1/kT\beta = 1/kTβ=1/kT) suppresses them.

    For an ordinary gas ρ(E)\rho(E)ρ(E) grows only like a power of EEE, so the Boltzmann factor always wins and equilibrium exists at every temperature. A string is different: its state count grows exponentially, ρ(E)∼eβHE\rho(E)\sim e^{\beta_H E}ρ(E)∼eβH​E. Now the two exponentials can tie. At the special temperature where β=βH\beta = \beta_Hβ=βH​ the suppression exactly cancels the proliferation of states, and above it (β<βH\beta<\beta_Hβ<βH​, i.e. T>THT>T_HT>TH​) the states win and the partition function diverges. That tie-point is the Hagedorn temperature TH=1/(kβH)T_H = 1/(k\beta_H)TH​=1/(kβH​).

    We derive this ceiling twice — once from entropy (the microcanonical route), once from the partition function (the canonical route) — and then ask what it physically means.

    Setup: strings at rest

    Take open strings with zero spatial momentum — for instance, endpoints stuck on a D0-brane. Then each quantum state's energy is just its rest mass, E=ME = ME=M, and we can talk about "the energy of a state" without tracking momentum. The mass-shell relation (Mass-shell relation) reads

    M^2 = \frac{1}{\alpha'}\left(N^\perp - 1\right) \simeq \frac{N^\perp}{\alpha'}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad \sqrt{N^\perp} = \sqrt{\alpha'}\,E. \tag{1}

    The −1-1−1 is the normal-ordering constant a=−1a=-1a=−1 of the open bosonic string (the intercept being −a=+1-a=+1−a=+1); at the large excitation numbers N⊥≫1N^\perp \gg 1N⊥≫1 that dominate the thermodynamics it is negligible, leaving N⊥=α′ E\sqrt{N^\perp}=\sqrt{\alpha'}\,EN⊥​=α′​E — the level number grows linearly with energy.

    Entropy linear in energy

    The number of states at level N⊥N^\perpN⊥ is the number of ways to distribute N⊥N^\perpN⊥ units of oscillation among the 24 transverse directions — a partition count p24(N⊥)p_{24}(N^\perp)p24​(N⊥), worked out in Partitions and oscillator counting. Its logarithm is

    \ln p_{24}(N^\perp) \simeq 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{24\,N^\perp}{6}} = 4\pi\sqrt{N^\perp}, \tag{2}

    so the entropy S=kln⁡p24(N⊥)S = k\ln p_{24}(N^\perp)S=klnp24​(N⊥) is

    S(N^\perp) = 4\pi k\sqrt{N^\perp}. \tag{3}

    Substituting N⊥=α′ E\sqrt{N^\perp} = \sqrt{\alpha'}\,EN⊥​=α′​E from (1) turns this into an entropy–energy law:

    \boxed{\,S = 4\pi k\sqrt{\alpha'}\,E\,} \tag{4}

    Entropy is linear in energy. A photon gas in three space dimensions has S∝E3/4S\propto E^{3/4}S∝E3/4 (from E∝T4VE\propto T^4VE∝T4V, so T∝E1/4T\propto E^{1/4}T∝E1/4 and S∝T3V∝E3/4S\propto T^3V\propto E^{3/4}S∝T3V∝E3/4); here SSS grows like EEE itself. That linearity is the direct fingerprint of the exponential density of states — ln⁡(states)∼N⊥∼E\ln(\text{states})\sim\sqrt{N^\perp}\sim Eln(states)∼N⊥​∼E, so S∝ES\propto ES∝E.

    A constant temperature falls out

    Temperature is defined by how much the entropy responds to added energy, 1T=∂S∂E\dfrac{1}{T} = \dfrac{\partial S}{\partial E}T1​=∂E∂S​. With SSS linear in EEE, that slope is a constant:

    \frac{1}{kT} = \frac1k\frac{\partial S}{\partial E} = 4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}. \tag{5}

    The temperature does not depend on the energy: add energy and the slope — hence TTT — does not budge. Its fixed value is the Hagedorn temperature,

    \boxed{\,\frac{1}{\beta_H} = kT_H = \frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}}\,} \tag{6}

    Dimension check: with ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1, α′\alpha'α′ is a length2^22 = energy−2^{-2}−2, so α′\sqrt{\alpha'}α′​ is an inverse energy and kTHkT_HkTH​ is an energy. Good.

    This ceiling is low. The first massive open-string level (N⊥=2N^\perp = 2N⊥=2) has rest energy M=1/α′M = 1/\sqrt{\alpha'}M=1/α′​, and

    \frac{kT_H}{1/\sqrt{\alpha'}} = \frac{1}{4\pi} \simeq \frac{1}{12.6}. \tag{7}

    So the thermal energy at the ceiling is about a twelfth of a single string quantum: below THT_HTH​, essentially every massive string state sits far above the thermal scale and is barely excited.

    Closed strings: identical THT_HTH​

    Redo the count for closed strings at rest. Their mass formula carries different factors of 2, and level matching (Level matching) forces N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp = \bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥:

    M^2 = \frac{2}{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp + \bar N^\perp - 2\big) \simeq \frac{4}{\alpha'}N^\perp \quad\Longrightarrow\quad 2\sqrt{N^\perp} = \sqrt{\alpha'}\,E. \tag{8}

    A closed string has independent left- and right-moving excitations, so the state count is a product, Ω=p24(N⊥) p24(Nˉ⊥)=(p24(N⊥))2\Omega = p_{24}(N^\perp)\,p_{24}(\bar N^\perp) = \big(p_{24}(N^\perp)\big)^2Ω=p24​(N⊥)p24​(Nˉ⊥)=(p24​(N⊥))2, and its log doubles: S=2⋅4πkN⊥=4πk (2N⊥)S = 2\cdot 4\pi k\sqrt{N^\perp} = 4\pi k\,(2\sqrt{N^\perp})S=2⋅4πkN⊥​=4πk(2N⊥​). Now use (8):

    S = 4\pi k\,(2\sqrt{N^\perp}) = 4\pi k\sqrt{\alpha'}\,E. \tag{9}

    The factor-of-2 in the mass relation and the factor-of-2 from doubling the entropy cancel, giving the same law (4) — hence the same THT_HTH​. (See the Exercise for the cancellation done factor-by-factor.)

    Why THT_HTH​ is a limiting temperature — the canonical view

    The entropy argument gave a constant temperature but did not show what breaks above it. The partition function does. For highly excited strings the density of states as a function of mass is

    \rho(M) \sim M^{-25/2}\,e^{\beta_H M}, \tag{10}

    obtained by converting the level count (2) into a mass integral: with N⊥≃α′M2N^\perp \simeq \alpha' M^2N⊥≃α′M2, the refined partition asymptotic p24(N⊥)≃12(N⊥)−27/4e4πN⊥p_{24}(N^\perp)\simeq \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(N^\perp)^{-27/4}e^{4\pi\sqrt{N^\perp}}p24​(N⊥)≃2​1​(N⊥)−27/4e4πN⊥​ becomes ρ(M)\rho(M)ρ(M) via p24(N⊥) dN⊥=ρ(M) dMp_{24}(N^\perp)\,dN^\perp = \rho(M)\,dMp24​(N⊥)dN⊥=ρ(M)dM, and 4πN⊥=4πα′M=βHM4\pi\sqrt{N^\perp} = 4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}M = \beta_H M4πN⊥​=4πα′​M=βH​M. The exponential eβHMe^{\beta_H M}eβH​M is the exponential density of states; the βH\beta_HβH​ in its rate is literally the inverse Hagedorn temperature.

    A string in a box also carries center-of-mass momentum, so the one-string partition function sums the relativistic-particle partition function over the mass tower. For the relevant heavy states (βM≫1\beta M \gg 1βM≫1) that particle partition function in d=25d = 25d=25 space dimensions is

    Z_{\rm particle}(M^2) \simeq V\left(\frac{M}{2\pi\beta}\right)^{25/2} e^{-\beta M}. \tag{11}

    Multiplying ρ(M)\rho(M)ρ(M) by ZparticleZ_{\rm particle}Zparticle​ and integrating over mass, the power-law prefactors cancel exactly — the M25/2M^{25/2}M25/2 from translational momentum kills the M−25/2M^{-25/2}M−25/2 in ρ\rhoρ — leaving the clean convergence question

    Z_{\rm str}(\beta) \sim \int^\infty dM\; e^{(\beta_H - \beta)M}. \tag{12}

    Now the boundary is sharp:

    • β>βH\beta > \beta_Hβ>βH​ (T<THT < T_HT<TH​): the exponent is negative, the integral converges — ordinary thermodynamics.
    • β=βH\beta = \beta_Hβ=βH​ (T=THT = T_HT=TH​): the integrand stops decaying, the integral diverges.
    • β<βH\beta < \beta_Hβ<βH​ (T>THT > T_HT>TH​): the exponent is positive, divergence is violent.

    Carrying the constant prefactors through gives the explicit single-open-string result

    Z_{\rm str} \simeq \frac{2^{11}}{\pi}\,V\,(kT_H)^{25}\,\frac{T_H}{T_H - T}, \qquad T \to T_H^-, \tag{13}

    a simple pole at T=THT = T_HT=TH​: the free single-string partition function blows up as the temperature approaches the ceiling from below. Equivalently, the mean string energy Estr=−∂βln⁡Zstr≃1/(β−βH)E_{\rm str} = -\partial_\beta \ln Z_{\rm str} \simeq 1/(\beta - \beta_H)Estr​=−∂β​lnZstr​≃1/(β−βH​) diverges there — you cannot push a free string past THT_HTH​ because it soaks up unlimited energy at that fixed temperature.

    Both routes say the same thing: an exponential density of states is equivalent to a limiting free-string temperature. Whenever ρ(E)∼eβHE\rho(E)\sim e^{\beta_H E}ρ(E)∼eβH​E, the Boltzmann factor e−βEe^{-\beta E}e−βE can only win for β>βH\beta > \beta_Hβ>βH​; the convergence boundary sits exactly at TH=1/(kβH)T_H = 1/(k\beta_H)TH​=1/(kβH​). What replaces the canonical ensemble above the ceiling is a physical question, not something this free-string calculation answers.

    Negative specific heat

    The leading law (4) dropped the power-law prefactor in p24p_{24}p24​. Keeping it exposes a genuinely strange subleading effect. With N⊥≃α′E2N^\perp \simeq \alpha' E^2N⊥≃α′E2,

    \frac{S(E)}{k} = \ln p_{24}(\alpha' E^2) = \underbrace{4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}\,E}_{=\,\beta_H E} \;-\; \frac{27}{2}\ln E + \text{const} + \cdots, \tag{14}

    where the −272ln⁡E-\tfrac{27}{2}\ln E−227​lnE comes from ln⁡((N⊥)−27/4)=−274ln⁡(α′E2)\ln\big((N^\perp)^{-27/4}\big) = -\tfrac{27}{4}\ln(\alpha' E^2)ln((N⊥)−27/4)=−427​ln(α′E2). Differentiating,

    \beta(E) = \frac{\partial (S/k)}{\partial E} = \beta_H - \frac{27}{2E} + \cdots. \tag{15}

    At any large but finite EEE, β(E)<βH\beta(E) < \beta_Hβ(E)<βH​, so the microcanonical temperature T(E)T(E)T(E) sits above THT_HTH​ and approaches it from above as E→∞E\to\inftyE→∞. And since β\betaβ increases with EEE, TTT decreases with EEE: dT/dE<0dT/dE < 0dT/dE<0, a negative specific heat. Adding energy makes a free string colder, not hotter — the opposite of everything ordinary. The leading lesson survives intact: THT_HTH​ is the asymptotic scale toward which the temperature settles, and where the usual canonical ensemble stops behaving.

    What THT_HTH​ physically means

    THT_HTH​ is probably not a maximum temperature of nature. The more likely reading is a phase transition. Because entropy is linear in energy, dumping energy into the string system is "free" in entropy terms — the system happily absorbs it by growing a few very long strings rather than by heating many short modes (long strings are entropically favored). That is the string analog of a latent-heat transition: energy in, temperature stuck, until the system reorganizes.

    There is also a genuine ensemble subtlety here. Ordinarily the microcanonical and canonical ensembles agree because the integrand Ω(E)e−βE\Omega(E)e^{-\beta E}Ω(E)e−βE has a sharp saddle point. But with Ω(E)∼eβHE\Omega(E)\sim e^{\beta_H E}Ω(E)∼eβH​E the integrand is ∼e(βH−β)E\sim e^{(\beta_H - \beta)E}∼e(βH​−β)E — a pure exponential with no saddle point. So the two ensembles need not give the same answer near THT_HTH​, and which one is physical is a modeling choice, not a theorem.

    Everything above is the free single string. The multi-string thermodynamic phase, interactions near THT_HTH​, and the modern Hagedorn/deconfinement interpretation are beyond this note — but the free-string calculation already pins the scale and shows the ceiling is real.

    Open questions

    • Is THT_HTH​ a true ceiling or a phase transition? The entropic argument (energy goes into long strings) favors a transition, but the free-string calculation alone cannot settle it — you need the interacting multi-string system.
    • Canonical vs. microcanonical: with no saddle point they can disagree near THT_HTH​. Which ensemble is physical for a given problem?
    • The open superstring's THT_HTH​ is larger by 2\sqrt22​. Deriving that needs NS/R sectors and the GSO projection, so it belongs with the superstring material, not this bosonic note.

    Exercise

    The note claims open and closed strings share the same THT_HTH​, with two factors of 2 conspiring to cancel. Verify this by carrying the factors explicitly. Starting from the closed-string inputs — mass relation M2≃4α′N⊥M^2 \simeq \tfrac{4}{\alpha'}N^\perpM2≃α′4​N⊥ and state count Ω=(p24(N⊥))2\Omega = \big(p_{24}(N^\perp)\big)^2Ω=(p24​(N⊥))2 — derive the closed-string entropy S(E)S(E)S(E) and its THT_HTH​. Identify precisely which factor of 2 comes from the mass formula and which from the state count, and show they cancel. Then, as a sign check, confirm that if closed strings had only one set of movers (state count p24(N⊥)p_{24}(N^\perp)p24​(N⊥), not squared) while keeping the closed mass relation, THT_HTH​ would come out a factor of 222 larger — i.e. the cancellation is not automatic.

    Solution

    Closed-string entropy. The state count squares, so its log doubles: Sk=ln⁡Ω=ln⁡(p24(N⊥))2=2ln⁡p24(N⊥)≃2⋅4πN⊥=4π(2N⊥).\frac{S}{k} = \ln\Omega = \ln\big(p_{24}(N^\perp)\big)^2 = 2\ln p_{24}(N^\perp) \simeq 2\cdot 4\pi\sqrt{N^\perp} = 4\pi\big(2\sqrt{N^\perp}\big).kS​=lnΩ=ln(p24​(N⊥))2=2lnp24​(N⊥)≃2⋅4πN⊥​=4π(2N⊥​). This is where the first factor of 2 (from the state count) enters — it turns 4πN⊥4\pi\sqrt{N^\perp}4πN⊥​ into 4π(2N⊥)4\pi(2\sqrt{N^\perp})4π(2N⊥​).

    Second factor of 2, from the mass relation. The closed mass formula M2≃4α′N⊥M^2\simeq \tfrac{4}{\alpha'}N^\perpM2≃α′4​N⊥ gives M=2α′N⊥M = \tfrac{2}{\sqrt{\alpha'}}\sqrt{N^\perp}M=α′​2​N⊥​, i.e. 2N⊥=α′ E2\sqrt{N^\perp} = \sqrt{\alpha'}\,E2N⊥​=α′​E (using E=ME=ME=M). Substitute this directly into the entropy: Sk=4π(2N⊥)=4πα′ E.\frac{S}{k} = 4\pi\big(2\sqrt{N^\perp}\big) = 4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}\,E.kS​=4π(2N⊥​)=4πα′​E. The bracket 2N⊥2\sqrt{N^\perp}2N⊥​ produced by the state count is exactly the combination that the mass relation sets equal to α′E\sqrt{\alpha'}Eα′​E. The two 2's cancel and we recover the open-string law S=4πkα′ES = 4\pi k\sqrt{\alpha'}ES=4πkα′​E, hence 1kT=∂(S/k)∂E=4πα′⟹kTH=14πα′,\frac{1}{kT} = \frac{\partial(S/k)}{\partial E} = 4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad kT_H = \frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}},kT1​=∂E∂(S/k)​=4πα′​⟹kTH​=4πα′​1​, the same THT_HTH​ as for open strings.

    Sign check. Suppose the count were p24(N⊥)p_{24}(N^\perp)p24​(N⊥) (not squared), so Sk=4πN⊥\tfrac{S}{k} = 4\pi\sqrt{N^\perp}kS​=4πN⊥​, while keeping the closed mass relation 2N⊥=α′E2\sqrt{N^\perp} = \sqrt{\alpha'}E2N⊥​=α′​E, i.e. N⊥=12α′E\sqrt{N^\perp} = \tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{\alpha'}EN⊥​=21​α′​E. Then Sk=4π⋅12α′E=2πα′ E⟹1kT=2πα′,kTH=12πα′=2⋅14πα′.\frac{S}{k} = 4\pi\cdot\tfrac12\sqrt{\alpha'}E = 2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}\,E \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \frac{1}{kT} = 2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'},\quad kT_H = \frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}} = 2\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}}.kS​=4π⋅21​α′​E=2πα′​E⟹kT1​=2πα′​,kTH​=2πα′​1​=2⋅4πα′​1​. Half the entropy slope means twice the temperature; the ceiling would rise by a factor of 222. So the equality of open and closed THT_HTH​ genuinely relies on the state-count doubling canceling the factor of 2 in the closed mass relation — remove one and the match breaks.
