Source identity and research cutoff

  • Publisher: Hashtag Basketball.
  • Principal named author on the current ranking and projection pages: Joseph Mamone.
  • Research cutoff and access date for every URL below: 2026-07-18.
  • Primary landing page: https://hashtagbasketball.com/

The next newest NBA fantasy items were the April 13 season rankings and points rankings, an April 13 last-night recap, an April 11 projection item, and an April 5 schedule-grid item. Hashtag's pages are dynamic. Crowd vote totals, values, teams, and ranks can change without a stable public edition history. All numbers in this note are therefore dated extraction snapshots, not permanent citations.

Genuinely forward material

Joseph Mamone's expert dynasty board

The July 2 expert board is Hashtag's cleanest public forward source. It can be filtered by overall, contending, rebuilding, rookies, or points-league rank; by the next three or five seasons; by Yahoo or Fantrax position; and by team. Those controls establish a multi-season dynasty horizon. They do not establish a 2026-27 games-played, minutes, or stat-line forecast.

The board had begun integrating the 2026 class: Cameron Boozer was No. 10, Darryn Peterson No. 18, AJ Dybantsa No. 29, Caleb Wilson No. 41, Mikel Brown Jr. No. 54, Darius Acuff Jr. No. 55, Keaton Wagler No. 58, Kingston Flemings No. 77, and Brayden Burries No. 79. These are long-horizon asset opinions, not redraft rankings or rookie-year projections. The rookie entries display empty 2025-26 and 2024-25 NBA rows rather than invented stats.

For veterans, the numerical columns under each dynasty rank are explicitly labelled 2025-26 Season and 2024-25 Season. Those columns are historical support for the forward opinion. They must never be copied into a 2026-27 projection table.

The page supplies short editorial outlooks for many players, but it does not publish a reproducible dynasty formula, aging curve, games model, discount rate, league depth, roster construction, or uncertainty interval. Treat the rank as Mamone's expert opinion under the selectable horizon—not as a transparent model output.

Crowdsourced dynasty market

The Keeper page reported 196,650 votes at extraction and said it updates daily. Its top five were Wembanyama, Flagg, Jokić, Gilgeous-Alexander, and Dončić. This is useful as a live dynasty-market signal distinct from Mamone's expert list.

The source itself warned that the 2026 draft class was still being loaded and needed a few days to normalize. The table simultaneously contained named 2026 rookies and generic assets such as 2026 Draft (Pick 1), 2026 Draft (Pick 2), and later pick bands. For example, the generic No. 1 pick asset remained tenth overall while named rookies were also present. The board was therefore in a transitional, potentially duplicative state on July 18.

The site does not explain publicly how pairwise votes become the displayed VALUE, how old votes decay, how duplicate or strategic votes are handled, or what league settings voters are imagining. Use the crowd board as market sentiment with a timestamp, not a projection or consensus probability.

Drafter is not safely season-labelled

Drafter reported 9,030 votes and a July 18 update. Its top five were Wembanyama, Jokić, Dončić, Anthony Davis, and Trae Young. For Cleveland it showed Donovan Mitchell No. 12, Evan Mobley No. 43, Jarrett Allen No. 48, and Jaylon Tyson No. 342.

The page never declares a scoring format, league depth, per-game versus totals basis, or season horizon. It also retained materially stale teams: James Harden was No. 16 as a Clipper and Darius Garland No. 51 as a Cavalier, while Hashtag's July dynasty pages showed Harden with Cleveland and Garland with the Clippers. Other entries likewise reflected older rosters. Drafter can be retained only as an ambiguous crowd-preference snapshot; it is not verified 2026-27 redraft evidence.

Dynasty trade calculator

The public dynasty calculator says it blends Hashtag's expert dynasty ranks with the crowdsourced dynasty ranks. That makes it directionally forward-looking. Its searchable player pool included 2026 rookies and many current July affiliations, but it also retained stale Cleveland names and teams—including Dean Wade and Larry Nance Jr. as Cavaliers on July 18. No public formula explains the blend or the adjustment for unequal player counts. Any calculator result should be stored with the exact inputs and extraction time and checked against the authoritative roster ledger.

Methodology and context

Category ranking controls

Hashtag's user guide defines the free ranking controls:

  • Based on: per-game averages or totals, with totals described as averages multiplied by games played.
  • Standard: the selected categories and weights determine the ranking.
  • H2H: the same framework, but a negative category is floored at -2.
  • Minus 1: removes each player's worst category.
  • Category customization: users can include or exclude categories and apply visible multipliers.
  • Z-scores: can be displayed, but the public documentation does not fully specify population, replacement level, percentage-volume implementation, or treatment of missing values.

Because rank type, categories, weights, games basis, position source, and minimum-games filter are mutable, a copied rank is incomplete without those settings.

Projection blends

The guide calls the underlying forecast my projections, making Mamone the model owner. During the season, BLEND7 is described as a 50/50 mixture of his projection and the player's last-seven-day data; BLEND14 is the analogous last-14-day blend. The live projection selector also offered BLEND30, short-term STREAM7/14/30, and games-played penalties, but the guide did not define every one of those variants in comparable detail. Do not infer an undocumented formula.

Public pages disclose no player-level minutes methodology, injury probability model, team-minute constraint, aging curve, rookie translation model, update log, or forecast-error history. They are useful external forecasts, but not fully reproducible projections.

Points and auction settings

The points pages allow custom weights for points, rebounds, assists, stocks, turnovers, threes, shooting events, double-doubles, triple-doubles, and other fields. A displayed points rank is meaningless without the exact scoring inputs. Auction tools additionally depend on budget, number of teams, roster size, selected categories or point system, totals/per-game choice, and games-played penalty.

Situational tools

  • Next Man Up compares a player's averages with and without a selected teammate in the lineup; it is a descriptive split and does not by itself isolate causality.
  • Defense versus position converts team allowances to a 48-minute positional basis; it is opponent history, not a stable defensive talent estimate.
  • The injury database says its underlying records come from Pro Sports Transactions and @jaseziv. Its recovery averages combine injuries across players and eras and should be used as broad priors only.
  • The dynasty calculator says it blends expert and crowd rankings, but the public page does not reveal the weighting or imbalance adjustment.

Access limits, paywall, and reliability warnings

  • The public category and points projection pages expose the top 30. Full projections require an active Patreon subscription and a Hashtag account.
  • Full auction values, the full sleeper list, and league-specific tools are likewise premium.
  • The premium page advertised Yahoo, ESPN, Fantrax, and Sleeper league imports plus tools such as schedule grids, draft tracking, matchup planning, waiver and trade machines, scouting reports, and draft modes. Availability varies by platform and format; the site should be checked rather than assuming feature parity.
  • The premium page said $2.50 per month, while the landing page and user guide also contained $2 per month language. The current price is internally inconsistent. This audit did not subscribe, authenticate, import a league, or inspect paid output.
  • ESPN import documentation asks users to pre-authenticate through a browser extension and enter cookie values into Hashtag's authorization flow. That is an account-security consideration for any future paid test; no credentials were supplied here.
  • Keeper calls itself up to date but still listed departed or stale Cavaliers such as Dean Wade and Larry Nance Jr. as CLE. Crowd rank freshness must not be confused with roster accuracy.
  • Player profiles and board pages sometimes disagreed on ranks and values, and two high-profile Cleveland player links returned error pages.
  • The site offers no public versioned download or archived July 18 snapshot. Dynamic values should always be accompanied by an access timestamp or captured table.

Responsible encyclopedia use

  1. Grade Mamone's expert dynasty board as specialist forecast/opinion evidence, not fact. Grade Keeper and Drafter as identified community sentiment.
  2. Use the expert and crowd dynasty ranks to identify disagreement—especially Tyson's No. 165 versus No. 507 gap—not to manufacture a single false-consensus number.
  3. Do not copy the dynasty board's 2025-26 stat columns into 2026-27 fantasy basketball player projections.
  4. Do not relabel the April ranks, projections, auction values, ADP, rookie ranks, sleepers, schedule tools, or lineup splits as 2026-27. Preserve them only as historical baselines.
  5. Do not use Drafter as 2026-27 ADP or redraft rank until Hashtag identifies its season and format and refreshes the player-team mapping.
  6. For Cleveland, verify every affiliation and transaction against official sources before using Hashtag's team filter. The site is an evaluation source, not a roster source of record.
  7. Re-audit after Hashtag rolls its public rankings and projection selectors to 2026-27. Capture the source update date, access date, format, categories/scoring, per-game versus totals, forecast variant, games penalty, and visible rank population.
