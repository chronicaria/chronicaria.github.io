The shape of this slot

Your sixteen picks are 1 · 20 · 21 · 40 · 41 · 60 · 61 · 80 · 81 · 100 · 101 · 120 · 121 · 140 · 141 · 160. Pick 1 is isolated; every later selection comes as a back-to-back turn.

A turn is one decision with two outputs. Use it to solve the scarcer remaining starting need, then take the best complementary value before ten opponents can react. Do not force a positional pair: the correct pair depends on what the room actually left.

The replacement model is the guardrail. In this 10-team, 1-QB, one-FLEX league, the board uses RB24, WR26, TE10 and QB10. The wire is materially better at QB/TE than at elite RB/WR roles. That is why the default build is three RB/WR starters through pick 41, not an early quarterback or a scheduled tight end.

Pick 1: choose the anchor, not a turn stack

The current FFC opening cluster is Jahmyr Gibbs, Bijan Robinson, Puka Nacua, Ja'Marr Chase and Christian McCaffrey. Who to take at 1.01 remains the separate final recommendation page; this page does not overwrite it.

Use the shared Data/research/receipts/2026-08-02/first-pick-candidates.md as a veto checklist:

  • Gibbs: elite bell-cow case, but resolve the current participation/contract-versus-back-status question before treating him as fully cleared.
  • Bijan: lead role intact; Brian Robinson Jr.'s potential goal-line/pass-down share remains a forecast branch.
  • McCaffrey: active in early 49ers camp work, but historical availability remains a real model counterweight.
  • Nacua: practicing; Stafford's managed camp schedule is a context risk, not proof of a Nacua limitation.
  • Chase: no verified contradiction to the alpha baseline, but a photo/attendance report is not a full-participation certification.

Pick the best candidate whose current status has no unresolved red flag. Do not choose based on a hoped-for teammate or stack at 20/21.

The 20/21 turn: RB/WR first

The market's boundary cluster is Omarion Hampton (20), George Pickens (21), Chris Olave (22), Nico Collins (23), Jeremiyah Love (24) and Josh Jacobs (25). Kenneth Walker (26), Zay Flowers (28), Garrett Wilson (29), Breece Hall (30) and DeVonta Smith (31) are the immediate fallback range.

No two of those names are guaranteed to survive. Treat 20–25 as fragile live and 26–31 as the more likely fallback range.

  1. If the 1.01 was an RB, select the best available WR from the two clusters, then the best remaining RB/WR value.
  2. If the 1.01 was a WR, select at least one RB unless the RB choices have fallen a full tier below the receiver choice.
  3. If both positions are equal, avoid a same-bye pairing where practical; do not pass a better tier merely to optimize one bye.

Status vetoes. Love is not a confirmed Arizona bell cow while QB1 and workload remain unresolved. Walker's Kansas City lead workload is not established in current official evidence. A projected carry line is not a camp depth chart. Use the player only if his value survives that uncertainty relative to the alternatives.

Do not plan a QB or TE here. Josh Allen's market ADP is 27, not 20/21. Trey McBride is 35 and Brock Bowers 40. A dramatic room-specific fall can be compared on the clock, but it is not this turn's default.

The 40/41 turn: elite TE or two more starters

This is the actual elite-TE decision point.

  • If McBride or Bowers is available: take one after you have two RB/WR anchors. McBride is a slide from current ADP 35; Bowers is a boundary outcome at 40. McBride's role is strong, but Arizona QB1 remains unresolved, so a market number alone is not a reason to ignore new status evidence.
  • If neither is available: take the two best RB/WR fits. The live neighborhood is Malik Nabers (41), Davante Adams (42), Terry McLaurin (43), Ladd McConkey (44), Bucky Irving (45), D'Andre Swift (46), Jameson Williams (47) and Jaylen Waddle (48).
  • If QB runs early: let it happen. Allen (27), Drake Maye (50), Lamar Jackson (53) and Joe Burrow (54) are normally gone by this turn. Their absence is not a reason to reach for the next quarterback.

After pick 41, the preferred roster has three or four RB/WR starters, with an elite TE only if the room supplied the fall.

60/61 through 101: let the board fill the luxury slots

60/61. The current market area includes David Montgomery (60), Alec Pierce (61), Tony Pollard (62), DK Metcalf (63), Tyler Warren (64), Dak Prescott (65) and Courtland Sutton (66). Fill an RB/WR starter or FLEX before taking a merely acceptable QB. Warren is a viable TE option only if the RB/WR alternatives are a weaker tier.

80/81. Jayden Daniels (72) and Jalen Hurts (76) would be falls, not assumed picks. If one reaches this turn and is the best available value, QB is defensible. Otherwise choose RB/WR upside or a TE value such as the remaining middle tier.

100/101 and later. QB10 is a strong fallback in a 10-team league. Do not panic-draft a quarterback just to complete the lineup; take the best starter when QB finally clears the RB/WR alternative. Once a QB is rostered, do not draft a second before K/DST.

The on-clock script

  1. Confirm the 1.01 candidate's current participation, injury and role status.
  2. At each turn, cross out all drafted players, then group the remainder as RB, WR, TE and QB—not as one generic ranked list.
  3. Ask which starter you cannot replace from the wire. At 20/21 and usually 40/41, that answer is RB or WR.
  4. Use ADP only to estimate whether the other member of a pair will survive. If two players are close, take the one less likely to return.
  5. Check the status receipt before choosing any player whose appeal depends on a workload projection, a new quarterback, or an active injury question.
  6. Do not spend an early pick on K, D/ST or QB2.

Trigger events that replace the script

  • Three or more QBs selected before a turn: revisit the QB queue, but do not draft one automatically.
  • McBride or Bowers still available at 40: run the elite-TE branch instead of the generic RB/WR branch.
  • A verified official injury, PUP placement, QB naming, role announcement, or transaction: remove the affected player's stale workload assumption.
  • ESPN room ordering differs materially from FFC: use the actual room as the availability source and retain the replacement-level construction logic.

Open questions
