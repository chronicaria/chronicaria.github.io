1c:T3d616,<h2 id="from-worldline-length-to-worldsheet-area">From worldline length to worldsheet area</h2>
<p>A particle moving through spacetime traces a 1D <strong>worldline</strong>, and its natural action is how long that line is — the proper length <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mo>∫</mo><mi>d</mi><mi>s</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\int ds</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1.1111em;vertical-align:-0.3061em;"></span><span class="mop op-symbol small-op" style="margin-right:0.1945em;position:relative;top:-0.0006em;">∫</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.1667em;"></span><span class="mord mathnormal">d</span><span class="mord mathnormal">s</span></span></span></span>. A string is a particle smeared into a line: it sweeps out a 2D <strong>worldsheet</strong>, and the natural action is how big that sheet is — its proper area. This note writes that area down.</p>
<p>Two facts do all the work:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Area is a Gram determinant.</strong> The area of the little parallelogram spanned by two tangent vectors is fixed by their lengths and the angle between them — dot products only, no coordinates. That is a <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mn>2</mn><mo>×</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">2\times2</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.7278em;vertical-align:-0.0833em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span><span class="mbin">×</span><span class="mspace" style="margin-right:0.2222em;"></span></span><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:0.6444em;"></span><span class="mord">2</span></span></span></span> determinant.</li>
<li><strong>The sheet lives in Minkowski space.</strong> One direction across the sheet is timelike (following the string forward in time), one is spacelike (running along the string at a fixed instant). Time and space enter the metric with opposite signs, so the Gram determinant comes out <strong>negative</strong>, and the area is <span class="katex"><span class="katex-mathml"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msqrt><mrow><mo>−</mo><mi>γ</mi></mrow></msqrt></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">\sqrt{-\gamma}</annotation></semantics></math></span><span class="katex-html" aria-hidden="true"><span class="base"><span class="strut" style="height:1.04em;vertical-align:-0.2606em;"></span><span class="mord sqrt"><span class="vlist-t vlist-t2"><span class="vlist-r"><span class="vlist" style="height:0.7794em;"><span class="svg-align" style="top:-3em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:3em;"></span><span class="mord" style="padding-left:0.833em;"><span class="mord">−</span><span class="mord mathnormal" style="margin-right:0.0556em;">γ</span></span></span><span style="top:-2.7394em;"><span class="pstrut" style="height:3em;"></span><span class="hide-tail" style="min-width:0.853em;height:1.08em;"><svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" width="400em" height="1.08em" viewBox="0 0 400000 1080" preserveAspectRatio="xMinYMin slice"><path d="M95,702
