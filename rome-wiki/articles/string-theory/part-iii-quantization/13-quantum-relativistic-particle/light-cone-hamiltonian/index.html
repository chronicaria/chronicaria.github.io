    Light-cone Hamiltonian

    The light-cone Hamiltonian H=\frac{1}{2m^2}\big(p^Ip^I+m^2\big) is the mass-shell condition p^2+m^2=0 solved for the light-cone energy p^- and rescaled so it generates \tau -evolution. Derive it once for a point particle; the string repeats the identical logic with m^2 promoted to an oscillator operator.

    Solve the constraint, don't impose it

    A free relativistic particle obeys one law: p2=−m2p^2=-m^2p2=−m2 — energy and momentum sit on the mass shell. Light-cone gauge does not fight this constraint; it solves it, reading off one component of the momentum in terms of the others. The Hamiltonian is that solution times a conversion factor, so HHH never imposes the mass shell — it is the mass shell, already solved.

    Compare a bead on the circle x2+y2=R2x^2+y^2=R^2x2+y2=R2: you don't carry yyy as an independent variable and enforce the circle at every step — you write y=R2−x2y=\sqrt{R^2-x^2}y=R2−x2​ and work with xxx alone. Light-cone gauge does exactly this to the relativistic energy.

    Setup: action, momentum, mass shell

    Start from the relativistic point-particle action with an arbitrary worldline parameter τ\tauτ (from the proper-time action): S=−m∫τiτf−ημνx˙μx˙ν  dτ=∫L dτ,L=−m−x˙2,S = -m\int_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}\sqrt{-\eta_{\mu\nu}\dot x^\mu \dot x^\nu}\;d\tau = \int L\,d\tau,\qquad L=-m\sqrt{-\dot x^2},S=−m∫τi​τf​​−ημν​x˙μx˙ν​dτ=∫Ldτ,L=−m−x˙2​, with x˙μ=dxμ/dτ\dot x^\mu = dx^\mu/d\taux˙μ=dxμ/dτ, ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1, mostly-plus signature, and τ\tauτ dimensionless — this last choice is why factors of m2m^2m2 (not mmm) appear below. The conjugate momentum is p_\mu = \frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot x^\mu} = \frac{m\,\dot x_\mu}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}. \tag{1} LLL depends only on τ\tauτ-derivatives of xμx^\muxμ, never on xμx^\muxμ itself, so each coordinate is cyclic: dpμ/dτ=0dp_\mu/d\tau=0dpμ​/dτ=0, every momentum component conserved. Separately, the action carries a redundancy that conservation laws do not touch — any reparameterization of τ\tauτ describes the same worldline — and fixing that freedom is light-cone gauge's job.

    Square (1) and contract — the arbitrary x˙2\dot x^2x˙2 cancels: p^2 = p_\mu p^\mu = \frac{m^2\,\dot x^2}{-\dot x^2} = -m^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\,p^2 + m^2 = 0\,}. \tag{2} This mass shell is the entire on-shell content of the theory, true in any parameterization. Everything below is this equation in a convenient disguise.

    The light-cone gauge

    Reparameterization freedom lets us choose how τ\tauτ tracks the motion. Light-cone gauge ties the coordinate x+=12(x0+x1)x^+=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(x^0+x^1)x+=2​1​(x0+x1) (see Light-cone coordinates) linearly to τ\tauτ: \boxed{\;x^+ = \frac{p^+}{m^2}\,\tau\;}\tag{3} Light-cone time runs uniformly in τ\tauτ. Dimensions demand some constant of length dimension in front of the dimensionless τ\tauτ; choosing precisely p+/m2p^+/m^2p+/m2 is what cancels every p+p^+p+ from the Hamiltonian below. This one choice also kills the square-root nonlinearity. Raise the index on (1), take the +++ component, and feed in x˙+=p+/m2\dot x^+ = p^+/m^2x˙+=p+/m2: p+=m x˙+−x˙2=1−x˙2 p+m  ⟹  x˙2=−1m2.p^+ = \frac{m\,\dot x^+}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-\dot x^2}}\,\frac{p^+}{m} \;\Longrightarrow\; \dot x^2 = -\frac{1}{m^2}.p+=−x˙2​mx˙+​=−x˙2​1​mp+​⟹x˙2=−m21​. The otherwise-arbitrary quantity −x˙2\sqrt{-\dot x^2}−x˙2​ is now fixed to 1/m1/m1/m, so the square root in LLL is gone and (1) linearizes to pμ=m2x˙μp_\mu = m^2\dot x_\mupμ​=m2x˙μ​. Combined with dpμ/dτ=0dp_\mu/d\tau=0dpμ​/dτ=0 this gives x¨μ=0\ddot x_\mu=0x¨μ​=0: free straight-line motion. Integrating, xI(τ)=x0I+pIm2τx^I(\tau)=x_0^I+\frac{p^I}{m^2}\tauxI(τ)=x0I​+m2pI​τ and x−(τ)=x0−+p−m2τx^-(\tau)=x_0^-+\frac{p^-}{m^2}\taux−(τ)=x0−​+m2p−​τ; only x+x^+x+ gets no integration constant, because (3) already pinned it to τ\tauτ.

    The key step: solve the mass shell for p−p^-p−

    In light-cone components p2=−2p+p−+pIpIp^2 = -2p^+p^- + p^Ip^Ip2=−2p+p−+pIpI (with η+−=−1\eta^{+-}=-1η+−=−1 and Euclidean transverse part; see Light-cone coordinates), so the mass shell (2) reads -2p^+p^- + p^Ip^I + m^2 = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;p^- = \frac{1}{2p^+}\big(p^Ip^I + m^2\big)\;}\tag{4}

    This is the crux. The mass shell is quadratic in the momentum components; solving it for p−p^-p− makes the light-cone energy a derived quantity — once p+p^+p+ and the transverse p⃗T=(pI)\vec p_T=(p^I)p​T​=(pI) are known, p−p^-p− is fixed. There is no independent p−p^-p− degree of freedom to quantize; the constraint is solved explicitly, not dragged along. With p−p^-p− derived, the entire motion is fixed by the independent data (xI,x0−,pI,p+)(x^I, x_0^-, p^I, p^+)(xI,x0−​,pI,p+) — exactly the variables that get promoted to operators.

    From p−p^-p− to the Hamiltonian

    Why isn't HHH simply p−p^-p−? Because p−p^-p− is the light-cone energy — it generates translations in x+x^+x+, not in τ\tauτ: ∂/∂x+↔p−\partial/\partial x^+ \leftrightarrow p^-∂/∂x+↔p−. We evolve operators in τ\tauτ, and the gauge (3) says a step in τ\tauτ is a (p+/m2)(p^+/m^2)(p+/m2)-sized step in x+x^+x+: ∂∂τ=p+m2∂∂x+  ⟷  p+m2 p−.\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau} = \frac{p^+}{m^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^+} \;\longleftrightarrow\; \frac{p^+}{m^2}\,p^-.∂τ∂​=m2p+​∂x+∂​⟷m2p+​p−. So the generator of τ\tauτ-evolution — the Hamiltonian — is \boxed{\;H = \frac{p^+}{m^2}\,p^- = \frac{1}{2m^2}\big(p^Ip^I + m^2\big)\;}\tag{5} by (4); the two factors of p+p^+p+ cancel. HHH has no explicit τ\tauτ-dependence, so it is conserved.

    Only two inputs went into (5): p−p^-p− is the mass shell solved for the energy, and HHH is p−p^-p− times the gauge factor p+/m2p^+/m^2p+/m2. Rearranged, 2m2H−pIpI=m22m^2H - p^Ip^I = m^22m2H−pIpI=m2 is just the on-shell statement that the invariant mass is mmm.

    Going quantum

    Everything so far was classical — pμ,xμp^\mu, x^\mupμ,xμ were numbers. Quantization keeps the relations and changes the type of object: the reduced variables (xI,x0−,pI,p+)(x^I, x_0^-, p^I, p^+)(xI,x0−​,pI,p+) become operators, with brackets promoted to commutators via [A,B]=i{A,B}[A,B]=i\{A,B\}[A,B]=i{A,B} (the postulates live in Canonical commutators). The Hamiltonian function (5) becomes the Hamiltonian operator H=12m2(pIpI+m2).H=\frac{1}{2m^2}\big(p^Ip^I+m^2\big).H=2m21​(pIpI+m2). There is no ordering ambiguity: the pIp^IpI commute with each other and with the number m2m^2m2. This is the gentle version of light-cone quantization — solve the constraint, then reuse the reduced Hamiltonian. For the string, this m2m^2m2 becomes an infinite oscillator sum, where ordering stops being bookkeeping and becomes real mathematics (the a=−1a=-1a=−1, D=26D=26D=26 story of Module 14).

    Check: does HHH generate the right motion?

    Verify that the Heisenberg equation i dξ/dτ=[ξ,H]i\,d\xi/d\tau=[\xi,H]idξ/dτ=[ξ,H] — with an extra i ∂τξi\,\partial_\tau\xii∂τ​ξ term for operators carrying explicit τ\tauτ (see Schrodinger and Heisenberg pictures) — reproduces the classical equations of motion, using Canonical commutators:

    • xIx^IxI: with [xI,pJpJ]=2i pI[x^I,p^Jp^J]=2i\,p^I[xI,pJpJ]=2ipI, i x˙I=[xI,12m2(pJpJ+m2)]=i pIm2  ⇒  xI(τ)=x0I+pIm2τ,i\,\dot x^I = \Big[x^I,\tfrac{1}{2m^2}(p^Jp^J+m^2)\Big] = \frac{i\,p^I}{m^2}\;\Rightarrow\; x^I(\tau)=x_0^I+\frac{p^I}{m^2}\tau,ix˙I=[xI,2m21​(pJpJ+m2)]=m2ipI​⇒xI(τ)=x0I​+m2pI​τ, the classical straight-line drift. ✓\checkmark✓
    • x0−x_0^-x0−​: commutes with every pIp^IpI, so x˙0−=0\dot x_0^-=0x˙0−​=0 — a constant, as it must be. ✓\checkmark✓

    Every classical equation returns, so (5) is the correct Hamiltonian, not merely a plausible one. This check is the rehearsal for the identically structured but far longer string version.

    The state space and the scalar field

    Take (p+,pI)(p^+, p^I)(p+,pI) as a maximal commuting set (momentum space, with p+>0p^+>0p+>0 so 1/p+1/p^+1/p+ is defined). States ∣p+,p⃗T⟩|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∣p+,p​T​⟩ are momentum eigenstates, and HHH acts as a number: H ∣p+,p⃗T⟩=12m2(pIpI+m2) ∣p+,p⃗T⟩.H\,|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle = \frac{1}{2m^2}\big(p^Ip^I+m^2\big)\,|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle.H∣p+,p​T​⟩=2m21​(pIpI+m2)∣p+,p​T​⟩. A superposition ∣Ψ,τ⟩=∫dp+dp⃗T ψ(τ,p+,p⃗T) ∣p+,p⃗T⟩|\Psi,\tau\rangle=\int dp^+d\vec p_T\,\psi(\tau,p^+,\vec p_T)\,|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∣Ψ,τ⟩=∫dp+dp​T​ψ(τ,p+,p​T​)∣p+,p​T​⟩ obeys i ∂τ∣Ψ,τ⟩=H∣Ψ,τ⟩i\,\partial_\tau|\Psi,\tau\rangle=H|\Psi,\tau\ranglei∂τ​∣Ψ,τ⟩=H∣Ψ,τ⟩, i.e. i\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\,\psi(\tau,p^+,\vec p_T) = \frac{1}{2m^2}\big(p^Ip^I+m^2\big)\,\psi(\tau,p^+,\vec p_T). \tag{6}

    Equation (6) is the classical field equation of a free scalar field in light-cone gauge — not an analogy, an identity. A scalar mode of momentum (p+,p⃗T)(p^+,\vec p_T)(p+,p​T​) has its light-cone energy fixed by the same mass shell, p−=12p+(pIpI+m2)p^-=\frac{1}{2p^+}(p^Ip^I+m^2)p−=2p+1​(pIpI+m2), and evolves in τ\tauτ with phase exp⁡[−i(p+/m2) p−τ]\exp[-i(p^+/m^2)\,p^-\tau]exp[−i(p+/m2)p−τ], so its mode coefficient obeys i∂τϕ=p+m2p−ϕ=12m2(pIpI+m2)ϕi\partial_\tau\phi=\frac{p^+}{m^2}p^-\phi=\frac{1}{2m^2}(p^Ip^I+m^2)\phii∂τ​ϕ=m2p+​p−ϕ=2m21​(pIpI+m2)ϕ — exactly (6). Wavefunction and field mode carry the same on-shell labels and the same light-cone time dependence.

    So the quantum mechanics of one relativistic particle is the one-particle sector of a free scalar field: ∣p+,p⃗T⟩↔ap+,p⃗T†∣Ω⟩|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle \leftrightarrow a^\dagger_{p^+,\vec p_T}|\Omega\rangle∣p+,p​T​⟩↔ap+,p​T​†​∣Ω⟩ (see One-particle states). First-quantizing the particle gives one-particle states; reinterpreting its Schrödinger equation (6) as a classical field equation and quantizing again — second quantization — builds the full multiparticle Fock space. Strings are quantized the same way, which is why a single string theory describes many particle species at once.

    Open questions

    • For the open string, the same construction gives α′M2=N⊥+a\alpha' M^2 = N^\perp + aα′M2=N⊥+a, with Lorentz invariance forcing a=−1a=-1a=−1 in D=26D=26D=26 — so the ground state has α′M2=−1\alpha'M^2=-1α′M2=−1 (a tachyon). This note rehearses only the point-particle mechanism; the oscillator normal-ordering argument that produces aaa lives in Module 14.
    • The p+=0p^+=0p+=0 sector lies outside this light-cone coordinate patch (division by p+p^+p+ fails). Covariant quantization is the later framework that can treat such boundary cases.

    Exercise

    The gauge factor in (5) is not decorative — it is forced by consistency, and dropping it is the classic light-cone mistake. Suppose a careless student, seeing that p−p^-p− is "the energy," declares Hwrong=p−H_{\text{wrong}} = p^-Hwrong​=p− instead of p+m2p−\frac{p^+}{m^2}p^-m2p+​p−.

    Compute the x˙I\dot x^Ix˙I that HwrongH_{\text{wrong}}Hwrong​ predicts via ix˙I=[xI,Hwrong]i\dot x^I=[x^I,H_{\text{wrong}}]ix˙I=[xI,Hwrong​], and compare with the correct classical drift x˙I=pI/m2\dot x^I = p^I/m^2x˙I=pI/m2 found above. What extra factor appears, and which equation — the gauge condition (3) or the mass shell (2) — fixes it?

    Solution

    Write Hwrong=p−=12p+(pJpJ+m2)H_{\text{wrong}} = p^- = \frac{1}{2p^+}(p^Jp^J+m^2)Hwrong​=p−=2p+1​(pJpJ+m2). Since [xI,p+]=0[x^I,p^+]=0[xI,p+]=0, the factor 1/(2p+)1/(2p^+)1/(2p+) passes through the commutator, and the same computation as in the check above gives i x˙I=[xI,Hwrong]=12p+ [xI,pJpJ]=12p+ (2i pI)=i pIp+,i\,\dot x^I = [x^I, H_{\text{wrong}}] = \frac{1}{2p^+}\,[x^I, p^Jp^J] = \frac{1}{2p^+}\,(2i\,p^I) = \frac{i\,p^I}{p^+},ix˙I=[xI,Hwrong​]=2p+1​[xI,pJpJ]=2p+1​(2ipI)=p+ipI​, so HwrongH_{\text{wrong}}Hwrong​ predicts x˙I=pI/p+\dot x^I = p^I/p^+x˙I=pI/p+.

    The correct answer is x˙I=pI/m2\dot x^I = p^I/m^2x˙I=pI/m2. The two differ by exactly the factor x˙correctIx˙wrongI=p+m2,\frac{\dot x^I_{\text{correct}}}{\dot x^I_{\text{wrong}}} = \frac{p^+}{m^2},x˙wrongI​x˙correctI​​=m2p+​, which is precisely the gauge factor multiplying p−p^-p− in (5). The reason: Hwrong=p−H_{\text{wrong}}=p^-Hwrong​=p− generates evolution in x+x^+x+, whereas we parameterize by τ\tauτ, and the two clocks are related by the gauge condition (3), x+=(p+/m2)τx^+ = (p^+/m^2)\taux+=(p+/m2)τ, i.e. ∂τ=(p+/m2)∂x+\partial_\tau = (p^+/m^2)\partial_{x^+}∂τ​=(p+/m2)∂x+​. Multiplying p−p^-p− by p+/m2p^+/m^2p+/m2 converts "rate per unit x+x^+x+" into "rate per unit τ\tauτ," restoring x˙I=pI/m2\dot x^I = p^I/m^2x˙I=pI/m2. (The mass shell (2) is what makes p−p^-p− a function of the transverse momenta in the first place, but it is the gauge condition, not the mass shell, that fixes the overall factor.)
