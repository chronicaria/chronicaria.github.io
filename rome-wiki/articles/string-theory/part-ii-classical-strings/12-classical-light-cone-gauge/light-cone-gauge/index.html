    Light-cone gauge

    Light-cone gauge is the one coordinate choice that untangles the classical string: pick X^+ as the worldsheet clock, and the constraints hand you X^- for free, leaving the D-2 transverse fields X^I as the only things the string is really doing.

    Three moves

    A relativistic string carries DDD embedding fields Xμ(τ,σ)X^\mu(\tau,\sigma)Xμ(τ,σ), but they are not all independent: reparameterization invariance (relabel τ,σ\tau,\sigmaτ,σ freely) plus two constraints tie them together. This note spends that freedom deliberately and counts what survives.

    Film a wave traveling down a rope, and you get to choose how the camera sweeps through spacetime — which slices count as "one instant." A clumsy sweep, ordinary time, leaves the rope's motion tangled with constraints you can only stare at. Tilt the notion of "now" 45° onto a light ray instead: in those slices the leftover bookkeeping collapses, and the rope's genuine wiggling — always transverse, never along its own length — is laid bare. That tilt is light-cone gauge.

    Three moves, in order:

    1. X+X^+X+ becomes the clock. Spend the τ\tauτ-relabeling freedom to declare X+∝τX^+ \propto \tauX+∝τ. Light-cone time is no longer a field to solve for; it is the time axis.
    2. X−X^-X− falls out of the constraints. Because the light-cone metric is off-diagonal, the constraint that looked quadratic is linear in X−X^-X− — solve, don't just impose.
    3. XIX^IXI is what's left. The D−2D-2D−2 transverse coordinates carry every physical degree of freedom.

    A classical string is therefore D−2D-2D−2 transverse oscillators; the other two coordinates are a clock and its shadow.

    Move 1 — Fix X+X^+X+ as the clock

    Light-cone coordinates split off the x0,x1x^0,x^1x0,x1 plane:

    X±=12(X0±X1),XI=(X2,…,XD−1).X^\pm=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(X^0\pm X^1),\qquad X^I=(X^2,\dots,X^{D-1}).X±=2​1​(X0±X1),XI=(X2,…,XD−1).

    Reparameterization freedom lets us demand that light-cone time run linearly in τ\tauτ. For the open string (natural units, ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1) this fixes

    X+(τ,σ)=2α′p+τ,x0+=0, αn+=0.X^+(\tau,\sigma)=2\alpha'p^+\tau,\qquad x_0^+=0,\ \alpha_n^+=0.X+(τ,σ)=2α′p+τ,x0+​=0, αn+​=0.

    X+X^+X+ has no oscillations and no starting offset — it is pure clock, ticking at the constant rate 2α′p+2\alpha'p^+2α′p+. One of the DDD coordinates is now spent. (Full derivation: Fixing X-plus as worldsheet time.)

    This needs p+=12(p0+p1)≠0p^+=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(p^0+p^1)\neq0p+=2​1​(p0+p1)=0. Any positive-energy state can be boosted to p+>0p^+>0p+>0; a state with p+=0p^+=0p+=0 is simply the wrong object for this chart.

    Move 2 — The constraints hand you X−X^-X−

    The two parameterization constraints, packaged, read

    (X˙±X′)2=0.(1)(\dot X\pm X')^2=0. \tag{1}(X˙±X′)2=0.(1)

    The trick is the light-cone dot product (mostly-plus signature), whose (+,−)(+,-)(+,−) block is off-diagonal:

    a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI.a\cdot b=-a^+b^--a^-b^++a^Ib^I.a⋅b=−a+b−−a−b++aIbI.

    There is no (a−)2(a^-)^2(a−)2 term, so the expansion of (1) is only linear in the X−X^-X− combination — no square root to fight. Feeding in X˙+±X+′=2α′p+\dot X^+\pm X^{+\prime}=2\alpha'p^+X˙+±X+′=2α′p+ (a constant, from move 1) and dividing:

      X˙−±X−′=14α′p+(X˙I±XI′)2  .(2)\boxed{\;\dot X^-\pm X^{-\prime}=\frac{1}{4\alpha'p^+}\big(\dot X^I\pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2\;}. \tag{2}X˙−±X−′=4α′p+1​(X˙I±XI′)2​.(2)

    Both derivatives of X−X^-X− are dictated by the transverse motion, so X−X^-X− is fixed up to a single additive constant x0−x_0^-x0−​ — it is not an independent field. The denominator 4α′p+4\alpha'p^+4α′p+ is 2×(2α′p+)2\times(2\alpha'p^+)2×(2α′p+): one 222 from the off-diagonal metric, the 2α′p+2\alpha'p^+2α′p+ from the clock rate. This is the module's most common factor slip. (Full derivation and integrability check: Light-cone constraints.)

    Neither ingredient alone works: light-cone coordinates kill the square root, light-cone gauge makes X˙+\dot X^+X˙+ a constant you can divide by.

    Move 3 — The transverse fields are everything physical

    What remains is the D−2D-2D−2 transverse coordinates. For the open string with free (Neumann) endpoints,

    XI=x0I+2α′pIτ+i2α′∑n≠0αnIne−inτcos⁡nσ.(3)X^I=x_0^I+2\alpha'p^I\tau+i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac{\alpha_n^I}{n}e^{-in\tau}\cos n\sigma. \tag{3}XI=x0I​+2α′pIτ+i2α′​n=0∑​nαnI​​e−inτcosnσ.(3)

    The oscillators αnI\alpha_n^IαnI​ are the free data, subject only to the reality condition α−nI=(αnI)∗\alpha_{-n}^I=(\alpha_n^I)^*α−nI​=(αnI​)∗. Feeding (3) into (2) and matching Fourier modes reconstructs X−X^-X− mode by mode:

    2α′ αn−=1p+Ln⊥,Ln⊥≡12∑p∈Zαn−pIαpI.(4)\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_n^-=\frac{1}{p^+}L_n^\perp,\qquad L_n^\perp\equiv\tfrac12\sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_{n-p}^I\alpha_p^I. \tag{4}2α′​αn−​=p+1​Ln⊥​,Ln⊥​≡21​p∈Z∑​αn−pI​αpI​.(4)

    Every longitudinal oscillator αn−\alpha_n^-αn−​ is quadratic in the transverse ones — the transverse Virasoro mode Ln⊥L_n^\perpLn⊥​, summed over III only. So X−X^-X− is a functional of the physical data, plus the constant x0−x_0^-x0−​; there is no freely choosable αn−\alpha_n^-αn−​. (Full mode derivation: Mode expansions in light-cone gauge.)

    The complete free data is therefore

    { x0I, pI, αnI, p+, x0− }:\big\{\,x_0^I,\ p^I,\ \alpha_n^I,\ p^+,\ x_0^-\,\big\}:{x0I​, pI, αnI​, p+, x0−​}:

    the data of D−2D-2D−2 dynamical fields plus two longitudinal constants. Why transverse? The constraint X˙⋅X′=0\dot X\cdot X'=0X˙⋅X′=0 says each point moves perpendicular to the string; sliding a point along the string is just relabeling σ\sigmaσ, pure gauge. Physical motion is transverse by definition, and light-cone gauge is the slicing where "physical" and "transverse" become the same word. (Count and picture: Transverse coordinates as physical degrees of freedom.)

    The zero mode: mass is transverse too

    The n=0n=0n=0 case of (4), with α0μ=2α′ pμ\alpha_0^\mu=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^\muα0μ​=2α′​pμ, gives 2p+p−=1α′L0⊥2p^+p^-=\tfrac1{\alpha'}L_0^\perp2p+p−=α′1​L0⊥​, where classically L0⊥=α′pIpI+∑n≥1α−nIαnIL_0^\perp=\alpha'p^Ip^I+\sum_{n\geq1}\alpha_{-n}^I\alpha_n^IL0⊥​=α′pIpI+∑n≥1​α−nI​αnI​. Combined with M2≡−p2=2p+p−−pIpIM^2\equiv-p^2=2p^+p^--p^Ip^IM2≡−p2=2p+p−−pIpI, the transverse-momentum pieces cancel and

    M2=1α′∑n≥1α−nIαnI=1α′∑n≥1n ∣anI∣2 ≥0,M^2=\frac1{\alpha'}\sum_{n\geq1}\alpha_{-n}^I\alpha_n^I=\frac1{\alpha'}\sum_{n\geq1}n\,|a_n^I|^2\ \geq 0,M2=α′1​n≥1∑​α−nI​αnI​=α′1​n≥1∑​n∣anI​∣2 ≥0,

    using reality and the normalized amplitudes αnI=n anI\alpha_n^I=\sqrt n\,a_n^IαnI​=n​anI​. Even the string's mass is built entirely from transverse oscillations, and it is manifestly non-negative: the classical string is never a tachyon.

    Why it is powerful and dangerous

    Powerful: it exhibits the physical modes directly and makes the classical string trivially solvable. Dangerous: singling out X+X^+X+ breaks manifest Lorentz covariance — a boost mixing +,−,I+,-,I+,−,I looks ugly, even though the physics is still Lorentz invariant. Classically this is a cosmetic price. Quantum mechanically it becomes a real test: the hidden Lorentz generators must still close into the right algebra, and for the bosonic string they do so only in D=26D=26D=26 (with the normal-ordering constant a=−1a=-1a=−1). Light-cone gauge shows you the spectrum for free but forces you to earn back Lorentz invariance after quantizing.

    Exercise

    The solved constraint (2) carries the coefficient 14α′p+\dfrac{1}{4\alpha'p^+}4α′p+1​. Derive that 444 from scratch, and then answer conceptually: if you had chosen static gauge (X0∝τX^0\propto\tauX0∝τ, a timelike clock) instead of light-cone gauge, would the constraint have been linear in some corresponding longitudinal derivative? Why is the null clock the magic ingredient?

    Solution

    The factor. Write Vμ=X˙μ±Xμ′V^\mu=\dot X^\mu\pm X^{\mu\prime}Vμ=X˙μ±Xμ′; the constraint (1) is V⋅V=0V\cdot V=0V⋅V=0. In light-cone coordinates, V⋅V=−2V+V−+VIVI=0,V\cdot V=-2V^+V^-+V^IV^I=0,V⋅V=−2V+V−+VIVI=0, the −2-2−2 coming from η+−=η−+=−1\eta_{+-}=\eta_{-+}=-1η+−​=η−+​=−1 (two equal off-diagonal terms, no diagonal (V−)2(V^-)^2(V−)2). From move 1, X+′=0X^{+\prime}=0X+′=0 and X˙+=2α′p+\dot X^+=2\alpha'p^+X˙+=2α′p+, so V+=X˙+±X+′=2α′p+V^+=\dot X^+\pm X^{+\prime}=2\alpha'p^+V+=X˙+±X+′=2α′p+, a constant. Thus −2(2α′p+) V−+VIVI=0 ⇒ V−=14α′p+VIVI,-2(2\alpha'p^+)\,V^-+V^IV^I=0\ \Rightarrow\ V^-=\frac{1}{4\alpha'p^+}V^IV^I,−2(2α′p+)V−+VIVI=0 ⇒ V−=4α′p+1​VIVI, and the denominator 4α′p+=2×(2α′p+)4\alpha'p^+=2\times(2\alpha'p^+)4α′p+=2×(2α′p+): the 222 is the metric's off-diagonal factor, the 2α′p+2\alpha'p^+2α′p+ is the clock rate. Both are needed.

    Why null. The linearity came from two facts: (i) there is no (V−)2(V^-)^2(V−)2 term, i.e. η−−=0\eta_{--}=0η−−​=0, and (ii) V+V^+V+ is a constant we can divide by. Fact (i) is exactly the statement that the coordinate direction ∂/∂X−\partial/\partial X^-∂/∂X− is null — a lightlike axis squares to zero, so its coordinate never appears quadratically; it only appears paired against the clock, whose coefficient is the constant from (ii). In static gauge no such partner exists: the timelike clock is its own metric partner (η00=−1\eta_{00}=-1η00​=−1, all off-diagonal (0,i)(0,i)(0,i) entries zero), and every unfrozen field is spacelike with ηii=+1≠0\eta_{ii}=+1\neq0ηii​=+1=0, so whichever one you try to solve for appears squared. The constraint X˙2+X′2=0\dot X^2+X'^2=0X˙2+X′2=0 can be imposed, but inverting it for any single field costs a square root and still tangles all the others. The null tilt is what converts a quadratic constraint into a linear reconstruction equation; that single algebraic fact is the engine of the entire gauge.
