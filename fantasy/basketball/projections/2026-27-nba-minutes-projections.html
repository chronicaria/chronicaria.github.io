What the columns mean

This page and the 30 team desks project two different quantities. They are not rival estimates of the same number, and a disagreement between them is not by itself an error. Read this section before comparing the two.

Column Symbol Definition Conditioned on
Active MPG \(m_i\) Minutes the player logs on a night he occupies his projected active role That role actually being his that night
Weight \(w_i\) Probability the player occupies that role in a randomly drawn team game Nothing — unconditional
Team-game min \(\mu_i = m_i w_i\) Games-weighted expectation: minutes the player contributes to the average team game, counting nights he is hurt, rested or out of the rotation as zero Nothing — unconditional
GP \(g_i\) Projected games played Nothing

The two hard constraints are

\[\sum_{i} \mu_i = 240 \qquad\text{and}\qquad w_i \le \frac{g_i}{82}.\]

The second is the availability ceiling: a player cannot occupy his role in a larger share of team games than the share of games he dresses for. It is the constraint the July 17 build violated (see the correction below).

The team desks project the other quantity. Each desk's 240-minute table is a per-active-game allocation — a single healthy night with the main rotation available, which also sums to 240. Call it \(h_i\). So each team carries two distinct partitions of the same 240 minutes:

  • \(h_i\) — the healthy-night allocation, on the team desk. What the rotation looks like when nobody is out.
  • \(\mu_i\) — the games-weighted expectation, on this page. What each player is worth to a season-long total.

Neither dominates the other, and \(\mu_i > h_i\) is expected for reserves, not a defect. Because both partitions sum to 240,

\[\sum_i (\mu_i - h_i) = 0,\]

so every minute a starter loses to absence is a minute some reserve gains. Starters have \(\mu_i < h_i\) because they miss games; bench players must therefore have \(\mu_i > h_i\). Requiring \(\mu_i \le h_i\) for all \(i\) is unsatisfiable unless every player plays all 82 games. Houston Rockets and Charlotte Hornets state this explicitly on their desks; it holds for all 30.

Which column to use. For per-game category or points value, use Active MPG — that is what a manager gets on a night the player is in the lineup. For season totals, roster construction and games-played risk, use Team-game min. Do not compare Team-game min against a team desk's healthy-night minutes and read the gap as a contradiction; compare Active MPG against the desk instead, and expect the desk to run lower for reserves for the reason above.

The output is a model baseline; each team desk supplies the human-audited base rotation and the causal low/high branches.

Correction 2026-07-18: the July 17 build violated the availability ceiling \(w_i \le g_i/82\) in 161 of 507 rows, letting players claim a larger share of team games than their own projected GP allowed. Weights are now clamped to \(g_i/82\) and the freed minutes redistributed to teammates with availability headroom, so every team still sums to exactly 240. Two role assignments were also overridden against the team desks and dossiers rather than merely rescaled: Bub Carrington's Active MPG drops from 33.3 to 20.8 (the Washington desk and his dossier both make him a 14-minute backup point guard behind Trae Young, lifted toward 26-30 only when Young sits — the old row made him Washington's minutes leader), and Donte DiVincenzo falls from 68 GP to 12 because he ruptured his right Achilles and the Minnesota desk carries him at zero healthy-night minutes; his vacated minutes go to Shannon, Green, Lyles, Beringer and Hyland in the proportions the desk uses. The former Weight column for deep reserves was a synthetic geometric ladder (0.350, 0.280, 0.224, … , ratio 0.8) reused across all 30 teams regardless of player; it is now a per-player function of projected GP.
