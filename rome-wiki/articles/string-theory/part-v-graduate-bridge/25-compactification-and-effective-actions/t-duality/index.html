    T-duality

    A string cannot tell a circle of radius R from one of radius \alpha'/R ; the two give identical physics once momentum and winding trade places ( n\leftrightarrow m ). This is the sharpest way a string sees what a point particle cannot.

    A point particle on a circle knows only how fast it goes around — its momentum p=n/Rp=n/Rp=n/R. A closed string knows that and how many times it is wrapped — its winding mmm. Shrinking the circle (R→0R\to 0R→0) makes momentum expensive but winding cheap; growing it does the reverse. Because winding and momentum enter the string's energy on exactly the same footing, the string cannot decide which is which: it reads a tiny circle as a huge one. That is T-duality, and a point particle, having no winding, never feels it.

    The symmetry of the mass formula

    From Toroidal compactification, a closed string on a circle of radius RRR has mass2^22

    M^2(R;n,m) = \frac{n^2}{R^2} + \frac{m^2 R^2}{\alpha'^2} + \frac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2), \qquad N^\perp-\bar N^\perp = nm, \tag{1}

    with nnn the momentum quantum number, mmm the winding number, and N⊥,Nˉ⊥N^\perp,\bar N^\perpN⊥,Nˉ⊥ the left/right oscillator levels. The two radius-dependent terms are mirror images. Send

    R \longrightarrow \tilde R = \frac{\alpha'}{R}, \qquad n \longleftrightarrow m. \tag{2}

    The first term becomes m2R~2=m2R2α′2\dfrac{m^2}{\tilde R^2}=\dfrac{m^2R^2}{\alpha'^2}R~2m2​=α′2m2R2​ — exactly the second term of the original — and vice versa. The oscillator term is untouched, and the constraint N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nmN^\perp-\bar N^\perp=nmN⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm is symmetric in n,mn,mn,m. Hence

    \boxed{\,M^2(R;n,m) = M^2(\alpha'/R;\,m,n)\,}. \tag{3}

    As n,mn,mn,m run over all integers, the two theories produce the same list of masses: momentum and winding trade places. The interchange is not a coincidence of the formula — it is the string being unable to distinguish "moving around a small circle" from "wrapping a big one." A point particle has no winding to swap, so no such symmetry exists for it; the duality is intrinsically stringy.

    The self-dual radius

    The transformation R→α′/RR\to\alpha'/RR→α′/R has one fixed point:

    \tilde R = R \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{\alpha'}{R}=R \;\Longrightarrow\; R^* = \sqrt{\alpha'} = \ell_s. \tag{4}

    R∗R^*R∗ is the self-dual radius, equal to the string length. Since every R<R∗R<R^*R<R∗ is physically the same as some R>R∗R>R^*R>R∗, you can never get below R∗R^*R∗ by shrinking — try to, and the theory reads it as a larger circle instead. This is one of string theory's built-in minimum-length features: toroidal compactification cannot probe distances shorter than the string scale.

    At R=R∗R=R^*R=R∗ the spectrum is richest — extra massless states appear and the gauge symmetry enhances. Setting α′=1\alpha'=1α′=1 so R∗=1R^*=1R∗=1, level matching N⊥−Nˉ⊥=nmN^\perp-\bar N^\perp=nmN⊥−Nˉ⊥=nm together with M2=0M^2=0M2=0 has these vector solutions (each with a spacetime index μ\muμ):

    • n=m=±1n=m=\pm1n=m=±1, N⊥=1,Nˉ⊥=0N^\perp=1,\bar N^\perp=0N⊥=1,Nˉ⊥=0: two states α−1μ∣0;p⟩\alpha_{-1}^\mu|0;p\rangleα−1μ​∣0;p⟩ (unbarred oscillator excited), where ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ is the oscillator ground state carrying spacetime momentum ppp together with compact charges (n,m)(n,m)(n,m); quantum numbers (n,m)=(±1,±1)(n,m)=(\pm1,\pm1)(n,m)=(±1,±1). These carry (pL,pR)=(±2,0)(p_L,p_R)=(\pm2,0)(pL​,pR​)=(±2,0): all their compact charge is left-moving, so they are SU(2)LSU(2)_LSU(2)L​ vectors.
    • n=−m=±1n=-m=\pm1n=−m=±1, N⊥=0,Nˉ⊥=1N^\perp=0,\bar N^\perp=1N⊥=0,Nˉ⊥=1: two states αˉ−1μ∣0;p⟩\bar\alpha_{-1}^\mu|0;p\rangleαˉ−1μ​∣0;p⟩ (barred oscillator excited), quantum numbers (±1,∓1)(\pm1,\mp1)(±1,∓1). These carry (pL,pR)=(0,±2)(p_L,p_R)=(0,\pm2)(pL​,pR​)=(0,±2): all their compact charge is right-moving, so they are SU(2)RSU(2)_RSU(2)R​ vectors.

    The oscillator sector and the charge sector sit on opposite sides here, and that is not a slip: the spacetime vector index comes from the transverse oscillator (α−1μ\alpha_{-1}^\muα−1μ​, our right-mover), while the SU(2)LSU(2)_LSU(2)L​ charge pL=±2p_L=\pm2pL​=±2 comes from the compact zero modes. Together they build the two charged W±W^\pmW± bosons of SU(2)LSU(2)_LSU(2)L​; the barred states do the same for SU(2)RSU(2)_RSU(2)R​. The level count is fixed by (1): nm=+1nm=+1nm=+1 forces N⊥−Nˉ⊥=+1N^\perp-\bar N^\perp=+1N⊥−Nˉ⊥=+1, giving (1,0)(1,0)(1,0), while nm=−1nm=-1nm=−1 forces (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) — the excited oscillator sits on the side dictated by the sign of nmnmnm. These four new vectors join the two ordinary photons already present at n=m=0n=m=0n=m=0 — one from the metric (Gμ,25G_{\mu,25}Gμ,25​) and one from the BBB-field (Bμ,25B_{\mu,25}Bμ,25​), see Metric, B-field, and dilaton as low-energy fields — giving 3+33+33+3 massless gauge bosons:

    U(1)\times U(1)\ \longrightarrow\ SU(2)\times SU(2). \tag{5}

    Charged massless spin-1 fields can only be consistent as non-Abelian gauge bosons, so the string has manufactured a Yang–Mills symmetry out of nothing but a circle sitting at one special radius. Detune RRR away from R∗R^*R∗ and those extra vectors gain mass — the symmetry Higgses back down to the Cartan U(1)×U(1)U(1)\times U(1)U(1)×U(1).

    The dual coordinate: a literal new direction

    Why are the two descriptions physically identical and not merely spectrally? Under (2) the left/right momenta transform as pL→pLp_L\to p_LpL​→pL​, pR→−pRp_R\to -p_RpR​→−pR​: the duality is a sign flip on the right-movers only. That suggests building a new field that flips XRX_RXR​'s sign,

    \tilde X \equiv X_L - X_R, \qquad\text{versus the ordinary}\qquad X = X_L + X_R. \tag{6}

    Using the compact mode expansion from Toroidal compactification (XL,XRX_L,X_RXL​,XR​ carrying pL=p+wp_L=p+wpL​=p+w, pR=p−wp_R=p-wpR​=p−w), the two combinations are

    X = x_0 + \alpha' p\,\tau + \alpha' w\,\sigma + (\text{osc}), \qquad \tilde X = q_0 + \alpha' w\,\tau + \alpha' p\,\sigma + (\text{osc}). \tag{7}

    In X~\tilde XX~ the roles of ppp and www are exchanged: www now multiplies τ\tauτ (so winding is the momentum of X~\tilde XX~) and ppp multiplies σ\sigmaσ (so momentum is the winding of X~\tilde XX~). The dual coordinate X~\tilde XX~ lives on the dual circle of radius R~=α′/R\tilde R=\alpha'/RR~=α′/R. Compactly (worldsheet indices α,β\alpha,\betaα,β; ϵτσ=+1\epsilon_{\tau\sigma}=+1ϵτσ​=+1),

    \partial_\alpha X = \epsilon_{\alpha\beta}\,\partial^\beta \tilde X, \tag{8}

    which just repackages ∂τX=∂σX~\partial_\tau X=\partial_\sigma\tilde X∂τ​X=∂σ​X~, ∂σX=∂τX~\partial_\sigma X=\partial_\tau\tilde X∂σ​X=∂τ​X~ from (7).

    Why X~\tilde XX~ is an equally good coordinate

    In the free CFT the two-point functions are all that matter, and X~\tilde XX~ has the same ones as XXX. Holomorphically,

    X_L(z)X_L(0)\sim -\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\ln z,\qquad X_R(\bar z)X_R(0)\sim -\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\ln\bar z,\qquad X_LX_R\ \text{regular}. \tag{9}

    The cross term vanishes, so

    \tilde X\,\tilde X = (X_L-X_R)(X_L-X_R)\sim -\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}\ln|z|^2 = X\,X. \tag{10}

    The minus sign on XRX_RXR​ squares away in every local contraction, so the stress tensor and the whole oscillator algebra are identical whether written in XXX or in X~\tilde XX~. T-duality is therefore not a map between two theories — it is one theory described in two coordinates, and the "new direction that opens up as R→0R\to 0R→0" is X~\tilde XX~, a perfectly ordinary circle of radius α′/R\alpha'/Rα′/R.

    Path-integral derivation: duality of the full quantum theory

    The spectrum argument only shows the free-string states match. The following proof survives interactions and any worldsheet topology. Write the compact scalar as X=RφX=R\varphiX=Rφ with φ∼φ+2π\varphi\sim\varphi+2\piφ∼φ+2π, so the radius sits out front (Euclidean action):

    S[\varphi] = \frac{R^2}{4\pi\alpha'}\int d^2\sigma\,\partial_\alpha\varphi\,\partial^\alpha\varphi. \tag{11}

    The trick is to gauge the shift symmetry φ→φ+λ\varphi\to\varphi+\lambdaφ→φ+λ with a worldsheet gauge field AαA_\alphaAα​ (replacing ∂αφ\partial_\alpha\varphi∂α​φ by ∂αφ+Aα\partial_\alpha\varphi+A_\alpha∂α​φ+Aα​), then add a Lagrange multiplier θ\thetaθ, periodic θ∼θ+2π\theta\sim\theta+2\piθ∼θ+2π:

    S[\varphi,\theta,A] = \frac{R^2}{4\pi\alpha'}\int (\partial_\alpha\varphi+A_\alpha)^2 + \frac{i}{2\pi}\int \theta\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha A_\beta. \tag{12}

    Now do the same path integral two ways.

    Integrate out θ\thetaθ first. It forces ϵαβ∂αAβ=0\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha A_\beta=0ϵαβ∂α​Aβ​=0 — the gauge field is flat. On a simply connected worldsheet a flat AαA_\alphaAα​ is pure gauge; gauge it away and (12) collapses back to the original theory (11). (On a worldsheet with handles, AαA_\alphaAα​ can have nontrivial holonomy around cycles; the periodicity of θ\thetaθ is exactly what makes those holonomies gauge-trivial, restoring the winding sectors. This is why the equivalence holds at any genus.)

    Integrate out AαA_\alphaAα​ first. Fix the gauge φ=0\varphi=0φ=0 and integrate the θ ∂A\theta\,\partial Aθ∂A term by parts:

    \frac{R^2}{4\pi\alpha'}\int A_\alpha A^\alpha \;-\; \frac{i}{2\pi}\int \epsilon^{\alpha\beta}(\partial_\alpha\theta)\,A_\beta. \tag{13}

    Now AαA_\alphaAα​ appears purely quadratically, with no derivatives — a Gaussian integral done pointwise. Complete the square (c≡R2/4πα′c\equiv R^2/4\pi\alpha'c≡R2/4πα′, source bα≡−i2πϵαβ∂βθb_\alpha\equiv-\tfrac{i}{2\pi}\epsilon_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}\partial_\beta\thetabα​≡−2πi​ϵα​β∂β​θ):

    c\,A^2 + b\!\cdot\! A = c\Big(A+\tfrac{b}{2c}\Big)^2 - \tfrac{b^2}{4c}. \tag{14}

    The shift is absorbed by translating the integration variable, leaving the source term −b2/4c-b^2/4c−b2/4c. Using (ϵ ∂θ)2=(∂θ)2(\epsilon\,\partial\theta)^2=(\partial\theta)^2(ϵ∂θ)2=(∂θ)2 and b2=−14π2(∂θ)2b^2=-\tfrac{1}{4\pi^2}(\partial\theta)^2b2=−4π21​(∂θ)2,

    -\frac{b^2}{4c} = +\frac{\alpha'}{4\pi R^2}(\partial\theta)^2 \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; S[\theta]=\frac{\alpha'}{4\pi R^2}\int d^2\sigma\,\partial_\alpha\theta\,\partial^\alpha\theta. \tag{15}

    The sign came out positive — a healthy Euclidean kinetic term. Since θ∼θ+2π\theta\sim\theta+2\piθ∼θ+2π, this is the same compact-scalar theory as (11) but with

    \frac{\tilde R^2}{4\pi\alpha'} = \frac{\alpha'}{4\pi R^2} \;\;\Longrightarrow\;\; \tilde R = \frac{\alpha'}{R}. \tag{16}

    Same partition function, dual radius — for worldsheets of any topology, hence for interacting strings, not just the free spectrum. The overall Gaussian factor ∝α′/R\propto\sqrt{\alpha'}/R∝α′​/R that we discarded is what produces the dilaton shift below.

    Buscher rules, the general case

    If the background is independent of a coordinate yyy, gauging y→y+λy\to y+\lambday→y+λ makes AαA_\alphaAα​ appear quadratically with coefficients built from Gyy,Gyi,ByiG_{yy},G_{yi},B_{yi}Gyy​,Gyi​,Byi​; integrating it out in the dual order inverts that quadratic form, so Gyy→1/GyyG_{yy}\to 1/G_{yy}Gyy​→1/Gyy​ and the off-diagonal metric and BBB components trade roles. The full rules live in Metric, B-field, and dilaton as low-energy fields; the rectangular-circle case here is just Gyy=R2/α′G_{yy}=R^2/\alpha'Gyy​=R2/α′ inverting to R~=α′/R\tilde R=\alpha'/RR~=α′/R.

    What else transforms

    • String coupling. A scientist in a stringy world must not be able to tell RRR from α′/R\alpha'/Rα′/R even by measuring the prefactor of the effective action, which scales as R/gs2R/g_s^2R/gs2​. Holding R/gs2R/g_s^2R/gs2​ invariant forces g_s \longrightarrow \tilde g_s = \frac{\sqrt{\alpha'}\,g_s}{R}. \tag{17} This is exactly the discarded Gaussian factor from (16) made precise; see Metric, B-field, and dilaton as low-energy fields.
    • Open strings / D-branes. T-duality along a direction transverse to a Dppp-brane turns its Dirichlet condition into Neumann (from (8), ∂τX=0⇒∂σX~=0\partial_\tau X=0 \Rightarrow \partial_\sigma\tilde X=0∂τ​X=0⇒∂σ​X~=0): a Dppp-brane becomes a D(p+1)(p{+}1)(p+1)-brane, and along a wrapped direction a Dppp-brane becomes a D(p−1)(p{-}1)(p−1)-brane. This is historically how D-branes were discovered — by tracking open strings under T-duality.
    • Type IIA ↔\leftrightarrow↔ IIB, and the two heterotic strings, map into each other under T-duality.
    • Mirror symmetry is the same confusion on Calabi–Yau targets: two topologically distinct manifolds give one CFT. Noted here only for orientation.

    Open questions

    • The dilaton shift (17) is argued from the effective-action prefactor and identified with the leftover Gaussian factor in (16), but the precise determinant/Jacobian computation is deferred to a fuller Buscher-rule treatment.
    • The path-integral argument establishes the equivalence at any genus; a fully explicit genus-by-genus proof tracking every measure factor and holonomy sum is beyond this module's first pass.

    Exercise

    The path-integral derivation (11)–(16) hinged on one number: the leftover kinetic coefficient after integrating out AαA_\alphaAα​. Suppose you had instead added the multiplier term with a different normalization, ik2π∫θ ϵαβ∂αAβ\dfrac{ik}{2\pi}\int\theta\,\epsilon^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha A_\beta2πik​∫θϵαβ∂α​Aβ​ for some constant kkk. Redo the complete-the-square and read off the dual radius R~(k)\tilde R(k)R~(k). What value of kkk reproduces the correct self-duality (i.e. dualizing twice returns you to RRR), and why is that the only consistent choice?

    Solution

    With the rescaled term, the gauge-fixed action (13) becomes R24πα′A2−ik2π(ϵ ∂θ) ⁣⋅ ⁣A.\frac{R^2}{4\pi\alpha'}A^2 - \frac{ik}{2\pi}(\epsilon\,\partial\theta)\!\cdot\! A.4πα′R2​A2−2πik​(ϵ∂θ)⋅A. So c=R2/4πα′c=R^2/4\pi\alpha'c=R2/4πα′ is unchanged but the source is bα=−ik2πϵαβ∂βθb_\alpha=-\tfrac{ik}{2\pi}\epsilon_\alpha{}^\beta\partial_\beta\thetabα​=−2πik​ϵα​β∂β​θ, giving b2=−k24π2(∂θ)2b^2=-\tfrac{k^2}{4\pi^2}(\partial\theta)^2b2=−4π2k2​(∂θ)2. The leftover −b2/4c-b^2/4c−b2/4c is +k2α′4πR2(∂θ)2=R~24πα′(∂θ)2  ⟹  R~2=k2α′2R2  ⟹  R~=k α′R.+\frac{k^2\alpha'}{4\pi R^2}(\partial\theta)^2 = \frac{\tilde R^2}{4\pi\alpha'}(\partial\theta)^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \tilde R^2 = \frac{k^2\alpha'^2}{R^2} \;\Longrightarrow\; \tilde R = \frac{k\,\alpha'}{R}.+4πR2k2α′​(∂θ)2=4πα′R~2​(∂θ)2⟹R~2=R2k2α′2​⟹R~=Rkα′​. Now dualize a second time: R~~=kα′/R~=kα′/(kα′/R)=R\tilde{\tilde R} = k\alpha'/\tilde R = k\alpha'/(k\alpha'/R) = RR~~=kα′/R~=kα′/(kα′/R)=R. So the double-dual returns to RRR for any kkk — that consistency alone does not fix kkk. What fixes it is matching the spectrum: T-duality must be R→α′/RR\to\alpha'/RR→α′/R (from the mass formula (1)–(3)), which is R~=kα′/R\tilde R=k\alpha'/RR~=kα′/R only when k=1.k = 1.k=1. The k=1k=1k=1 normalization is the one for which the multiplier θ\thetaθ has the standard period 2π2\pi2π and lives on a genuine dual circle. Any other kkk would describe duality onto a circle of the wrong size, inconsistent with the momentum/winding swap the spectrum already forces. The moral: the factor of 1/2π1/2\pi1/2π in front of the θ ∂A\theta\,\partial Aθ∂A coupling is not cosmetic — it is fixed by demanding the path integral reproduce the known duality.
