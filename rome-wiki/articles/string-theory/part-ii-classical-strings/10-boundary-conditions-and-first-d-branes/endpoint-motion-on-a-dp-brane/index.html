    Endpoint motion on a Dp-brane

    Ending a string on a D p -brane softens the "endpoints move at c " theorem to the product law \sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}\,\hat n_\parallel=0 : the brane can catch sideways tension, so a string that meets it perpendicularly may move at any speed, while any other contact still forces v=c .

    Why the brane changes the verdict

    A free open-string endpoint is forced to move transversely at the speed of light (Endpoint velocity and endpoint force). That theorem assumed the end was free in every direction — a space-filling brane. The endpoint whips sideways at ccc because nothing out there absorbs the string's tension; the only way tension can vanish at a truly free end is for the relativistic factor to collapse, and that means v=cv=cv=c.

    Now bolt the endpoint to a lower-dimensional Dppp-brane. The brane is a table the end must stay on: free to slide within the brane (Neumann), pinned in the normal directions (Dirichlet). The pinned directions change the outcome, because the brane absorbs momentum along its normals and the string may pull straight into it. When the string meets the brane perpendicularly, all its tension points into the brane and drains there; nothing is left to fling the endpoint, and it may move as slowly as it likes. Only a leftover tangent component along the brane revives the light-speed conclusion.

    The entire result is one boundary law:

    \boxed{\ \sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\;\hat n_\parallel=0.\ }\tag{1}

    Either the tangent has no component along the brane (n^∥=0\hat n_\parallel=0n^∥​=0: perpendicular contact, vvv free) or the end moves at light speed (v=cv=cv=c). Everything below derives (1) and reads off the cases.

    Setup: which directions are free

    Work in ddd spatial dimensions with static gauge X0=ctX^0=ctX0=ct (Static gauge; mostly-plus signature). The Dppp-brane is a flat hyperplane; split the spatial coordinates into

    x1,…,xp⏟brane-tangent (Neumann, free)xp+1,…,xd⏟brane-normal (Dirichlet, fixed).\underbrace{x^1,\dots,x^p}_{\text{brane-tangent (Neumann, free)}}\qquad \underbrace{x^{p+1},\dots,x^d}_{\text{brane-normal (Dirichlet, fixed)}}.brane-tangent (Neumann, free)x1,…,xp​​brane-normal (Dirichlet, fixed)xp+1,…,xd​​.

    The boundary conditions at the endpoint are then

    Pσi=0  (i=1,…,p),Xa(τ,end)=xˉa  (a=p+1,…,d),Pσ0=0.\mathcal P^{\sigma i}=0\ \ (i=1,\dots,p),\qquad X^a(\tau,\text{end})=\bar x^a\ \ (a=p+1,\dots,d),\qquad \mathcal P^{\sigma 0}=0.Pσi=0  (i=1,…,p),Xa(τ,end)=xˉa  (a=p+1,…,d),Pσ0=0.

    Each brane-tangent direction is Neumann: the endpoint may vary there, so its boundary momentum flux Pσi\mathcal P^{\sigma i}Pσi must vanish. Each brane-normal direction is Dirichlet: the coordinate is frozen, and Pσa\mathcal P^{\sigma a}Pσa is left unconstrained — that nonzero flux is momentum draining out of the string into the brane. Time is always Neumann (a Dirichlet condition on X0X^0X0 would freeze the endpoint at one instant — see Neumann versus Dirichlet conditions).

    Write the unit tangent to the string at the endpoint, n^≡∂X⃗/∂s\hat n\equiv\partial\vec X/\partial sn^≡∂X/∂s (a unit vector because sss is arc length), and split it the same way:

    n^=n^∥+n^⊥,n^∥=(n1,…,np,0,…,0),n^⊥=(0,…,0,np+1,…,nd).\hat n=\hat n_\parallel+\hat n_\perp,\qquad \hat n_\parallel=(n^1,\dots,n^p,0,\dots,0),\qquad \hat n_\perp=(0,\dots,0,n^{p+1},\dots,n^d).n^=n^∥​+n^⊥​,n^∥​=(n1,…,np,0,…,0),n^⊥​=(0,…,0,np+1,…,nd).

    Because the normal coordinates are frozen, the endpoint velocity has no normal component — it lives entirely in the brane:

    v⃗=∂X⃗∂t=(v1,…,vp,0,…,0).(2)\vec v=\frac{\partial\vec X}{\partial t}=(v^1,\dots,v^p,0,\dots,0).\tag{2}v=∂t∂X​=(v1,…,vp,0,…,0).(2)

    This is the geometric content of the Dirichlet conditions: the endpoint slides along the brane, never off it.

    Two conditions survive on the brane

    Transversality, from the time condition. The always-present Pσ0=0\mathcal P^{\sigma 0}=0Pσ0=0 is unchanged by going to a Dppp-brane. Its explicit form (Endpoint velocity and endpoint force) is

    Pσ0=−T0c n^⋅v⃗1−v⊥2/c2=0,\mathcal P^{\sigma 0}=-\frac{T_0}{c}\,\frac{\hat n\cdot\vec v}{\sqrt{1-v_\perp^2/c^2}}=0,Pσ0=−cT0​​1−v⊥2​/c2​n^⋅v​=0,

    where v⊥v_\perpv⊥​ is the part of v⃗\vec vv perpendicular to the string. The denominator is finite, so the numerator vanishes:

    n^⋅v⃗=0at the endpoint.(3)\hat n\cdot\vec v=0\qquad\text{at the endpoint.}\tag{3}n^⋅v=0at the endpoint.(3)

    The endpoint velocity is orthogonal to the string: whatever freedom the end has, it moves sideways. The velocity is therefore purely transverse, v⊥=vv_\perp=vv⊥​=v, and we write vvv from here on. This much is exactly the space-filling result; the brane cannot touch it, since it comes from the time direction, which is Neumann on every brane.

    The tangent-force law, from the brane-tangent conditions. With transversality in hand, the spatial boundary momentum collapses to

    P⃗σ=−T01−v2c2  n^.\vec{\mathcal P}^{\sigma}=-T_0\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\;\hat n .Pσ=−T0​1−c2v2​​n^.

    The Neumann conditions demand this vanish only in the brane-tangent directions, and the components (n1,…,np)(n^1,\dots,n^p)(n1,…,np) are exactly n^∥\hat n_\paralleln^∥​:

    Pσi=−T01−v2c2 ni=0  (i=1,…,p)⟺1−v2c2  n^∥=0,\mathcal P^{\sigma i}=-T_0\sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\,n^i=0\ \ (i=1,\dots,p) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sqrt{1-\frac{v^2}{c^2}}\;\hat n_\parallel=0 ,Pσi=−T0​1−c2v2​​ni=0  (i=1,…,p)⟺1−c2v2​​n^∥​=0,

    the boxed law (1). There is no condition in the normal directions: Pσa=−T01−v2/c2 na\mathcal P^{\sigma a}=-T_0\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}\,n^aPσa=−T0​1−v2/c2​na is generically nonzero, and that flux is the tension the brane absorbs. In the space-filling case every component is constrained, n^∥=n^\hat n_\parallel=\hat nn^∥​=n^ is a full unit vector, cannot vanish, and (1) forces v=cv=cv=c. Lowering the brane dimension is exactly what opens the second escape route, n^∥=0\hat n_\parallel=0n^∥​=0.

    Reading off the cases

    (1) is a product: it holds when either factor is zero. The classification is bookkeeping on the two factors, though transversality (3) does extra work in low dimensions.

    brane argument endpoint motion
    D0 no brane-tangent directions, so n^∥\hat n_\paralleln^∥​ has no components — (1) is empty endpoint pinned to a point; all conditions automatic
    D1 (3) and (1) together force n^∥=0\hat n_\parallel=0n^∥​=0 (see below) string meets the line perpendicularly; speed along the line is free (subluminal allowed)
    Dppp, p≥2p\ge2p≥2 either n^∥=0\hat n_\parallel=0n^∥​=0 or v=cv=cv=c perpendicular contact ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ any speed; non-perpendicular contact ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ v=cv=cv=c transverse
    Dddd (space-filling) n^∥=n^\hat n_\parallel=\hat nn^∥​=n^ is a unit vector, cannot vanish end must move at ccc — the original theorem

    Why D1 cannot use the v=cv=cv=c escape. With one brane direction x1x^1x1, the velocity is v⃗=(v1,0,…,0)\vec v=(v^1,0,\dots,0)v=(v1,0,…,0) by (2), and transversality (3) reads n1v1=0n^1v^1=0n1v1=0. If v1≠0v^1\neq0v1=0, (3) forces n1=0n^1=0n1=0; if v1=0v^1=0v1=0, the square root equals 111 and (1) forces n1=0n^1=0n1=0. Either way n^∥=0\hat n_\parallel=0n^∥​=0: the string always meets a D1 perpendicularly, and once n^∥=0\hat n_\parallel=0n^∥​=0 the boundary equations leave the speed along the line undetermined. The v=cv=cv=c escape decouples from perpendicular contact only with two or more brane directions, where a nonzero in-brane velocity can be orthogonal to a nonzero n^∥\hat n_\paralleln^∥​.

    The extremes as a sanity check. D0 is all-Dirichlet: the endpoint is nailed to a point and there is nothing to solve. Dddd is all-Neumann: no normal directions exist for tension to escape into, so the end is flung at ccc. Every intermediate brane interpolates: the more normal directions, the more room for the string to plunge perpendicularly and slow down.

    Why this belongs with D-branes

    The same list — which directions are Neumann, which Dirichlet — sets both the brane's geometry and the endpoint's dynamics:

    • Neumann (brane-tangent) directions build the brane's dimensions and forbid momentum from leaving the endpoint there; in them the light-speed pressure survives.
    • Dirichlet (brane-normal) directions locate the brane and let the string's tension flow into it; they are what let the endpoint slow down.

    A D-brane is therefore not merely "where the endpoint lives." It is the object that decides which components of the string's tension must vanish at the boundary and which components the brane soaks up. Endpoint speed is a direct readout of the boundary-condition pattern.

    Open questions

    • "Speed along a D1 is free" is a local boundary-condition statement. A complete string solution must also satisfy the bulk wave equation and global constraints, which can pin the speed down.
    • This treatment holds the brane rigid. A dynamical brane recoils, carrying off the normal momentum that the fixed-brane picture simply lets leave the string; that back-reaction is deferred to later modules.

    Exercise

    A string ends on a D2-brane in d=3d=3d=3: the brane is the (x1,x2)(x^1,x^2)(x1,x2) plane and x3x^3x3 is normal. At the endpoint the unit tangent is n^=12(0,1,1)\hat n=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(0,1,1)n^=2​1​(0,1,1) — the string leaves the brane at 45∘45^\circ45∘. What do the product law (1) and transversality (3) together force for the endpoint velocity v⃗\vec vv?     Solution

    Tangent at 45∘45^\circ45∘. The brane-parallel part of n^=12(0,1,1)\hat n=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(0,1,1)n^=2​1​(0,1,1) is n^∥=12(0,1,0)\hat n_\parallel=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(0,1,0)n^∥​=2​1​(0,1,0), which is nonzero. The product law (1), 1−v2/c2 n^∥=0\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}\,\hat n_\parallel=01−v2/c2​n^∥​=0, then forces the square root to vanish: 1−v2/c2=0 ⟹ v=c.\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ v=c.1−v2/c2​=0 ⟹ v=c. The velocity lives in the brane, v⃗=(v1,v2,0)\vec v=(v^1,v^2,0)v=(v1,v2,0), and (3) gives n^⋅v⃗=12 v2=0\hat n\cdot\vec v=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\,v^2=0n^⋅v=2​1​v2=0, so v2=0v^2=0v2=0. This is the p≥2p\ge2p≥2 non-perpendicular branch.

    Perpendicular tangent. For n^=(0,0,1)\hat n=(0,0,1)n^=(0,0,1) the tangent is entirely normal: n^∥=0⃗\hat n_\parallel=\vec 0n^∥​=0. The endpoint may move at any subluminal speed, in any direction along the brane. Physically: the string pulls straight into the brane, all its tension is absorbed in the normal direction (Pσ3≠0\mathcal P^{\sigma 3}\neq0Pσ3=0 is allowed), and nothing is left to force the end to ccc.
