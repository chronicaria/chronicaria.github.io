    Strings between parallel branes

    Put two parallel D p -branes a distance |\Delta\bar x| apart; an open string whose two ends land on different branes is pulled taut across the gap, and the energy stored in that stretch, tension \times length, shows up directly as extra mass: M^2 = \big(\tfrac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\big)^2 + \tfrac1{\alpha'}(N^\perp-1) .

    Separation buys mass

    A relaxed rubber band costs nothing to hold; a stretched one pulls back. A fundamental string carries tension T0=1/(2πα′)T_0 = 1/(2\pi\alpha')T0​=1/(2πα′), so pinning its two ends to branes held apart forces it into a taut segment with rest energy T0×(length)T_0\times(\text{length})T0​×(length). Rest energy is mass, so a stretched string is a heavier particle than an unstretched one. Slide the branes together and the extra mass melts to zero. That one fact — separation buys mass — is what turns a stack of branes into a gauge theory with a built-in Higgs mechanism.

    Setup: two branes, four sectors

    Put brane 1 at xa=xˉ1ax^a = \bar x_1^axa=xˉ1a​ and brane 2 at xa=xˉ2ax^a = \bar x_2^axa=xˉ2a​ in the normal (Dirichlet) directions a=p+1,…,da=p+1,\dots,da=p+1,…,d. Same dimensionality and parallel, so they share their tangent and normal directions; they coincide iff xˉ1a=xˉ2a\bar x_1^a=\bar x_2^axˉ1a​=xˉ2a​ for all aaa, otherwise they are separated by Δxˉa≡xˉ2a−xˉ1a\Delta\bar x^a \equiv \bar x_2^a-\bar x_1^aΔxˉa≡xˉ2a​−xˉ1a​.

    An open string has two endpoints, each free to land on brane 1 or brane 2, giving four sectors. Label a ground state ∣p+,p⃗ ;[ij]⟩|p^+,\vec p\,;[ij]\rangle∣p+,p​;[ij]⟩, where [ij][ij][ij] means the σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 end sits on brane iii and the σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π end on brane jjj:

    [11], [22](both ends, same brane),[12], [21](stretched).[11],\ [22]\quad(\text{both ends, same brane}), \qquad [12],\ [21]\quad(\text{stretched}).[11], [22](both ends, same brane),[12], [21](stretched).

    The [11][11][11] and [22][22][22] sectors are just two copies of the single-brane story in Open strings on one brane. The new physics is entirely in the stretched sectors [12][12][12] and [21][21][21].

    These two are oriented oppositely — one runs brane 1 →\to→ brane 2, the other brane 2 →\to→ brane 1 — and orientation is physical: reversing a string flips the sign of its charge. So [12][12][12] and [21][21][21] are genuinely distinct sectors, not one string read backwards.

    Picture the branes as two nodes and an oriented open string as a directed edge. The label [ij][ij][ij] records where the edge starts and ends. When the nodes coincide these directed edges become the matrix indices of Chan-Paton factors.

    The stretched mode expansion

    Take a [12][12][12] string. The NN coordinates (X+,X−,XiX^+,X^-,X^iX+,X−,Xi) quantize exactly as on one brane — nothing about the second brane touches the tangent directions. Only the DD (normal) coordinates change: the two ends are now pinned at different values,

    X^a(\tau,\sigma)\big|_{\sigma=0} = \bar x_1^a, \qquad X^a(\tau,\sigma)\big|_{\sigma=\pi} = \bar x_2^a. \tag{1}

    The single-brane solution already enforces the σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 end via a difference of one function (see Open strings on one brane):

    X^a(\tau,\sigma) = \bar x_1^a + \tfrac12\big(f^a(\tau+\sigma) - f^a(\tau-\sigma)\big). \tag{2}

    Imposing the σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π condition on (2) gives the new constraint

    f^a(u+2\pi) - f^a(u) = 2\,\Delta\bar x^a. \tag{3}

    This is the entire difference from one brane. There faf^afa was strictly periodic; now it must grow by a fixed step 2Δxˉa2\Delta\bar x^a2Δxˉa each period, so only its derivative f′af'^af′a stays periodic and faf^afa itself picks up a term linear in its argument — exactly the piece a periodic function forbids. Writing fa(u)=f0a u+(periodic)f^a(u) = f_0^a\,u + (\text{periodic})fa(u)=f0a​u+(periodic) and matching (3) fixes the slope f0a=Δxˉa/πf_0^a = \Delta\bar x^a/\pif0a​=Δxˉa/π. Substituting back and doing the trig yields

    X^a(\tau,\sigma) = \bar x_1^a + \Delta\bar x^a\,\frac{\sigma}{\pi} + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac1n\,\alpha_n^a\,e^{-in\tau}\sin n\sigma. \tag{4}

    Reading (4) term by term:

    • xˉ1a+Δxˉa σπ\bar x_1^a + \Delta\bar x^a\,\tfrac\sigma\pixˉ1a​+Δxˉaπσ​ is a straight line in σ\sigmaσ running from brane 1 at σ=0\sigma=0σ=0 to brane 2 at σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π — the classical taut string spanning the gap.
    • The sin⁡nσ\sin n\sigmasinnσ oscillators vanish at both ends, so they wobble the middle of the string but never nudge an endpoint off its brane.
    • There is still no term linear in τ\tauτ, hence no center-of-mass momentum in the normal directions: a string pinned to branes cannot drift off them.

    The new zero mode

    The linear-in-σ\sigmaσ term means the mode sum now carries a nonzero n=0n=0n=0 piece. Compute the two derivatives from (4):

    \dot X^a = -i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_n^a e^{-in\tau}\sin n\sigma, \qquad X'^a = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_n^a e^{-in\tau}\cos n\sigma, \tag{5}

    with

    \boxed{\ \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0^a = \frac1\pi\,\Delta\bar x^a\ } \tag{6}

    Watch where this zero mode goes. In X˙a\dot X^aX˙a it multiplies sin⁡(0⋅σ)=0\sin(0\cdot\sigma)=0sin(0⋅σ)=0, so α0a\alpha_0^aα0a​ drops out of the velocity — consistent with zero normal momentum. In X′aX'^aX′a it multiplies cos⁡(0⋅σ)=1\cos(0\cdot\sigma)=1cos(0⋅σ)=1, so α0a\alpha_0^aα0a​ survives in the slope — exactly what a tilted, stretched string should have. A nonzero α0a\alpha_0^aα0a​ with zero momentum is not a contradiction; it is the algebraic fingerprint of stretch. From (6), α0a\alpha_0^aα0a​ vanishes precisely when Δxˉa=0\Delta\bar x^a=0Δxˉa=0, when the branes coincide. The same zero mode — present in the slope, absent from the velocity — reappears for a closed string wound around a compact circle, where it measures winding rather than momentum.

    The mass: tension times length, squared

    Feed (6) into the light-cone M2M^2M2. The oscillator sum and the normal-ordering intercept are identical to the single brane: the boundary conditions only touched the zero mode, so the regularized constant is still a=−1a=-1a=−1 (the value forced at D=26D=26D=26, obtained by cutting off ∑nn\sum_n n∑n​n and keeping its finite part ζ(−1)=−112\zeta(-1)=-\tfrac1{12}ζ(−1)=−121​, never the naive divergent sum). The only new ingredient is the zero-mode term 12α′α0aα0a\tfrac{1}{2\alpha'}\alpha_0^a\alpha_0^a2α′1​α0a​α0a​:

    M^2 = \frac{1}{2\alpha'}\alpha_0^a\alpha_0^a + \frac1{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp - 1\big), \qquad N^\perp = \sum_{n\ge1}n\,a_n^{i\dagger}a_n^i + \sum_{m\ge1}m\,a_m^{a\dagger}a_m^a. \tag{7}

    Now substitute (6), α0aα0a=12α′ 1π2(Δxˉ)2\alpha_0^a\alpha_0^a = \tfrac{1}{2\alpha'}\,\tfrac{1}{\pi^2}(\Delta\bar x)^2α0a​α0a​=2α′1​π21​(Δxˉ)2:

    \frac{1}{2\alpha'}\alpha_0^a\alpha_0^a = \frac{1}{2\alpha'}\cdot\frac{1}{2\alpha'}\cdot\frac{(\Delta\bar x)^2}{\pi^2} = \left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2. \tag{8}

    So

    \boxed{\ M^2 = \left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2 + \frac1{\alpha'}\big(N^\perp - 1\big)\ } \tag{9}

    The denominator 2πα′2\pi\alpha'2πα′ is 1/T01/T_01/T0​, the inverse string tension, so the new term reads

    (∣Δxˉ∣2πα′)2=(T0 ∣Δxˉ∣)2=(tension×length)2=(rest energy of a taut static string)2.\left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2 = \big(T_0\,|\Delta\bar x|\big)^2 = (\text{tension}\times\text{length})^2 = (\text{rest energy of a taut static string})^2.(2πα′∣Δxˉ∣​)2=(T0​∣Δxˉ∣)2=(tension×length)2=(rest energy of a taut static string)2.

    Stretching a string between separated branes costs energy =T0×length= T_0\times\text{length}=T0​×length, and for a particle at rest energy is mass, so that cost lands squared inside M2M^2M2. It vanishes exactly when the branes coincide — the rubber band goes slack.

    Spectrum of the stretched sector

    Ground state [12][12][12]: |p^+,\vec p\,;[12]\rangle, \qquad M^2 = -\frac1{\alpha'} + \left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2. \tag{10} A Lorentz scalar on the world-volume. The intercept −1/α′-1/\alpha'−1/α′ makes it tachyonic when the branes are close, but the stretch term lifts it: it is exactly massless at the critical separation

    |\Delta\bar x| = 2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}, \tag{11}

    and genuinely massive beyond. The tachyon is a signal of instability at sub-string-scale separations; push the branes past one string length and it disappears.

    Level 1. One oscillator on the ground state, all now with M2=(∣Δxˉ∣2πα′)2M^2 = \big(\tfrac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\big)^2M2=(2πα′∣Δxˉ∣​)2 — massive whenever the branes are apart:

    • a1i†∣p+,p⃗ ;[12]⟩a_1^{i\dagger}|p^+,\vec p\,;[12]\ranglea1i†​∣p+,p​;[12]⟩, i=2,…,pi=2,\dots,pi=2,…,p: a world-volume vector, p−1p-1p−1 states.
    • a1a†∣p+,p⃗ ;[12]⟩a_1^{a\dagger}|p^+,\vec p\,;[12]\ranglea1a†​∣p+,p​;[12]⟩, a=p+1,…,da=p+1,\dots,da=p+1,…,d: d−pd-pd−p scalars.

    Counting states exposes a Higgs mechanism hidden in this list. In a (p+1)(p+1)(p+1)-dimensional world-volume a massless vector has p−1p-1p−1 polarizations, but a massive vector has ppp — one more. The a1i†a_1^{i\dagger}a1i†​ states supply only p−1p-1p−1, so the missing longitudinal polarization must come from the scalars. Exactly one scalar combination is singled out, the one polarized along the inter-brane direction:

    \sum_{a=p+1}^{d}\Delta\bar x^a\,a_1^{a\dagger}|p^+,\vec p\,;[12]\rangle . \tag{12}

    The other d−p−1d-p-1d−p−1 scalar directions, orthogonal to Δxˉa\Delta\bar x^aΔxˉa, stay ordinary massive scalars. The net level-1 content is one massive vector plus d−p−1d-p-1d−p−1 massive scalars. This is a stringy Higgs mechanism: separating the branes "eats" the scalar pointing from one brane to the other and hands its degree of freedom to the gauge field as mass. Which scalar gets eaten is not put in by hand — the background Δxˉa\Delta\bar x^aΔxˉa picks it out.

    Coincident-brane limit: gauge symmetry enhancement

    Let the separation →0\to 0→0. Then the stretch term vanishes, the [12][12][12] and [21][21][21] tachyons and vectors join the [11],[22][11],[22][11],[22] ones, and all four sectors carry a massless gauge field. Two coincident Dppp-branes therefore support not two independent photons but a single U(2)U(2)U(2) Yang–Mills field, the four sectors filling out the 2×22\times22×2 matrix of gauge bosons; NNN coincident branes give U(N)U(N)U(N), with the [ij][ij][ij] labels serving as Chan–Paton matrix indices. See Chan-Paton factors for the matrix dictionary and Yang-Mills from D-branes for the low-energy gauge theory.

    Run it backwards and you have the Higgs story in geometric form: separating a stack breaks U(N)→U(1)NU(N)\to U(1)^NU(N)→U(1)N, the off-diagonal (stretched) gauge bosons gaining mass ∝\propto∝ separation while the diagonal ones stay massless.

    Open questions

    • Orientation ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ charge sign (the [12][12][12] vs [21][21][21] distinction) rests on the string carrying an oriented endpoint charge; taken on faith here, justified when open-string charges are set up carefully later.
    • Where do the [12][12][12] fields "live"? Their momenta are (p+1)(p+1)(p+1)-dimensional like a single-brane field, but the two branes are on equal footing, so the fields cannot be assigned to either one alone — in some sense they live on both. Making this precise seems to need noncommutative geometry; beyond first-pass scope, flagged.

    Exercise

    The critical separation (11) is where the stretched ground state (10) turns from tachyonic to massless. (a) Derive it directly from (10). (b) Then check the same number a completely different way: set it equal to the reduced Compton wavelength λ=ℏ/(mc)\lambda = \hbar/(mc)λ=ℏ/(mc) associated with the unstretched ground-state tachyon mass ∣M0∣|M_0|∣M0​∣, and confirm you land on the same scale (work in units ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1). What does this coincidence say about why the transition happens at one string length?

    Solution

    (a) The ground state is massless when the two terms in (10) cancel: 0=−1α′+(∣Δxˉ∣2πα′)2  ⟹  (∣Δxˉ∣2πα′)2=1α′  ⟹  ∣Δxˉ∣2=(2πα′)2α′=4π2α′.0 = -\frac{1}{\alpha'} + \left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2 \;\Longrightarrow\; \left(\frac{|\Delta\bar x|}{2\pi\alpha'}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{\alpha'} \;\Longrightarrow\; |\Delta\bar x|^2 = \frac{(2\pi\alpha')^2}{\alpha'} = 4\pi^2\alpha'.0=−α′1​+(2πα′∣Δxˉ∣​)2⟹(2πα′∣Δxˉ∣​)2=α′1​⟹∣Δxˉ∣2=α′(2πα′)2​=4π2α′. Taking the positive root, ∣Δxˉ∣=2πα′|\Delta\bar x| = 2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'}∣Δxˉ∣=2πα′​, which is (11). Below it the intercept wins and M2<0M^2<0M2<0 (tachyon); above it the stretch term wins and M2>0M^2>0M2>0.

    (b) The unstretched tachyon (Δxˉ=0\Delta\bar x=0Δxˉ=0) has M02=−1/α′M_0^2 = -1/\alpha'M02​=−1/α′, so its magnitude is ∣M0∣=1/α′|M_0| = 1/\sqrt{\alpha'}∣M0​∣=1/α′​. Its reduced Compton wavelength is λ=1∣M0∣=α′.\lambda = \frac{1}{|M_0|} = \sqrt{\alpha'}.λ=∣M0​∣1​=α′​. This differs from the critical separation only by the factor 2π2\pi2π: ∣Δxˉ∣crit=2π λ|\Delta\bar x|_{\rm crit} = 2\pi\,\lambda∣Δxˉ∣crit​=2πλ. Both are set by the single length scale α′\sqrt{\alpha'}α′​, the string length. The tachyon signals an instability whose natural size is its own Compton wavelength ∼α′\sim\sqrt{\alpha'}∼α′​; once you hold the branes farther apart than that, the taut-string energy cost T0∣Δxˉ∣T_0|\Delta\bar x|T0​∣Δxˉ∣ overwhelms the tachyonic intercept and the mode can no longer go unstable. The transition happens at one string length because that is the only length in the problem — there is no other dimensionful quantity for ∣Δxˉ∣crit|\Delta\bar x|_{\rm crit}∣Δxˉ∣crit​ to be built from.
