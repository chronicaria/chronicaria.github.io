    Neumann versus Dirichlet conditions

    Varying the open-string action leaves one leftover term at each endpoint, \mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,\delta X^\mu = 0 . Killing it forces a binary choice per coordinate — Neumann ( \mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0 , free) or Dirichlet ( X^\mu pinned) — and that per-axis pattern is the data that defines a D-brane.

    A guitar string in DDD dimensions

    Think of the string as a guitar string, but relativistic and living in DDD-dimensional spacetime. When you shake a real guitar string, its ends do something definite: they are either clamped (a fret) or free (past the bridge, if you let it flap). Those are the only two clean options, and you must pick one — the wave equation alone does not decide for you.

    The open relativistic string is the same, with one twist: the choice is made independently in every spacetime direction. An endpoint can be free to slide in x1x^1x1 while being nailed to a fixed value of x2x^2x2. The list of "free or fixed" over all the spatial directions is not fine print — it is the specification of the surface the endpoint must live on. That surface is a D-brane. This note derives where the choice comes from and why it is exactly binary.

    Where the boundary term comes from

    Start from the Nambu–Goto action (mostly-plus signature η=diag(−,+,…,+)\eta=\mathrm{diag}(-,+,\dots,+)η=diag(−,+,…,+) throughout),

    S=−T0c∫dτ dσ (X˙ ⁣⋅X′)2−X˙2 X′2  ≡  ∫dτ dσ L(X˙μ,X′μ),S=-\frac{T_0}{c}\int d\tau\,d\sigma\, \sqrt{(\dot X\!\cdot X')^2-\dot X^2\,X'^2} \;\equiv\;\int d\tau\,d\sigma\,\mathcal L(\dot X^\mu,X'^\mu),S=−cT0​​∫dτdσ(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2​≡∫dτdσL(X˙μ,X′μ),

Define the two momentum densities as the derivatives of L\mathcal LL with respect to the two velocities:

    Pμτ≡∂L∂X˙μ=−T0c (X˙ ⁣⋅X′) Xμ′−X′2 X˙μ(X˙ ⁣⋅X′)2−X˙2 X′2,Pμσ≡∂L∂X′μ=−T0c (X˙ ⁣⋅X′) X˙μ−X˙2 Xμ′(X˙ ⁣⋅X′)2−X˙2 X′2.\mathcal P^\tau_\mu \equiv \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial\dot X^\mu} =-\frac{T_0}{c}\, \frac{(\dot X\!\cdot X')\,X'_\mu-X'^2\,\dot X_\mu} {\sqrt{(\dot X\!\cdot X')^2-\dot X^2\,X'^2}}, \qquad \mathcal P^\sigma_\mu \equiv \frac{\partial\mathcal L}{\partial X'^\mu} =-\frac{T_0}{c}\, \frac{(\dot X\!\cdot X')\,\dot X_\mu-\dot X^2\,X'_\mu} {\sqrt{(\dot X\!\cdot X')^2-\dot X^2\,X'^2}}.Pμτ​≡∂X˙μ∂L​=−cT0​​(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2​(X˙⋅X′)Xμ′​−X′2X˙μ​​,Pμσ​≡∂X′μ∂L​=−cT0​​(X˙⋅X′)2−X˙2X′2​(X˙⋅X′)X˙μ​−X˙2Xμ′​​.

    Here Pμτ\mathcal P^\tau_\muPμτ​ is the density of spacetime momentum the string carries forward in worldsheet time, and Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ is the flux of that momentum along the string, toward an endpoint. The boundary condition turns entirely on this second density: it is a statement about how much momentum reaches the end.

    Vary Xμ→Xμ+δXμX^\mu\to X^\mu+\delta X^\muXμ→Xμ+δXμ. Since δX˙μ=∂τδXμ\delta\dot X^\mu=\partial_\tau\delta X^\muδX˙μ=∂τ​δXμ and δX′μ=∂σδXμ\delta X'^\mu=\partial_\sigma\delta X^\muδX′μ=∂σ​δXμ,

    Integrate by parts in each variable — move the derivative off δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ, picking up a boundary term for each:

    δS=∫dσ [Pμτ δXμ]τiτf⏟τ-boundary+∫dτ [Pμσ δXμ]σ=0σ=σ1⏟σ-boundary−∫dτ dσ (∂τPμτ+∂σPμσ) δXμ.\delta S= \underbrace{\int d\sigma\,\Big[\mathcal P^\tau_\mu\,\delta X^\mu\Big]_{\tau_i}^{\tau_f}}_{\text{$\tau$-boundary}} +\underbrace{\int d\tau\,\Big[\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,\delta X^\mu\Big]_{\sigma=0}^{\sigma=\sigma_1}}_{\text{$\sigma$-boundary}} -\int d\tau\,d\sigma\,\Big(\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu+\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\Big)\,\delta X^\mu.δS=τ-boundary∫dσ[Pμτ​δXμ]τi​τf​​​​+σ-boundary∫dτ[Pμσ​δXμ]σ=0σ=σ1​​​​−∫dτdσ(∂τ​Pμτ​+∂σ​Pμσ​)δXμ.

    The τ\tauτ-boundary term vanishes because Hamilton's principle holds the string's configuration fixed at the initial and final times (δXμ=0\delta X^\mu=0δXμ=0 there). Two pieces remain, and each carries a distinct job:

    • Bulk term ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ equation of motion. For arbitrary interior δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ it forces ∂τPμτ+∂σPμσ=0.\partial_\tau\mathcal P^\tau_\mu+\partial_\sigma\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0.∂τ​Pμτ​+∂σ​Pμσ​=0. This is local conservation of spacetime momentum on the worldsheet.

    • σ\sigmaσ-boundary term ⇒\Rightarrow⇒ the endpoint conditions. A closed string has no boundary in σ\sigmaσ (the ends are glued), so this term is absent and we are done. An open string has two edges, and the variations δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ there — one independent function per coordinate per edge — force every term in the sum to vanish separately: \boxed{\ \mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\,\delta X^\mu\Big|_{\text{endpoint}}=0\ \text{(no sum)}\quad\text{for each }\mu,\text{ at }\sigma=0\text{ and }\sigma=\sigma_1.\ } \tag{1}

    The variational principle does not tell you what happens at the ends. It only demands that each product vanish. You supply the physics by choosing, coordinate by coordinate, which of the two factors you send to zero.

    The mechanical analogy. For a single-particle action ∫L(q,q˙) dt\int L(q,\dot q)\,dt∫L(q,q˙​)dt, integration by parts leaves an endpoint term p δqp\,\delta qpδq. A free end has arbitrary δq\delta qδq, so the momentum ppp must vanish there; a fixed end has δq=0\delta q=0δq=0, so nothing is asked of ppp. The string repeats this exactly, once per spacetime coordinate at each edge — with Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ playing the role of ppp.

    The binary choice

    Equation (1) is a product of two factors; killing it means killing at least one. That gives precisely two clean options per coordinate per endpoint.

    Neumann (free) — kill the momentum factor: Pμσ=0at the endpoint.\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0\quad\text{at the endpoint.}Pμσ​=0at the endpoint. The displacement δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ is then unconstrained, so the endpoint is free to move in the XμX^\muXμ direction. Read Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ as the flow of μ\muμ-momentum along the string toward its end: setting it to zero says no momentum leaks off the end — there is nothing out there to catch it, so the tension force reflects back. (When every direction is Neumann, this no-leak condition is stringent enough to force the endpoint to move at ccc; see Endpoint velocity and endpoint force.)

    Dirichlet (fixed) — kill the displacement factor instead: Xμ(τ,endpoint)=const⟹δXμ=0 at the endpoint.X^\mu(\tau,\text{endpoint})=\text{const}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \delta X^\mu=0\ \text{at the endpoint.}Xμ(τ,endpoint)=const⟹δXμ=0 at the endpoint. The endpoint is pinned to a fixed value of XμX^\muXμ for all time, so its value is not allowed to vary and δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ dies. Now Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ is generally nonzero — momentum does flow off the end, into whatever is holding the string fixed. That "whatever" is a D-brane, and this leaked momentum is the force the string exerts on it.

    Neumann / free Pμσ\mathcal P^\sigma_\muPμσ​ yes none
    Dirichlet / fixed δXμ\delta X^\muδXμ no, pinned allowed — flows into the brane

    Why these names. "Dirichlet" fixes the value of the field XμX^\muXμ at the boundary — a Dirichlet boundary-value problem. "Neumann" fixes a derivative: after the later conformal/light-cone gauge, Pμσ∝∂σXμ\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu\propto\partial_\sigma X^\muPμσ​∝∂σ​Xμ, so Pμσ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0Pμσ​=0 becomes the normal-derivative condition ∂σXμ=0\partial_\sigma X^\mu=0∂σ​Xμ=0. Same dichotomy every PDE course teaches — it transfers directly.

    Why time is always Neumann

    The choice is not symmetric between the two factors. You may never impose Dirichlet on the time coordinate X0X^0X0.

    A Dirichlet condition X0(τ,end)=constX^0(\tau,\text{end})=\text{const}X0(τ,end)=const would freeze the endpoint at a single instant of time. But the string must persist through time — its endpoint has to exist at every moment, tracing a worldline. Pinning X0X^0X0 would delete the endpoint at all other times, which is nonsense. Equivalently: Dirichlet says ∂τXμ=0\partial_\tau X^\mu=0∂τ​Xμ=0, and since τ\tauτ advances time, ∂τX0=0\partial_\tau X^0=0∂τ​X0=0 contradicts the fact that time must flow as τ\tauτ flows. So

     Always Neumann along X0:P0σ=0  at every endpoint. \boxed{\ \text{Always Neumann along }X^0:\quad \mathcal P^\sigma_0=0\ \text{ at every endpoint.}\ } Always Neumann along X0:P0σ​=0  at every endpoint. ​

    This is precisely why a D-brane always extends in the time direction: it has a worldvolume, never a purely spatial one. An endpoint is free in ppp chosen spatial directions and time, pinned in the rest; the surface it sweeps is a (p+1)(p{+}1)(p+1)-dimensional Dp-brane. The counting and the resulting geometry are developed in D-branes (first appearance).

    Worked instance: the D2-brane

    Take three spatial dimensions, d=3d=3d=3. Choose Neumann along x1,x2x^1,x^2x1,x2 and Dirichlet along x3x^3x3, pinning X3(τ,end)=0X^3(\tau,\text{end})=0X3(τ,end)=0:

    P1σ=0,P2σ=0 (free);X3=0 (fixed);P0σ=0 (time, always free).\mathcal P^\sigma_1=0,\quad \mathcal P^\sigma_2=0\ (\text{free}); \qquad X^3=0\ (\text{fixed}); \qquad \mathcal P^\sigma_0=0\ (\text{time, always free}).P1σ​=0,P2σ​=0 (free);X3=0 (fixed);P0σ​=0 (time, always free).

    The endpoints glide freely on the (x1,x2)(x^1,x^2)(x1,x2) plane but can never leave it — x3=0x^3=0x3=0 for all time. That plane, swept through time, is a D2-brane. The string body can flop around anywhere in space, but its two ends are stuck to the brane. If instead every spatial direction is Neumann (p=dp=dp=d), the ends roam freely everywhere: a space-filling D-brane, the fully-free case of Endpoint velocity and endpoint force.

    Open questions

    • The two ends of one open string can carry different conditions in the same direction (Neumann at one end, Dirichlet at the other). Later Strings stretched between Dp- and Dq-branes names these ND/DN coordinates and finds their oscillators carry half-integer moding.
    • Here the free condition is Pμσ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_\mu=0Pμσ​=0 in the full nonlinear Nambu–Goto form; the textbook ∂σXμ=0\partial_\sigma X^\mu=0∂σ​Xμ=0 Neumann form only emerges after the conformal/light-cone gauge fixing of the next module.

    Exercise

    Consider an open string endpoint in a two-dimensional spatial world (x1,x2)(x^1,x^2)(x1,x2). Suppose you try to impose both conditions in the same direction x1x^1x1 at the same endpoint: Neumann (P1σ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_1=0P1σ​=0) and Dirichlet (δX1=0\delta X^1=0δX1=0) simultaneously.

    (a) Is this over-determined, contradictory, or redundant? (b) Now the physically interesting case: what goes wrong if you try to impose Dirichlet on x1x^1x1 at one end while insisting the endpoint carry no momentum flow (P1σ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_1=0P1σ​=0) there too — and how does this differ from the illegal Dirichlet-on-time case?

    Solution

    (a) For the variational principle, nothing goes wrong — the demand is the single product P1σ δX1=0\mathcal P^\sigma_1\,\delta X^1=0P1σ​δX1=0, and either factor's vanishing already satisfies it, so the pair is redundant, not contradictory. As a condition on the dynamics, however, it is an extra restriction the variational principle never asked for: it selects the special "no-force pinned endpoint," pinned and with no momentum leaking into whatever pins it. It is not what a generic D-brane does.

    (b) This is exactly case (a) read physically. Dirichlet pins X1=constX^1=\text{const}X1=const, which alone kills the boundary term. Additionally demanding P1σ=0\mathcal P^\sigma_1=0P1σ​=0 is a further physical condition, not required by the variational principle: it says the string exerts no force on the brane in the x1x^1x1 direction. Generic configurations violate it — a stretched, pinned string tugs on its anchor — so imposing it restricts to force-free special solutions rather than defining the boundary condition.

    This is the opposite of the time case. Dirichlet-on-X0X^0X0 is forbidden because pinning X0X^0X0 deletes the endpoint at all other times (it contradicts τ\tauτ advancing time). Dirichlet-on-x1x^1x1 is perfectly legal — spatial pinning is fine, and it just anchors the end to a fixed spatial point. The asymmetry is entirely about time: you can freeze where an endpoint sits in space, never when it exists.
