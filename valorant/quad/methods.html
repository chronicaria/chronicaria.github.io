What the number is, and what it is not

MWPA divided by the matches the player was in: the share of a match win their credited actions are worth, per match played. +6.4% is six extra wins per hundred matches. It is the per-100-rounds rate rescaled by the player's own rounds per match, a positive constant, so the BCa endpoints carry across unchanged and so does covers_zero.

Impact per match

One division, and it happens once: a player's season total of MWPA across the act, over the matches they played. Trzzcko's +0.280 over 17 matches is +1.6%. SN0RLAX's +0.875 over 70 matches is +1.3%. The two totals are 0.596 apart; the two exposures are 4.1 times apart, and it is the second number that separates the two headlines.

MWPA per 100 rounds is the estimator's own denominator, and it is not one anybody plays. A match is what these four queue for and what a win is scored in, so the headline divides by matches. The order of the four is the same in either denominator, so the choice changes no finding on this site — only whether the number can be read without arithmetic.

Inside one player the rescale is a positive constant — rounds per match runs 18.8 to 20.7 across the four — and BCa respects a monotone transformation, so the interval endpoints are the same endpoints and covers_zero is the same flag. There is no second bootstrap: the low end of an impact interval is the low end of the rate interval times that constant, and nothing was resampled again to get it.

A percentage of a match win is a count of match wins, so the headline multiplies back out: +1.6% across the 17 matches behind it is +0.280 of one match win, which is Trzzcko's season total. All four intervals cover the null, so read a sign as a direction this much play cannot confirm.

MWPA per 100 rounds is the same measurement in the other denominator: the season total divided by the rounds the player was credited in, times 100. It survives here, and on the five breakdown tables and the synergy table of a player page, where it is the only correct unit — those cells hold a slice of a match rather than a match, so their match count is a count of matches the cell appeared in and not an exposure to divide by. The component rail is the one cut that does convert, because its rounds and its matches are the headline's own, and it is drawn per match.

Two steps, and what they produce

Match win probability added. A player's round credits, each centered on the mean credit of everyone else who played that round, each multiplied by the round's marginal match value, and summed. A value of +62.00% is six tenths of a match win added.

Every round produces a ledger: kill credit, death debit, plant, defuse, and a share of the clock drift, all of them in round win probability points. MWPA does two things to that ledger and nothing else.

Centring. A player's credit in a round has the mean credit of everyone else credited in that round subtracted from it. The number of players credited is whatever the round had, so a four-against-five round centres on four others rather than on an assumed nine. After centring, the credits in a round sum to zero exactly, which is the property that makes a lobby's surplus impossible to bank.

Leverage. The centred credit is then multiplied by the round's marginal match value: how much one round of win probability is worth to the match, evaluated at the score entering the round. A round at twelve apiece is worth several times a round at twelve to three, and the multiplication says so. The mean round in this act carries a marginal match value of 0.1605, and the index printed beside a round's leverage is that round divided by this mean.

The product is in match win probability points. A season total is the product summed over the act, in matches added. That total is the numerator the headline above divides.

How to read an interval

Every interval here is a 95.0% BCa bootstrap over 100,000 replicates at seed 20260726, resampling whole matches so that the rounds of one match move together.

An interval that covers zero means this exposure does not separate the number from no effect at all. It does not mean the player had no effect, and it does not mean the point estimate is wrong. It means the sample cannot tell the two apart. Every card and every breakdown cell on this site prints its interval next to its number for that reason.

The 20-round floor marks a cell rather than suppressing it: below it a cell carries thin and still prints its number and interval. Per-round MWPA has a standard deviation of 0.0443 in this act, so a 20-round mean carries a pure-noise standard error of about 0.99 per 100 rounds against a spread between the four of 0.15 — read a thin cell as description, not estimate.

The bootstrap resamples whole matches, so a cell whose rounds all come from one match has a zero-width interval that reads as excluding zero and does not: one cluster, nothing for the resample to vary. Check matches before covers_zero.

What is not measured

Abilities do not exist in this data at any grain — four integers per player-match, null in every round record — so smokes, flashes and mollies are absent from every number here. Revives are UNCREDITED rather than absent: a revive is a timed event, so the curve moves under one and the round timeline prints that move beside whoever cast it, but revive is not one of the five credit types and no player's ledger carries it. The five are the whole ledger, and no cell renders a zero for utility.

MWPA is a descriptive allocation of modelled probability movement, not a causal player effect. The model behind it carries no player, team or lobby term, so every credit is a residual against a baseline pooled across ranks.

Reading conventions

Sign
Carried three ways: the + or − the number itself is printed with, a colour, and the side of the zero line the bar sits on. Red and green are the one pair the common dichromacies cannot separate, so neither colour is ever asked to carry the sign alone. There were direction arrows here as a fourth channel and they are gone: they repeated the printed sign, and every signed field on this site is written to a format that prints one.
Em dash
Nothing unmeasured renders as a zero. A cell with no number carries an em dash and, on hover, the reason it is absent. A breakdown cell with no rounds is not in the payload at all and does not appear as a row.
Middot
A measured zero. In a round ledger a player who did not plant has a plant credit of exactly zero, which is a fact about the round rather than a missing value, and reads as a middot so it cannot be confused with either an em dash or a small number.
Bars
No table on this site draws a bar beside a number any more: the number is the number, and a magnitude bar next to it was a second rendering of a fact already written. What is still drawn is a MARK, and a mark fills against a fixed axis, never against the largest row on the screen, so the same value is the same width on every page. Three of those axes are the 90th percentile of their quantity across the whole act: 1.503 for a rate, 5.0% for one component per match, and 5.5% for one action's move on the win probability curve. The END that ran past its axis is the end that is flagged, so a span which stopped at the axis cannot be read as one that stopped on its own. Two axes are not percentiles and here is why. A mark on the win probability curve has no number printed beside it, so a mark that clamped would simply be wrong: the gate an action must clear before it gets a mark of its own is the 99th percentile, 12.0%, and only the act's top hundredth of moves are drawn as marks at all. And the headline axis — the season tracker's y, and the interval under the number on a player's card — runs to 16.1%, the maximum of the twelve headline numbers: four players, three endpoints each, every one of them on screen at once. The 90th percentile of those twelve is 12.2% and would clip two of the four intervals, on the one figure whose entire subject is how wide they are.
Opponents
Everyone but the four is a match-scoped pseudonym: the same person in two matches carries two unrelated names, and nothing here links them.

The 29 caveats that come with these numbers

act scope
Every number here is act e11a4 alone — 78 matches, 1,617 rounds — with nothing pooled across acts.
two scoring provenances
65 of these matches were priced inside the model release, each by a fold fit without it. 13 were collected after the release was cut, and cross-fitting keeps its predictions but not its models, so those are priced by a refit on the release's own core at the parameters all five folds already chose. Neither kind was in the training data of the model that scored it; what differs is that one model saw four fifths of the corpus and the other saw all of it, worth a mean 0.6% of round win probability across the act's states.
surrendered rounds are not played rounds
7 rounds in this act ended in a surrender. A surrendered round is awarded, not played — no events, no kills — so it is outside every number here, while the rounds played before it stay in: they ran under a real race to 13, against a score nobody yet knew would be conceded. The scoreline on such a match is the played one, and the round count reconciles against it.
rank by decision
The four are ordered on impact per match because that decision was taken for this site, not because the data separates them. All four intervals cover zero. No page prints a rank number any more: what survives is the order the four are listed in, on the season tracker's legend and in the columns of the match cross-tab. Read that order as a sort, and the interval on each card as the result.
side split unresolvable
The attack/defense split cannot resolve at this exposure: it needs roughly 14,000 rounds at this credit variance and the act has 1,617. Both cells are published with their intervals; the width there is the sample, not the player.
no abilities
Abilities do not exist in this data at any grain — four integers per player-match, null in every round record — so smokes, flashes and mollies are absent from every number here. Revives are UNCREDITED rather than absent: a revive is a timed event, so the curve moves under one and the round timeline prints that move beside whoever cast it, but revive is not one of the five credit types and no player's ledger carries it. The five are the whole ledger, and no cell renders a zero for utility.
pseudonymous opponents
Everyone but the four is a match-scoped pseudonym: the same person in two matches carries two unrelated names, and nothing here links them.
noindex is not access control
A noindex tag and a robots.txt keep this out of search results, which is not access control: the pages sit in a public repository, readable by anyone with the URL, including round-level detail on 644 players who did not consent to any of it. The point was raised before the build and the decision to publish was reaffirmed.
thin cells shown
The 20-round floor marks a cell rather than suppressing it: below it a cell carries thin and still prints its number and interval. Per-round MWPA has a standard deviation of 0.0443 in this act, so a 20-round mean carries a pure-noise standard error of about 0.99 per 100 rounds against a spread between the four of 0.15 — read a thin cell as description, not estimate.
no zero for absence
A breakdown cell with no rounds is omitted rather than emitted as zero, and the view renders that absence. A synergy pair is the exception: it keeps its row with a null rate, because two players never sharing a round is the finding.
duel tags incomplete
Duel tags cover 8,095 of 8,150 earned rows; the missing 55, 0.7%, are 50 clutch_lost, 3 opening_death and 2 traded across 54 rounds. They have no round-player row to attach to, because the ledger scores nobody who was alive and produced no kill, death, plant, defuse or clock share — a clutch lost without firing is the modal case.
single match interval
The bootstrap resamples whole matches, so a cell whose rounds all come from one match has a zero-width interval that reads as excluding zero and does not: one cluster, nothing for the resample to vary. Check matches before covers_zero.
measured zero at display precision
A magnitude that rounds to zero at its own format is MEASURED, not missing: it prints as less than the smallest number that format can write, with no sign and no direction colour. The em dash stays reserved for the unmeasured, the middot for an exact zero.
the null is the loudest mark
The heaviest ink on every figure is the line at the value that means no claim, 50.0% on the match win probability curve, drawn as one unbroken element through a whole block rather than a tick per row. On the curve it sits UNDER the line, because a rule that heavy would otherwise erase the data wherever the match sat near even.
the duel is one row
Kill credit and death debit are drawn as one signed row, the duel credit, and the two are still printed underneath it in words. The reason is legibility and it is arithmetic: the two are an order of magnitude longer than every other component and pointed opposite ways, so drawn apart they set an axis on which plant, defuse and the alive clock are a pixel each. They are also the two ends of one event — a duel resolves, somebody is paid, somebody is debited — so their sum is a quantity rather than a convenience. Its interval is resampled on the summed column and is not the two intervals added: within a round the two are strongly negatively correlated, so their widths do not add.
classic is the free pistol
The most-used guns on a player card exclude the Classic. Every player starts every pistol round holding it, whatever they meant to buy, so a Classic count measures how many pistol rounds the act contained and not what this player chooses to hold. Nothing else is excluded, and the guns are counted in rounds rather than matches because a gun is a per-round choice.
the card is counts not estimates
The block at the top of a player page carries two registers and they are drawn apart. The impact per match is an estimate and it keeps its interval and its verdict against the null. The kills, deaths, assists, rounds, agents and guns beside it are counts: they happened, they carry no interval, and no null rule is drawn through them. A card that put a confidence band on a kill count would be claiming uncertainty about something nobody is uncertain about.
components include the lobby adjustment
The five credit types decompose the raw ledger before the lobby centering, so a further row carries that centering and the rows sum to the headline exactly. Across the four the raw five sum to +2.6565 against a headline sum of +0.5843, and the lobby adjustment is the -2.0722 between them.
wp curve boundary seam
The curve steps at a round boundary and the step is real: the match model runs every future round at a constant win probability of 0.5, so a boundary is the score talking, and the round-start node then moves it to the model's price on the round about to be played. Across the 1,617 rounds of this act the median step is 1.5 match win probability points and the largest is 10.4; neither end of it is a player.
leverage widens
Weighting each round by its marginal match value widens every interval rather than narrowing it: the rounds needed to exclude zero rise from about 546 to about 726. Of the four, martin and snorlax clear that threshold; the rest do not.
buy bands local
The buy classes are cut from this corpus's own team-loadout distribution, not from Counter-Strike convention. Eco rounds are won 42.2% of the time in this act, against roughly one in thirty in professional Counter-Strike, which is direct evidence that the imported cut points describe a different game.
synergy is ordered
Synergy is ordered: each row is the subject's own MWPA over the rounds a given partner also played, not a joint estimate, so the two directions of a pairing are different numbers. martin and snorlax are the only pairing with real shared exposure, at 790 shared rounds; themarias and trzzcko never shared a round in this act, and that cell is null, not zero.
descriptive not causal
MWPA is a descriptive allocation of modelled probability movement, not a causal player effect. The model behind it carries no player, team or lobby term, so every credit is a residual against a baseline pooled across ranks.
tier placement window
A player's first 5 act matches are played before the platform has issued a rank: not a missing value and not a zero, but the measured answer that no division existed yet. Verified rather than assumed — 134 of the 648 act players who have such a match carry it as a strict prefix, with no counterexamples.
tier is not mwpa
The ladder is a second instrument and it is not measuring this: over the 129 focal player-matches with at least 5 ranked others in the lobby, the lobby's mean division correlates with that match's MWPA at r = +0.044, with the lobby adjustment at r = +0.093, and with that adjustment as a rate at r = +0.043. Lobbies are not division-homogeneous either — the median spread inside one is 4 divisions and the widest is 8.
imagery is not a mark
No picture here is a measurement, because of where they are put: three families ship — 29 agent portraits, 11 division badges, 19 weapon silhouettes — each beside a word that is always rendered, and the header control removes all 59 files at once, so flipping it costs recognition and not one fact. What clears the 3:1 mark floor is the 1-pixel box around each picture, at 3.42:1 worst, not the picture inside it — of the 29 portraits only 6 clear that floor on every surface they land on.
where imagery is allowed
The round ledger stays words: across 16,073 named cells Vandal alone is 34.2%, so an icon column there repeats and is texture rather than navigation. The silhouettes ship in the by weapon breakdown, where every row is a distinct weapon; a division badge ships only beside the division word and never in a cell carrying a player hue, because Riot's gold sits within OKLab ΔE 3.5 of one player's own hue; map art is refused. The player card is the one place the art is not eighteen pixels tall — everywhere else it rides inside a table row and the row's line box is the constraint, and a card is not a row, so the agents and the guns on it are drawn at the size the files actually are. Nothing else about them changes: the word is beside every picture, the weapon plate comes with the weapon art, and the header control still takes all of it away without losing a fact.
riot assets
All three families are Riot Games' own artwork, fetched once from valorant-api.com and served from this directory: 29 agent portraits at 40 pixels square, 11 division badges at 48 square, and 19 weapon silhouettes 96 pixels wide, used under Riot's fan content policy. This site is not endorsed by or affiliated with Riot Games.
unrated is not a division
19.2% of this act's player-matches carry no division, and no badge ships for that state: Riot's enum puts Unrated at id 0 on the ladder that has Iron 2 at 4, which would draw the absence of a rank as a rung below the lowest one, and Unrated is also the name of a queue while all 78 of these matches are Competitive. It is the word placement everywhere it appears — not an em dash, which on this site means NOT MEASURED — and on the ladder it gets an empty slot in a dashed hairline.
