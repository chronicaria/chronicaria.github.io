    Mode expansions in light-cone gauge

    An open string vibrates as a superposition of standing-wave harmonics whose amplitudes are spacetime vectors. In light-cone gauge only the transverse amplitudes \alpha_n^I are freely chosen — X^+ is the clock, every mode of X^- is computed from the transverse ones — so the string's whole motion, and its mass, is built from transverse oscillators alone.

    A mode expansion is nothing more than writing a vibrating object as a sum of standing waves. What makes the string interesting is that it lives in spacetime, so the amplitude of each harmonic is itself a spacetime vector αnμ\alpha_n^\muαnμ​. A gauge choice plus two constraints then strip the unphysical directions off those vectors, leaving only the transverse harmonics. Everything below is that stripping, done explicitly.

    Step 1 — The equation of motion is just the wave equation

    In the gauge class of Fixing X-plus as worldsheet time the momentum densities collapse to Pτμ=12πα′X˙μ\mathcal P^{\tau\mu} = \frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}\dot X^\muPτμ=2πα′1​X˙μ and Pσμ=−12πα′Xμ′\mathcal P^{\sigma\mu} = -\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha'}X^{\mu\prime}Pσμ=−2πα′1​Xμ′ (natural units, ℏ=c=1\hbar=c=1ℏ=c=1). Feeding them into the field equation ∂τPτμ+∂σPσμ=0\partial_\tau\mathcal P^{\tau\mu} + \partial_\sigma\mathcal P^{\sigma\mu} = 0∂τ​Pτμ+∂σ​Pσμ=0 leaves

    X¨μ−Xμ′′=0.\ddot X^\mu - X^{\mu\prime\prime} = 0.X¨μ−Xμ′′=0.

    This is the reward for the gauge work. The raw Nambu–Goto equations are nonlinear (a square root of derivatives sits in the action); the constraints (X˙±X′)2=0(\dot X\pm X')^2=0(X˙±X′)2=0 are exactly what flattens them into free waves — a guitar string, one per spacetime direction μ\muμ.

    The general solution is any left-mover plus any right-mover, Xμ=12(fμ(τ+σ)+gμ(τ−σ))X^\mu = \tfrac12\big(f^\mu(\tau+\sigma) + g^\mu(\tau-\sigma)\big)Xμ=21​(fμ(τ+σ)+gμ(τ−σ)).

    Step 2 — Free ends pick out cosines

    A space-filling D-brane leaves every endpoint free, so Pσμ=0\mathcal P^{\sigma\mu}=0Pσμ=0 at σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π, i.e. Neumann conditions ∂σXμ=0\partial_\sigma X^\mu=0∂σ​Xμ=0 there — see Neumann boundary conditions.

    • At σ=0\sigma=0σ=0: fμ′=gμ′f^{\mu\prime}=g^{\mu\prime}fμ′=gμ′, so left- and right-movers are the same function, Xμ=12(fμ(τ+σ)+fμ(τ−σ))X^\mu = \tfrac12\big(f^\mu(\tau+\sigma)+f^\mu(\tau-\sigma)\big)Xμ=21​(fμ(τ+σ)+fμ(τ−σ)).
    • At σ=π\sigma=\piσ=π: fμ′f^{\mu\prime}fμ′ must be periodic with period 2π2\pi2π. This is the payoff of choosing σ∈[0,π]\sigma\in[0,\pi]σ∈[0,π] — the natural period drops out for free.

    A periodic fμ′f^{\mu\prime}fμ′ is a Fourier series; integrating and collecting constants gives the conventional open-string expansion,

    X^\mu(\tau,\sigma) = x_0^\mu + 2\alpha' p^\mu\,\tau - i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\big(a_n^{\mu*}e^{in\tau} - a_n^\mu e^{-in\tau}\big)\cos n\sigma. \tag{1}

    Term by term: x0μx_0^\mux0μ​ is where the string sits; 2α′pμτ2\alpha' p^\mu\tau2α′pμτ is its uniform drift (the coefficient is fixed by pμ=∫0πPτμ dσp^\mu=\int_0^\pi\mathcal P^{\tau\mu}\,d\sigmapμ=∫0π​Pτμdσ); the sum is the ringing, with cos⁡nσ\cos n\sigmacosnσ enforcing the free-end standing-wave shape — antinodes at both ends. Kill every anμa_n^\muanμ​ and the string shrinks to a point particle coasting along.

    Step 3 — The α\alphaα-oscillator notation

    Two changes of variable turn (1) into the standard oscillator form: absorb n\sqrt nn​ into the oscillator, and promote the momentum to an n=0n=0n=0 "oscillator":

    α0μ≡2α′ pμ,αnμ≡n anμ,α−nμ≡n anμ∗    (n≥1),\alpha_0^\mu \equiv \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^\mu, \qquad \alpha_n^\mu \equiv \sqrt n\,a_n^\mu,\quad \alpha_{-n}^\mu \equiv \sqrt n\,a_n^{\mu*}\;\;(n\geq1),α0μ​≡2α′​pμ,αnμ​≡n​anμ​,α−nμ​≡n​anμ∗​(n≥1),

    so reality of XμX^\muXμ becomes the single condition α−nμ=(αnμ)∗\alpha_{-n}^\mu=(\alpha_n^\mu)^*α−nμ​=(αnμ​)∗. The expansion and its two most useful derivatives are then

    X^\mu(\tau,\sigma) = x_0^\mu + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0^\mu\,\tau + i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac{1}{n}\,\alpha_n^\mu\,e^{-in\tau}\cos n\sigma, \tag{2}

    \boxed{\;\dot X^\mu \pm X^{\mu\prime} = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_n^\mu\,e^{-in(\tau\pm\sigma)}\;}. \tag{3}

    Equation (3) is the workhorse. The constraint we must impose is built from precisely X˙μ±Xμ′\dot X^\mu\pm X^{\mu\prime}X˙μ±Xμ′, and written as a single exponential sum it is diagonal in the mode label — so imposing the constraint will reduce to matching Fourier coefficients, one nnn at a time.

    Everything so far holds for every μ\muμ, in any gauge of this class. The light-cone choice now decides which of these modes are physically free.

    Step 4 — Impose light-cone gauge

    From Fixing X-plus as worldsheet time, the +++ direction is frozen into a clock — no position zero mode, no oscillators:

    X+=2α′ α0+τ=2α′p+τ,x0+=0,αn+=0 (n≠0).X^+ = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0^+\tau = 2\alpha' p^+\tau, \qquad x_0^+ = 0,\quad \alpha_n^+ = 0\ (n\neq0).X+=2α′​α0+​τ=2α′p+τ,x0+​=0,αn+​=0 (n=0).

    The transverse directions XIX^IXI (I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1, so D−2D-2D−2 of them) are the free physical fields — just (2) with μ→I\mu\to Iμ→I:

    \boxed{\;X^I(\tau,\sigma) = x_0^I + \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0^I\,\tau + i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac{1}{n}\,\alpha_n^I\,e^{-in\tau}\cos n\sigma,\qquad \alpha_0^I = \sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^I\;}. \tag{4}

    This is the open-string light-cone solution for the physical degrees of freedom. The only free data at this point are the transverse constants x0I,pI,αnIx_0^I,p^I,\alpha_n^Ix0I​,pI,αnI​ plus the two longitudinal numbers p+p^+p+ and x0−x_0^-x0−​. The last step computes the remaining longitudinal motion — it adds no new freedom.

    Step 5 — The constraint fixes the X−X^-X− modes

    X−X^-X− obeys the same wave equation and Neumann conditions, so it has the same expansion (2) with μ→−\mu\to-μ→−. To pin its modes, impose the solved constraint from Light-cone constraints,

    X˙−±X−′=14α′p+(X˙I±XI′)2,\dot X^- \pm X^{-\prime} = \frac{1}{4\alpha' p^+}\big(\dot X^I \pm X^{I\prime}\big)^2,X˙−±X−′=4α′p+1​(X˙I±XI′)2,

    and expand both sides using (3):

    2α′∑n∈Zαn−e−in(τ±σ)=14α′p+⋅2α′∑p,qαpIαqI e−i(p+q)(τ±σ)=12p+∑p,qαpIαqI e−i(p+q)(τ±σ).\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_n^- e^{-in(\tau\pm\sigma)} = \frac{1}{4\alpha' p^+}\cdot 2\alpha'\sum_{p,q}\alpha_p^I\alpha_q^I\,e^{-i(p+q)(\tau\pm\sigma)} = \frac{1}{2p^+}\sum_{p,q}\alpha_p^I\alpha_q^I\,e^{-i(p+q)(\tau\pm\sigma)}.2α′​∑n∈Z​αn−​e−in(τ±σ)=4α′p+1​⋅2α′∑p,q​αpI​αqI​e−i(p+q)(τ±σ)=2p+1​∑p,q​αpI​αqI​e−i(p+q)(τ±σ).

    Squaring (3) produces a factor 2α′2\alpha'2α′, which cancels half of the 4α′p+4\alpha' p^+4α′p+ in the denominator, leaving exactly 1/(2p+)1/(2p^+)1/(2p+). Track the α′\alpha'α′ carefully here: the leftover 1/(2p+)1/(2p^+)1/(2p+) is what turns into the transverse Virasoro mode below.

    Relabel q=n−pq=n-pq=n−p and match the coefficient of each e−in(τ±σ)e^{-in(\tau\pm\sigma)}e−in(τ±σ):

    \boxed{\;\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_n^- = \frac{1}{2p^+}\sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_{n-p}^I\alpha_p^I = \frac{1}{p^+}\,L_n^\perp\;}, \qquad L_n^\perp \equiv \frac12\sum_{p\in\mathbb Z}\alpha_{n-p}^I\alpha_p^I. \tag{5}

    Every X−X^-X− oscillator is a quadratic in the transverse oscillators — the transverse Virasoro mode Ln⊥L_n^\perpLn⊥​. The spacetime index on the right runs over transverse labels only: that is what "X−X^-X− is a function of the physical data" means concretely (see Transverse coordinates as physical degrees of freedom). Only x0−x_0^-x0−​ stays free.

    Substituting (5) into the X−X^-X− expansion gives X−X^-X− explicitly in terms of the physical modes:

      X−(τ,σ)=x0−+1p+L0⊥ τ+ip+∑n≠01nLn⊥e−inτcos⁡nσ  .(6)\boxed{\; X^-(\tau,\sigma) =x_0^-+\frac{1}{p^+}L_0^\perp\,\tau +\frac{i}{p^+}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac{1}{n}L_n^\perp e^{-in\tau}\cos n\sigma \;}. \tag{6}X−(τ,σ)=x0−​+p+1​L0⊥​τ+p+i​n=0∑​n1​Ln⊥​e−inτcosnσ​.(6)

    Choose the transverse oscillators, form the quadratics Ln⊥L_n^\perpLn⊥​, and X−X^-X− is written out — every non-constant piece is fixed, with x0−x_0^-x0−​ the single leftover integration constant.

    So the complete open-string solution is an algorithm, not a search:

    1. Pick p+>0p^+>0p+>0, x0−x_0^-x0−​, and transverse data x0I,pI,αnIx_0^I,p^I,\alpha_n^Ix0I​,pI,αnI​.
    2. Set X+=2α′p+τX^+=2\alpha'p^+\tauX+=2α′p+τ; build XIX^IXI from (4).
    3. Form the Ln⊥L_n^\perpLn⊥​ and drop them into (6) for X−X^-X−.

    No longitudinal oscillator is ever chosen independently — that is the whole content of "the transverse modes are the physical degrees of freedom."

    Step 6 — The mass is pure transverse energy

    The n=0n=0n=0 case of (5), using α0μ=2α′pμ\alpha_0^\mu=\sqrt{2\alpha'}p^\muα0μ​=2α′​pμ, is the mass-shell relation:

    2p+p−=1α′L0⊥.2p^+p^- = \frac{1}{\alpha'}L_0^\perp.2p+p−=α′1​L0⊥​.

    Expand L0⊥L_0^\perpL0⊥​, splitting the zero mode from the rest:

    L0⊥=12∑pα−pIαpI=12α0Iα0I⏟= α′pIpI+∑n≥1α−nIαnI⏟= n anI∗anI=α′pIpI+∑n≥1n anI∗anI.L_0^\perp = \tfrac12\sum_p \alpha_{-p}^I\alpha_p^I = \underbrace{\tfrac12\alpha_0^I\alpha_0^I}_{=\,\alpha' p^Ip^I} + \sum_{n\geq1}\underbrace{\alpha_{-n}^I\alpha_n^I}_{=\,n\,a_n^{I*}a_n^I} = \alpha' p^Ip^I + \sum_{n\geq1} n\,a_n^{I*}a_n^I.L0⊥​=21​∑p​α−pI​αpI​==α′pIpI21​α0I​α0I​​​+∑n≥1​=nanI∗​anI​α−nI​αnI​​​=α′pIpI+∑n≥1​nanI∗​anI​.

    Feed this into M2=−p2=2p+p−−pIpIM^2 = -p^2 = 2p^+p^- - p^Ip^IM2=−p2=2p+p−−pIpI (mostly-plus signature); the pIpIp^Ip^IpIpI terms cancel:

    \boxed{\;M^2 = \frac{1}{\alpha'}\sum_{n\geq1} n\,a_n^{I*}\,a_n^I\;}. \tag{7}

    A string's mass is the energy stored in its transverse vibrations — nothing else. Each term is n ∣anI∣2≥0n\,|a_n^I|^2\geq0n∣anI​∣2≥0, so M2≥0M^2\geq0M2≥0: the classical string is never a tachyon, and a non-vibrating string (anI=0a_n^I=0anI​=0) is a massless point. See Mass-shell relation.

    Warning

    This is the classical mass, and it does not survive quantization.     Static gauge vs light-cone gauge

    Both gauges freeze one coordinate (the static-gauge column keeps ccc, as Module 11 did). The difference is whether the leftover constraints can be solved or only imposed.

    Constraints X⃗˙⋅X⃗′=0\dot{\vec X}\cdot\vec X'=0X˙⋅X′=0,  X⃗′2+X⃗˙2/c2=1\ \vec X'^2+\dot{\vec X}^2/c^2=1 X′2+X˙2/c2=1 (X˙±X′)2=0(\dot X\pm X')^2=0(X˙±X′)2=0

    Physical fields D−1D-1D−1 spatial XiX^iXi, still entangled D−2D-2D−2 transverse XIX^IXI, free
    X−X^-X− analogue no clean separation determined: αn−∝Ln⊥\alpha_n^-\propto L_n^\perpαn−​∝Ln⊥​

    In static gauge the constraints are nonlinear relations tangling all D−1D-1D−1 spatial fields — no single variable is isolated. In light-cone gauge the cross term −2X˙+X˙−=−2(2α′p+)X˙−-2\dot X^+\dot X^- = -2(2\alpha'p^+)\dot X^-−2X˙+X˙−=−2(2α′p+)X˙− is linear in X˙−\dot X^-X˙−, so you divide it out. The null direction is exactly what turns a quadratic-looking constraint into a linear reconstruction. The price: the gauge singles out x1x^1x1, so Lorentz invariance is real but no longer manifest.

    Exercise

    The mass formula (7) fixes M2M^2M2 but says nothing about spin. Take the first excited open string: excite only the lowest mode, in a single transverse direction, so a12≠0a_1^2\neq0a12​=0 and every other anI=0a_n^I=0anI​=0.

    (a) Compute α′M2\alpha'M^2α′M2 for this configuration. (b) How does it transform under rotations of the transverse space — as a scalar or as a vector? (c) Use this to argue that the massless first-excited level — the same a1Ia_1^Ia1I​ excitation, which the quantum shift a=−1a=-1a=−1 pushes to α′M2=0\alpha'M^2=0α′M2=0 — has a state count running over the D−2D-2D−2 transverse directions, and say what familiar particle that suggests.

    Solution

    (a) From (7), M2=1α′∑n≥1n anI∗anIM^2=\frac{1}{\alpha'}\sum_{n\geq1} n\,a_n^{I*}a_n^IM2=α′1​∑n≥1​nanI∗​anI​. Only n=1n=1n=1, I=2I=2I=2 survives, so α′M2=1⋅a12∗a12=∣a12∣2>0.\alpha' M^2 = 1\cdot a_1^{2*}a_1^2 = |a_1^2|^2 > 0.α′M2=1⋅a12∗​a12​=∣a12​∣2>0. Classically this is a continuous positive number set by the amplitude ∣a12∣|a_1^2|∣a12​∣ — the string is genuinely vibrating in the x2x^2x2 direction. (Quantum mechanically the shift a=−1a=-1a=−1 moves the first excited level to α′M2=1+a=0\alpha'M^2 = 1 + a = 0α′M2=1+a=0: it becomes massless. But that is Part III; here M2M^2M2 is whatever the classical amplitude makes it.)

    (b) The excitation carries the transverse index I=2I=2I=2: it points along a specific direction in the (D−2)(D-2)(D−2)-dimensional transverse space. So it is not a scalar — it transforms as a vector under SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2) rotations of the transverse space. Exciting a13a_1^3a13​ instead would give a rotated partner; the set {a1I}\{a_1^I\}{a1I​}, I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1, rotates into itself.

    (c) The distinct single-oscillator excitations a1Ia_1^Ia1I​ come in exactly D−2D-2D−2 flavors, one per transverse direction, forming a vector of SO(D−2)SO(D-2)SO(D−2). A massless vector particle is precisely a photon (a massless spin-1 gauge boson has D−2D-2D−2 physical polarizations). So the open string's lowest nontrivial excitation is a candidate photon — the transverse count D−2D-2D−2 is what makes the polarization bookkeeping come out right. This is why the "physical = transverse" logic of light-cone gauge is not just tidy but predictive: it hands you the right number of photon polarizations automatically.
