Chicago Bulls

Chicago enters 2026-27 as a fast, unusually long development team whose fantasy value concentrates in Josh Giddey, Matas Buzelis, Caleb Wilson, Norman Powell, and Nic Claxton.

Team snapshot

  • Base outcome: 31 wins; plausible range 24-39. Chicago went 31-51 in 2025-26 while scoring 116.3 and allowing 121.5 points per game; its official season review also records 28.5 assists per game. NBA roster/team page · Bulls 2025-26 season review
  • Identity: continue to run through Josh Giddey, attack in transition, and use the Wilson/Buzelis/Claxton length to improve a defense that finished 28th in opponent points per game. New executive Bryson Graham explicitly described size, length, athleticism, and physicality as the roster vision; the draft then added Wilson and Swain to that template. Bulls introduction, May 4, 2026 · AP/Yahoo draft report, June 24, 2026
  • Coach: Tiago Splitter replaced Billy Donovan on June 16. Splitter inherited Portland in 2025-26 and went 42-40; his previous teams emphasized pace, development, ball movement, and defensive accountability, but a new scheme makes the early rotation less certain. Bulls announcement, June 16, 2026 · El País profile, June 16, 2026
  • Fantasy environment: pace and passing should support counting stats. The constraint is half-court spacing: Claxton does not stretch the floor, Wilson's jumper is developing, and Giddey's volume efficiency remains merely average.

Category map

  • Assists: Giddey is the anchor; Jones is a clean secondary source. Chicago ranked seventh in assists in 2025-26.
  • Blocks: Buzelis (1.5 in 2025-26), Claxton, and Wilson can make this an above-average team category. Buzelis is the rare forward who materially changes a fantasy block build.
  • Rebounds: Giddey plus Wilson/Claxton creates strong positional rebounding. Smith and Awaka add depth.
  • Threes/FT%/points: Powell supplies veteran scoring and percentage stability. Buzelis is the next-best volume shooter.
  • Efficiency hazards: Claxton's .616 FT% in 2025-26 makes him build-dependent; Wilson shot .713 in college; Giddey and the young forwards can add turnovers.

Role battles, health, and transaction watch

  1. Backup center — Collins vs. Smith. Smith's spacing and rebounding earned 20.7 MPG last year, but a right calf strain first suffered on January 29 recurred repeatedly and Chicago shut him down for the remainder of the season in March. Collins was limited to 10 games by right-toe surgery in February, then agreed to a two-year, $17 million extension on June 30 rather than reaching free agency. This model now gives Collins the edge on the strength of that commitment; the open question is toe clearance, not depth-chart standing. NBA.com, July 1, 2026 · CBS, Smith out for season
  2. Reserve wing — Swain vs. Okoro vs. Dillingham. Draft capital favors Swain, but his first Summer League was poor while Chicago tested him at point guard; he then missed the July 17 finale with right-wrist soreness. The report said there was no expectation of a training-camp limitation. Update 2026-07-19: the closing Vegas ledger was 4.3 points and 5.2 turnovers per game on 4-of-31 shooting (0-of-7 from three) across four games — the worst statistical Summer League of any 2026 first-rounder — while Caleb Wilson finished at 23.5 points, 7.2 rebounds, and 2.5 blocks on 41.9% from three (7.8 attempts). This widens the gap between the two rookies' opening-night claims without changing Swain's draft-capital case. HoopsRumors Bulls Notes, 2026-07-19 · Bulls recap, July 15, 2026 · RotoWire/CBS, July 17, 2026
  3. Development forwards — Essengue vs. Miller vs. Williams. Essengue lost nearly all of 2025-26 to left-shoulder surgery and missed the July 17 Summer League finale with a thumb issue. Miller produced 11.7 points and 5.8 rebounds in 23.1 minutes after arriving. Williams shot 37.2% in a sharply reduced role. This is the least stable branch of the depth chart. Bulls Essengue medical update, December 6, 2025 · CBS, July 17, 2026
  4. Giddey's ankle. He underwent right-ankle arthroscopy on May 13 after hamstring and ankle trouble limited him to 54 games; Chicago expected him ready for training camp. Availability—not role—is his central totals risk. NBA.com, May 13, 2026
  5. Claxton's finger. Public fantasy injury lists still carried a finger flag in mid-July. Treat him as tentatively available but re-check camp participation. CBS Bulls injuries, July 17, 2026
  6. Wilson's hands. A fractured left hand and then a broken right thumb ended his college season, but he was cleared in time to play four Summer League games. The history raises durability uncertainty without constituting a current restriction. NBA.com, March 7, 2026 · NBA Summer League debut, July 10, 2026

Transactions that reset the fantasy hierarchy

  • Chicago drafted Caleb Wilson fourth and Dailyn Swain fifteenth on June 23. NBA draft profile · Bulls Swain release
  • Chicago acquired Nic Claxton in a four-team trade finalized July 10. Bulls release
  • Chicago signed All-Star Norman Powell to a reported two-year, $45 million deal, making him the likely starting shooting guard. NBA.com, July 11, 2026
  • Chicago re-signed Zach Collins on a two-year, $17 million extension agreed June 30, keeping him under contract rather than letting him reach free agency. NBA.com, July 1, 2026

The February 2026 deadline teardown. The single most important input to the 2026-27 projections is not an addition but a subtraction: Chicago moved four impending free agents in separate deals and netted nine second-round picks, no firsts.

  • Nikola Vucevic to Boston with a future second, for Anfernee Simons and a 2026 second. Vucevic was the starting center and averaged roughly 16 points per game for Chicago before the deal.
  • Coby White to Charlotte on February 4, alongside Mike Conley, for Collin Sexton and Ousmane Dieng. White had been the team's leading scorer and re-signed with the Hornets on a three-year, $74 million deal on June 25 — he is not coming back.
  • Kevin Huerter in a three-team deal that returned Jaden Ivey from Detroit and Conley from Minnesota. Ivey was waived on March 31, so that branch produced nothing durable.
  • Ayo Dosunmu and Julian Phillips to Minnesota on February 5 for Rob Dillingham, Leonard Miller and four second-round picks. This is the only deadline trade that returned rotation players Chicago still holds.
  • Dalen Terry went to New York for Guerschon Yabusele; Dieng was later flipped in a three-team deal for Nick Richards.

NBC Chicago deadline roundup · Hoops Rumors offseason preview, June 2026 · AP, February 5, 2026 · NBA.com, June 25, 2026

Yabusele's exit compounds it. Guerschon Yabusele started 19 of 26 games for Chicago after arriving and averaged 10.0 points and 5.7 rebounds. On July 7 he signed a three-year deal with Panathinaikos rather than returning to the NBA, removing the interim starting power forward from the board. Hoops Rumors, July 7, 2026

Why it matters for fantasy. Vucevic, White, Dosunmu, Huerter and Yabusele were, between them, the bulk of Chicago's non-Giddey shot volume in 2025-26. None are on the roster. Powell is the only established veteran scorer brought in to replace them, which is the structural reason the usage projections for Powell, Matas Buzelis and Caleb Wilson sit above their prior-season rates rather than at them.

Floor and ceiling team outcomes

  • Floor — 24-27 wins: Giddey's ankle lingers, Powell shows age decline, the rookie shooting is not ready, and the deep forward logjam produces unstable roles. This version remains fast but inefficient and again lives in the bottom five defensively.
  • Base — 29-33 wins: Giddey/Buzelis improve modestly, Wilson is productive but uneven, Claxton repairs some rim defense, and Powell closes games. Fantasy output is better than the win total because pace remains high.
  • Ceiling — 36-39 wins: Wilson is immediately a two-way impact starter, Buzelis sustains post-deadline usage with better efficiency, Giddey plays 70-plus games, and Splitter turns the length into an average defense. That is a play-in ceiling, not a title-contending forecast.
