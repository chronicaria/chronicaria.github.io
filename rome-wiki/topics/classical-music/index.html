    5. Bloch Baal Shem and Jewish musical identityErnest Bloch's Baal Shem turns three sharply contrasted violin-and-piano scenes into a study of confession, inward song, and communal rejoicing without reducing Jewish identity to a catalogue of borrowed tunes.

    33. John Field and the nocturne before ChopinJohn Field made the piano nocturne into a durable way of hearing melody, accompaniment, resonance, and suspended time; understanding that achievement requires treating his pieces as a varied Russian-era repertory, not merely as rough drafts for Chopin.
    34. Lalo Symphonie espagnole and genre ambiguityÉdouard Lalo’s Symphonie espagnole is best understood not by deciding whether it is “really” a symphony or a concerto, but by hearing its title as a claim about scale and organization: a five-movement, symphonically conceived orchestral cycle whose argument is carried by a virtuoso solo violin.
    35. Liszt Consolation No. 3Liszt's third Consolation shows that Romantic virtuosity can mean sustained singing control: the difficulty is not speed, but keeping melody, inner resonance, and harmonic delay alive inside apparent simplicity.

    37. Louis Spohr and the early Romantic violin concertoLouis Spohr’s concertos make the violin sing, declaim, and ornament within a carefully controlled orchestral drama, linking the Classical concerto to Romantic ideas of character without reducing virtuosity to display.

    41. Pedaling evidence in Romantic scoresPedaling in Romantic piano music is evidence work: a printed pedal sign proves a source reading, not an automatically correct sound on every piano.

Romantic phrase rhythmRomantic phrase rhythm is the way motives, cadences, phrase spans, and larger metrical accents cooperate—or deliberately fail to cooperate—across time; hearing that hierarchy keeps a pianist from turning every slur, bar line, or four-measure unit into the same kind of “phrase.”

Sibelius Violin Concerto Op. 47In the revised Violin Concerto in D minor, Op. 47, Sibelius makes virtuosity carry the form: the first movement turns its cadenza into the development, the Adagio tests lyric expansion against restraint, and the finale converts rhythmic instability into a hard-won D-major close.
    65. Smetana From the HomelandBedřich Smetana's Z domoviny ( From the Homeland ), JB 1:118, is a pair of compact but fully partnered duets in which violin and piano turn Czech-associated dance rhythm, lyric declamation, and abrupt shifts of character into a miniature dramatic cycle.

    67. Thalberg's three-hand textureThalberg's three-hand texture is a Romantic piano illusion: two hands make a middle-register melody seem to sing independently while arpeggios, basses, chords, pedal, and voicing create a larger body of sound around it.
    68. The character piece as concentrated formThe Romantic piano character piece creates form by concentrating one musical situation until title, texture, harmony, rhythm, register, and return all point toward the same small but complete act of listening.
    69. The nineteenth-century piano as a changing instrumentThe piano of “Romantic piano music” was not one fixed machine: changing actions, frames, strings, ranges, pedals, rooms, and markets continually altered what composers could write and what listeners could hear.
    70. The Romantic piano cycleThe Romantic piano cycle turns a sequence of short pieces into a larger listening experience through order, return, contrast, persona, tonal memory, and sometimes an external frame such as a calendar, literary scene, or publication series.

The Tourte bow and nineteenth-century phrase possibilitiesThe bow model associated with François-Xavier Tourte gave violinists a newly powerful combination of sustained contact, graded attack, and controlled rebound, but historical evidence does not permit us to turn that material change into one inevitable “Romantic” way of phrasing.
    72. The two versions of Sibelius's Violin ConcertoSibelius’s 1904 and 1905 Violin Concertos are two authorial states of one work: revision removed a second first-movement cadenza, reduced and redistributed virtuoso writing, tightened the form, and clarified the soloist–orchestra balance without changing the concerto’s fundamental identity.
    73. Urtext does not mean interpretation-freeAn Urtext edition tries to make the musical text historically accountable; it does not turn notation into a complete, self-executing interpretation.

    75. Violin source layers from autograph to performer editionA violin score is evidence only after the composer’s manuscript, working copies, first prints, later editions, practical parts, and performer additions have been separated and assigned the right kind of authority.

Wieniawski Violin Concerto No. 2Henryk Wieniawski's Violin Concerto No. 2 in D minor, Op. 22 turns virtuosity into drama: its soloist first enters an argument already shaped by the orchestra, sings the central Romance almost as an operatic protagonist, and finally converts D-minor tension into a brilliant D-major à la Zingara close.
    78. Ysaÿe Poème élégiaque and the late-Romantic bridgeEugène Ysaÿe's Poème élégiaque , Op. 12, turns violin virtuosity into dramatic form: its 1892 violin-and-piano text abandons concerto architecture for an elegiac, Wagner-shadowed narrative, and in doing so helps explain how the nineteenth-century showpiece became the fin-de-siècle violin poème .
