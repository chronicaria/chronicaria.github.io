Fantasy translation

  • Nine-category: target range 45-70 per game. Elite three-point volume, points and free-throw impact carry the profile; modest assists/blocks prevent true early-round insulation.
  • Points: top-45-to-65 target, depending on bonuses for threes and double-doubles.
  • Dynasty: contending hold, roughly top 65-85. He turns 28 before the season and has more immediate value than developmental value.

Role, floor and ceiling

Julius Randle and Mikel Brown Jr. should reduce Porter's need to create every possession, which can improve shot quality while lowering raw usage. His floor is approximately 19 points, six rebounds and 2.5 threes if he becomes one of three equal offensive engines. His ceiling is another 24-plus-point, top-40 season if Randle facilitates, Brown struggles early and Porter keeps the first-option shot diet.

Risk monitor

Brooklyn shut him down with a left-hamstring strain after earlier ankle/back/knee issues; 52 games is the relevant durability baseline, not an 82-game expectation. NBA/AP, 2026-03-19 He is also entering an extension/transaction decision window, so any contract or trade news can change both usage and late-season incentives.

Career record

Pre-NBA: A five-star recruit and consensus top-of-class prospect out of Father Tolton Regional Catholic in Columbia, Missouri, then Nathan Hale in Seattle for his senior year, where he averaged 36.2 points and 13.6 rebounds on a 29-0 team and won 2017 McDonald's All-American Game MVP. His Missouri career was effectively erased by injury: three games in 2017-18, 17.7 minutes, 10.0 points, 6.7 rebounds on .333 from the field and .300 from three, after a microdiscectomy on his L3-L4 discs on 2017-11-22 cost him the season until a brief SEC tournament return. There is no meaningful college statistical record to project from. Draft: 2018, first round, 14th overall by Denver — he slid from potential first-overall to 14th purely on medical reports. Traded to Brooklyn on 2025-07-08 with a 2032 first-round pick for Cameron Johnson.

Fantasy history: Season-end nine-category finishes were not verifiable for any season from available public sources, so none is asserted. The one verified market data point is Yahoo's High Score 100 season review for 2025-26, which ranked him 37th in that points format after a 30.4% usage season. His category shape has been stable and unusual: consistently strong field-goal percentage, three-point volume and rebounds for a wing, with assists that only became non-punitive in 2025-26 and blocks that have hovered near replacement since 2020-21. Free-throw percentage swings season to season — .768 in 2024-25 versus .859 in 2025-26 — and on low attempt volume that variance is mostly noise. Points leagues have generally rated him higher than categories because his rebounding and scoring travel well and his weak assist and block lines cost less there.

Durability: 0, 55, 61, 9, 62, 81, 77, 52. Three back surgeries: the L3-L4 microdiscectomy on 2017-11-22 at Missouri, a second back surgery on 2018-07-19 that cost him the entire 2018-19 NBA season, and a third announced on 2021-11-29 that ended 2021-22 after nine games. The spine held up for three seasons after that — 62, 81 and 77 games from 2022-23 through 2024-25, the healthiest stretch of his career. The 2025-26 shortfall was soft-tissue rather than spinal: a sprained right ankle cost three games, he last played on 2026-03-10, a left hamstring strain was diagnosed on 2026-03-19 with a 2-3 week timeline, and Brooklyn ruled him out for the rest of the season on 2026-04-03 with the team at 18-58. The remaining absences across the season are not individually documented in the sources found, so the specific causes of roughly two dozen missed games are unverified.

Trajectory: The 2020-21 season was the first leap and it was pure minutes and role — 16 to 31 minutes under Denver's second-unit-to-starter promotion — while the 2025-26 season is the first genuine usage leap, from a fourth option next to Jokic to a 30.4% usage first option in Brooklyn. That distinction matters for 2026-27. His per-minute scoring jumped from 0.54 in 2024-25 to 0.74 in 2025-26 on essentially identical minutes, which is real, but it came with field-goal percentage falling from .504 to .463 and three-point percentage from .395 to .363 — the classic signature of a player taking harder shots, not a player who improved. His assists tripling from 1.0 to 3.0 over three seasons is the most encouraging trend, since it suggests the passing developed rather than merely being unnecessary in Denver. He turns 28 before the season, past the typical peak for a wing, and Randle's arrival plus a 52-game floor argue strongly against treating the 24.2 points as a baseline; the honest expectation is a partial regression toward the low 20s with better efficiency, on a games-played distribution whose left tail is fatter than most players his age.
