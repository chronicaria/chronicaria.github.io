Projection logic

Dončić produced 33.5 points, 7.7 rebounds, 8.3 assists, 1.6 steals, and 4.0 threes in 35.8 minutes over 64 games in 2025-26. The median trims his minutes and scoring slightly while lifting assists: LeBron James is gone, Austin Reaves remains an important secondary creator, and the rebuilt frontcourt supplies better screen-and-roll finishers without threatening Dončić's offensive control. ESPN's 2025-26 game log and splits anchor the rate baseline.

The 66-game expectation explicitly prices in left-hamstring recurrence risk. A Grade 2 strain ended his 2025-26 regular season after an earlier hamstring absence, although he returned for the playoffs. There was no later report of a new restriction by this cutoff. NBA injury report, April 3

Redraft, dynasty, and scenarios

  • Median: A 32/8/9 engine who returns first-round profit even in nine-category leagues.
  • Ceiling: 70-plus games, double-digit assists, and a credible path to the fantasy No. 1 slot if the free throws approach 82%.
  • Floor: Recurrent soft-tissue absences pull him toward 55-60 games; per-game output still belongs in the first round.
  • Build note: Turnover-punt builds unlock his cleanest category profile; managers should protect FT% if pairing him with Walker Kessler.
  • Market read: Dan Titus ranked him fourth in an early industry first round, while Hashtag Basketball's July dynasty board placed him third. That validates the range rather than creating a discount. NBA/Yahoo early rankings · Hashtag dynasty rankings

Confidence

  • Roster/role: High — the offense is built around him.
  • Minutes/rates: High — several seasons of superstar usage make the statistical shape unusually stable.
  • Availability: Medium — the hamstring history is material, but the median does not assume a current injury.

Career record

Pre-NBA: No college. Three senior seasons with Real Madrid, debuting at 16 in ACB and EuroLeague play. 2015-16: 4.5 points, 2.6 rebounds, 1.7 assists in 39 ACB games and 3.5/2.3/2.0 in 12 EuroLeague games. 2016-17: 7.5/4.4/3.0 in 42 ACB games and 7.8/4.5/4.2 in 35 EuroLeague games, winning EuroLeague Rising Star by unanimous vote and ACB Best Young Player. 2017-18: 16.0 points, 4.9 rebounds, 4.3 assists in 33 EuroLeague games, the season he became the youngest EuroLeague regular-season MVP, added Final Four MVP and the EuroLeague title, and was also named ACB MVP. Draft: 2018, first round, third overall by Atlanta, traded to Dallas that night for the draft rights to Trae Young at No. 5 plus a protected 2019 first-round pick. Dallas traded him to the Lakers in February 2025 for Anthony Davis, Max Christie and a 2029 first-round pick.

The 2024-25 season splits at the February 1, 2025 trade: 22 games in Dallas and 28 with the Lakers, 50 total. The rates barely moved across the move — 28.1 to 28.2 points — but his field goal percentage fell from .464 to .438, consistent with playing through the tail of a calf strain and a mid-season conditioning disruption. He led the league in scoring in 2025-26 at 33.5, his second scoring title.

Fantasy history: No season-end 9-cat or points rank for Dončić is verifiable from a primary ranker in the sources I could reach, so none is stated. What is structurally reliable across his career is the format gap: the points-league shape is materially better than the category shape, because points formats reward the scoring, rebounding and playmaking volume without penalizing him proportionally for a 4.0-turnover rate and a career .755 free throw mark on high attempts. In 9-cat he is an elite points, threes, guard-rebounds, assists and steals contributor whose turnover line is one of the worst in the league, which is why turnover-punt and turnover-tolerant builds have historically extracted the most from him.

Durability: 72, 61, 66, 65, 66, 70, 50, 64 games. The 2019-20 and 2020-21 totals come against shortened league schedules, so the honest read is that he has missed roughly 10-16 games a year for most of his career, mostly ankle and lower-body soft tissue. The two outliers are recent and both matter. 2024-25 fell to 50 games after a left calf strain on December 25, 2024 cost roughly five weeks, immediately followed by the trade. 2025-26 ended with a Grade 2 left hamstring strain suffered in a loss to Oklahoma City on April 2, 2026; he was given an eight-week timeline, sought treatment in Europe, and missed the final five regular-season games. Two of the earlier absences were December games for the birth of his second child. The causes of the remaining eleven missed games are not verified.

Trajectory: 2025-26 was the best rate season of his career, which the raw scoring title understates. His per-36 scoring reached roughly 33.7, above the 32.5 of his 2023-24 peak, on better efficiency than 2024-25 and with his three-point volume holding at 4.0 makes. That confirms 2024-25 was a conditioning-and-disruption outlier rather than the start of a decline. The cost of the Lakers structure shows in assists: per-36 playmaking fell from about 9.4 in 2023-24 to about 8.3, because Austin Reaves and LeBron James absorbed creation reps, and rebounds fell from 9.2 to 7.7 for similar reasons. With James gone, the assist figure is the most likely thing to move up in 2026-27. Entering an age-27 season he is squarely in prime and the rates are not the question; the question is the left hamstring, because a Grade 2 strain that took an eight-week timeline and cost an entire postseason is the kind of soft-tissue event that recurs, and his games-played history was already the weakest part of an otherwise No. 2-caliber profile.
