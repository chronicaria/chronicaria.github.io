Points leagues versus category leagues

Points and category leagues price the same box score with different mathematics — points is a linear functional on eleven stats, 9-cat is nine independent binary contests — and the practical consequence is that punting is unavailable in points, availability dominates it, and no two platform "points" defaults are the same formula.

Two caveats worth carrying. Note also that a sixth family exists — DFS-style scoring such as FantasyData's 3/2/1 shot-value system with STL and BLK at +2 — which is not a season-long default anywhere but shows up in third-party projection exports.

CBS Free is the outlier that nobody flags: steals and blocks at +1 collapse the entire defensive-specialist archetype, and CBS averages a player's score across games in the scoring period rather than summing.

ESPN's formula is not "points plus extras" — rewrite it

ESPN's eleven terms look intimidating and get misquoted constantly. Collapse them. Since \(2\cdot\mathrm{FGM}-\mathrm{FGA}=\mathrm{FGM}-\mathrm{FG_{miss}}\) and \(\mathrm{FTM}-\mathrm{FTA}=-\mathrm{FT_{miss}}\):

\[ \mathrm{ESPN}=\underbrace{\mathrm{PTS}+\mathrm{3PM}+\mathrm{FGM}-\mathrm{FG_{miss}}-\mathrm{FT_{miss}}}_{\text{shooting}}+\mathrm{REB}+2\,\mathrm{AST}+4\,\mathrm{STL}+4\,\mathrm{BLK}-2\,\mathrm{TO} \]

Per shot event that is: made three = +5, made two = +3, made free throw = +1, any miss = −1. ESPN says exactly this in its explainer.

The useful question is not "does ESPN punish volume" — in isolation it barely does. A two-point attempt is worth $4p-1$, positive above 25%; a three is $6p-1$, positive above 16.7%. The useful question is ESPN minus Yahoo per attempt, because that is the rank-swing driver:

Shot type ESPN value Yahoo value ESPN − Yahoo Break-even
Two-point attempt $4p-1$ $2p$ $2p-1$ FG% = 50.0%
Three-point attempt $6p-1$ $3p$ $3p-1$ 3P% = 33.3%
Free-throw attempt $2p-1$ \(p\) $p-1$ FT% = 100%

Read the third row again. ESPN taxes every free-throw attempt by \((1-\mathrm{FT\%})\). There is no free-throw volume that is neutral. Luka Dončić shot 10.1 FTA at 78.0% in 2025-26 — that alone is −2.2 FP/G in ESPN relative to Yahoo, the largest FT penalty in the elite pool.

The winners in ESPN are efficient, low-turnover, three-point-volume wings and guards (Mikal Bridges at 11.7 FGA/1.2 FTA/1.0 TO is almost purpose-built for it). The losers are volume bigs who shoot under 50% from the floor and rebound for a living — Bam Adebayo and Karl-Anthony Towns both go negative, because ESPN's rebound weight is 1.0 instead of 1.2 and their shooting term stops subsidising them. The intuition "ESPN rewards efficient bigs" is only half right: it rewards efficient bigs like Jalen Duren (+3.5 shooting) and punishes inefficient high-usage bigs, and the rebound downweight partially cancels the gain for both.

Steals and blocks: the format's real fault line

In points, a steal is 3 or 4 fantasy points and nothing more. Dyson Daniels led the NBA with 2.0 STL/G in 2025-26 and finished 28th of 37 in Yahoo FP/G in the elite pool above. His league-leading skill bought him 6.0 FP — about 17% of his own line, and roughly 3.6 FP of margin over a 0.8-STL replacement. That is a tenth of a starter. Points leagues do not care that the steals are scarce; they only care about the coefficient.

Nine-cat cares about scarcity, because each category is a separate binary contest and aggregation compresses extremes. Within the same 37-player pool:

Quantity mean SD max max z
STL/G 1.11 0.37 2.00 (Daniels) +2.37
BLK/G 0.79 0.61 3.10 (Wembanyama) +3.78
PTS/G 20.98 5.80 33.5 (Dončić) +2.16
Yahoo FP/G 39.62 8.58 62.1 (Jokić) +2.62

(These SDs come from a 37-player convenience sample of high-minute scorers and are narrower than a true 150-player rosterable pool would produce; the ordering is the point, not the decimals.)

Victor Wembanyama is +3.78σ in blocks. No player is +3.78σ in fantasy points — the best in the pool is Jokić at +2.62σ, because a sum of eleven imperfectly-correlated terms always has lower relative variance than its most extreme component. That is the whole format difference in one line: 9-cat lets a single roster slot be more extreme than any player can ever be in aggregate, and lets that extremity decide a contest outright. A points league cannot express that, by construction. It is also why CBS Free's +1 stocks is a materially different game from Yahoo's +3, and why ESPN's own format explainer frames category leagues as rewarding "diverse collection of statistics" while points leagues make accumulation king.

Turnovers

Nine-cat treats TO as a ninth category you can simply concede — punt-TO builds are a standard archetype and cost nothing beyond one of nine slots. Points leagues make TO a permanent linear drag that follows the player everywhere. The size of the drag differs by a factor of two across platforms: Dončić's 4.0 TO/G costs −4.0 on Yahoo and −8.0 on ESPN. Between an ESPN and a Yahoo league, his turnovers alone move 4.0 FP/G — more than the entire ESPN-vs-Yahoo net differential for any player in the table above. High-usage creators (Cade Cunningham 3.7, Jaylen Brown 3.6, Alperen Şengün 3.2) are the ones this hits, and on ESPN it is partly repaid by the doubled assist weight — Cunningham's +4.9 assist gain outruns his −3.7 turnover loss.

Punting does not exist in points leagues

This is structural, not stylistic. Punting works because in H2H categories a 5–4 win counts the same as an 8–1 win, so you can trade a guaranteed loss in FT% for a decisive edge in five others. Formally: 9-cat scoring is a sum of nine step functions of category margins, so margin above the win threshold is worthless and can be reallocated. Points scoring is a single monotone linear functional \(\sum_j w_j x_j\) — there is no threshold, no wasted surplus, and no category you can concede for a rebate. Conceding a stat in points is simply scoring fewer points.

What replaces it is volume, minutes, and games played, in that order, plus a lineup-slot discipline that has no 9-cat equivalent:

  • Minutes are the format's real currency. Points-per-minute is close to flat across the middle of the player pool; total minutes is not. Read 2026-27 NBA minutes projections before 2026-27 points-league rankings, not after.
  • Roster-slot throughput. A points team with one more startable body playing four games this week beats a better team playing three, every time, and there is no offsetting category cost.
  • Streaming is unambiguously good. In 9-cat, streaming a low-efficiency body actively damages FG%/FT%; in points, any positive-FP body in an empty slot is free.

Games played: why it dominates points and merely matters in 9-cat

Nine-cat H2H normalises much of the schedule automatically, because per-game rates decide FG%, FT%, and ratio contests regardless of volume, and roto leagues typically cap games at 82 per position (Yahoo, ESPN) — Yahoo's H2H default carries no games cap at all. Points leagues sum an unbounded total. The result is that availability is not a tiebreaker, it is a primary term.

In 9-cat those same three players are not remotely equivalent, because Bridges wins you nothing and Ball wins you 3PM and AST. This is why 2026-27 points-league rankings uses a draft index of \(\mathrm{FP/G}\times\sqrt{\mathrm{GP}/82}\) rather than pure per-game value, and it is the single largest reason a points board and a category board should never be the same list. Efficiency: ratio in 9-cat, linear in points

The mathematical difference is real and produces reversals, not just re-weightings.

Nine-cat FG% is a team-level ratio \(\sum_i m_i / \sum_i a_i\). A player's marginal impact is approximately

\[ \Delta \approx \frac{a_i\,(p_i - P)}{A + a_i} \]

— attempts multiplied by the gap from your team's rate. Three consequences: it is volume-weighted (a 67% centre on 4 FGA is nearly inert), it is context-dependent (the reference \(P\) is your own roster, so the same player helps a bad-shooting team more), and it is non-additive (you cannot pre-compute a player's FG% value in isolation). See Percentage categories and volume.

ESPN points is \(\mathrm{FGM}-\mathrm{FG_{miss}}\): an absolute, context-free, additive constant per player. No reference rate, no teammates, no punt.

The reversal case is free throws. Compare 2025-26:

Player FTA FT% FT misses/G ESPN FT term 9-cat impact vs .780 ref
Zion Williamson 7.5 .716 2.13 −2.13 −0.48
Evan Mobley 4.6 .606 1.81 −1.81 −0.80

ESPN says Zion Williamson is the worse FT liability (he misses more shots). Nine-cat says Evan Mobley is roughly 1.7× worse (his rate is far further from reference). Both are correct within their own arithmetic, and they disagree about which player to avoid. The general rule: ESPN prices raw misses, 9-cat prices the rate gap times volume. Low-volume terrible shooters (Dyson Daniels, 1.6 FTA at 61.5%) are close to harmless in ESPN points and only mildly damaging in 9-cat; high-volume mediocre shooters are the ones ESPN quietly hates.

Critical finding: this vault's two "points" boards are not the same formula

2026-27 points-league rankings is computed on the Yahoo formula — \(\mathrm{PTS}+1.2\,\mathrm{REB}+1.5\,\mathrm{AST}+3\,\mathrm{STL}+3\,\mathrm{BLK}-\mathrm{TO}\) — and states so explicitly. Meanwhile a large number of individual player dossiers carry a bolded "ESPN-default points:" verdict line, and many cite ESPN's April 2026 way-too-early points board as a market anchor. These are different scoring systems and the verdicts are not interchangeable.

Worse, the discrepancy is currently uncloseable from the vault's own data. In Data/player-projections-2026-07-18.csv, of the 262 rows that clear the 20 GP / 15 MPG screen, only 54 carry fga, fta, fg_pct, and ft_pct — the four fields ESPN's formula requires. Coverage is not random; it is by team desk:

Desks with complete shot data Desks with none
DAL (11/11), MEM (11/11), DEN (8/8), MIL (8/8), PHX (8/8), LAL (7/7), FA (1/1) the other 24 teams, 0 rows each

So no ESPN-default board can be produced for 79% of the projection pool, and the 54-player subpool that can be scored is a documentation artifact, not a talent tier. Within that subpool the Yahoo and ESPN draft indices correlate at ρ = 0.993 with a maximum swing of 5 slots — reassuring, but it is a sample of six teams' rosters and cannot be extrapolated.

Where the discrepancy bites hardest, based on the archetype analysis above:

  • Overstated by an ESPN verdict on a Yahoo board: high-FGA sub-.470 shooters and heavy free-throw merchants — Jalen Green (.435 on 16.1 FGA projected), Bam Adebayo, Karl-Anthony Towns, Luka Dončić, Jaylen Brown.
  • Understated: efficient low-turnover bigs and 3PM-volume wings — Daniel Gafford (.660 projected FG%), Walker Kessler, Chet Holmgren, Mikal Bridges, Payton Pritchard, Reed Sheppard.
  • Structurally understated on the Yahoo board only: elite stocks specialists, whose STL and BLK are worth 4 rather than 3 — Victor Wembanyama, Dyson Daniels, Ausar Thompson, Donovan Clingan, Myles Turner.

Remediation: the 24 team desks should backfill fga/fta/fg_pct/ft_pct into the projection export before draft season, at which point a genuine ESPN-default column can sit beside the Yahoo one. Until then, every dossier line that says "ESPN-default points" should be read as a directional claim, and the board rank next to it as a Yahoo number.

The decision, for a 10-or-12-team autumn draft

If you want the draft to be decided by preparation and in-season management, take 9-cat: punt architecture, ratio management, and scarcity pricing give a prepared manager far more edge surface. If you want the season decided by roster throughput and injury luck, take points — it is a simpler, higher-variance, more football-like game where the winning skill is squeezing games played out of thirteen roster slots. And whichever you pick, read the scoring settings screen before you rank anything: the CBS-versus-Yahoo stocks gap (+1 vs +3) is a larger distortion than the entire ESPN-versus-Yahoo differential analysed above.

Open questions

  • Sleeper's and Fantrax's numeric defaults are not published in official documentation that survives a fetch. Both rows in the top table are sourced from editorial/primer text rather than product docs and should be verified on the league-creation screen before being treated as fact.
  • The z-score and rank-swing figures here use a 37-player convenience pool of 2025-26 top-100 scorers. A proper 150-player rosterable pool would widen every SD and probably widen the maximum ESPN-vs-Yahoo swing beyond 7 slots — that recomputation has not been done.
  • Whether 2026-27 platform defaults survive the offseason unchanged is unverified; ESPN's explainer was last updated 2025-09-11 and CBS's public Free rules were still labelled 2025-26 at cutoff.
  • The 9-cat FG% impact model above uses a fixed reference rate. A proper treatment prices impact against your own drafted roster's running rate, which makes efficiency value path-dependent within a draft — no note in this vault currently models that.
