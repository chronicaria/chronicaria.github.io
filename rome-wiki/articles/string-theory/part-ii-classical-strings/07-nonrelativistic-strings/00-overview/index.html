    What a plucked string already knows

    Pluck a violin string and it hums a definite pitch. That single everyday fact contains, in embryo, every idea we will later dress up relativistically. The point of this module is to earn those ideas on home turf — pure PDEs on an interval — so that when the relativistic string arrives, nothing about the mechanics is new; only the geometry is.

    Strip a real string down to three ingredients: uniform mass density μ0\mu_0μ0​, constant tension T0T_0T0​, and a small transverse displacement y(t,x)y(t,x)y(t,x) on 0≤x≤a0\le x\le a0≤x≤a. Everything follows:

    1. Curvature drives the motion. A short segment feels a net transverse pull toward its concave side, proportional to how sharply it bends: the vertical force is ∝∂x2y\propto \partial_x^2 y∝∂x2​y. Newton's law then reads μ0 ∂t2y=T0 ∂x2y\mu_0\,\partial_t^2 y = T_0\,\partial_x^2 yμ0​∂t2​y=T0​∂x2​y — the wave equation, with speed v0=T0/μ0v_0=\sqrt{T_0/\mu_0}v0​=T0​/μ0​​. Tighter or lighter string, faster waves. This is 7.1 The wave equation on an interval.

    2. Boundary data is physics, not bookkeeping. The bulk equation cannot say what happens at the two ends, and the choice fixes the mode shapes and decides whether momentum is conserved. Dirichlet (glue the end to a wall, y=0y=0y=0) versus Neumann (thread the end on a frictionless ring, ∂xy=0\partial_x y=0∂x​y=0) are the only two ways to make the endpoint term in the varied action vanish — the action tells you which conditions are legal instead of you imposing them. That same boundary term is where D-branes will later hide. This is 7.2 Fixed versus free endpoints.

    3. Modes are eigenvectors; frequencies are eigenvalues. Standing waves are the eigenfunctions of −∂x2-\partial_x^2−∂x2​ on the interval. Both boundary conditions quantize the wavenumber to kn=nπ/ak_n=n\pi/akn​=nπ/a, giving the same harmonic frequency tower ωn=nπv0/a\omega_n = n\pi v_0/aωn​=nπv0​/a — the reason a plucked string sounds like one pitch with integer overtones rather than noise. The only difference is sine profiles for Dirichlet against cosine profiles for Neumann, plus one extra Neumann mode at ω0=0\omega_0=0ω0​=0: a rigid transverse drift of the whole string. This is 7.3 Normal modes and frequencies.

    4. Energy diagonalizes. The conserved energy E=∫(12μ0y˙2+12T0y′2) dxE=\int(\tfrac12\mu_0\dot y^2+\tfrac12 T_0 y'^2)\,dxE=∫(21​μ0​y˙​2+21​T0​y′2)dx is a sum of kinetic and elastic pieces. In the mode basis its cross-terms die by orthogonality, leaving one independent harmonic oscillator per mode: E=∑n14μ0a ωn2An2E=\sum_n \tfrac14\mu_0 a\,\omega_n^2 A_n^2E=∑n​41​μ0​aωn2​An2​. This is 7.4 Energy in string oscillations.

    One sentence carries forward: a string is a collection of decoupled oscillators — one per standing wave — and its energy is their sum. When we quantize, each of those classical oscillators becomes a quantum oscillator with its own creation and annihilation operators, and the string's excitations become particles. Everything downstream is that sentence, made relativistic.

    Two methods, one answer: we derive the wave equation twice — once from Newton's force balance, once by varying an action — because the action route is the one that survives into relativity, and it delivers the complete menu of legal boundary conditions in the same stroke, where Newton needs a separate force model (a wall, a massless ring) for each kind of endpoint.

    Exercise

    Close the notes and reproduce, on a blank page, the spine of the whole module. Do all five from scratch:

    1. Derive the wave equation for small transverse oscillations of a string with tension T0T_0T0​ and density μ0\mu_0μ0​, and identify the wave speed v0v_0v0​.
    2. Explain the endpoint term that appears when you vary the action, and why it forces either y=0y=0y=0 or ∂xy=0\partial_x y=0∂x​y=0 at each end.
    3. Impose fixed (Dirichlet) endpoints y(t,0)=y(t,a)=0y(t,0)=y(t,a)=0y(t,0)=y(t,a)=0. Find the mode profiles and the frequencies ωn\omega_nωn​, and say why n=0n=0n=0 is excluded.
    4. Switch to free (Neumann) endpoints ∂xy=0\partial_x y=0∂x​y=0 at both ends. State exactly what changes in the profiles, what stays identical in the frequencies, and what new n=0n=0n=0 solution appears.
    5. Write the conserved energy and show why one normal mode behaves like one harmonic-oscillator coordinate.
