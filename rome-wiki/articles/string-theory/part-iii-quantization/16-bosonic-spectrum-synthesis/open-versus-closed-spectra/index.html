    Open versus closed spectra

    An open string has one ladder of vibrations and mass formula \alpha' M^2 = N^\perp - 1 ; a closed string has two ladders, left- and right-moving, locked together by level matching, giving \tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2 = N^\perp - 1 = \bar N^\perp - 1 .

    One ladder or two

    A guitar string with fixed ends can only vibrate in standing waves: the left- and right-travelling waves are the same wave, bounced back and forth. That is the open string — one set of vibrations. A rubber band has no ends, so a wave can circle it clockwise or counter-clockwise, and the two directions are genuinely independent. That is the closed string — two sets of vibrations.

    Quantizing each vibration turns "how hard the string is buzzing" into "how heavy the spacetime particle is." The number operator N⊥N^\perpN⊥ counts the buzz; one line of algebra converts it to mass. Everything below is bookkeeping on that idea:

    • one ladder →\to→ open string →\to→ first rung a photon;
    • two ladders →\to→ closed string →\to→ first rung a graviton.

    Same machine, different boundary conditions, different force of nature.

    Shared setup: from vibrations to masses

    After Light-cone gauge the only independent quantum operators are the D−2D-2D−2 transverse coordinates XIX^IXI, I=2,…,D−1I=2,\dots,D-1I=2,…,D−1. Gauge fixing has already solved for X+X^+X+ and X−X^-X− in terms of these, so no other degrees of freedom survive. Each XIX^IXI is a free 2D field, and its Fourier modes obey [\alpha_m^I,\alpha_n^J]=m\,\delta^{IJ}\delta_{m+n,0}. \tag{1} These are harmonic-oscillator ladder operators in disguise. Setting anI=αnI/na_n^I=\alpha_n^I/\sqrt nanI​=αnI​/n​ recovers the textbook algebra [anI,amJ†]=δnmδIJ[a_n^I,a_m^{J\dagger}]=\delta_{nm}\delta^{IJ}[anI​,amJ†​]=δnm​δIJ, so α−nI\alpha_{-n}^Iα−nI​ creates a quantum of vibration in direction III at mode number nnn. Because only spacelike transverse indices appear, the Fock space built on the momentum vacuum ∣0;p⟩|0;p\rangle∣0;p⟩ (shorthand for the light-cone ground state ∣p+,p⃗T⟩|p^+,\vec p_T\rangle∣p+,p​T​⟩, with αnI∣0;p⟩=0\alpha_n^I|0;p\rangle=0αnI​∣0;p⟩=0 for n>0n>0n>0) has no negative-norm states. The price is that Lorentz invariance must be rechecked afterward, and that check is what forces D=26D=26D=26 (Critical dimension D=26).

    The mass-shell relation in mostly-plus signature is M2=−pμpμM^2=-p_\mu p^\muM2=−pμ​pμ. Solving the light-cone constraint for p−p^-p− turns this into a count of how excited the string is, weighted by mode number: N^\perp = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_I n\,a_n^{I\dagger}a_n^I = \sum_{n>0}\sum_I \alpha_{-n}^I\alpha_n^I. \tag{2} N⊥N^\perpN⊥ is the level: a mode-nnn quantum costs nnn units. The extra factor of nnn cancels the 1/n1/\sqrt n1/n​ in each anI=αnI/na_n^I=\alpha_n^I/\sqrt nanI​=αnI​/n​, so N⊥N^\perpN⊥ measures total vibrational energy, not merely the number of quanta.

    Normal-ordering the operators leaves a finite zero-point shift, the same Casimir regularization that turns the divergent zero-point sum 12∑nn\tfrac12\sum_n n21​∑n​n into a finite value. The bare series regularizes to ζ(−1)=−1/12\zeta(-1)=-1/12ζ(−1)=−1/12. Per transverse direction the zero-point energy carries an extra 12\tfrac1221​, contributing 12ζ(−1)=−1/24\tfrac12\zeta(-1)=-1/2421​ζ(−1)=−1/24; summing over the D−2=24D-2=24D−2=24 transverse directions at D=26D=26D=26 makes the shift exactly −1-1−1 (Normal ordering constant). That one constant produces the tachyon below and lifts the photon and graviton to exactly massless above.

    Open string: one ladder

    An open string obeys Neumann boundary conditions ∂σXI=0\partial_\sigma X^I=0∂σ​XI=0 at σ=0,π\sigma=0,\piσ=0,π, which reflect any travelling wave back on itself. The result is a standing wave with a single oscillator set αnI\alpha_n^IαnI​: X^I(\tau,\sigma)=x_0^I+2\alpha'\,p^I\tau + i\sqrt{2\alpha'}\sum_{n\neq0}\frac1n\,\alpha_n^I\,\cos n\sigma\,e^{-in\tau}. \tag{3} The cos⁡nσ\cos n\sigmacosnσ is the standing-wave profile forced by the two reflecting ends; the center of mass drifts as x0I+2α′pIτx_0^I+2\alpha' p^I\taux0I​+2α′pIτ. That linear coefficient is fixed, not chosen: the physical momentum is pI=12πα′∫0πX˙I dσp^I=\frac1{2\pi\alpha'}\int_0^\pi \dot X^I\,d\sigmapI=2πα′1​∫0π​X˙Idσ, and only the coefficient 2α′2\alpha'2α′ makes the oscillator terms integrate to zero and leave pIp^IpI on the left. This is exactly where a factor of 2 is easy to drop — the zero mode is α0I=2α′ pI\alpha_0^I=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^Iα0I​=2α′​pI, so the linear term carries 2α′ α0I=2α′pI\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,\alpha_0^I=2\alpha' p^I2α′​α0I​=2α′pI, not 2α′ pI\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^I2α′​pI.

    Quantizing and normal-ordering gives the open mass formula: \boxed{\;\alpha'M^2=N^\perp-1\;} \tag{4} Mass2^22 climbs in equal steps of 1/α′1/\alpha'1/α′ as you stack oscillators; the −1-1−1 is the zero-point energy that drags the ground state below zero. The lowest rungs:

    Closed string: two ladders, locked together

    A closed string is periodic, XI(τ,σ)=XI(τ,σ+2π)X^I(\tau,\sigma)=X^I(\tau,\sigma+2\pi)XI(τ,σ)=XI(τ,σ+2π), with no ends to reflect waves. So the left-movers αˉnI\bar\alpha_n^IαˉnI​ (functions of τ+σ\tau+\sigmaτ+σ) and right-movers αnI\alpha_n^IαnI​ (functions of τ−σ\tau-\sigmaτ−σ) are independent — two full ladders. The mass draws on both: solving the light-cone constraint for p−p^-p− collects a level from each sector, so \boxed{\;M^2=\frac{2}{\alpha'}\bigl(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2\bigr)\;} \tag{5} at D=26D=26D=26, where each sector contributes its own zero-point −1-1−1. This is the genuine closed mass formula — a sum over the two towers, not a readout from one.

    Level matching is a separate constraint. Solving for p−p^-p− actually produces two expressions for the same zero mode α0−=αˉ0−\alpha_0^-=\bar\alpha_0^-α0−​=αˉ0−​ — one built from right-movers, giving L0⊥−1L_0^\perp-1L0⊥​−1, the other from left-movers, giving Lˉ0⊥−1\bar L_0^\perp-1Lˉ0⊥​−1. Since the two must describe the same number, L0⊥=Lˉ0⊥L_0^\perp=\bar L_0^\perpL0⊥​=Lˉ0⊥​, hence \boxed{\;N^\perp=\bar N^\perp\quad(\textbf{level matching})\;} \tag{6} This is not a choice. The loop has no marked starting point, so the rigid rotation σ↦σ+σ0\sigma\mapsto\sigma+\sigma_0σ↦σ+σ0​ is an unfixable gauge symmetry. Its generator is the worldsheet momentum around the loop, P=L_0^\perp-\bar L_0^\perp=N^\perp-\bar N^\perp, \tag{7} where the center-of-mass pieces α′4p2\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}p^24α′​p2 in L0⊥L_0^\perpL0⊥​ and Lˉ0⊥\bar L_0^\perpLˉ0⊥​ are equal and cancel. Physical states must be invariant, P∣ψ⟩=0P|\psi\rangle=0P∣ψ⟩=0: a state more excited clockwise than counter-clockwise secretly depends on where you called "the start" of the loop, and no such point exists, so the state is a gauge copy rather than a physical one.

    Once (6) holds, N⊥+Nˉ⊥=2N⊥N^\perp+\bar N^\perp=2N^\perpN⊥+Nˉ⊥=2N⊥ and (5) collapses to α′4M2=N⊥−1=Nˉ⊥−1\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2=N^\perp-1=\bar N^\perp-14α′​M2=N⊥−1=Nˉ⊥−1 — the mass in terms of a single level, with the closed prefactor 4/α′4/\alpha'4/α′ instead of the open 1/α′1/\alpha'1/α′. Read (5) as an average of two open-string levels, α′4M2=12[(N⊥−1)+(Nˉ⊥−1)]\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2=\tfrac12[(N^\perp-1)+(\bar N^\perp-1)]4α′​M2=21​[(N⊥−1)+(Nˉ⊥−1)], not a literal sum of two open M2M^2M2's: that naive sum would give only 1α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)\tfrac{1}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2)α′1​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2), half the closed value. The missing factor of 2 is the same zero-mode effect that produces the factor of 4.

    Where the factor of 4 comes from

    The open and closed mass formulas differ by a factor of 4, and it is not a normal-ordering effect — it is pure zero-mode normalization. The momentum enters each expansion through the zero mode: α0I=2α′ pI  (open),α0I=αˉ0I=α′2 pI  (closed).\alpha_0^I=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^I\ \ (\text{open}),\qquad \alpha_0^I=\bar\alpha_0^I=\sqrt{\tfrac{\alpha'}{2}}\,p^I\ \ (\text{closed}).α0I​=2α′​pI  (open),α0I​=αˉ0I​=2α′​​pI  (closed). The closed string spans twice the parameter range (σ∈[0,2π]\sigma\in[0,2\pi]σ∈[0,2π] versus [0,π][0,\pi][0,π]), which halves the momentum carried per unit zero mode — a factor of 2 in α0\alpha_0α0​, hence a factor of 4 in M2M^2M2. Re-derive this factor from the zero modes; never trust it from memory.

    Open strings give gauge fields, closed strings give gravity, and both start with a tachyon that says the bosonic vacuum is unstable. See Photon from open strings and Graviton from closed strings for the massless rungs in detail.

    Open questions

    • The normal-ordering shift is imported as a result here; the cutoff derivation of −1/12-1/12−1/12 and the D=26D=26D=26 that pins it to exactly −1-1−1 live in Normal ordering constant and Critical dimension D=26.
    • Light-cone gauge shows only the physical D−2D-2D−2 polarizations. The spacetime gauge symmetries of the photon, graviton, and BBB-field are invisible here and reappear in the covariant/CFT description, e.g. Vertex operators and physical-state conditions.

    Exercise

    The closed-string formula (5) can be assembled from two copies of the open formula (4). Suppose you had never derived the factor of 4, only the open result α′Mopen2=N⊥−1\alpha'M^2_{\text{open}}=N^\perp-1α′Mopen2​=N⊥−1 and the fact that a closed string is "two open ladders glued at the zero mode." Reconstruct the closed mass formula: (a) write M2M^2M2 as the sum of a right-moving and a left-moving open-like contribution, (b) fix the overall constant using the zero-mode relation α0I,closed=α′/2 pI\alpha_0^{I,\text{closed}}=\sqrt{\alpha'/2}\,p^Iα0I,closed​=α′/2​pI versus α0I,open=2α′ pI\alpha_0^{I,\text{open}}=\sqrt{2\alpha'}\,p^Iα0I,open​=2α′​pI, and (c) state the ground-state mass and confirm the closed tachyon is heavier than the open one.

    Solution

    (a) The closed Hamiltonian collects both ladders, each carrying the same zero-point shift −1-1−1 at D=26D=26D=26. Symmetrizing over the two sectors, α′4M2=12[(N⊥−1)+(Nˉ⊥−1)]=12(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2),\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2=\tfrac12\bigl[(N^\perp-1)+(\bar N^\perp-1)\bigr]=\tfrac12(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2),4α′​M2=21​[(N⊥−1)+(Nˉ⊥−1)]=21​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2), i.e. M2=2α′(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2)M^2=\tfrac{2}{\alpha'}(N^\perp+\bar N^\perp-2)M2=α′2​(N⊥+Nˉ⊥−2). Level matching sets N⊥=Nˉ⊥N^\perp=\bar N^\perpN⊥=Nˉ⊥, so the two sectors give identical values and we may write the mass either way: α′4M2=N⊥−1=Nˉ⊥−1\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2=N^\perp-1=\bar N^\perp-14α′​M2=N⊥−1=Nˉ⊥−1.

    (b) The factor of 4 is fixed by the zero mode. Mass2^22 is built from α0Iα0I\alpha_0^I\alpha_0^Iα0I​α0I​. The ratio of closed to open zero modes is α′/2/2α′=12\sqrt{\alpha'/2}\big/\sqrt{2\alpha'}=\tfrac12α′/2​/2α′​=21​, so a fixed physical momentum pIp^IpI appears with a coefficient 4 times smaller inside M2M^2M2 for the closed string — equivalently, extracting the same M2M^2M2 requires multiplying the level by 4. Hence the prefactor 4/α′4/\alpha'4/α′ in the closed formula versus 1/α′1/\alpha'1/α′ in the open one. (Root cause: the closed string spans σ∈[0,2π]\sigma\in[0,2\pi]σ∈[0,2π], twice the open range, halving the momentum per zero mode.)

    (c) Ground state: N⊥=Nˉ⊥=0N^\perp=\bar N^\perp=0N⊥=Nˉ⊥=0. Open: α′M2=−1⇒M2=−1/α′\alpha'M^2=-1\Rightarrow M^2=-1/\alpha'α′M2=−1⇒M2=−1/α′. Closed: α′4M2=−1⇒M2=−4/α′\tfrac{\alpha'}{4}M^2=-1\Rightarrow M^2=-4/\alpha'4α′​M2=−1⇒M2=−4/α′. The closed tachyon is exactly four times heavier (in M2M^2M2) than the open one — the factor of 4 again — and both are negative, so both vacua are unstable.
